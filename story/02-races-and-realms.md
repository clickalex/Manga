# 02 — RACES AND REALMS (Complete Roster)

**Design rule: no race is a monster. Every race is a civilization.** Each race has a culture, a law, a cuisine, a festival, a prejudice it carries, and a prejudice other races hold about it. That's what makes 49 races feel real instead of decorative.

**World map logic:** Vael is one world shaped like a **nine-pointed crown** (the world is literally the shape of the shattered Aurelion). Eight realms ring a center: **the Seams** — the in-between lands where no realm fully claims. The Veil runs along the seams; RiftFalls are places the Veil is tearing.

## DEEP PROFILES — The Pillar Races (the 15 the media will feature first)

### 1. Humans (Terrania)
- **Look:** Endless variation. Short lives, fast art. Cities of a hundred competing styles.
- **Society & law:** No single human government — the **Hundred Courts**: ~200 nations that *permanently debate*. Law is negotiated, revised, litigated. The human genius is *institutional invention*: every other realm copies human government and fails, because humans are the only race that expects the law to be wrong.
- **Cuisine & festival:** Street-food culture; the **Festival of Open Doors** (every household feeds a stranger once a year — the moral heart of the race).
- **Prejudice they carry:** "Mortals are temporary." Every long-lived race says it. Humans have built a whole culture around being temporary *on purpose*.
- **Story role:** Kaelen & Senna; Empress Aeloria's arc (the realm that must give up its crowns); the Openbloods canary.
- **Manga art direction:** Warm ambers, iron gray, crowded bustling pages; human pages are *busy* — the world's visual baseline.
- **Card game hook:** The **Hundredfold** archetype — small, cheap, combo-heavy, can borrow a keyword from any card on the field. The "any-race" deck.
- **PC game hook:** The starter race; class-agnostic, stat-agnostic, skill-maxed.

### 2. High Elves (Sylvaris)
- **Look:** Tall, silver-green hair, long-veined skin that shows *growth-rings* like a tree's (they visibly age in rings — the art tells time).
- **Society & law:** Time-cities built in concentric centuries. **The Centuries** as calendar: a contract can be "three centuries" long and that's normal. Memory is legal property; to lose a memory is a crime *you commit against yourself*.
- **Cuisine & festival:** Century-wine (one vintage per hundred years, named for the hundred it remembers); the **Ring-Counting** festival, where every elf marks a new growth-ring and says the name of someone they outlived.
- **Prejudice they carry:** Patience as superiority. They cannot understand why the orcs' "one lifetime" matters as much as their "ten."
- **Story role:** Season 1 home; Lyra; the Withering King; the grove-council law.
- **Manga art direction:** Silver-green, deep forest light shafts, *quiet* pages — the contrast to human busyness.
- **Card game hook:** **Everbloom** — slow ramp, huge late-game, "century" tokens that persist across turns (the long-game archetype).
- **PC game hook:** The ranger/time-weaver class family; the "100-year plan" questline.

### 3. Shadow Elves (Sylvaris under-grove)
- **Look:** Pale, moth-winged hair, cave-garden light. The high elves call them "the quiet ones" and are privately terrified of them.
- **Society & law:** Exiles who *chose* decay over denial. They keep the **Under-Grove** — where every elf's dead things are composted. Their law: *what ends feeds what begins; to deny an ending is to poison the beginning.* They are the realm's secret sages, and they are the only elves who understand what Vaelthorn is.
- **Story role:** Lyra's mentors; they name the Unraveling first.
- **Manga art direction:** Under-ground light, moth-gold on deep green; the quietest pages in the book.

### 4. Deep Dwarves (Khazadûm)
- **Look:** Stocky, multi-braided beards, stone-scarred hands. A dwarf's beard is a *family ledger* — each braid records a kin.
- **Society & law:** **The Mountain-Family law**: the mountain is not property, it is a *relative*. You do not mine the mountain; you *ask it*. The mountain's "consent" is read in stone-song (resonance of the bedrock). The Reforgers' mining of raw Sigil-stone is not theft to them — it's *fracture of a relative*. (This is why the Khazadûm arc is about family, not resources.)
- **Cuisine & festival:** Forge-bread baked in smithy heat; the **Anvil-Naming** festival, where every year the mountain "names" a new boulder (a child-blessing for the stone).
- **Prejudice they carry:** "Surface folk don't belong to anything." (They are wrong, and it costs them the Seam-trade.)
- **Story role:** Grimm; Grand-Anvil Khazur; the "who is the mountain's family" arc; golem Heartforge.
- **Manga art direction:** Iron and ember, deep blue stone-light, wide horizontal panels for the mountain's scale.
- **Card game hook:** **Everburn** — the tank/armor archetype; "the mountain is a relative" = your permanents are a *family* (linked: one hurt, all defend).
- **PC game hook:** The forge/stone class family; the "ask the mountain" interaction (a consent-check gameplay system — the theme in mechanics).

### 5. Orcs (Ulgar)
- **Look:** Massive, scar-ornamented (scars are *written* — each is a clause of an oath), bone-and-iron regalia. An orc's body is a library of promises.
- **Society & law:** **Blood-clan honor-law**: an oath is a physical mark; breaking it is not punishable, because a broken oath *unmakes* the breaker in the clan's eyes — the true death is *not being counted*. The **Ultravote**: when a clan-split can't be resolved, all clans gather and one throat speaks for all. The Barrens' genius is *enforced accountability*: you cannot hide a broken word from your own body.
- **Cuisine & festival:** Fire-cauldron feasts where the recipe is the story (each dish names the oath that was kept); the **Scar-Reading** festival, where elders read each other's scars aloud and the whole clan *remembers* the promise with them.
- **Prejudice they carry:** "Soft lives make soft oaths." (They underestimate humans completely.)
- **Story role:** Morgatha; War-Chief Ulrik the Unbroken; the "code vs. one life" arc that *reforms* the code.
- **Manga art direction:** Rust red, bone white, thunder gray; heavy-ink pages, close-ups of scar-text as visual storytelling.
- **Card game hook:** **Titanvein** — the oath-armor archetype: you can *spend* oaths (permanent commitments) for power; break one, and the card leaves your library (the theme is the mechanic).
- **PC game hook:** The warrior class family; the honor-system as a *reputation* gameplay layer (the world remembers your broken oaths).

### 6. Goblins (Ulgar swarms)
- **Look:** 1 m, many-armed (four to six), marketplace-clever, pockets *everywhere*.
- **Society & law:** **Swarmlink** — a goblin is never an individual in law; a *swarm* is. A goblin's crime is the swarm's crime; a swarm's debt is every goblin's debt. Everyone hates them (they swarm, they bargain, they show up *anywhere*), and everyone needs them (they are the only couriers that can cross a Rift).
- **Cuisine & festival:** Street-market everything; the **Big Bargain**, a once-a-century trade fair where every race is *required* to lose one deal (the festival's joke: the goblins make sure you lose it).
- **Story role:** The logistics heart; the comedy duo with kobolds; the team's messengers.
- **Manga art direction:** Fast, chaotic panel-rhythm; the goblin pages *move*.
- **Card game hook:** **Swarmlink** — swarm tokens, shared abilities, "the swarm pays" (life-payment) mechanics.

### 7. Merfolk (Thalassa)
- **Look:** Tide-cities in coral-crown regalia, scale-armor, bioluminescent markings that *change with their mood-law* (a merfolk's markings are legally legible — you cannot lie by face, but you can by *silence*).
- **Society & law:** **Chorus-parliament**: a law only binds when it is *sung true* — in harmony, by enough voices. A solo voice can propose; only a chorus can pass. The "first song" (the Leviathan's dream-song) is the *first law*, and it is gone.
- **Cuisine & festival:** Tide-cuisine (everything that moves); the **Deep-Listening**, a festival where the whole realm goes silent for a day and *listens to the Leviathan breathe* (the realm's equivalent of a national heartbeat-check).
- **Prejudice they carry:** "Surface folk are deaf." (They can't hear the tide, so they can't hear the law.)
- **Story role:** Nereia; Thalarch Maelis; the chorus Re-Oath (the first thousand-voice oath).
- **Manga art direction:** Deep teal, bioluminescent cyan, *flowing* panel lines; underwater pages are the visual opposite of the sky realm.
- **Card game hook:** **Tidesong** — the tempo archetype: "chorus" tokens (multiple cards must resolve together to trigger); the song is a *combo you build with others*.
- **PC game hook:** The tide-mage class family; the "chorus" mechanic (multiplayer/crew systems where a spell needs 3+ voices to resolve).

### 8. Demons (Emberfall)
- **Look:** Horned, fire-veined, robes of contract-gold (the gold is *ink* — their clothing is literally written). A demon's word is a physical thing: spoken oaths glow in the air until fulfilled or burned.
- **Society & law:** **Oathfire**: a contract is sacred; breaking one does not punish you with a fine, it *burns you* — the oath you broke becomes the fire that unmakes your Sigil. The **Fire-Court**: seven Archdukes, and a public trial of oaths where *every demon in the realm testifies to what the oaths were worth to them*. Hell here is not chaos — it is the most *accountable* society in the world, and it is *furious* about it.
- **Cuisine & festival:** Fire-kitchen (everything is cooked by *oath-heat* — a dish is only as good as the promise behind it); the **First Contract**, a festival reenacting the first oath ever broken (a comedy — the first demon to break an oath did it *by accident*, and the whole realm still jokes about it).
- **Prejudice they carry:** "Outside oaths are cheap." (They are horrified by human contract law and find it *adorable*.)
- **Story role:** Malachar, the Oath-Eater (the Usurper who made a religion of breaking); the "villain defeated by their own law" arc — the Fire-Court re-contracts him.
- **Manga art direction:** Fire-ember, obsidian black, gold ink; the glowing oaths are *visible text in the art* — the panel literally shows the contract.
- **Card game hook:** **Oathflame** — the contract archetype: cards that are *oaths* (commitments that trigger on conditions); break your own card's condition and it burns (destroyed + you take damage). The "your deck is a legal document" theme.
- **PC game hook:** The fire/contract class family; the "oath" gameplay layer (quests you *commit* to on-screen; breaking them has world consequences).

### 9. Djinn (Emberfall)
- **Look:** Smoke-and-flame figures, word-carved armor (their armor is *grammar*). A djinn's body is a sentence; when they speak, the words are *load-bearing*.
- **Society & law:** **Wish-engineers**: a wish is a contract with *clauses*. A djinn does not grant wishes — they *draft* them. The Reforgers' "Scribe" is a djinn who discovered that a *perfectly drafted* wish can be a weapon (a wish with no escape clause). The comedy and the horror are the same thing.
- **Story role:** The Reforgers' Scribe; a recurring "the law is a weapon" motif.
- **Card game hook:** **Wordfire** — the clause archetype: cards with *conditions and exceptions* written in the flavor text that are *mechanically real*.

### 10. Angels / Seraphim (Seraphel)
- **Look:** Winged orders (2/4/6 wings — wing-count is rank), law-etched halos (the halo is a *visible statute*). A seraphim's halo literally shows the law they enforce.
- **Society & law:** **The Light-Orders**: purity-as-duty. An angel's law is *internal* — they do not enforce law on others, they *are* the law. The problem: a law that has no *reason* left, only *form*. Archon Seravon is the apex of this: the purest man in the sky realm, and the most dangerous, because he has *genuinely forgotten why* the light protects anyone.
- **Cuisine & festival:** Dawn-bread (baked at first light, the only food an angel "enjoys"); the **Wing-Counting**, a festival where every angel counts the wings of the realm (a census of *order* — when the count is off, something is *wrong*, and it is always wrong when the count is off).
- **Prejudice they carry:** "The in-between is a flaw." (This is the prejudice the whole story is against.)
- **Story role:** Seravon (the "good man with the wrong answer"); the Purity arc; Azrael's trial.
- **Manga art direction:** White-gold, dawn pink, storm blue; the *cleanest* pages in the book — the visual opposite of the Seams.
- **Card game hook:** **Seraphic** — the purity/efficient archetype: high power, low cost, but *cannot be played with "impure" cards in the deck* (a deck-constraint archetype).
- **PC game hook:** The light/wing class family; the "purity" build (high power, low flexibility — the theme in the stat block).

### 11. Fallen Angels (Seraphel exile faction)
- **Look:** Gray-winged, dimmed halos (the halo is a *cracked statute*). A fallen angel's halo shows the oath they broke.
- **Society & law:** **The Wandering Order**: oaths broken *to protect*. A fallen angel is not punished — they are *released*. The Wandering Order's law: *the wing is broken because it protected something; that is not a wound, that is a record.* They are the moral heart of Seraphel and the sky realm's open shame.
- **Story role:** Azrael; the "is an oath that demands cruelty an oath at all" question; the gray wings in the Re-Oath sky.
- **Manga art direction:** Gray-gold, cracked halo-light; the *saddest* pages in the book.
- **Card game hook:** **Seraphic dimmed** — the oath-broken archetype: high power, *visible cost* (the card's cost is the oath it represents; spending it reveals the oath to all players).

### 12. Vampires (Nocturne)
- **Look:** Death-nobility, afterlife manners, second-life elegance. A vampire's "blood" is not a curse — it is a *second life with a second set of manners*. They do not hunt; they *transact* (blood is currency, given freely, the afterlife's economy of *exchange*).
- **Society & law:** **The Regency Council**: the dead's government. The "Second Life" is a *society*, not a monster. The central question of the race: *can the dead love the sun?* Viv's whole arc is the vampire answer: *yes, but you have to earn it, and it has to be given, not taken.*
- **Cuisine & festival:** Night-banquets (everything is *given*, never taken); the **First Dawn**, a festival where the vampires *do not go out* (a communal act of restraint — the realm's equivalent of a prayer).
- **Prejudice they carry:** "The living are too loud to hear the truth." (They are often right, and it costs them.)
- **Story role:** Viv; Prime Regent Morwenna Nox; the "dead can love the sun" arc; the funeral Re-Oath.
- **Manga art direction:** Violet, ash gray, candle gold; the *quietest night* pages in the book.
- **Card game hook:** **Veilward** — the exchange archetype: cards that *give* something to take something (a trade, not a drain); the "blood" is a resource you *spend* (not leech).
- **PC game hook:** The shadow/memory class family; the "blood-economy" as a resource system (give to take, not drain).

### 13. Wraiths & Revenants (Nocturne)
- **Wraiths:** Memory-echoes, candle-lit figures. The realm's living archive. A wraith is a dead person's *memory of themselves* — they are not the person, they are the *remembering of the person*. The Reforgers' theft of memories is not murder to them — it is *amputation*.
- **Revenants:** Dead warriors, Nightwatch armor, oaths unbroken. The Covenant's dead-knight order; they guard the Veil from the other side. A revenant's oath *keeps beating* after death — the only "living" thing in the dead realm is an *unbroken promise*.
- **Story role:** The wraiths hold the stolen memories (the vault in the Nocturne arc); the revenants are the quiet recurring guard-force; the "grief kept is love with somewhere to go" theme.
- **Manga art direction:** Violet-ash, candle-gold, *flickering* panel-light; the pages that make the reader quiet.
- **Card game hook:** **Eidolon / Oathgrave** — the memory-keep archetype: cards that *store* an effect and release it later (a vault, not a spell).

### 14. Beastkin (Terrania)
- **Look:** Ears, tails, inherited features (cat/fox/wolf/bear/rabbit lines). A beastkin's face is a *family crest*.
- **Society & law:** **Clan by beast-line**; the **Fence Laws** are the conflict: the human empire fenced the beastkin colonies, and the Fence Laws are the moral wound of Act 2's Terrania arc. A beastkin's "Clanmask" is inherited — they do not *choose* their gift, they *grow into* it. The question of the race: *is a gift you didn't choose still yours?*
- **Story role:** The Fence Law arc; Senna's future path; the "is a borrowed gift still mine" theme.
- **Manga art direction:** Warm ambers with *one* accent-hue per beast-line (cat=gold, fox=ember, wolf=steel, bear=amber, rabbit=pearl); the visual language of *belonging*.
- **Card game hook:** **Clanmask** — the inherited-ability archetype: your deck's *starting* ability is fixed (your line); the rest you build.
- **PC game hook:** The beastkin class family; the "line" as a *fixed starting trait* (the theme in the character sheet).

### 15. Chimeras & Mosaic-born (The Seams)
- **Look:** Patchwork-born, multi-part bodies, proud. A chimera's parts are *visible* — a cat's ears, a demon's horn, an elf's ring-mark. They are the *visible face of the prejudice the story fights*.
- **Mosaic-born:** Beings with *multiple Sigils*, scar-shifted. The rare "blank canvas" folk. Kaelen's kind. A Mosaic's scar *shifts color* depending on which realm they're near (the visual language of "the world is in you").
- **Society & law:** The Seam Moot — no crown, no throne, no sovereign. The in-between folk's law: *a face is a door; a name is a home; a self is a choice.* The Mosaic-born are the *future* of the race — more appear every season as the world heals (the healed world grows the in-between).
- **Story role:** Bramble (changeling); Kaelen (Mosaic); the "I am what I choose" theme; the Seams' Re-Oath (the outcasts bind the world *first*).
- **Manga art direction:** Every hue, no single one (the mosaic palette); the *busiest, brightest* pages in the book — the visual opposite of the sky realm.
- **Card game hook:** **Patchskin / Nullsigil** — the hybrid archetype: cards that can *copy* a keyword from any other card on the field (the Mosaic mechanic); the "you are what you choose to be" theme.
- **PC game hook:** The Mosaic class (the hybrid build); the "scar-shift" as a *visual* indicator of which realm's mechanics are active (the theme in the UI).

## RACES RESERVED FOR THE SEQUEL (Realm 10 — the stars)

The post-credits hook opens the stars. These races are *planted but not shown* in the main saga — they exist in the Starborn's fate-patterns and Fenn's final map. Reserved for the sequel scope:

**Voidborn** (the ones who fell out of the dream) · **Clockwork Star-Smiths** (a race that *is* a machine, and doesn't know it) · **Coral Star-Divers** (a sea-race that lives in *space*, because the stars are also water) · **The Silent Choir** (a race that has no voice, only *meaning*) · **The Glass Kin** (a race that is *transparent*, and cannot hide, and has built a civilization on *total honesty*) · **The Ember Remnant** (a race that is *the last of the First Dreaming's un-spent energy* — they are what Aurelion *didn't* become) · **The Nameless** (a race that has no names, and has built a civilization on *being unnamed* — the direct opposite of Kaelen's weapon) · **The Threadkin** (a race that is *the seams themselves*, given form — they are what the Seams will become if the world heals fully) · **The Last Dreamers** (a race that is *still dreaming the First Dreaming* — they are the proof that the dream is not over).

**Nine reserved races for Realm 10.** The sequel is *the same world, one realm further out*. The franchise math holds.
