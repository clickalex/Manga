# AURELION · Volume 7 — Chapter 38: "The First Signature"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc G: "The Oath-Eater" — Volume 7 opens in a realm where the law is *visible*.** Emberfall is not chaos and it has never been chaos: it is **contract law with fire penalties**, the most *accountable* society in the world, and it is *furious* about it. A demon's word is a physical thing — say a promise out loud in this realm and **it hangs in the air in front of you, written, in contract-gold**, until it is fulfilled or burned; break it, and the fire that unmakes your Sigil is your own sentence, alight. The realm's architecture is a courtroom, its robes are *written*, its kitchens cook by **oath-heat** — a dish is only as good as the promise behind it — and its oldest festival is a comedy about the first oath anybody ever broke, which was broken **by accident**, and which the realm has been laughing about for four thousand years because it invented the difference between a crime and a mistake and has never once let anybody forget it. Into this arrives the Nine, on the ash road, with a sea's verdict in their pockets and a Barrens habit in their ledger — and Emberfall asks them for the one thing no realm has asked them for yet: **a signature.** Not a vote, not a reading, not a cut in skin: a **name**, written, witnessed, and *burnable*. And the Nine discover, at a table in a rented room, that **six of them cannot write their own name** — Viv's is held by a contract in Nocturne, Kaelen has no realm that files him, Lyra's is inside a tree and a tree does not sign, Morgatha's is already cut into her own forearm, Nereia's realm may not be bound by anything written down at all — and that the Fire-Court's Entry is not a formality: it is a *term*, a sentence that binds you to be bound later, with your name standing in the court's air as collateral. The chapter's spine is therefore a legal problem that turns into a moral one: **what do you sign, when your name is not yours, in a realm where signing is a physical risk?** The Fire-Court's answer is the volume's engine — **each of the Nine signs in their own realm's form of binding**, which means the Court must overrule four thousand years of one-signature law and admit *plural forms*: a cut (the Barrens), a reading at water (Thalassa), a stone (Khazadûm), a vote (Sylvaris), a register (Ashenmere), a thumbprint (the Seams). And **one of the Nine does not sign at all**: Viv enters her name **as reserved**, in writing, beside the eight — *reserved until she has it back* — and the chapter lets the whole volume's question stand in that blank line, in the same room as a chair the realm has kept empty for the man who voided his own oath.

> **Arc beat:** Ch. 38 must do five things. **One — establish the realm's law as a *sight*, not an explanation.** Every oath spoken in this realm is **legible text hanging in the air** until it is fulfilled or burned, and the art team's grammar must be unwavering from the first page: **oath-text is contract-gold `#d4af37`, it is a thing in the world (it occludes faces, it lights rooms, it can be walked around), and when an oath is broken the text itself *ignites* in the realm's own oath-red `#d1495b` and burns away.** Readers must learn to read this realm by looking, exactly as they learned to read the Barrens by looking at forearms. **Two — the ask.** The Fire-Court cannot open its own Re-Oath, because Malachar's void-clause left the realm's oldest contract void and the Court's own chair uncontracted: before a realm may re-oath itself, the Court must enter a **term** with outside witnesses — which is Emberfall's word for *the parties who will answer for the witnessing*. ## Page 1 — *The Ash Road*

### P1.1 — The first visible oath (half page)
**Art:** And Volume 7's first image teaches the reader the entire grammar of a realm in one panel, and the art team's rule: **show it happening before anybody explains it.** Setting: the ash road down off the stone shelf into Emberfall — obsidian country, black glassy ground underfoot, the sky a low red-brown, the air visibly hot; a caravan of ash-workers ahead of the party with a bull-drawn cart of **kiln-bricks**; the party on foot, the cart and **Marginalia** behind, the mule trailing; and, at the roadside, an ordinary small accident the panel must not make a fuss about: a worker's barrow has tipped, and **the man who tipped it turns to another worker and says something** — and the art team draws the **words** in the air in front of his mouth, at head height, **in contract-gold, lettered in the realm's script**: ***I'll have it up before the bell.*** legible, still glowing, hanging there, with the worker walking through it on his way to the barrow like a man walking through his own breath. Four frames: **(1)** the ash road, wide: the obsidian shelf, the caravan, the low red sky, the party small against the country; **(2)** the tipped barrow and the worker's oath, close enough to read, with the art team's staging instruction that **the oath-text is drawn between the man and the reader, occluding a third of his face**, because in this realm a promise is a thing you have to look around; **(3)** the party's faces — and the art team must draw **eight of the nine reading it and one of them not looking at it**, which is Fenn, who is looking at the *kiln-bricks* and already doing arithmetic about how the realm cooks; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the road's last rise, and beyond it, in the ash haze, **the city of Emberfall drawn as a courtroom that grew a roof**: terraces of black stone, arcades, a wall of fused glass, and — the art team must draw this and never explain it — **the air over the whole city speckled with faint gold text, hundreds of it, in layers**, promises rising off the streets like sparks that stopped.
**Viv (flat, walking, to the party, and the advocate's line is the reader's translation and the chapter must deliver it *early and plainly*, because this realm kills people over grammar):** "— Right. Everybody stop and look and listen once, because there is a rule in this realm and it is not a metaphor and I would like the whole party to hear it in the same minute: **when these people say a thing out loud, it is written, and it hangs, and it stays there until it is done or burned.** — Nobody here says *probably*, nobody here says *I'll try*, and nobody here says a sentence about the future in the shape of a maybe, because a maybe in this realm is a **contract with vague clauses**, and vague clauses are how a man gets *cremated* by his own grammar." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her watching the worker's sentence burn down to nothing as the barrow goes back up)* "…Also: they find human contract law *adorable*. Someone at that gate is going to laugh at us before lunch. Let them. **We are here to read, not to sign**, and those are two different acts in every realm I have ever been in, and in this one I suspect the difference is fatal enough to be worth the carriage."

### P1.2 — The ash gate, and a term read before it is signed (half page)
**Art:** And the realm's first test is bureaucratic and the art team must play it **totally straight**, which is this saga's oldest joke. Setting: the **ash gate** — a gatehouse of black stone with a fused-glass lintel, a queue of arrivals (traders, a pilgrim, two imps in enormous coats), a clerk at a lectern with a **stack of thin obsidian tablets** (the realm's writing surface: they do not use paper, they use **slate-thin stone**, and the reader must note it because the realm's whole archive is stone), a brazier, and above the gate, in enormous gold letters, mounted crossbars and all, the realm's own law **hanging permanently in the air**: ***OUTSIDE OATHS ARE CHEAP.*** Four frames: **(1)** the gate, wide, with the law hanging over it and the queue under it; **(2)** the clerk's lectern: the tablets, the brazier, a bowl of black ash, a seal-press of red iron; **(3)** the clerk reading the **Entry** aloud for the Nine, and the art team must draw the Nine's reaction as **one** reaction: eight faces doing the arithmetic; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Viv's hand on the tablet**, and the reader must be able to read the tablet's first three lines, because the chapter wants the reader signing alongside them: ***ENTRY OF OUTSIDES. — The undersigned name themselves, in their own names, freely. — They bind themselves to nothing yet. — They stand as witnesses to the realm's re-contracting, and answer, in their own names, for their witnessing. — They burn, if they lie.***
**The gate-clerk (flat, at the lectern, and the clerk's line must be *courteous* and must contain the realm's whole prejudice as a *compliment*, which is worse):** "— Entry of Outsides, four leaves, seal and witness, and you may read it as long as you like, nobody's ever asked but nobody's ever been *stopped*, and if you'd care for my opinion you'll sign at the third line and be in the city by the second bell. — You're from the *dry* realms. I can tell by the boots and by the *delight*; everybody from outside looks at our gates like a child looks at a mousetrap. So let me save you the week: **your oaths are cheap.** Not *you* — your *form.* You say a thing and mean it and nothing happens; you break a thing and nothing happens; you do it again and the *law looks away*, and half of you think that's freedom and the other half think that's shame, and from where I'm standing, friends, it's *the same thing*. Here it is not cheap." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him stamping the brazier's ash and not smiling)* "…Sign or don't. You'll not be refused the gate for refusing the form — **we're not animals**, we're *lawyers.* But the Court's asked for outside witnesses, and a witness who won't put his name down isn't a witness, he's a *tourist*, and we've got four thousand years of tourists and no witnesses."
**Viv (flat, at the lectern, and the advocate's question is the chapter's first manoeuvre and it must be *small*, and the art team must draw the clerk's sentence dying mid-air as she asks it, because in this realm a sentence that *was not an oath* must be seen to be a sentence that was not an oath):** "— Before anybody signs: **what does the burning burn?** — You've written *they burn, if they lie*, which is a beautiful sentence, and I have been in six realms and I have *never once* been handed a penalty clause that names the thing it takes. Take a lie and burn it — fine, I'll sign that today. Take a *name* and burn it and we're having a different conversation, because I have seen what happens to a person whose name is taken, and I have a *client*, and the client is a **realm**." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her looking at the third line of the tablet and not at the clerk)* "…I'm not insulting your law. I'm *reading* it, in front of you, out loud, which I notice nobody in this *gate* has done in four thousand years, and I'll do it again at the Court this afternoon and I'd *like* somebody to be embarrassed about it."

## Page 2 — *The City of Oaths*

### P2.1 — The market, the kitchen, and the rehearsal (2/3 page)

That's a realm with no *maybe* in it.**" *(a beat)* "…I hate it and I also want to live here and I've had the fruit and I'm keeping the pip."

### P2.2 — The Fire-Court, and the ask (half page)

**Ashavane (flat, at the head of the table, and the chair of the Fire-Court states the ask in the realm's own legal grammar and it must be *short*, because a man whose office is *the first signature* does not waste a sentence):** "— ***The Court is in session on the matter of its own void.*** — State it plainly for the outsiders, clerk, and then I'll state it again plainly for the room, because I want these people to hear it twice and I want the gallery to hear that I said it twice. — Four thousand years ago this realm's oldest contract was cut into that wall and it held everything we are. It is four lines. It binds you to nothing yet and to everything later, and I will not pretend otherwise, because pretending is a sin in this realm and a **felony** in this chamber. — Read it, sign it, or refuse it and be our guests anyway, and we will ask you again in the morning, and the morning after that, and every morning until either you leave or the realm dies of an uncontracted chair."

## Page 3 — *Six Names That Are Not Names*

### P3.1 — The Court's own wound, and the empty chair (half page)

**Viv (flat, at the table, working the list aloud, and the advocate's inventory is the chapter's heart and it must be delivered in her oldest register — short clauses, one fact per sentence, no comfort anywhere in it):** "— Right. *It took one gate, one form, one blank line, eight signatures in eight grammars, sixty-one thousand voices in four days, and a hound with one warm letter on its collar. Emberfall is not chaos and it never was: it is the most accountable society in the world, and it is furious about it. Into a realm where a spoken promise hangs in the air in contract-gold and a broken one burns the name off everything it stood on, the Nine walked with a sea's verdict in their pockets and a Barrens habit in their ledger, and were asked for the one thing no realm had yet asked of them — a **signature**. What followed was not a battle and not a mystery; it was a hearing. Viv read the Court's own form aloud in front of a gallery, which nobody in four thousand years had done, and asked the question that had been waiting behind every law in this saga since Chapter 1: *what does the burning burn?* — and the penalty-keeper answered honestly, because that is what he is for: **not the liar, the liar's absence.** Then the Nine discovered, at a rented table over a list written in chalk, that six of them could not put their own names on a line: a held name, a tree's vote, a Mosaic nobody files, an arm that already carries four lines of law, a sea that may not be bound by anything written down at all. So the Court did the harder thing — it overruled four thousand years of its own statute and admitted **plural forms**, and the chair who signed that order signed a narrowing of his own office to do it, and stood, and said so, in front of a full gallery. Eight signed: a ring, a bowl, a stone, a wound, a slate, a manifest, a plain name, and a thumbprint that glowed. One did not sign, and had the reason entered in the Court's own hand as a **reservation** — not a hole, as the chair was careful to say, but a line a careful party leaves when they intend to come back, and the whole volume now stands on that ninth field. And under all of it, the realm's oldest wound: a wall with one beautiful, functionless sentence cut into it and a chalk-line beside it in a word-forge hand, a chair the Court dusts and lights and does not sit in, an archive with a *cut-out* field and one empty column, and seven hounds at the ash gate who have been wearing a signature nothing can countersign for eight years, fed by a bath-house because the law has no word for them. On the fourth night one letter on one collar came warm, and a changeling saw it and said nothing, and on the fifth day the Court will ask for a signature again. Sixty-one thousand demons have already said what their oaths are worth to them. There is a queue in the street. Somewhere past the way-marker, a man with a mender's cloth over his pocket is walking toward the city with a long-handled scoop over his shoulder, and the realm has not been told, and the hounds are still sitting at the gate.*
**The record's seventeenth card is hide, and its own movement is a signature-line scored inside a closed ring: eight cuts in one rhythm and a ninth field left blank in another — the outside oaths, entered — and, in the corner, in the realm's own warm gold, the first letter of a word the volume is not going to translate until it has earned the right to.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 38)

- **The card (P12.2) is the seventeenth hide card, Volume 7's first** (the run's thirty-third), captioned ***Here sign the first.*** — its movement is a **signature-line scored inside Ch. 37's closed ring**, eight cuts in one rhythm and a ninth field left blank in another, with the realm's plate-type above it (*the outside oaths, entered*), and **one letter pressed into the hide in contract-gold**, untranslated and never to be translated until the volume has earned it.
- **The realm's law is a *sight*, and the art team's grammar for it is fixed here for the whole volume.** Every oath spoken in Emberfall hangs in the air as **legible text in contract-gold `#d4af37`** until it is fulfilled or burned: it **occludes faces**, it **lights rooms**, it casts **a warm light on skin** (by the fourth day of the trial it is a *weather*, not a decoration), and when an oath is broken it **ignites in the realm's own oath-red `#d1495b`** and burns away to nothing. **Colour reconciliation, and the gate that guards it: `#d1495b` is Emberfall's realm colour (per `location/nine-realms.md`) and is therefore available in Volume 7 as the realm's *scenery, enforcement and fire* — while Titanvein's use of the same hex as an orc body-glow remains spent and barred from panels and notes alike** (Ch. 24's rule stands; Vol. 7 uses the hue on architecture, oath-fire and the Court's lamps, never on a person's skin as a Sigil-light). Nothing in this chapter glows that is not an *oath* or a *fire*.
- **Emberfall's materials are the realm's own and must stay consistent:** writing surfaces are **slate-thin black stone** (tablets, frames, shelves — the realm's archive is stone, and paper is an outside form they regard as slightly adorable); robes are **written cloth** whose weave carries the law, and each archduke's robe must carry a legible fragment of their own office; the architecture is **obsidian and fused glass**; the kitchens cook by **oath-heat** (a promise spoken into a range and coming back as heat); the market's promise-lines hang **in layers**, some old and dim (promises kept for years and left hanging out of habit), and **a sentence that is not an oath produces no gold text at all** — the panel where an ember-fruit is given away for nothing must be drawn in a way that makes that absence *visible* to the reader.
