# AURELION · Volume 7 — Chapter 43: "The Reader Who Paid in Advance"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc G: "The Oath-Eater" — the fourteenth day, and the first paper in nine realms that charges for a thing this realm gives away.** The realm's new ordinary holds for one more morning, and it holds *boringly*, which is the chapter's first fact: the field read first, the second book's queue read aloud with the day's business, and the day's one expense a bill for the gate's own door. Then the gate's desk gets a strip of paper from a crossing two days east, and the paper knows things about this city that only a public realm could have taught it: *reading at the great gate — paid — show it at the door and you will not be charged again.* The realm has no entry for a paid reading and no way to write one, and the chapter is the three things that paper drags in behind the woman holding it: **what she actually bought** (permission, which nobody sells and everybody has), **what the realm owes a person who has paid a stranger for its free thing** (the truth, flat, and not the chips), and **what a realm can do to a price it cannot reach** (publish — a copy that declares itself, cut by the thousand, given away, and marked so that a traveller in the ninth realm can tell the realm's own words from a story about them). The woman is not a fool and the seller is not a monster; he is a man selling the only thing this realm has never had a price for, out of a tray, at a crossing, to people who have never once been let into a room for nothing.

## Page 3 — *No Field For It*
