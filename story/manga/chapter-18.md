# AURELION · Volume 4 — Chapter 18: "The Reading"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc D: "The Mountain-Ask" (the year comes out of the book one entry at a time — four thousand and nine of them, twenty-five hours, no adjournment in the law — and after four hundred years of reading its own half of a sentence out loud, a realm finally uses the one word its book has always had and nobody has ever said: *I object*)**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Viv (**the procedure, all day, and the case by midnight**), Grimm (**his own entry, read out in front of nine thousand people**), Kaelen (**Askstone's carrier; one question, asked at dusk**), Lyra (**the noticing — the realm has no pens**), Morgatha (**the count — eleven, twelve**), Nereia (**the flask's last measure; the reading's clock**), Azrael, Bramble (the small dusty person, listening), Fenn (the map, the pins), Marginalia, Sprocket (**the board, and a new column**). **The support** — **Roderick** (his one quiet panel: the witness end of the low stone table). **The stone realm** — **Duelist Molka** (**the keeper — she has no line in this chapter: she writes**), **Grand-Anvil Khazur** (the reader, who holds a hundred and sixty an hour until his voice goes), **Orik Khazur** (bread, in the fourth hour), **Echo** (**the voice that does not crack for eleven hours**), **Mountain-Speaker Khazri** (**silent — and this chapter he turns around**), **Stonehand Drek** (**the honest bill, and the trap he offers as hospitality**), **Urka** (**the first objection in this realm's recorded history**), **Ragna** (the second — hands, not names), **a lad of nineteen** (the third — a clause read past), **Torvik** (**sitting**), **Pick · Norga · Hildrun · Tova · Perra · Varda · Yrsa** · **Skaal** (in the ranks), **Maren Ashwood** (**a pencil, unasked**), **Tilda Ashwood** (a coat, over an old man), **the dusty child** (water, and the formula), **Cinder** + the grummpling clan, **Pebble** (one boot; one claim, withdrawn), nine thousand dwarves (**listening, and then knocking**).
> **Locations / threads:** the Court Floor (the low stone table, the reader's seat, the exhibit at the mouth) → the aisles (the realm's bedrolls, the soup, the sleeping children) → the rack-walk's dark end → the cistern and the water-lamps → the deep gallery at dawn, day two (**the year's last leaf**).
> **Arc beat:** The Anvil-Naming opens and immediately cannot close. The keeper opens the book, the Grand-Anvil sits down on the reader's seat, and the realm finds out, in the first hour, what nobody in four hundred years has had to find out: their law has no word for *tomorrow*. The year is four thousand and nine entries long, and it comes out of the book at a hundred and sixty an hour: lamp-oil, weddings filed as maintenance, a withdrawn claim about an eaten boot, widow-stays paid in advance, roof falls and the four hands under them, nine hundred forfeitures, and after every single one of them, the same four words — *sworn by the mountain* — said out loud to a room that has spent its entire history saying them back to itself. Khazur's voice goes at nine hundred. The keeper carries the book to the apprentice, silently, and Echo reads the rest of the year in a young voice that never cracks. By dusk they are half through and Viv has done the arithmetic; by midnight a boy has heard his own forfeiture read aloud with the last Khazdûrin's name on the same plate — and he stands up, in the ninth rank, in front of every person he has ever worked for, and says the word. The realm hears three objections it has never heard before: *ask the witness*, *the record has no people in it, only hands*, *the record has no clauses in it, only promises*. Molka writes the first three lines a page has been waiting for since the Cut opened. Drek, entirely reasonably, agrees with all of it — because if the realm calls the witness and the witness stays silent, his form is finished forever, and the mine has been calling it for forty years. And at dawn, when the reading arrives at the last leaf of the year, the realm sees the page it has all been walking toward: nine hundred blank lines where the mountain is supposed to have named its anvils — and one entry left, and it is not a reading at all. It is *tomorrow*.
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**the year read aloud; the objections page; four hundred years of blank lines**), the count (**Morgatha eleven → twelve; Nereia's reading-line: 1 → 400 → 1,204 → 2,000 → 3,412 → 4,008**), the witness (**a sentence with two halves; nine thousand swearing by something that has never answered**), the door (**a law with no word for tomorrow**), the empty (**nine hundred blank name-lines; the flask at its last measure**), the lamps (**the last of the grove's water, burning in a court lamp**), the kept thing (**a wrapped hammer at a reading; a pencil in a mortal's hand**), the stone grammar (**the cut of the plates; the mine's own *yes*, nine hundred anvils knocking**), the ink-and-stone (**the realm has no pens; the mine has all of them**). **Not in this chapter:** the stone-song; the Ask; Bastion's sentence; any line at all from Khazri, Molka, Torvik, Grudka, or Bastion; the naming of the anvils; the judgment of the year; the geode; the golems' verdict; what the hairline is for; and the answer to the question the realm did not say in Ch. 17.

## Page 1 — *The Book Opens (dawn, day one)*

### P1.1 — The reader's seat (2/3 page)
**Art:** The Court Floor at first light, and the composition is the whole of Chapter 17 turned ninety degrees and *quieted*: nine thousand dwarves **standing** at nine hundred anvils (the realm rose when the book opened in Ch. 17 and it has not been told to sit down since), the eight Speaker chairs in their arc facing the raw wall, the two slates on the eighth chair's seat, the exhibit at the mouth in the mine's own bolted frame, and at the head of the middle aisle the **low stone table** with the festival's book lying closed on it. **Khazur** comes down the aisle alone and takes the **reader's seat** — a stump with a cushion, which the art team has already used twice (Ch. 14, Ch. 15) and must draw a third time without comment. **Molka** stands at the table, opens the book at the year's first leaf, and steps back. And in the frame's quietest corner, the chapter's clock: **Nereia**, at the table's end, capping the flask and setting it down, with the **last measure of the grove's water** gone into the **court lamp** — the one lamp on the table, the one the year will be read by. The art team's key direction: the flask gives up its last drop, the crack runs the whole way across the glass, and *nobody in the frame turns to look*. The frame-edge carries no day-mark any more; the tally's twelve cuts sit finished at the top, and under them the art team begins the chapter's new clock: a small cut figure — **1**.
**Khazur (gruff, settling onto the stump, to nine thousand people, and this is the whole chapter in one line):** "The keeper opens the book. I read it out loud. You listen. That's the realm's law and you've heard my voice say it every year of your lives." *(a beat; he looks up)* "…No you haven't. This is the first time the third part's ever happened."
**Khazur (flat, to the room, turning the first leaf):** "Entry one. Read by me, witnessed by *you*."
**Plate-type (stamped, small caps, no tail — the mine's own lettering, and the art team should keep this register for every entry all chapter):** "**ENTRY ONE. LAMP-OIL, DEEP GALLERY: SIXTY JARS, BOUGHT OF THE SEAMS AT THE TOLL. SWORN BY THE MOUNTAIN.**"

### P1.2 — Twenty-five hours (bottom third)
**Art:** Three frames, and the chapter's arithmetic. **(1)** **Viv** with the book's back cover open on her knee, counting pages with a finger, Sprocket at her shoulder with the board. **(2)** **Sprocket's board**, and the new column: **"READ: 1"** — and the board now has three columns that were not on it a week ago (the art team: the board is a *living* thing this volume and should visibly be running out of room). **(3)** **Drek**, at the gallery's rail, with the mine's night-chit in one hand — and behind him, ordinary as weather, two of his clerks with bread and a barrel of water on a hand-cart.
**Viv (flat, fast, the only person in the mountain who has read the last page):** "Four thousand and nine entries. He's good for a hundred and sixty an hour and he'll not stop for his dinner. **Twenty-five hours.**" *(a beat; she closes the book's cover)* "Your law has got no word for *tomorrow*."
**Khazur (without looking up):** "It's got no word for *lunch*, either."
**Drek (flat, west-country, entirely reasonable, mid-page — and this line must land as *helpful*):** "The mine's got words for both. Bread at the door, water at the pipe, lamps at cost, everything at the year's price, on the year's bill." *(a beat)* "You can argue the entry next year. …I'm rather looking forward to that."
**Marginalia (tiny italics, in the margin of the frame, and the cart has come down into a mine on purpose):** *"The cart notes that a realm with one book and no pens has just discovered that its own law takes a day and a night to read out, and that the man who owns the hole it's being read in has offered to cater."*

## Page 2 — *A Shopping List and a Graveyard (mid-morning)*

### P2.1 — The year, out loud (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's first great *montage* page: five plate-entries read aloud, each one a small window on a year of ordinary life, drawn as **insert frames cut against the room** — the art team's direction: these frames are not flashbacks, they are *what the room is hearing*, and they should be drawn in the same ink with one more line of softness, as if remembered by someone who wasn't there. **Entry eleven:** a wedding in gallery nine, forty-one guests and a table of bread. **Entry forty:** a grummpling, small and pink, with a boot in his mouth, caught in a mine corridor by a lamp — and the art team should make the reader's stomach turn over with recognition. **Entry one hundred and six:** a cellar room with a bed in it, the widow-stay, twelve years paid in advance. **Entry two hundred and nine:** the cistern, and a family drawing water above its marks, and a clerk's hand *not* writing it down. **Entry three hundred and twelve:** a roof fall, four hands, a shaft sealed, and three houses on the same street with the same lamp lit. Through it all, in the room: children mouthing the entries, an old woman saying *"that's my sister's"* out loud at entry one hundred and six and being *quietly shushed by her own family*, traders doing business in the aisles, a soup pot, a queue.
**Khazur (reading, flat, the mine's voice borrowed by the realm's law — and the reader should hear how *little* he dramatizes it):** "…Filed as maintenance. Allowed. Entry twelve. Claim entered by the mine against the grummpling clan, tunnels: one boot, mine property, eaten." *(a beat; he turns a page)* "Claim withdrawn. Twice."
**The room (one balloon, unsourced — the art team must draw it as *the whole gallery*, one soft murmur under the reading):** "…*Twice.*"
**Sprocket (tiny, at the board, flat):** "Four hundred and six entries. Nobody's thrown anything. I don't know what I expected."

### P2.2 — The formula (bottom third)
**Art:** Three frames, and this is the page the chapter's horror lives in. **(1)** The plate-type formula again, lettered identically to P1.1's — no change, no emphasis, the art team must repeat it *exactly* — and it is being said for the four hundred and sixty-eighth time that morning. **(2)** The room in the middle distance: nine thousand faces, and not one of them is bored any more; a man in the fourth rank has his jaw set; a woman in the second has her hands flat on her anvil; a child has stopped mouthing it. **(3)** The raw wall behind the chairs, wet and unstruck, and the frame is *held* on it — the thing every entry in this book has been swearing by, drawn like weather.
**Viv (quiet, to Viv, which is to say: out loud, in a court, because she is counsel and everything she says is on the record — and this must be the *flat* reading of a fact, not a speech):** "— Every one of them ends the same. Four words. *Sworn by the mountain.*" *(a beat)* "That's a sentence with two halves. You've been saying your half out loud for four hundred years."
**Azrael (dry, from the rail at the gallery's mouth, no wind, no wings, and he has earned this one since Ch. 11):** "I broke one oath and my realm made me a *saint* for it. This lot have read out nine hundred promises this morning to a wall that has never once answered, and they call it *Thursday*."
**Bramble (flat, from the aisle, not looking up):** "…It's *Monday*. You've been in this hole nine days, angel."
**Azrael (a beat):** "It's all Monday down here."

## Page 3 — *The Voice Goes (noon)*

### P3.1 — Entry nine hundred (half page)
**Art:** The reader's seat, noon, and the chapter's first physical event: **Khazur's voice fails.** The art team's key direction: do *not* dramatize this as collapse — draw the ordinary mechanics of an old man's throat giving out at his work. Three frames: **(1)** Khazur mid-entry, hand up flat, *stopping himself mid-word*, eyes closed, waiting for it to come back. **(2)** The realm in silence — nine thousand people not moving, not whispering, not helping, because the law says the year is read and nobody knows what happens if it isn't. **(3)** Khazur trying again: two words, then a **crack** the whole gallery hears, and his hand comes down on the book like a man steadying a bench. **Orik Khazur** (his son) is already off the rail and coming; **the dusty child** — who has been in this mine nine days and is nobody's and is *therefore* the one who has been carrying water all morning — puts the mine's tin cup on the table beside him and steps back before anyone can stop her. Khazur does not drink it. He looks at it.
**Khazur (hoarse, to the room, and it is *bad form* to apologize in this realm, so he does not):** "…Nine hundred and four. Give me a moment. I'll not have the year read by half a man."
**Viv (standing, careful, precise, and she asks the *keeper* rather than ruling herself — counsel's job is to put the question on the record):** "— The law says the year is read out. It doesn't say by *which* voice in this room." *(a beat)* "…Keeper. Whose voice is it read in?"
**Art note (the page's last frame):** **Molka** does not answer. She closes the book, picks it up in both hands, and walks out from behind the table — with the whole gallery watching her carry four hundred years of law down the aisle on foot.

### P3.2 — The record reads the record (half page)
**Art:** The ninth rank, and the page's image: **Molka lays the book into Echo's hands** — silently, in front of everybody, without a single word of explanation, because the realm's law does not require the keeper to explain anything. **Echo** is nineteen and has spent fourteen years copying the shape of a line he cannot read; he looks at the book the way a man looks at a lit fuse. Around him: **Ragna** (his sister) rising half off her anvil in the sixth rank with a hand over her mouth; **Pick** grinning; **Norga** flat-faced; **Khazri**, at the wall, with his back to all of it; the six old women of the fifty-eight in the first rank, each with a sketchbook on her knees. Then the reading resumes, and the art team's key direction for the rest of the chapter: **Echo's voice is better than Khazur's** — cleaner, faster, and he *never* misreads a plate; the realm notices in the second entry; and it must be drawn as *pride*, not as pathos.
**Echo (the smallest beat first, to himself, looking at the page):** "…Nine hundred and five."
**Echo (reading, level, pitched to carry to the back of the deepest gallery in the mountain):** "**Entry nine hundred and five. Wage, gallery nine: raised by the mine's own hand, one part in six, no claim entered. SWORN BY THE MOUNTAIN.**" *(a beat; he turns the page, and this is his chapter)* "Entry nine hundred and six."
**Viv (quiet, on the record, half to herself and half to Sprocket's board):** "— The record's reading the record. Write that down."
**Sprocket (flat):** "Wrote it down at nine hundred and one. Board's nearly full."

## Page 4 — *Kept (afternoon)*

### P4.1 — The cuts (half page)
**Art:** Afternoon, the Court Floor, and the reading has come to the part of the year the mine sells. The art team's key direction: **the forfeitures are read by a nineteen-year-old in a level voice, and the room recites.** Six plate-entries in a row, lettered identically, each one a tag and a shaft and a number of braids; and in the room, three ranks of ordinary dwarves saying the **tag numbers** along with the reader — not shouting, not protesting, just *saying them*, because tags are how the realm knows itself and every family in that gallery has a tag in its mouth. **Norga**, the braider, in the front rank, hands flat on her knees (the Ch. 15 bench, the knots, the way of tying a thing kindly). In one insert frame, the rack-walk's dark end, the tags exactly where they were left (Ch. 17), and a **hand** — one — resting on the rail.
**Echo (reading, level):** "**Entry one thousand and forty. Forfeit: tag nine-oh-four, shaft nine, three braids, cut and filed. Buyer: the mine. SWORN BY THE MOUNTAIN.**"
**Three ranks (one balloon, unsourced, soft, and it must read as *weather* rather than as protest):** "…Nine-oh-four."
**Marginalia (tiny italics, in the margin, and the cart's register drops here for the second time this volume):** *"The cart notes that a realm with no archive has one, and that it is in everybody's mouth, and that the mine has been reading it back to them as a *bill*."*

### P4.2 — A perfect ledger (half page)
**Art:** The other half of the afternoon, and the art team's hardest direction of the chapter: **twenty pages of *kept*.** Montage of the mine doing exactly what it said it would — the widow-stays, the hands paid out, the roofs shored, the shafts sealed when they went wrong, the wage raised without a claim, a family's rent forgiven after a fall, the water not pursued, a boot forgotten twice. No irony in the drawing. The mine kept every promise it ever made, entry after entry, for hours, in the realm's own book, and the art team must make the reader *feel* how much easier the chapter would be if it hadn't. In the room: **Grimm**, in the front rank, listening to a year of his employer being, factually, a keeper of promises, with his hand nowhere near his braids. **Maren Ashwood** beside him with her sketchbook shut on her knee and **a pencil** in her hand (the art team: plant it, do not point at it — it appears again on P12); **Tilda** on her other side. The **six old women**, sketchbooks open, waiting.
**Viv (flat, on the record, and this is the volume's thesis said as arithmetic):** "— Twenty-one pages of *kept*. Nothing broken, nothing unpaid, nothing filed wrong." *(a beat)* "He's never broken a promise in his life. He bought nine thousand of them and then he kept every one, and there's not a page of this year that says otherwise."
**Grimm (quiet, not to her, not to anybody, and the art team must keep his face *plain*):** "…Aye. I know. I've worked for him."
**Viv (next, sharper, and this is the turn):** "Then you've worked for the most honest man in the mountain, and he's *still* the one on trial. That's the case: not what he did with the promises. It's *whose name was on the other half of them*."

## Page 5 — *The Law Has No Word for Tomorrow (dusk)*

### P5.1 — Nobody leaves (half page)
**Art:** Dusk, the lamp-rows coming on (the mine's oil now: the water-lamps Nereia filled are the ones nearest the table, and they are the *dim* ones — the art team's cost-grammar, paid quietly). Half the year read: the frame-edge's cut figure stands at **2,000**. And the page is a *question of procedure*: Viv on her feet with the book's fifth rule on her tongue, Khazur hoarse, and nine thousand dwarves waiting to be told whether they may go home. **And then the answer, which belongs to nobody:** the art team should draw the realm **not leaving** — a man picking up his coat and putting it back down; a mother lifting a sleeping child onto her shoulder and sitting; bedrolls unrolled in the aisles; a woman handing her neighbour half a loaf; the whole enormous room making up its mind without one word said out loud. **Lyra** in the second rank, watching the elves' law and the dwarves' law be two different animals.
**Viv (flat, on the record):** "— Your law reads the year. It doesn't adjourn. It doesn't recess, it doesn't sleep, it's got no word for *tomorrow*, and whoever cut it thought a realm would only ever do this *once* and would do it standing up."
**Khazur (hoarse, from the reader's seat, and this is the sovereign's whole character in one dry line):** "…We're a patient people. Nobody ever wrote us down as a *quick* one."
**Lyra (quiet, to Viv, without taking her eyes off the ranks):** "…I sat in a court where the witness was a tree and the tree answered. It was easier." *(a beat)* "They don't even have a witness they can *point at*."
**Sprocket (flat, from the board, and this is the chapter's best joke because it is not one):** "Attendance, dusk: same as dawn. Every single one. …Board's got no column for that so I've used the *margin*."

### P5.2 — The mine's night rate (bottom third)
**Art:** Four frames. **(1)** Drek at the rail with the evening chit, and behind him the mine's ordinary machinery of *hospitality*: bread, water, lamps, a clerk with a tally-board, all of it honest, all of it priced, all of it *working*. **(2)** **Orik Khazur**, the Grand-Anvil's son, in work clothes, carrying bread down a rank with two cousins, and — the art team must draw this without emphasis — the fourth rank letting the mountain-court's heir hand them their supper, because in this realm that is *also* law. **(3)** Drek's face, at the rail, in the dark above a lit room, watching nine thousand people decide to stay another twelve hours in his hole. The art team: the man is not smiling. He is *satisfied*, which is worse, and nothing in this chapter explains why. **(4)** A bench at the edge of the aisle at dusk: **Khazur** sitting with his voice gone to gravel, a dish of soup he is not eating, and **Kaelen** across from him with **Askstone's sack** laid along the bench between them — the boy with the wrapped hammer asking the sovereign of the realm a question about *procedure*, in daylight, on the record, with no drama in it at all.
**Drek (flat, to the clerk, handing over the chit, and the reader should be able to hear that this is *true*):** "Bread at the door, water at the pipe, lamps at cost. Nobody owes me for anything they didn't eat." *(a beat)* "And nobody's going home tonight. Bill the night at the day rate; I'll not have it said I charged the realm for its own law twice."
**Kaelen (quiet, flat, two men on a bench, and this is the arc's whole stake in four lines):** "When the anvils are named tomorrow — who *answers*?"
**Khazur (a beat; hoarse; not looking at him):** "The stone. That's what a Naming is. Nine hundred anvils, and the mountain puts a name on each one."
**Kaelen (next):** "…And if it doesn't?"
**Khazur (and this is the sovereign's *whole* position, said the way a man says the weight of a thing he has carried forty years):** "Then the year's word is judged, and the word is what it is, and there's no *lever* in it, boy. That's the law. I'd not change it if I could."
**Fenn (flat, over his map, in the margin, and it is the shortest log entry in Volume 4):** *"Day one. The room didn't go home. The map's got a new room on it and the room is *full*. Six pins left. Still no letters."*

## Page 6 — *Night One (the dark reading)*

### P6.1 — Eleven hours (half page)
**Art:** Deep night on the Court Floor, and the art team's key direction: **the room is domestic and the reading has not stopped.** The lamp-rows nearest the table are the dim ones — the diviner's water, burning down — and past them the mine's oil burns bright and hard. **Khazur** asleep on a bench behind the reader's seat with **Tilda Ashwood's coat** over him (Tilda standing over him with her arms folded, guarding an old man's nap like a door). **Echo**, on the reader's seat, eleven hours in, still going, still level. **Nereia** at the table's end, counting aloud in a flat voice, because somebody has to and a diviner is a counting animal. **Sprocket** asleep on his board. **Pebble**, in Grimm's lap in the front rank, coat buttoned, one hand flat on the pocket, apparently following the reading. **Morgatha**, at the rail, counting something else.
**Echo (reading, level, twelve hours in):** "**Entry two thousand, four hundred and one. Water, cistern, twelve families drawing above their marks. Not pursued. SWORN BY THE MOUNTAIN.**"
**Nereia (flat, counting, not looking at anybody — and this is her beat, and the art team must draw the *lit* lamps and the *empty flask* in the same frame):** "Two thousand, four hundred and one. …Their water's down under the lowest mark on the wall, by the way. Nobody's asked me, so nobody's asked."
**Morgatha (flat, from the rail, in the dark, one number, and the art team must put it in a frame where the reader can see she is *listening to the reading* while she counts something else entirely):** "…Eleven."

### P6.2 — The mouths in the dark (half page)
**Art:** The formula again — the *twelfth* time the lettering must be identical — and this time the art team draws the room's faces in the dark in a wide, low band of frames: **nine thousand mouths moving.** Not speaking: *moving*, in time, on the four words, the way a congregation moves on a familiar line. It is the realm's oldest sentence and it has never once been answered, and the page must make the reader *uncomfortable* rather than solemn. At the end of the band, in a frame of its own, **the dusty child** sitting on the floor beside the eighth chair with a mine rag over her knees, saying the four words too — *perfectly*, every syllable, with the small precision of a person who has spent nine days listening to the only sentence anybody in this realm says out loud. Across the aisle, **Bramble**, stopped dead, watching her. **Khazri**, at the wall, still facing stone. No dialogue in the band; one balloon for Bramble; one SFX for the room.
**SFX:** *(the reading. A kettle. Somebody's grandfather snoring in the third rank. Nine thousand people breathing the same four words.)*
**Bramble (quiet, to nobody, flat, and the mask gives nothing and the voice gives it all — the Ch. 9 register):** "…She's got the *formula* perfect. Every syllable. Nine days in this mine and she can say the thing this realm says to its own mountain better than the men who *cut* it."

## Page 7 — *Plate Nine Hundred and Six (deep night)*

### P7.1 — Khazdûrin, Grimm (half page)
**Art:** The reading reaches plate nine hundred and six at some hour nobody records, and the art team's direction: **this must be the quietest page in the chapter and the loudest thing in it is a name.** Six frames: **(1)** Echo, on the reader's seat, turning to the plate. **(2)** The plate-type, cut bigger in the panel than any entry so far: **"KHAZDÛRIN, GRIMM. ONE WORD, HELD. THIRTEEN BRAIDS, FORFEIT TO THE MINE. PLATE NINE HUNDRED AND SIX."** **(3)** The room's faces turning, rank by rank, at a *surname* — because the realm has not heard it out loud in front of nine thousand people in a hundred years, and the reader knows exactly what every dwarf in that gallery is thinking: *there's one left.* **(4)** **Grimm**, front rank, sitting still, and the art team must draw **his hands empty and nowhere near his braids**. **(5)** The **six old women of the fifty-eight** in the same frame — sketchbooks, pencils, six faces of one dead man, all looking at the boy with the thirteen braids. **(6)** **Maren Ashwood's** shut sketchbook and the pencil in her hand, and Tilda's hand closing on her sister's wrist.
**Echo (reading, level and slow, and he does not look at Grimm while he does it, which is the kindest thing anybody does all night):** "**…Plate nine hundred and six. SWORN BY THE MOUNTAIN.**"
**SFX:** *(nothing. This frame has no sound in it at all, and the letterer must leave the panel empty of type except the entry.)*

### P7.2 — The next line on the same plate (half page)
**Art:** Two frames later, same plate, same page, different hand: **the mine's ledger does not know that it has just cut a boy's night in half.** Echo reads on, and the next entry on plate nine hundred and six is the one underneath Grimm's — because the plate was signed in a queue at a shaft-mouth and the clerk wrote top to bottom. The art team's direction: the two plate-lines must be drawn in the same frame, one above the other, in **identical type** — the last Khazdûrin and a nine-year man on the same page, indistinguishable to the book. Then: the ninth rank, in the third row from the back, where **Urka** — three braids cut, tags in his beard for anybody to read, Torvik's son, twenty-six years old, never once asked what he thought about any of it — stands up. Around him: **Pick** looking at him; **Norga** looking at her hands; **Torvik** two ranks back, **sitting**, and the art team must hold that frame exactly one panel longer than is comfortable.
**Echo (reading, level):** "**Entry three thousand, four hundred and twelve. Urka, son of Torvik. Shaft nine. Three words broken, three braids cut, forfeit to the mine. Plate nine hundred and six. SWORN BY THE MOUNTAIN.**"
**Art note (the held panel):** the gallery noticing a man on his feet — heads turning in three ranks — the art team: **do not draw the crowd surprised.** He has worked next to these people for six years. They know exactly who he is and it has never mattered until this second.

## Page 8 — *"I Object."*

### P8.1 — The third rule fires (2/3 page)

**END OF CHAPTER 18.**

*The realm opened its own book and found out that its law is twenty-five hours long and has no word for tomorrow, and then it sat down in a hole in the ground for a day and a night and let a hoarse old man and a nineteen-year-old apprentice read every promise it has ever made out loud — the wedding filed as maintenance, the boot, the widow-stay, the four hands under the roof that came down at shaft seven — and after each of them, the same four words, sworn by the mountain, said nine thousand times to a wall that has never once answered. And somewhere in the fourth quarter of the year the book came to plate nine hundred and six and read out a surname the whole realm flinched at, and two lines later, underneath it, in exactly the same type, the name of a miner who has never been asked a question in his life. He stood up. He said the word. And the realm — which has had that word in its law for four hundred years and has never once needed it, because a mine is a very good place to be silent in — said it after him, twice more, and then put its knuckles on its own anvils, all nine hundred of them, and made a sound like patient rain on iron, which is not in the book, and which is older than the book, and which is this realm's word for yes. Three objections went into a page that had been cut blank four hundred years before any of them were born. The Speaker turned around. And at dawn, the year ran out one entry short of its ending, and the realm stood in front of the page the whole volume has been walking toward: nine hundred lines with a family on each of them and a blank where the mountain is supposed to have written the name. Nobody in this realm can write. There is one pencil in the room.*
**The record's cards are being cut now, and the blank lines are the card.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 18)

- **The clock changes hands** (all pages). The frame-edge tally of Ch. 14–17 was twelve day-marks cut *down*; Ch. 18 opens on them **spent** — all twelve cut, the last of them cut in Ch. 17 P11.1, and nothing left to mark on a day that has already arrived — and starts the volume's second clock — the **reading-line**, cut small in the frame edge and rising through the chapter: **1 → 400 → 900 → 1,204 → 2,000 → 2,400 → 3,412 → 4,008**, and on the last page a **fraction: 4,008 of 4,009**. The day count is over and nobody in the chapter ever says so. **Nereia speaks the numbers** — she is the reading's clock now that the flask is done — and she must say them *flat*, in the middle of somebody else's line, the way a person reads a meter.
- **Three lettering registers, and they must never be confused** (all pages). **(1) Plate-type:** the mine's entries — stamped, small caps, no tails, no italics, **identical every single time** (the art team's most important discipline this chapter: the formula *sworn by the mountain* appears full-size seven times and must not be lettered with any more weight the seventh time than the first; the horror is arithmetic, not emphasis). **(2) Balloons:** the realm's speech, as usual. **(3) The record's hand:** anything written *into* the book — the keeper's entries — which is never a balloon (Ch. 16's convention) and is lettered as writing-on-a-page, in the book's own voice. The three objections go into the book in register 3 and are **facts only**: *"Objection entered. Urka, son of Torvik, shaft nine."* No grounds, no arguments, no names of crimes. That is the realm's law and it is not a limitation, it is a *shape*.
- **The objections page** (P9.1, P10.1): four hundred years old, ruled, blank, with a cut heading. It has never been used. Molka enters three lines by the end of the night, in the book's own pen, and the art team should let the reader see how much of the page is still empty — nine-tenths of it, and now a realm that knows the word.
- **Khazur's voice goes at entry nine hundred and four** (P3.1): draw the *mechanics*, not a collapse. He is a working man whose throat has quit after four hours. He does not apologize (the realm considers that bad form) and he does not leave. **Molka answers the procedural question by carrying the book to Echo and saying nothing** — the keeper has **no dialogue in this chapter at all** (her one Volume 4 line was spent in Ch. 17 P12.1; the forge-duel's line is still hers) — and **Echo's reading must be visibly better than the Grand-Anvil's**, and the realm's pride in him must be drawn as *pride*, not pathos. He is nineteen, he has spent fourteen years copying a shape he cannot read, and for eleven hours tonight he reads four hundred years of law without one mistake. **That is his chapter.**
- **The mine's ledger is clean and the chapter must not cheat** (P4.2): twenty-odd entries of *kept* — widow-stays, wage rises, sealed shafts, rent forgiven, the water not pursued. No irony in the drawing, no villainous underlight on Drek in these frames. The volume's whole argument depends on the reader understanding that the mine never broke a promise; the crime is *whose name went on the other half of them*. **Viv's P4.2 turn** ("not what he did with the promises — whose name was on the other half") is the case's real shape and must be the quietest line on the page.
- **The knock** (P10.2, P11.1): the realm's *yes* is **knuckle on iron, three times**, and it is the mine's oldest gesture, used only at a Naming — never before on the page in this saga. It starts with **one** dwarf and spreads out of time, and the SFX is **a slow patient rain on iron**, never a cheer, never rhythmic unison (the art team: the sound is *gathering*, not *together*). It is answered by **Khazur's explanation**, not by a caption, and **Viv's "that's not in your book"** is the line that names what the realm has just done. **Morgatha does not knock** — her hands stay on her knee, she says her number (**eleven → twelve**, tracked across P6.1 and P10.2), and neither the not-knocking nor the number is explained in this volume.
- **Drek's agreement is the trap and must be played as generosity** (P9.2): he does not object, does not threaten, does not manoeuvre visibly — he *grants* the objection, and offers the Naming as the place to test it, because if the realm calls the witness and the witness is silent, the form he sells is vindicated forever and no plate in Khazadûm will ever be questioned again. Play him **entirely fair**, and let the reader be the only person who sees the shape of it. **Viv has no counter** — the art team must show her *not speaking*, which is the most frightening thing she does all volume.
- **Khazri turns around** (P8.2): the single largest character event of Volume 4 so far and it must cost **no dialogue**. Three frames, drawn slowly; his face in full for the first time in the volume; the room turning to look at **him** rather than at Urka; and **one balloon in the whole sequence**, Echo's, because Echo is the realm's memory. He remains turned, facing the room, for the rest of the chapter (P11.1 must draw him facing *out*, which is the opposite of every frame he has ever had). **He does not speak, does not sit in his chair, and does not approach the slates.** His line (03a) remains the arc's pivot and is not in this chapter.
- **Roderick — one quiet panel** (P11.2, frame one): the **witness end of the low stone table**, standing, hand flat on the stone, six feet from the open book, no dialogue. This is his fifth threshold (river, mine-rail, road, doorless mouth, and now a **table**), it must be drawn with *no* emphasis whatsoever, and no character may remark on where he is standing. **Rule 4 (the chorus panel)** is used **once**, P10.2: the ring around the **table**, nine frames for the Nine, and **one of the nine frames carries no balloon** (Grimm's). **Rule 3 (costs drawn)**: the flask at its last measure and then empty, the court lamp burning grove water and going out, the crack across the whole glass, the cistern above the waterline, thirteen braids, the tags in the aisle, the tally spent and the reading-line rising.
- **The last leaf** (P12.1) is the chapter's destination and the arc's runway: the year's final item is **the naming of the anvils** — a page ruled for nine hundred of them, each line carrying a shaft, a door and a family, and each line ending in a **blank space where the mountain's name goes**. The first line is the **Khazdûrin forge's anvil** (one hundred and eleven years at one door, three generations of hands, no name). The formula on that page — **"Named by the mountain. Witnessed by the realm."** — is the exact mirror of every plate read out during the chapter, and the art team should set it so a rereader recognises the turn without one character pointing at it. **The pencil in Maren Ashwood's hand appears on P4.2, P12.1 and nowhere else**, no one ever looks at her holding it, and nobody asks her for it — the payoff is the next chapter's, not this one's.
