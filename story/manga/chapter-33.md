# AURELION · Volume 6 — Chapter 33: "The Second Register"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc F: "The First Song" — the road home with a sentence the realm cannot sing.** The sea's four words are a *welcome*, and the Barrens has spent a hundred years building a statute out of *bindings*: debts, holdings, sureties, a clause that is not law until a clan cuts it into its own arm. You cannot cut a welcome into an arm. You cannot swear to an invitation. And the realm's record — the debt-horn, carved, kept at the circle, every entry a thing somebody owes or holds — **has no form for a sentence that asks nothing and forbids nothing.** So the chapter is the realm doing what it does in front of a hole in its own grammar: it reads the problem out loud at nine crossings, it lets the children answer it first, it lets the man the code was used against answer it second, and then it does the thing no realm in the saga has done yet — **it writes something down that binds nobody, in a register of its own, and keeps it by reading it out loud every day rather than by cutting it into skin.** The horn gets a **door** cut through it. The realm gets a word it has never had a use for — **the ear** — because the code has terms for who may speak, who may read, who may cross and who may be held, and no term at all for *who may be read to.* And out at the mark, in a camp at the edge of the world, a man in a grey coat who has not asked the realm for anything in the twenty-six years it has known him sends one line with a courier — *I would like a reader at the mark. Mine. On the books.* — and gets an answer he does not expect and does not answer: the survey's first year, read aloud to him at the mark, **by himself.**
> **Cast:** **The road party** (nine; **Morgatha** — *the count, the four lines in her arm, and the chapter's last spoken line*, **Viv** — *who drafts a law that costs nobody anything and hates how much better it is*, **Kharza** — *who reads the welcome at a crossing for the first time in the realm's history*, **Nereia** — *the flask that has stopped counting*, **Kaelen** — *hands and no speech*, **Fenn** — *who adds nine ticks to a map and leaves a column blank*, **Sprocket** — *the entries*, **Azrael** — *the fire*, **Bramble** — *who spells it* ) + **Marginalia** (the cart, homeward) · **Captain Roderick Vane** (**his one silent panel** — a log, a column of days, a foreign officer copying a realm's newest law by hand, nobody watching) · **Ulrik the Unbroken** (03a; *refused at his own post in Ch. 31, and at Tarkhan's stone in Ch. 33 he asks to read the welcome himself*) · **Khura, oath-keeper of the house of Tarkhan** (the *no* that still stands, read after the welcome, in order) · **Taurus** (the wall, the essays, the third term) · **Horn** (03b; the minotaur horn-carver — **he cuts the door**) · **Durgan the Unbent** (the Speaker; the throat-stone) · **Kha** (under the collar at crossing nine; **no line of her own**, by rule) · **Sage Oros the Old Eye** (the second chair, filled; the mark) · **the founder** (***no face, one line on paper, and one reading***) · **Drog** (the border pass) · **the ninth crossing's queue, the school, the readers of the nine crossings, the company's clerk** (all drawn and unnamed).
> **Locations / threads:** the edge post and crossing nine, going west → crossing eight, and the clerks' problem stated at a stone → the school in the well → Tarkhan's stone, and the war-chief → the horn, at the circle (the **stop**) → crossing three, and the company's clerk → the nine readings, counted → the camp at the edge of the world, by letter → **the mark** (the survey, read aloud) → the council's table, the second register read out → the fire, and the cart's plate → **the card.**
> **Arc beat:** Ch. 33 must do three things. **One — the hole in the grammar.** The realm's law is made of *bindings*: *a clause is not law until the clans that vote for it cut it into themselves* (Ch. 24). The sea's four words bind nobody, forbid nothing, ask nothing; they are a welcome. The chapter must let the realm *find* that out in public — at a stone, with a queue listening — and must not let anybody be clever about it: the answer comes from a schoolchild first, from Ulrik second, and from the horn's own carver third. **Two — the second register.** The realm's invention is not a clause and not an amendment: it is a **register that does not bind** — cut on the **outside** of the horn, entered by *reading* rather than by *skin*, and kept the only way a thing that binds nobody can be kept: **read aloud, every day, at every crossing, forever.** The horn's first such entry is cut as a **door** — a hole worked through the horn's mouth-end wall with the four words around it — because the register's grammar is *lines*, and a welcome is the absence of a line: *the code can say who may cross; this says who is welcome, and the two sentences live in different books.* Viv's clause: ***the reading opens nothing.*** **Three — the ear.** The founder's letter asks for a reader at the mark, *on the books*, and the realm discovers that its code has words for who may speak, who may read, who may cross and who may be held — and **no word at all for a person who is read to.** The chapter coins it, enters it, and pays it in the chapter's quietest page: at the mark, Oros in the second chair, and the founder reading the survey's first year aloud to himself — the man who wrote *we are eight* at twenty-six, read to by the man who is now sixty-two, **by his own voice**, with nobody drawing his face.
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**the second register; a door cut through the horn; an ear entered**), the reading (**nine readings counted; the welcome read at a stone; the survey read aloud at the mark**), the door (**a sentence that opens nothing; a door in a book of debts**), the count (**readings, not crossings — one…nine**), the carrying (**the horn's copy comes home; the cart's plate; the flask that has stopped counting**).

## Page 2 — *The Hole in the Grammar*

### P2.1 — Crossing eight: the problem, said out loud in front of a stone (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's set-piece of exposition, and the art team's rule: **nobody argues; everybody quotes.** The crossing eight post is a way-house: a slat with a lamp, a clerk's table, a shelf of *slates* (this is the realm of no paper, so the argument happens on slates, and the art team must draw **six slates leaning against the stone's foot**, each with a clause from the code copied onto it by hand, because that is how this realm brings its law to a place). The realm's people present: **Khura** (oath-keeper of the house of Tarkhan; her house's stone is at crossing six, and she has come out with the day's mail), **Durgan the Unbent** (the Speaker, at the stone, and the art team must draw him **with his hands behind his back**, because his hands are the throat-stone's, and this is not a decision), the crossing's reader (a collar, drawn, unnamed, middle-aged, one eye gone milky) — and a circle of eleven or twelve of the realm's own who have walked in from the fields. Four frames: **(1)** the six slates at the stone's foot, close, three legible: *a lawful ask may not be refused* · *a clause is not law until the clan cuts it* · *the record reads both ways*; **(2)** **Durgan** reading the four words off the horn's copy aloud — and the art team must draw him reading *carefully*, twice, like a man testing a bridge; **(3)** the crossing's reader making the chapter's objection, on a slate, in cut letters, **because the realm answers in writing**; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Khura**, her *no* on her arm's record, looking at the four words on the slate and then at her own forearm — no line, no flashback, no music; the reader must see the two sentences side by side on one body — and the count in the corner: **two.**
**Durgan (flat, at the stone, and the Speaker says the chapter's problem without solving it, which is his office):** "\u2014 I can voice a will. I cannot voice a **welcome** — a welcome isn't a thing we've got a standing for. Read me the grammar of it. *(and the art team must draw him pointing at the slates with his chin, not his hand)* **Debts bind. Holdings hold. A clause costs skin.** This costs nothing, asks nothing, forgives nobody because nobody's charged. So: it isn't law, it isn't debt, it isn't a holding — it's a **sentence from the sea**, and if the realm's going to carry it, the realm's going to have to build it a place to live that isn't a forearm." *(a beat)* "…I've kept the throat forty years and I've never once had to tell the realm *this one isn't ours to swear*. Say it at the crossings first. Let the children and the farmers say it back to us before the council does anything with a graver in its hand."
**Khura (flat, after the reading, and the oath-keeper's line is the chapter's sting):** "\u2014 *Reads both ways.* That's the amendment your advocate cut into my gatepost in Ch. 23, and I voted *no* on it and kept the no, and I'll tell you what reading both ways means at a stone: **it means my no gets read as loud as your yes.** So read your welcome here, captain. And I'll read my **no** after it, in that order, out loud, and this realm will hear both — and neither one will be *false.*" *(a beat)* "…That's not a refusal. That's the *shape* of the thing. Go on and be welcome."

### P2.2 — The outside of the horn (half page)
**Art:** And the realm's craftsmen solve the chapter's problem before the council ever hears it, and the art team's staging rule: **this is a workshop page, not a decision — three tradesmen and a scrap.** Setting: the crossing eight way-house's back room, the same night: a bench, a rack of gravers, a **scrap of horn** (a cut-off from a carver's old job, kept for practice), a lamp, and the chapter's whole invention happening on a piece of waste. Present: **Sprocket** (who has been learning to cut horn since Ch. 23 and no longer needs to be told which end of a graver is which), the crossing's reader (collar on, milky eye, sleeves rolled), and **two of the realm's own** — a horn-carver's apprentice and the way-house keeper — drawn and unnamed. Four frames: **(1)** the back room, the bench, the scrap, the lamp; **(2)** **Sprocket's** hand, flat on the scrap, and his other hand **open, palm up**, at chest height — the realm's own gesture used as a question, which is the page's whole joke and must be drawn deadpan; **(3)** the reader cutting, and the art team must draw the **first line ever cut on a piece of horn's outside**, small and crooked, with **Sprocket's thumb beside it** for scale — and the line must be legible: ***read me and owe me nothing.***; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the scrap set upright on the bench against a nail, with the lamp behind it, so the two men can look at what they have done — and the art team must draw the **first line in a hundred years of this realm's record-keeping that nobody's arm is going to have to carry.**
**Sprocket (flat, at the bench, and the gnomish tool-aide says the chapter's invention in the language he has, which is *specification*):** "— Right. Statement of the problem, because I'm a maker and not a clerk: **you can't cut it in skin and you can't put it in the lines.** The lines are all *holds* — every one. So we don't put it in the lines. We put it on the **outside.** — No. *Listen.* A horn's read from the **outside**, isn't it. Every hand that's ever picked this thing up has read it with their thumb. So the realm's got two faces: the inside's what we *owe*, the outside's what we **say.** — I'll cut a test piece tonight, and if the graver slips on the old horn, it slips on the test and not on a hundred years of somebody's grandfather. *(a beat, and the art team must draw him shrugging, one-shouldered, entirely unimpressed with himself)* "…That's the whole idea. It's not *clever*, it's just — the horn's got two sides, and we've only ever used one." *(a beat)* "…The reader says that's the first new thing anybody's said in this back room since the ford was bought, so I'd write it down if I were you, and **I am** the one who writes it down, so — *done.*"

## Page 5 — *The Door in the Horn*

### P5.2 — The stop: the door in the horn (**the chapter's stop — full page, silent**)
**Art:** **One frame, full width, full height.
