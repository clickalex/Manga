# AURELION · Volume 5 — Chapter 22: "War-Song"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc E: "The One Throat" — VOLUME 5 OPENS · the road west runs out of road and into a pass that makes every traveller say the worst thing about themselves out loud before it lets them in; a realm with the best procedure in the world has not been able to *vote* for a year; a dead man's house is lawfully holding its own War-Chief as surety for a debt owed by strangers; and a captain who has been counting her breaths in a war-song since Chapter 3 walks through a door that has no lock and finds out that the thing she came to break is her father's own honour.**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Morgatha (**the count; the debt she carries; her realm**), **Viv** (**the clause, and the first time she has ever asked a realm for its *text* and been shown a *forearm***), Kaelen (**the coat, the scar — one line, flat**), Lyra (the noticer, measuring a realm against the grove), Nereia (**the flask, mended; no line at all — her markings do it**), Azrael, Bramble (**the barometer — and one frame at the debt-line that she does not explain**), Fenn (the map's west edge; the couriers; six pins), Marginalia (the cart — stopped at the line and not permitted past it, which she takes *very* well), Sprocket. **The Barrens** — **War-Chief Ulrik the Unbroken** (Morgatha's father; housed, unharmed, and *lawful*), **Khura the oath-keeper** (the house of Tarkhan's voice — and the horn), **Drog** (the elder who keeps the debt-line), **Ulgan** (the song-keeper), **Ura** (healer, who reads scars like a book before she mends them — and who *shows* Viv the realm's text), **Durgan the Unbent** (**the Ultravote's speaker — who cannot speak**), **Mender Glintfin** (TROLL — **03a's line, spent verbatim**), **Tarkhan** (dead six years; a name in a stone and a debt on a horn), **Gorakh the Scarred** (**a name cut in a horn, and nothing else — yet**), **Fast** (goblin courier; one line, and it is the season's cold water). **Also:** **Roderick** (**his one quiet panel** — the empty stone), and — one frame each, no lines — **Fast** (a goblin courier, with news nobody wants), **Tauron** at the maze gate, **Durgan** at the throat-stone.
> **Locations / threads:** the debt-line (the pass, the queue, the recitation) → the ford and the river-caste's gardens → the closed fords and the checkpoints (the realm's *thin* war) → the Ultravote ground (the stones, the throat-stone, the stone with nobody at it) → the surety-hall (a door with no lock) → the maze gate (promise only) → the circle, convened at last → the camp, and the first words of a new clause.
> **Arc beat:** Volume 5 opens the way Volume 4 opened — with a realm in the middle of being *lawful* at itself. The Barrens' one procedure is the **Ultravote** (all clans, one throat), and it has not spoken in a year, because a vote needs every clan in the circle and **one clan has stopped coming**. The missing clan is the house of **Tarkhan**, Morgatha's brother, dead six years at a Reforgers' cell — because a blood-debt that cannot be collected from the debtor **comes home**, and this one came home to the dead man's own household, which now holds the realm's War-Chief as **surety**: not in a cell, not in chains, *housed*, with a fire and a chair, because that is what the code says care looks like. The clans have been eating each other for a year without one battle, over the question of who is *wronged* — which is the Reforgers' whole method in this realm: they did not break the code, they **used** it. So the chapter does three jobs: it shows the realm's law as a *beautiful, unbearable procedure* (the debt-line, where every traveller reads their own worst debt aloud; the Ultravote, where nothing can be decided and nobody will decide *for* anyone); it puts the team's lawyer in a realm whose law is **carried in skin** — the code is cut into forearms and kept in memory, there is no text, and Viv's reaction to that is the volume's engine; and it lands the wound on the one person who cannot lawyer her way around it: Morgatha walks through an unlocked door, finds her father comfortable, cared for and *unbreakable*, hears him defend the code with his own honour, and cannot answer him — **the count stops** (the pilot's rule: when the count stops, that's the panel) — and then, because her creed is *readable*, she asks her father to recite the clause to her, out loud. The chapter ends with the realm's circles closed and the vote *not* taken: a captain standing at the throat-stone with a clause she has drafted in words and not yet cut into anything, and Durgan the Unbent, the realm's voice, keeping the whole circle waiting in a silence that is *procedure*, not drama.
> **Motifs advanced:** the code (**law without text — the body as the archive; the amendment that has to be *carried* to pass**), the count (**the war-song count returns as the volume's clock, set in the frame's corner, and it STOPS once, on the chapter's wound**), the debt (**a debt that came home; a surety-hall with a fire in it; the ledger carved in horn**), the door (**a door with no lock, which is worse**), the mended thing (**the troll menders — the flask, the ford, the wall, and a War-Chief's mended body**), the blank (**Vol. 4 ended with a blank line in a book; Vol. 5 opens with a blank patch of forearm where the new clause will be cut**). **Not in this chapter:** **Titanvein** (`#d1495b` — reserved for the Ultravote's own chapter; it does **not** appear once in Ch. 22); the stone-song's bloom (Khazadûm's; never restaged); the sea's first line (Nereia has **no dialogue** in this chapter); Kaelen's second and third pilot uses; the Forge-Duel, the Ark, or any Khazadûm object; the Tenth Frame; and **any resolution** — the vote is not taken, the clause is not passed, the hostage is not freed, and Gorakh the Scarred is a name on a horn and nothing more.

## Page 1 — *The Debt-Line*

### P1.1 — The pass that makes you talk (2/3 page)
**Art:** The border of the Barrens, and the art team's key image for the whole volume: **the debt-line.** A pass in red rock; iron-grass on the shoulders; wind; and standing in the middle of the road a **black slab**, twice a dwarf's height, its whole face cut over with **oath-script in hundreds of hands** — different chisels, different centuries, different languages, all of it *legible as writing* if you know how to look (canon's rule for this realm: *the body as book* — and this is the realm's front door, so the whole border is a *page*). No gate, no gatehouse, no soldiers. A **queue**: a troll family with a mended cart, a minotaur with a horn slung on his back, four goblins moving like weather, a cyclops bent under the weight of his own eye, orc grandmothers, orc children, a wagon-train. And the rite, drawn in three of the queue's figures in the foreground: **each traveller steps up and reads their debt out loud** — flat, unhurried, in their own register — while an old orc sits on a stone beside the slab with his hands on his knees, listening, having listened for so long that the work of it has settled into his posture. The art team's absolute direction: **nobody is ashamed, nobody is detained, and nobody pays.** This is a doorway with a toll of the truth, and the realm walks through it forty times a day, and it is *not a punishment*; it is the culture at the door.
**Grandmother (at the slab, reading, entirely bored, the way you'd give your name):** "— I owe my sister eleven years of a garden, and I've two goats of my brother's that died, and I said a thing to my daughter's husband in the spring I've not taken back. …That's the whole of it."
**The old orc (Drog, on his stone, nodding, one hand moving — he is not writing, there is nothing to write on):** "Aye. Go on through, mother."

### P1.2 — The queue from the east (bottom third)
**Art:** The team at the back of the queue, drawn small and foreign: the cart (and **Marginalia's** cart is stopped with the queue, which she is clearly *delighted* about), Sprocket, Fenn with the map rolled and his hat down, Bramble with the scarf; and at the front, **Morgatha**, standing the way she stands — still, hands at her sides, forearms bare in the wind (the art team: **this is the first time the manga has ever shown both forearms in full sunlight**, and the oath-script must read as *writing*, dense, cut deep, dozens of clauses in dozens of hands — and one long, loose, unfinished one down the right, which the reader has been seeing since Ch. 3). Ahead of her the queue moves. **Drog** looks up at her and does not stand.
**Drog (flat, to the whole team, no hostility at all — a man at a door doing his job):** "You'll be reading, or you'll be walking back the way you came."
**Viv (flat, immediate, standing in the road with her arms folded, and this is the first thing she has ever been asked at a border):** "— Requirement, or *liturgy*?"
**Drog (a beat; and the old man takes an unreasonable amount of pleasure in the answer):** "…Aye."
**Art note (the page's last frame):** **Morgatha at the slab.** The queue has stopped behind her because everyone in the pass has recognised who is reading. And the corner of the frame carries, for the first time in Volume 5, the **count** — cut small, four marks and a rest-bar, the way the art team has lettered it since the pilot (Ch. 3's rule: **the count runs in the corner of her frames**).

## Page 2 — *What She Reads*

### P2.1 — The debt she carries (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's first held image, and the art team's direction: **the smallest page in the volume.** Morgatha stands at the slab and reads across it, and behind her the pass goes silent *without anyone being told to* — a queue of forty people and a wagon-train and four goblins, all of them stopping mid-step, because half the realm knows the name she is about to say and the other half has heard it for six years. And it is *not a speech*: she reads the way the grandmother read — flat, bored, giving her name at a door.
**Morgatha (flat, to the stone, and this pays Ch. 3 P4.2 — *his oath went with him — unkept. Unfinished*):** "— Tarkhan, my brother. A Reforgers' cell took him six years ago, and his oath went with him. **Unkept. Unfinished.** It's mine now. I carry it." *(a beat)* "…And I've an exile's year against my name, and I'm not sorry for it, and the realm can read that off my arms if it wants to."
**Drog (after a long beat; quiet, and the old man says the thing the realm will spend the volume deciding about):** "…You'll want to know whose stone stands empty, then."
**Morgatha (flat, and the count in the corner does not move, and the art team is to make the reader notice that):** "I know whose stone stands empty."

### P2.2 — The realm's answer (half page)
**Art:** Four frames, and the art team's rule: **there is no hostility in any of them.** **(1)** The queue behind her, at work again — the troll family's cart rolling, the grandmother's goats, the minotaur with his horn — and every single one of them, as they pass her, doing the same small thing: **a hand up, palm out, no word** (the Barrens' courtesy: not a salute, not a wave: *the debt-acknowledgement*, given to somebody who has just read)*. **(2)** **Drog**, standing up at last, to walk her in — and stopping to give her the *same* palm, because the code says the door-keeper owes a reading back: he reads *his* debt to her, flat, in exchange for hers — that is the courtesy, and it is the entire realm in one panel. **(3)** **Bramble**, at the back of the team, watching the palms go up — and the art team's one job with her here: **she does not make a joke**, and she is drawn looking at her *own* hands for one frame, and **nothing in the chapter or the volume explains why** (her tag, her nine faces: the changeling who has never once been able to read herself out loud). **(4)** Fenn, pen out, adding the pass to the map — and the *pin*, this time, is a **debt-line**, not a capital.
**Drog (reading his own, flat, to Morgatha, palm out, the exchange complete):** "— I owe my son a name, and I've never given it. …Come in, captain. The realm's been *waiting* to be ashamed in front of you."
**Marginalia (tiny italics, at the frame's foot, and the cart has been stopped at the line for exactly four panels and is furious about it in writing):** *"The cart notes that the border of the Barrens is a stone that makes everyone who touches it tell the truth, and that the cart was not permitted to read a debt because the cart has none, which the cart's driver found funnier than the cart did."*

## Page 3 — *The Menders (the realm's hands)*

### P3.1 — Glintfin, and the flask (half page)
**Art:** The ford below the pass, and the Barrens' first *surprise* — this realm is not a wasteland: the **river-caste's gardens** run along the water in worked terraces (troll country: irrigation ditches cut with an artisan's hand, fish-traps shaped like baskets, a **bridge with nine different repairs** in it, each one labelled in a different hand with a small cut mark), and everything in frame has been *fixed* — a mill-wheel with mismatched paddles, a wall of river-stones of forty sizes all mended flush, a cart with a wheel that was not made for it and fits. A troll — **Glintfin**, aproned, hands permanently wet, three tools in his belt — is kneeling at a fish-trap, and he looks up as the team comes down, and he looks *first*, before anything else, at **Nereia** — at her hip, at the flask (two-thirds cracked since Ch. 16, and every reader has been watching it for six chapters). And then he stands up, takes it out of her hand without asking, holds it to the light, and spends his line.
**Glintfin (flat, entirely practical, already reaching for thread, and this is 03a's line, spent verbatim):** "*Your flask's cracked. That's not a* monster *problem. That's a* mender *problem. Sit down. I've got thread.*"
**Art note (the beat the page exists for, and it must be drawn as *work*):** five frames of it — the thread, the fire, the tin of glue, the *clamp* (a mender's clamp, a real tool), and the flask going back into Nereia's hand **whole**, with the crack visible as a *mend* — a line of grey thread and glue across the glass, not hidden. **Nereia says nothing in this chapter at all** — the art team does it with her markings instead: they come up, slow and unguarded, from the low-tide quiet she has been drawn in since Ch. 14 to the *rising* colour, and the reader who has been reading her before her dialogue for twenty chapters gets the scene without a word. **And the sea's first line is not here** — she does not sing, does not speak, and does not explain what is in the flask.

### P3.2 — Viv asks for the text (half page)
**Art:** Later, same page or the next frame set: **Viv** at the menders' bench with a lamp, doing what Viv does at every border in this saga — asking for the *document*. And the art team's key panel of the volume's legal engine: **Drog and Ura and two menders, and the answer is arms.** Ura (canon: the healer who *reads scars like a book before she mends them*) rolls up both sleeves without ceremony, puts her forearms on the bench under the lamp, and **that is the text.** The art team must draw them *readable*: dozens of short clauses in cut script, the oldest faded silver, the newest still pink, some crossed by an amendment cut *through* them (the realm's way of striking a clause: a second cut through the first), and — the detail the whole volume turns on — **no clause anywhere on the four arms that has a word for a debt that has been *moved***. Around them, the menders' endless ordinary work: a wheel, a pot, a doll's leg, a mended boot.
**Viv (flat, and this is the lawyer arriving in her worst nightmare, and the art team should let her look *sick*):** "— You've no *text*. Your law is cut into *people*."
**Ura (flat, unbothered — she has had this conversation with outsiders before):** "Aye. And every one of us *is* a page, and half of us are wrong, and when we die the page goes in the fire with us. …You'll be wanting the *carrying*, then."
**Viv (a beat; and the chapter's thesis, flat, precise, and the first thing in Volume 5 that makes the reader hopeful):** "…I'll be wanting the clause you *haven't got*."

## Page 4 — *A Thin War*

### P4.1 — The checkpoints (half page)
**Art:** The road in, and the art team's job is to make the realm's war feel like a **family argument with axes in it**: three frames and one wide. **(1)** A **closed ford**: two clans' young men on either bank, arms folded, not one weapon drawn, and a grandmother between them doing the actual work — reading *out loud* at the water, both clans' grievances, alternately, from memory, for hours, because the code says the ford may be closed but the *reading* may not stop. **(2)** A market where two clans trade — and eat in separate rings, drawn as two circles of firelight with one table in the middle and nobody's back to anybody (the code: you may not turn your back on kin; so the realm has invented a way to eat at war *very politely*). **(3)** **A marriage, suspended** — laugh-out-loud and awful: a bride and groom sitting on a bench in their good clothes with a mender between them, and a *stone* on the bench between all three; the art team's note: the caption for this frame is one line in a goblin's mouth, and it is the funniest line in the chapter. **(4)** Wide: the road onward, and, in the distance, the **Ultravote ground** — a shallow bowl in the plain with a ring of standing stones, **one to a clan**, and one of them with *nothing at it*.
**Goblin trader (flat, chewing, to Sprocket, gesturing at the bench):** "— Marriage holds till the blood-debt's read. Two years now. They'll have to *re-wed* it. …I'd have charged less the second time."
**Fast (goblin courier, flat, arriving at a run, dropping a satchel — empty, and that is the point — beside Fenn, and the line is the season's coldest because of how flatly it is delivered):** "— South road's still shut, uncle. No letters out of the green or the gray. …The swarm's *got* nothing, so the swarm's *saying* nothing. That's the news."
**Fenn (flat, and the art team's key beat for him in Volume 5 — he does not write *no letters* again: he puts the pen down and writes a **date**):** "…Then I'll be logging *days* instead."
**Lyra (flat, from the cart, and the elf gets the line because she has spent her whole life inside a law that talks):** "…That's a *wall* with a wedding on it."

### P4.2 — The line the realm eats at (half page)
**Art:** The market's two firelight rings, held for a full half page, and the art team's direction: **nobody in this frame is enjoying the war.** Draw the same faces over both fires — cousins, in-laws, men who learned to fight shoulder to shoulder; and over the empty table in the middle, *food that both sides have put there*, going cold. **Drog** sits on the outermost bench with his hands on his knees, and **Khura** — the oath-keeper of the house of Tarkhan, apron, sleeves rolled, a knife at her belt and a **horn** on a thong over her shoulder (the art team's key prop of the volume: the debt-record carved in horn, Ch. 23's dark, and the reason nobody in the Barrens needs paper) — sits on the other side, and neither of them will look at the other, and both of them are *absolutely correct, by law.* **Durgan the Unbent** passes behind them with a lamp and does not stop walking; and the reader should notice what he does not do: **he does not speak to anyone at all.**
**Khura (flat, to the table, not to Drog, and it is not an insult — it is an inventory):** "Nine months. Two weddings held, one funeral split, forty-readings-a-day at the line, and no *throat* in the circle. …My house will come when the debt comes *in*, elder. Not before. The code's the code."
**Drog (equally flat, equally to the table):** "Aye. And the realm's got no *mouth* till your stone's got feet under it. …That's the code too."

## Page 5 — *The Ultravote Ground*

### P5.1 — The arena of one throat (half page)
**Art:** The **Ultravote ground**, and the art team's design job: the pilot's phrase *all clans, one throat* made *architecture*. A shallow, hand-cut bowl in the plain; a ring of **standing stones** around the rim, one per clan, each cut with that clan's oath-script and that clan's own mender-marks and *patches* (the art team: **the stones look like the people who own them** — one is old silver-grey and repointed, one is a red-black slab with a chisel-worn edge, one is two stones mended into one); and at the bowl's floor-centre, low, flat, waist-high: **the throat-stone**, worn smooth on its top by hands, and *empty*. Around it, the realm: hundreds of orcs, minotaurs, trolls, goblins, kobolds, cyclopes — sitting, standing, kids on shoulders, kettles going, because this is *also* the realm's gathering place and the realm has been gathering here for a year to not vote. And the one detail the whole page hangs on: **one stone in the ring, in the best light, with nobody standing at it and nothing carved on its face yet** — Tarkhan's house's stone, drawn *blank* (the art team: yes, this is deliberate; the volume started with a blank in Khazadûm and it starts here with one too, and there is no line about it).
**Ulgan (flat, from the ring, an old orc with a drum the size of a fist, being *utile* about it):** "— We'll hold the reading. We always hold the reading. …The *vote's* the thing we can't do, and it's not *us* that can't." *(she taps the drum twice)* "That's the *song*, if you want it. The realm's been singing a verse with a hole in it for a year."

### P5.2 — Durgan does not speak (half page)
**Art:** **Durgan the Unbent**, the Ultravote's speaker, and the art team's direction for him: **a man doing a ceremony that has nothing to do.** Tall, old, iron-grey, in the plainest clothes in the realm — because the speaker is not a chief, he is a *throat* — and he stands at the throat-stone with his hands flat on it, facing the ring, with the empty stone in his eyeline. Four frames: **(1)** his hands on the stone; **(2)** the ring's faces; **(3)** the empty stone; **(4)** his mouth not opening. And the vignette's rule, lettered in the caption: **the speaker may only read the assembled will of *every* clan; with a stone empty, the throat-stone is a rock.** He does not orate, does not explain, does not apologise; he stands there for the whole scene like a man holding a door open for nobody, because that is the office. **He speaks one line in this chapter and it is not his canon line** (held for the chapter the clause passes).
**Durgan (flat, to the ring, hands still on the stone, and this is the volume's problem in nine words):** "…One stone empty. **One throat. No voice.** The realm may read. The realm may not *decide*."
**Viv (quiet, to Morgatha, at the rim, and the lawyer's assessment of the best procedure she has ever seen):** "— There's no chair, no gavel, no writ, and no *chairman*. Your realm's got the cleanest vote I've ever watched and it can't take it because one family's *grieving correctly*."
**Morgatha (flat, watching the empty stone, and the count in the corner ticks over once):** "Aye."

## Page 6 — *The House of the Dead Man*

### P6.1 — A door with no lock (half page)
**Art:** The **surety-hall** — the house of Tarkhan, and the art team's hardest design problem in the chapter, because it must be *both*: a house where a man is held, and **a house.** Two storeys of red stone and cut timber; a garden with a trench dug and plants in; a goat; washing on a line; a child's handprints in the wall-plaster; a **bench outside the door** where neighbours have clearly sat; and, under all of it, the one thing that makes it a lock: **the bracket by the door, empty, with the four bolt-holes in the stone — a door that *has* a bar and no longer keeps it, and the bar is leaning inside the doorframe, in reach, where the held man can see it and take it up whenever he likes.** The art team's rule, absolute: **do not draw a cell.** No chains, no guard, no shutter, no iron. There is a fire inside, plainly, in the chimney's smoke; there is bread on the sill; and the door is open in the daytime, and has been for a year.
**Marginalia (tiny italics, a small flat note at the frame's foot, because the cart is at the line and the team is here):** *"The cart notes the door of a house in the Barrens standing open with a bolt leaning inside it, and that the cart has spent a month in a realm that locked a mountain and never once needed a key, and that the cart would like it on the record that it does not know which of the two doors it would rather be behind."*
**Khura (from the door, flat, apron, horn on its thong, and she has come out to meet them the way a householder meets a party — because that, too, is the code):** "Captain. …He's had his breakfast. You can go in or you can go away, but you'll not stand in my yard and *work* on it."

### P6.2 — The horn (half page)
**Art:** The bench outside the door, midday, and the chapter's legal page drawn as **an object**: Khura puts the **debt-horn** into Morgatha's hands and lets her read it — a war-horn of black horn, its whole surface carved in cut script (the Barrens' archive: no paper, no ink, no clerk — the record is a *horn*), and the art team must draw, legible in the smear of the candle-hold and the reader's own eye, the *shape* of what it says: **the name TARKHAN**; the cell; the date; and then, lower, a second, newer cut in a different hand — **the carrying**: a clause that moves the debt onto the nearest kin of the dead, so that the debt comes home and may be held as **surety** until it is *paid*. Under the second cut: a mark the reader has not seen before, small, foreign, cut by someone who did not use the realm's chisel: **a name. GORAKH.** The art team's direction: this reveal is three panels of *reading*, no reaction shots, no exclamation, and the team's faces are only in the fourth frame.
**Morgatha (flat, reading, one finger moving down the horn, and the count in the corner has stopped moving — the art team: not stopped, *paused*, and the reader should feel the difference):** "…'The debt comes home.' Whose *hand* is that?"
**Khura (flat, and she has read it a thousand times and has her answer ready, and there is not one excuse in her):** "A cyclops. One eye. Reads maps. He came, he asked for the *words*, and my house's keeper gave them — because he asked *lawfully*, captain, and a lawful ask may not be refused. That's the code. …You'll want to hate me for it. I'd rather you read it."
**Viv (quiet, taking the horn, turning it to the light; and the lawyer names the crime that this realm has no word for):** "— He didn't break your law. He *read* it, and then he *used* it, and you've no word in your whole grammar for a man who does that." *(a beat)* "…I'm going to have to *invent* one."

## Page 7 — *Through the Door*

### P7.1 — The housed man (half page)
**Art:** Inside, and the art team's key direction: **warm, well-lit, mended.** A stone-walled room with a big hearth; a chair with a cushion *made* for a large old man's back; a table with a bowl on it; a window with real glass, mended (a mender's work: the glass has a leaded seam and a mender's cut mark); tools on a nail; a child asleep in a corner on a folded blanket, entirely unbothered by the War-Chief in the room, because that is what this house is. And **Ulrik the Unbroken**, sitting in the chair, drawn *huge* — a man whose body is a mender's catalogue: old wounds, troll-cut seams, a shoulder that has been *re-set* by somebody who knew what they were doing, hands that have been rebuilt. He is **not** restrained, **not** thin, **not** bruised. He is *housed*, and he is *kind*, and he is *glad to see her*, and he does not get up.
**Ulrik (quiet, warm, in the huge flat voice of a man who has been a war-chief for forty years and knows exactly what he is doing):** "…Girl. You took your time." *(a beat; and then, before she can say a word — because he is a war-chief and he has already read the whole board)* "— And no. I'll not walk out of this house."
**Morgatha (flat; and the art team must draw her the way the canon says — no monster-face, no raised voice; the count in the corner, cut, *paused*):** "You'd sit in another man's chair for a *debt you don't owe*."
**Ulrik (flat, and this is the chapter's blow, delivered kindly, which is why it lands):** "I sit in *my son's* chair, in *my son's* house, and I'm *fed*, and I've a garden to pull weeds in, and I'll not walk out that door and tell nine clans that a war-chief's word is a curtain you can push through. **I'd rather be held than be the reason the realm's law was a lie.**"

### P7.2 — The count stops (half page)
**Art:** And here the art team does the volume's first **stop** — the pilot's rule, stated in Ch. 4 and paid at Ch. 11, and now the rule of Volume 5: **when the count stops, that is the panel.** One frame, full width, no dialogue, no count in the corner, and the art team's whole craft in one face: a woman who has been counting her breaths since she was fourteen so that her rage stays a *tool* — looking at the only person alive who can out-argue her, in a warm room, on a chair she cannot break, holding a debt she cannot pay. **No Titanvein. No glow. No monster-face. Nothing in the panel but her and a pause** — and the reader, who has spent twenty-one chapters watching this count, will feel the silence like a dropped weight. Then the next frame, no dialogue either: she **sits down on the floor** with her back against the hearth, like a child, and puts the horn on the flags in front of her.
**Then (the turn, and it must be quiet):** **Morgatha (flat, looking at the horn and not at him):** "…Read me the clause, Father. All of it. Every word you've been sitting on for a year." *(a beat)* "**I'm not breaking it. I'm reading it.**"

## Page 8 — *Recitation*

**Ulrik (reciting, level):** "…*The fifth holding: a debt unrecovered comes home, and the house it comes home to holds it, and the holder may hold the surety of the debtor's nearest blood until the debt is answered.*" *(a beat)* "That's the one they used, girl. **Viv (flat, from the floor, eyes closed, and this is the exact moment the volume's engine turns over):** "…It assumes it *comes*. It's got no word for a debt that's been *carried*." *(her eyes open)* "Do you know what your realm's got instead?"

## Page 10 — *The Clause, Out Loud*

### P10.1 — Two women at a fire (half page)
**Art:** The team's camp that night, and the art team's page for **the drafting**: the *campfire* frame the volume has been earning — **Viv and Morgatha**, the horn on a flat stone between them with a lamp on it, Viv with her sleeves rolled to her elbows and her hands *empty* (no pen, no paper — this realm has taken her *instruments* away, and the art team should draw the deprivation as *work*: she is drafting with her fingers on her own forearm, testing the wording on skin, because that is the medium this realm thinks in). Around them, the team's domestic register: the cart's kettle on the fire; Lyra's rings; Kaelen at the edge of the firelight with the coat open and the scar steady; Fenn asleep on the map; Sprocket trying to work out how to *record* a proceeding that produces no paper, and being quietly thrilled by the difficulty; Azrael's one line — he gets one, and it is *his* register. And in the frame's far corner, one small panel: **Nereia**, mended flask on her knee, the mend's grey thread catching the firelight, **her markings rising**, saying nothing at all.
**Azrael (flat, watching Viv work, and the fallen angel's contribution to a discussion of law without text):** "…I broke my oath on *parchment*. Somebody wrote it down, and sealed it, and I tore it. Two hundred years I've thought the *paper* was the problem." *(a beat)* "You've got a realm where the law's a *scar*, and they still had to trust somebody's *memory* of a hand that moved a debt. Paper's not the problem. **People are the archive in every realm. Some of us just lie about it.**"
**Viv (flat, not looking up, and this is the exact register of her winning an argument she has been in for twenty-one chapters):** "— Then we'll write it where they *keep* things."

### P10.2 — The clause (half page)
**Art:** The clause itself, and the page the volume's spine runs through: four frames, no crowd, all close. **(1)** Viv says the words; **(2)** Morgatha says them *back*, testing them for weight, once, flat; **(3)** the sentence, lettered large — **the art team should set it as a cut line, because that is what it is about to become: a clause in the realm's register, cut script, in the same grammar as the arms**; **(4)** and the beat that makes it *cost something*: **Viv's hand on Morgatha's forearm, stopping it.** Because the realm's amendment procedure is on the page in this frame, and it is the whole of Volume 5's price: **a clause is not law until the clan that voted for it cuts it into itself.** The art team must draw Viv — the lawyer, the vampire, the girl who has argued with this whole saga — looking at a forearm as a *ballot box*, and hating it, and not saying no.
**Viv (flat, four frames, and the words land like a *finding*):** "— *The oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code.*" *(a beat)* "It's got a *subject* — a life; it's got a *lien* — the code; and it's got an order of payment, and it doesn't *strike* one clause, it *subordinates* it." *(a beat)* "In my realm, a clause like that costs a *vote*." *(and then she puts her hand on the captain's arm)* "…Here it costs *skin*, and you're going to stand in front of nine clans and offer them your *arm*."
**Morgatha (flat, looking at the bare patch of forearm where nothing is cut yet — the art team: **hold on this frame**, it is Volume 5's blank):** "Aye. …It'll be *readable*."

## Page 11 — *Rehearsal*

### P11.1 — The father, rehearsing her (half page)
**Art:** The surety-hall, next morning, and the chapter's quietest and most painful scene, drawn as **work between two professionals**: Morgatha has come to *rehearse the vote* with the realm's best legal mind — her father — and **Ulrik argues against her, kindly, clause by clause, for a whole page.** Four frames of it, and the art team's rule: it must be *interesting*, not sentimental — he is a war-chief testing a clause the way a smith tests iron: *where's the limit — does it outrank the code if the life is a traitor's — what about a life freely given away — what stops the next man invoking it for a *feud* — girl, you're holding a blade with one edge.* And she answers, flat, and gets it *wrong* twice, and he catches her twice, and on the third exchange he stops arguing and *nods*, because she has said it the way it has to be said. And in the background of the last frame, the household at its ordinary work — a nephew sweeping, the goat in the yard, the child who sleeps in the corner now awake and *reading to herself off her own arm*, practising, six years old.
**Ulrik (flat, the last exchange, and the art team must draw him the way he was in his chair: unbroken):** "…And when it *passes*. When the realm's cut it into nine hundred arms and the code says a life outranks it. What then — the code's *soft*, and the next cell comes and finds the soft place?"
**Morgatha (flat, and this is her answer, and the volume will spend five chapters proving it):** "Then the code'll say a life outranks it, and *everything else stays*. You don't get the new holding *instead* of the old ones, Father. You get it **first**. …The code's not soft. It's got an *order* now."
**Ulrik (a beat; and then — the nod, one frame, no line, and the child reading off her arm in the background of it)**

### P11.2 — The held man and the sea (half page)

**END OF CHAPTER 22.**

*The Barrens meets you at a stone and makes you say the worst thing about yourself before it lets you in, and it does that forty times a day, and calls it manners, and nobody there finds it hard. It has one procedure for settling anything — every clan, one throat — and it has not been able to use it for a year, and the reason is not an army but a *clause*: a blood-debt that could not be collected from the men who owed it came home, as the code says such debts do, to the house of the man they killed, and that house is now lawfully holding its own War-Chief as surety, in a warm room, with a fire, and a bolt by the door that nobody has picked up in a year. That is the code taken to its end, and the man who took it there did not break one word of it: he read it, and then he used it. Into the middle of that walks a captain who has been counting her breaths in a war-song since she was fourteen, whose brother's name is on the horn and whose brother's house is the one holding their father — and the first thing her realm's law does to her is make her *correct*: her father will not leave, because leaving would make the realm's word a curtain, and he would rather be held. So she does not break anything. She sits down on the floor of his house with a horn in front of her and asks him to recite, and a lawyer with no pen finds out that this realm keeps its statute in people's memories and its amendments in people's skin, and that a realm can be argued with — because it has a vote, and the vote is where the Barrens keeps its door. Nine clans, one throat, a clause nobody has ever said out loud, and an arm with a clean patch on it: that is where Volume 5 starts, and the count in the corner is going up.*
**The record's cards are being cut into hide now, and there is one hash-mark at the bottom of the first one that nobody in the story ever reads out.**
