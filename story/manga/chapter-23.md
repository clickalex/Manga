# AURELION · Volume 5 — Chapter 23: "The Record of Losses"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc E: "The One Throat" — the realm's only building that *is* a document; a champion whose office is to lose; a clause taken off a captain's mouth and handed back; the oldest maps in the world kept under one eye a mile high, and the margin where the whole crime was *lawful*; and a hundred-year-old family map given back to the realm it was drawn from.**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Morgatha (**the count; the walk; the loss**), **Viv** (**the two holes — and the reason she wants them *documented*, not hidden**), **Fenn** (**the archive: the greatest map-room in the world, and the oldest copyist in it**), Kaelen (one frame, one line, and he is holding the coat), Lyra (the noticer, at the walls), Nereia (the flask, mended; **no dialogue**), Azrael (the fallen angel, at a wall of law, and his one line is *about* law), Bramble (**the barometer; and the gate-joke that lands wrong**), Sprocket (**his first cut**), Marginalia (the cart, forbidden the maze on *hygiene* grounds, and keeping notes about it). **The Barrens** — **Duelist-Champion Tauron** (**03a's line, spent; and his yield**), **Maze** (the labyrinth-keeper — the map, and the rules), **Horn** (the horn-carver — the duel-record, and the realm's *first clause in it*), **Taurus** (Tauron's rival, in the gate crowd, and one frame that is pure appetite), **Oros the Old Eye** (the sage — **his 03a line is HELD, and the chapter is built so that it must be**), **One-Eye · Iron-Eye · Ash-Eye** (the old-forges; the family map), **the kobold cave-carts** (Crystal · Stone · Fire-Knob — the copyists, and the best joke in the chapter), **Durgan the Unbent** (the office, silent again), **Khura** (at the gate, and one line), **Drog · Ulgan · Ura · Ghorak** (in the crowd), and **Roderick** (**his one quiet panel** — the margin, and the hand that wrote it).
> **Locations / threads:** the maze gate and its horn-slab (a queue of people who have come to lose) → the walls (ten thousand cut clauses, and the whole code read at a walker) → the chamber at the centre → the old-forges of the cyclopes and the map-room → the margin, and the lawful ask → the copyists' bench (Fenn's day) → the gate, again, at dusk, and the record being cut.
> **Arc beat:** The realm's law has no paper, so the place where a disputed *word* gets tested is not a court — it is the **maze**: a labyrinth cut down into red rock, its walls carrying the entire code in cut script, clause after clause, in order, and at its centre a champion who has never once needed to win. The minotaurs' whole philosophy is on the surface of this chapter: **the maze cannot be beaten, only paid** — every fighter who walks it loses, the loss is carved into horn, and the loser is given the maze, because in the Barrens a lesson you paid for is the only kind that counts. Morgatha goes in with the clause she drafted at a campfire and comes out without it: she fights Tauron at the centre, and he takes the words off her the way a wall takes a fighter's wind — she drops the clause to win one exchange, and he stops, and says it back to her, and yields, because she *lost something in his house* and that is the toll. What comes out of the maze is not a victory but a **finding**: two holes in the clause, in writing-shaped words and no writing, carried in her head to the circle. The second movement goes east to the **old-forges**, where one-eyed smiths and sages keep the oldest maps in the world in a room that cannot refuse a reader — and where the chapter's actual crime is found, in a **margin**: the request slip of a cyclops who came lawfully and asked for a copy of the realm's holdings, signed *Gorakh*, in a hand his own teacher taught. So the maps do the work no interrogation could: they show that the Reforgers did not need to break the Barrens' law, because the Barrens' law is *helpful*, and the volume's theme gets its second realm — **a rule that cannot refuse is an open door** — and the realm's answer is not to burn the archive but to *give the map back*: **Ash-Eye** returns the family map Gorakh's own family drew, so the blame for the Rift is shared, and the realm's oldest document comes home with the fault on it. The chapter ends at the maze gate at dusk with a horn and a knife: **Horn carves the realm's duel-record, and into a record of ten thousand losses somebody has cut a clause that lost.** The realm's archive gains its first *legal* entry — and it is a *failure*, on purpose, which is the most Barrens sentence in the volume.
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**a labyrinth that is a document; a horn that is a ledger; a *loss* entered as law**), the code (**readable — and read *past*; a realm's virtue as its attack surface**), the count (**five… six… seven, going up, and one stop**), the gift (**a lost duel is a gift; a map returned is blame shared; the maze given to the one who paid**), the door (**a gate that lets anybody in and asks only that they lose something**), the blank (**Vol. 5's blank arm has two holes in it now; nobody is going to pretend otherwise**). **Not in this chapter:** **Titanvein** (`#d1495b` — still barred; the count stops and the vein does **not** come), **Oros's 03a line** (*"I taught you to see the wound…"* — HELD; the chapter is built so that his *silence in front of his own handwriting* does the work instead), **Durgan's 03a line** and **Urzun's** and **Khura's reading** (all held for the vote), the stone-song's bloom, the sea's first line (Nereia silent), the Tenth Frame, and **any confrontation with Gorakh** — he is a signature in a margin, and the volume does not let anybody meet him yet.

## Page 1 — *The Gate of Ten Thousand Losses*

### P1.1 — The horn-slab (half page)
**Art:** The maze gate at midday, and the art team's key image for the chapter: **the horn-slab** above it — a slab of red rock three men wide, its whole face cut with short vertical strokes, packed shoulder to shoulder, thousands of them, the oldest weathered shallow at the top, the newest still pale at the bottom edge, and the newest *of those* still bright, cut that morning. Under the slab, the slot of the gate, and through the slot, dark. **Horn** — the horn-carver, small, bandy, wearing an apron made from a horn-shard, sitting on a stool at the gate with a knife and a **war-horn on his knees** (black, cut over with fine script, and in his hand a graver): he works while he talks, which is to say he barely talks. The queue: not the debt-line's pilgrims — **fighters.** A young orc with a bound wrist; two minotaurs, one old; a troll with a grudge and a stick; a goblin with a betting-book; kids on the wall; and a grandmother selling fried dough to the whole crowd, because a queue is a market. The art team's absolute direction for the page: **this is a queue of people who have come to lose**, and nobody in it looks ashamed or unhappy; several look *pleased*, the way people look before a test they have prepared for.
**Horn (flat, not looking up, graver moving, and this is how the realm talks about the maze):** "…Everybody loses. That's the record. You want to lose somewhere that *counts*, you queue here."

### P1.2 — The rules, in four frames (bottom third)
**Art:** The gate's rules, drawn as the realm's own signage — cuts, not letters, and the art team should draw them as *idiograms* cut into the gateposts (no dialogue needed): **(1)** one walker; **(2)** the walls are read, not fought; **(3)** the champion waits at the centre and never leaves it; **(4)** the loss is carved, and the maze goes to the loser. Beside the gate: a **rack of belts, blades, boots** — weapons left outside, and *not* for safety: the art team's note is that a fighter may carry a blade in, they simply may not use one *instead of* reading. And the frame's last panel: **Tauron**, in the slot of the gate, in the shade — huge, scarred, arms folded, watching the queue with the patience of a man at a job he has done ten thousand times, and behind him, at his shoulder, his rival **Taurus**, who is not on duty and came anyway, and whose face the art team must draw as **pure appetite:** he wants a walker *today*.
**Bramble (flat, at the back of the team's group, taking in the queue and the slab: the barometer at her driest, and the joke must land as a joke):** "…So the *door prize* for winning a fight here is *the fight*."
**Fenn (flat, folding his map, and the cartographer's version of the same joke):** "Aye. It's the only building I've ever seen that's *honest* about the price."

## Page 2 — *A Building That Is a Document*

### P2.1 — Inside (half page)
**Art:** The maze, three turns in, and the art team's design invention for Volume 5 — **the walls are the code**: every surface is cut, floor to shoulder-height, with the Barrens' oath-script, clause after clause, in a hand that changes every few feet because the walls have been written by *generations*; and the script is *legible* (canon's rule for this realm: a reader who has paid attention can read it), and the cuts are worn smooth where ten thousand fighters have run their hands along them in the dark. It is not a ruin and it is not a temple: it is a **book you walk through**, in daylight at the top and lamplit by the realm's own lamplighters below (the art team: **a lamplighter on a ladder, ordinary, doing a shift** — the realm maintains its own law's binding, and a maze-lamp is part of the municipality). And at the third turn, in the wall: **the fifth holding**, and next to it, in a newer cut, the four clauses this new one would outrank — and Viv, walking the wall with one hand flat on it, reading like a woman reading a wall.
**Maze (the labyrinth-keeper — an old minotaur with a shoulder-brace and a lamplighter's eyes, walking with them, flat and practical, and he is the chapter's *teaching* voice):** "— You'll be reading it out loud as you go. Not for us. For *you*. The wall's got the whole of it in *order*, and a walker who reads quietly gets to the centre with somebody else's law in his mouth."
**Viv (flat, hand on the wall, and she has just heard the most beautiful sentence of her professional life):** "…Your code's got a *shelf order*."

### P2.2 — How the maze is walked (half page)
**Art:** Four frames of the *method*, drawn as a demonstration the reader can follow, with **Maze** narrating: **(1)** a walker at the first turn, reading aloud, hands open at his sides; **(2)** the corridor *bending* — the art team's key mechanic: at every turn the wall's clause **contradicts the one before** (the code is not tidy: the third holding against the fifth, the debt against the guest-right, the blood-price against the ford), so a walker who tries to carry them all in order gets turned around and *has* to choose — the maze is a labyrinth of *priorities*, and its branches are the places where the code disagrees with itself; **(3)** a walker who has chosen wrong, sitting in a dead end with his arms on his knees, breathing, and a lamplighter stepping past him with a courteous murmur; **(4)** the wrong walker getting up and walking *back*, which is allowed, and which is why the queue outside is cheerful. The art team's captioning rule: **no character ever explains the maze's metaphor.** It is a maze. It is also the law. The panels do both and say neither.
**Maze (flat, watching the dead-end walker, and he is not contemptuous — he is *fond*):** "…He'll be back in an hour. Third time this season. He's got a *ford* argument, and the wall's got him on guest-right every time, and one day he'll read it right and then he'll be *dangerous*." *(a beat)* "Nothing in here is *mean*, captain. It just won't say a thing's *settled* when it isn't."

## Page 3 — *The Walker, and the Terms*

### P3.1 — The petition (half page)
**Art:** The centre chamber — a round room, low-roofed, with the walls *bare* (the art team's detail: the centre is the only place in the maze with nothing cut into it, because the centre is where somebody has to *say* it) — and the realm's terms, laid out on the floor in the form of three objects, which **Maze** sets down one at a time and names: **(1)** a **horn** — the record; **(2)** a **graver** — the marking; **(3)** a **stone bowl of water** and a **rag** — because the walls come away on your hands. **Durgan** is present as the office (silent; the speaker attends walks that may change the code, and says nothing until there is a will to speak); **Khura** stands at the wall with her arms folded; **Tauron** waits at the far door, arms folded, watching. And the chapter's *terms* are stated in the realm's flat grammar: the walker goes in with **the thing they want tested**, and comes out either having held it or having lost it; if it is held, the maze's count of losses is unchanged and the walker *wins the argument*; if it is lost, the walker's loss is carved and the walker receives the maze — **and either way the realm will know which.**
**Maze (flat, hands open over the three objects, and this is the volume's legal procedure being *offered*):** "— You'll be walking it with a *word* in your mouth. There's no rule against it. There's also no rule that says a word's easier to carry than a *blade*."
**Morgatha (flat, looking at the water and the rag, and not at Tauron, and the count in the corner is going up: five):** "Aye. I'll walk it."

### P3.2 — Viv's terms (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's smartest page, and the team's contribution to a realm that has never had one: **Viv, on the floor of the centre chamber, writing nothing, and *demanding a second object*** — she is not letting her client walk into a test where the only record is a carved hash-mark she cannot read from the outside. Four frames: **(1)** Viv stating the terms flatly; **(2)** Maze's face — an old minotaur being asked something nobody has ever asked; **(3)** Durgan's eyes, the first movement of the chapter from the office, and he does not object, because **the office may not refuse a lawful term that costs the code nothing**; **(4)** **Horn**, called in from the gate, sitting down in the doorway with his knife, and *listening*, because what is about to be said is going to be cut.
**Viv (flat, standing in the middle of a bare room in a foreign realm, doing the only thing she has ever been good at):** "— Terms. **One:** the walker may name what was lost, and it goes in the record *in words*, and the realm's keepers read it out where the walker says. **Two:** a cut goes in when the loss is *declared*, not when it's *decided* — no scribe of yours gets to decide later whether it counted. **Three:** the record reads *both* ways — your ten thousand losses are a *record of walkers*, not a wall of shame, and from today it says so."
**Maze (a long beat; and the old keeper looks at the woman the size of his forearm and gives her the realm's whole heart in one sentence):** "…Aye. That's *readable*. Cut it, Horn."
**Art note (the page's last frame):** **Horn**, in the doorway, already cutting — but not into the horn yet: he is cutting into the *gatepost*, in the open air, where the queue outside can see it: **Viv's three terms, in the realm's own script.** The realm's law got its first *procedural amendment in a hundred years* fifteen minutes after a stranger asked for it, and the queue applauds — the art team: **the queue applauds**, lightly, because it means next time *their* loss will be read out too.

## Page 4 — *The Walk*

### P4.1 — Ten thousand clauses, and one mouth (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's set piece, drawn as **the longest page in the volume so far** — a single sustained sequence down corridors, panel after panel, each one a *wall* filling the frame with cut script and a woman walking through it reading out loud. The art team's key device, and it must be beautiful rather than frightening: **as the walk goes deeper, the script gets older, coarser, and more beautiful** — the walls near the gate are the tidy work of the realm's clerks-that-never-existed; the walls at the bottom are *scratched*, huge, cut by hands in a hurry with a war on, and *still legible*, and the older law is *plainer* — the realm's oldest clauses are one line long, because the people who cut them had no time for elaboration. **Morgatha reads**, and her reading is drawn *lettered into the art*, not in balloons — the lettering team's instruction: **her voice is the only text on the page that is not part of a wall.** And the count in the frame's corner goes: **five… six… seven… eight**, and the reader should notice it is *not* her breathing count anymore — Ulgan's drum, outside, is inaudible here, and what the corner is counting is now **the number of walls she has read past without answering.** She passes: the third holding; the ford-law; the guest-right; the blood-price; the debt-come-home; and, at the turn where the code disagrees with itself, she *chooses*, out loud, twice, and the art team shows the reader which corridors closed behind her.
**Morgatha (reading, flat, to a wall, the whole of her realm's law in a woman's voice, and this is the volume's love letter to the culture it is about to hurt):** "…*The guest may not be turned from the fire; a debt may not be read at the fire.* …*The ford belongs to the clan that keeps it; a ford kept against the hungry is a debt.* …*The blood-price is read at the stone; a reading may not be interrupted, and a stone may not be moved.*"
**Art note (three frames, as she walks, wordless, and they are the chapter's spine):** **(a)** her right forearm, in the lamplight: the oath-script, the old clauses, the *unfinished* line — a woman walking through a building that is the same document as her own arm; **(b)** her mouth, moving, and this is the *only* living text in the maze; **(c)** a wall that has been *read smooth* by ten thousand hands, with one hand's worth of shine at shoulder height, and **the cut marks underneath it still legible** — the realm wearing its law down by using it.

### P4.2 — The centre (half page)
**Art:** The centre chamber, and **Tauron**, and the fight within *five frames* — the art team's rule for this duel, absolute: **it is not a spectacle and it is not a beating.** No blood, no gore, no screamed dialogue, no impact SFX. Four frames of the fight drawn **tight and quiet** — a huge minotaur's shoulder into a corridor wall; a hand on a wrist; a horn against a blade-that-is-not-used; two fighters' breath fogging in a cold room — and the reader must feel the thing the fight is actually about: **she is carrying words and he knows it.** And the beat that decides everything, one frame, big: **Tauron removes the walls' advantage and gives her a clean opening** — the art team must draw him *refusing* a winning line (he steps out of the angle on purpose) — and she takes it, exactly as he knew she would, and her mouth stops moving for one beat to do it, and **the words fall out of the room.** He stops. He does not press. He stands up straight, in the middle of a fight he is winning, and says the clause back to her — *whole* — because he spent the last four corridors *listening*.
**Tauron (huge, flat, breathing hard, speaking the clause perfectly, and it is the first time the words have been said by anybody but her):** "— *The oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code.*" *(a beat; and then, entirely without triumph)* "…You dropped it. **On purpose.** You wanted the last exchange." *(he wipes his mouth)* "I'd have done it too. That's the whole reason I've got this job."

## Page 5 — *The Yield*

### P5.1 — The champion loses (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's turn, and the art team's direction: **stage it as a *procedure*, not a scene.** Tauron walks to the bare wall — the centre's one uncut wall — and **puts his hand flat on it**, which is the realm's gesture for *yielding*, and gives it: five frames of a champion **declaring his own loss** to a woman who has just lost an argument, in front of the realm's two offices (Maze, Durgan), with the logic spelled out in the realm's own flat grammar and *in his own words*: she came in carrying a word; the word was the thing being tested; the word is in his mouth now, not hers — **and a walker who loses the word loses the walk.** Therefore the loss is hers to declare or his to declare, and *he* declares it, because he is the one who took it, and the code says the holder of a thing answers for it. So: **the champion loses, the captain lost the clause, and both are true, and that ambiguity is not a loophole — it is the chapter's cost.** In the corner, the count: **stopped** — one frame, no dialogue, full width, and the art team's standing rule applies (Ch. 4 P8.1, restated for Vol. 5): **when the count stops, that is the panel.** **No Titanvein. No flare. Nothing but a woman standing in a bare room and a count that has gone quiet.**
**Tauron (flat, hand on the wall, and this is 03a's line, spent verbatim, unaltered, at the *end* of the loss, delivered to the loser as a gift and never as a consolation):** "**You lost. Which means the maze is yours. Walk it. It'll teach you more than any map.**"
**Maze (flat, from the doorway, because a procedure needs its officer):** "…Losses: two. Walkers: one. **Record:** one clause, taken at the fourth turn, held by the champion, returned at the centre." *(a beat)* "Cut it as she says it, Horn. That's her term."
**Horn (from the doorway, graver already moving, flat, and this is the realm's archival voice — the same register as a Khazadûm plate and *nothing like it*):** "…Say the words, captain. I'll cut them *as* you say them, and the realm'll read them off the horn; and if you say them wrong the horn will say them wrong forever. That's the whole of my office."

### P5.2 — Two holes (half page)
**Art:** And then the chapter's *payload*, drawn as four frames of **a woman standing in a bare room listing her own failures in a stranger's archive** — and the art team must make this the most dignified page in the volume, because Morgatha's creed is *readable* and this is the creed being *spent*: **(1)** she states the first hole: *the clause has no limit on who may invoke it* — a feud can be dressed as a life; **(2)** the second: *the clause has no word for a life freely given away* — a man may spend himself and no ruling is needed; **(3)** Horn cuts, one line per statement, into the horn — the realm's first *legal* entry in its duel-record, and the art team should show **the horn's face changing in three panels**, so the reader sees the archive *grow*; **(4)** Durgan, in the doorway, watching the cutting, and — **the office's single movement of the chapter** — he *nods*, once, and leaves, which is the realm's way of saying: *this will be read in the circle.*
**Morgatha (flat, facing the bare wall, hands at her sides, and each sentence is a *hole* in the thing she is trying to pass):** "One. It's got no word for *who* can say a life's at stake. A feud can dress itself as a life and the clause will hold the door. Two. It's got no word for a life *sold* — a man who spends himself gets no ruling out of this, and my realm has *clans* who'd read that as permission." *(a beat; and then the last sentence, which is the point of the whole walk)* "…Three. It's *mine*, and I'm the one it'll be named for, and the realm's going to have to vote on whether a captain's word can carry a *word*."
**Viv (flat, from the doorway, and the lawyer gets the page's last line, and she is *pleased* in the exact register her character uses for pleasure — that of a woman reading a filed brief):** "— That's a finding. Two holes, one question of standing, **on the record, in a horn, with the realm's own carver's mark on it.** We can walk into that circle with something better than a hope: we can walk in with a *defect list*."

## Page 6 — *The Old-Forges*

### P6.1 — One eye a mile high (half page)
**Art:** The road east, and the reveal of the **old-forges**: a valley terraced with stone workshops, and above them, cut into the cliff, **the eye** — the art team's central spectacle for this realm, and the key design note is that it is *architecture, not anatomy*: a cyclops smith's forge is built *around* its owner, so the forge-hall has a wall of a face at one end, and set into it, six metres across, **one great eye**, lid shut, and the lid is *worked stone* with the seam leaded and mended by a mender's hand. **One-Eye** sits on a stool in front of his own forge with a map on his knees; **Iron-Eye** works a hammer the size of a door; and the workshop's whole floor is *map-stones* — flat slabs laid flush, each cut with a country, each with **amendments**: stones replaced, stones re-cut, one stone mended into two. And **Oros the Old Eye** — oldest of them, the sage who taught Gorakh — comes out of the map-room small against the architecture, with a lamp, and looks at the team *and says nothing at all*, and the art team must draw him looking **once at Fenn's rolled map under his arm**, and then at the door of the map-room he is about to hold open. **The whole chapter's Oros-scene is in that look** — his canon line is HELD, and the reader must feel that he *has* one, and that he is not spending it on strangers.
**Fenn (quiet — and the 312-year-old gnome gets the volume's single pure moment of joy in five words, so it must be drawn small):** "…That's a *room* of the world."
**One-Eye (flat, from his stool, entirely matter-of-fact about the greatest archive in the world):** "It's a *shelf*. Rooms are what's round it. …You'll be wanting the holdings, then. Come in. Nobody's ever been *refused* a reading in my house, and I'll not start being interesting today."

### P6.2 — The map-room that cannot say no (half page)
**Art:** The interior, and the chapter's moral engine drawn as **inventory and hospitality**: a long, low, lamplit room; **maps on rails** (pulled out like drawers, hung like hides, stacked like cloth — this is the only place in the Barrens with *pages*); the **copyists' bench** with the kobold cave-carts' equipment (Crystal with a fine chisel, Stone with a measuring-cord, Fire-Knob with the lamp-wick and, in the art team's best comic detail, a **queue of small clients** — a goblin cartel's factor, a minotaur herald, two orc young from rival clans, all of them waiting on maps of the same ford, and none of them looking at each other); and the room's rule, cut over the door in the realm's script, which Fenn reads out because he is the saga's reader of signage: ***A READING MAY NOT BE REFUSED. A COPY IS THE ASKER'S WORK.*** And a **request-shelf** by the door — hundreds of slivers of horn tied with cord, hanging like a curtain: every lawful ask ever made in this room, kept as a request slip, with the asker's name and the asker's *mark*, because the room that cannot say no keeps the names of everyone it has said yes to.
**Oros (flat, in the doorway, lamp in hand, and he speaks one sentence and it is not the canon line, and the art team should ensure it *reads* as a man being careful):** "…You'll read whatever you like. But you'll read the *margins*, too. A map's a piece of paper with a *history* on it, and half this room's history is in the seams." *(a beat; and he does not look at the request-shelf, and the reader will)*

## Page 7 — *The Margin*

### P7.1 — The family map (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's artifact, and the art team's craft page: **the family map**, brought out on its frame-rail — the oldest document in the Barrens, drawn by a cyclops family across generations (the art team: **the map is *layered*** — the paper is four generations of hide sewn into one sheet, each generation's work in a different hand, the oldest section coarse and confident, the newest meticulous), and it covers, in one document, the whole of Ulgar: the fords, the clans, the holdings, the debt-stones, the crossing-roads — **and at the bottom right, marked in the Rift's own old colours, the wound**: the Riftfall's edge, drawn as a *fault*, with the cyclopes' own annotations in the margin from a century ago. **Ash-Eye** carries it, and **Ash-Eye's beat is this page**: he sets it down flat in front of the team, and then — with no ceremony at all, in front of the realm's own keeper One-Eye and Oros and Iron-Eye — **he says he is giving it back**: the map was Gorakh's family's; his family kept it because a war happened; and a document that a realm needs in order to be **a realm** does not belong in the house of the family that *left*. Around him, the other cyclopes do not object, and the art team must draw the *reason* they do not object: **Oros's hand on the rail, and Oros saying nothing**, because the sage who taught the cell-leader is standing in front of the shelf where the crime got its copy.
**Ash-Eye (flat, both hands flat on the map, and this is the 03b beat, paid as a *transfer* and not an apology):** "— It's ours by *keeping* and it was theirs by *drawing*. …The Rift wasn't one family's doing. A hundred years we've kept it here so the blame'd have a *house* to live in. I'm not doing that any more. Captain — it goes back to the Barrens, and it goes *with* the fault on it, in the margin, in my hand."
**Morgatha (quiet, and the count in the corner, cut: six):** "…Aye. My realm'll read it."

### P7.2 — The lawful ask (half page)
**Art:** The page the whole volume has been walking toward, drawn as **three frames of a margin** — no crowd, no reaction shots, no shock; the art team's rule is that this must read like somebody finding a receipt. **(1)** The request-shelf: a sliver of horn taken down from among hundreds — and the reader recognises it, because Ch. 22 drew the same *kind* of object (a horn, a cut script, a name): the sliver is cut with the ask, the date, the name of the asker, and the asker's mark. **(2)** Close: the script, legible — *copy of the holdings, all fords, all debts, the Rift-line included* — and, under it, in a second hand (a room-keeper's, tidy, dutiful): *asked lawfully; a lawful ask may not be refused; copy made, one, for the asker's hand.* **(3)** The signature: **GORAKH**, cut in the hand of a cyclops — and beside it, small, the mark the art team has been seeding all chapter: **the same mark that is on the map's margin from a century ago**, because the man who asked for the holdings *asked for his family's own map's subject* — he isn't a stranger to this room's history; he is its heir, using his inheritance as a key. **END OF CHAPTER 23.**

*The Barrens settles its disputes by making people walk into a building that is their own law and come out having lost something, and it carves every loss into horn, and it gives the maze to the person who paid — which means the realm's archive of ten thousand entries is an archive of *lessons bought*, and nobody in it is a loser, and the queue outside on a good day is cheerful. Into that walked a captain with a clause in her mouth and came out with an empty mouth, a champion's yield, and two faults she had to say out loud in front of the realm's own carvers, because the only way this culture knows to honour a word is to write it down where it can be *argued with*. Then the road went east and the maps did the rest: the oldest document in Ulgar, kept by one-eyed smiths in a room that cannot refuse a reader, with a request-shelf by the door — and on that shelf, a sliver of horn with a lawful ask on it, a copy taken, four companions who read the holdings twice, and a signature in a hand the room's own sage taught to write. Not one law broken: a realm whose virtue is *helpfulness* got read the way a ledger gets read. The answer was not fire and it was not a raid; it was a family map given back with the century's fault written in the margin by the man who kept it, and a gnome of three hundred years learning to cut a country so that he could not be accused of having walked away with one. And at the gate at dusk, at the bottom of a wall of ten thousand losses, somebody cut a clause and two strokes under it — the realm's first *legal* record, and it is a *critique* of the thing its own captain wants passed. Volume 5's argument is now on paper, in a realm with no paper, and the count in the corner is at eight, and the bowl is full.*
**The record's cards are cut into hide, and this one has a scar across it with nothing written on the other side.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 23)

- **Two new design systems, both load-bearing** (P2, P6). **(1) The maze: a building that is a document.** Every wall is cut with the Barrens' oath-script floor-to-shoulder, in hands that change every few metres, worn smooth at hand height by ten thousand walkers, maintained by an ordinary lamplighter on a shift.
