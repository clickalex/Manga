# AURELION · Volume 4 — Chapter 16: "The Street Question"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc D: "The Mountain-Ask" (the count of the twelve days — a realm whose memory is oral, and whose archive has been dark for a century; fifty-eight doors with a family's mark on them; six old women and six different faces; and the one drawing in the mountain that disagrees with all of them)**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Grimm (**the asker**), Kaelen, Lyra, Morgatha (the count — **five, six**), Nereia (the flask, the crack, the cistern), Azrael, Viv (**the exhibit**), Bramble (**the coat**), Fenn (the map, the days), Marginalia, Sprocket. **The support** — **Roderick** (his one quiet panel: a chiselled-off forge-mark). **The stone realm** — **Maren Ashwood** (the sketchbook, the stranger's face), **Tilda Ashwood**, **Pebble** (**the lining, the hole, the rescue**), **the six** (the old women of the district — **Hildrun · Tova · Perra · Varda · Yrsa · Ragna** — each with a face in her memory and no two faces alike), **Mountain-Speaker Khazri** (**silent; one frame; he must not speak**), **Echo** (the slate, both sides now), **Pick**, **Norga**, **Skaal** (the ledger, the standing order), **Duelist Molka** (the festival's book, at the mine, silent), **Grand-Anvil Khazur**, **Stonehand Drek** (**the honest invoice**), **dusty children** (the district's, one of whom owns a knee), the **grummpling clan** (a siege, with lunch).
> **Locations / threads:** **the old forge district — "the fifty-eight"** (the forges whose lintels carry the Khazdûrin mark, cut there two and three generations back) → the Ashwood kitchen (the sketchbook, six descriptions) → Varda's keadsake box (the drawing) → the Hall of Speakers (the same hand) → the mine's office (the standing order) → the coat (the lining, the hole) → a doorway in the district (no mark) → the house at the rim (both faces, on the wall).
> **Arc beat:** The count reaches nine days and the realm's real wound shows: it has no *papers*. Khazadûm keeps everything in hair and in mouths, so the only archive of a family's faces is a district's memory — and the memory is forty years of smiths' opinions, no two alike. Grimm walks the fifty-eight, door by door, and comes back with six faces; Maren draws a seventh and admits it is a stranger's. Then an old woman opens a keepsake box and produces the only *drawn* Durin in the mountain: a laughing man on the back of a slate, signed with one letter — **K.** — in the hand that also wrote the shape on Echo's slate. The family's portrait is a solemn man; the realm remembers a man who laughed; and the difference between those two faces is a hundred years of a song that stopped keeping anything. Meanwhile the mine's books show what the mine has actually been selling, bale by bale, to a standing order out of Terrania. Viv stops arguing and starts building an **exhibit** — the six sketches, the slate, the rack, the tags — because the realm's court in nine days is not a trial to be won by a speech. It is a room that has to be *shown its own face.* And a grummpling's coat finally comes open, in a kitchen, in front of everybody: forty centimetres of hoard, a boot-heel, a good nail, and **one pocket kept empty, padded, and ready**, which is what a grummpling's faith looks like when you can finally see it.
> **Motifs advanced:** the count (**days in the frame's edge, nine to eight**; **Morgatha's, five and six**), the account (the realm has no papers — so a *face* has to be asked for, door by door), the archive (**the sketchbook; the keadsake slate; the empty tenth frame**), the empty (**one pocket, kept open**), the theft of a witness (**now with a customer — severed oaths sold by the bale to a standing order**), the door (fifty-eight lintels, and one with the mark *chiselled off*), the mirror (Drek's theology, at its most reasonable and most terrible), the ink (Maren's), the flask (the crack, further), the record (**the card: *Here ask the named*** — the braid mark grows a fifth strand), the ink and the stone (**Viv's exhibit — the saga's formula, loaded for the festival**). **Not in this chapter:** the stone-song; the Ask; Bastion's sentence; Khazri's voice; Molka's, Torvik's, Grudka's voices; the geode; the golems; what is under the Cut.

## Page 1 — *The Fifty-Eight (day nine)*

### P1.1 — A street that used to have a name (2/3 page)
**Art:** The **old forge district**, morning — and the chapter's whole premise drawn in one panel: **lintels**. Fifty-eight doorways down one long street, and every lintel has the **nine-strand mark** chiselled into it (the art team: not signs, not lettering — *marks*, the way a mason signs a wall; some fresh enough to be a grandfather's work, some weathered to a smudge, and a few *re-cut* by somebody who cared). The forges under them are *alive* — this is not a dead street, people work here — but the mark over the doors belongs to a family that no longer owns a single one of them. In the frame's edge, the tally is cut into the stone: **NINE**. **Grimm** mid-street with a scrap of paper and a stub of chalk, counting doors; **Kaelen** behind him; **Pebble** scouting the gutters; a **dusty child** sitting on a step, watching, chewing something.
**Grimm (flat, chalk moving, the accounting voice — the whole chapter's engine, stated like a job):** "Fifty-eight. That's what my grandfather sold, gave, or lost, and the street's still got his mark over every one of them, and not one of the fifty-eight has got *him*." *(a beat)* "So I'm going to knock on fifty-eight doors and ask old people what my grandfather looked like. That's the plan."
**Kaelen (quiet, walking, dry):** "It's the best plan we've had."
**Grimm (a beat):** "Aye. That's the worrying bit."

### P1.2 — The first knock (bottom strip)
**Art:** Three frames, the *texture* of the chapter. **(1)** A door, opening; **Hildrun** (03c: Anvil's granddaughter, great-forge) with her sleeves rolled and her arms floured; behind her, a kitchen that is *clearly* mid-bake; she takes one look at the dwarf on the step and doesn't need the question. **(2)** Grimm, hat in both hands, asking it anyway. **(3)** The child on the step, now following them at a distance of five metres, entirely uninvited, entirely unbothered, eating.
**Hildrun (dry, wiping her hands, and this is the realm's actual relationship with the Khazdûrins in one line):** "Sit down. You're going to get *cake* and you're going to get an *opinion*, and I'll not be rushed on either."

## Page 2 — *Six Faces (the archive is a mouth)*

### P2.1 — The six (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's money page — **six panels across two rows**, each one a woman, a room, and a *face* the art team must draw as *different from every other one* (the point is visual: the reader should be able to hold six sketches side by side and see six strangers). Under each panel, in Maren's hand (the sketchbook is in the frame, open, being filled by somebody else's memory): a **one-line caption** and a *sketch* being started. **(1) Hildrun** — her kitchen, cake, a **quiet** man, "he had the quiet of a man who'd already said his piece" → sketch: a still face, closed mouth, level eyes. **(2) Tova** (03c: Ash's sister, flame-forge) — a forge, a dog, half-shouting over it — "broad as a *lintel* and he laughed like a hammer on cold iron" → sketch: huge, laughing, head back. **(3) Perra** (03c: Clang's daughter, great-forge) — a bell-loft, tuning forks — "small hands and *big ears*; always had his head turned like the room was saying something to him" → sketch: a man listening, head cocked. **(4) Varda** (03c: Mountain-Song's daughter) — a music room, a **tuning-slate** — "he *sang* under his breath. Off-key. Everybody let him" → sketch: mid-note. **(5) Yrsa** (03c: Anvil-Old's granddaughter) — a shop counter, an abacus — "never once in a hurry, which for a smith is the worst thing I can say about a man" → sketch: slow, deliberate hands. **(6) Ragna** (03c: Echo's sister) — a schoolroom, forty years younger than the rest — "…I was *six*. I remember a *beard* and a *knee*." → the sixth sketch: **a beard and a knee**, in Maren's honest line, and it is both the funniest and the most devastating panel on the page.
**Tova (shouting over the forge, delighted, the reader's own thought):** "Faces? Love, this street's been *arguing* about that man's face for forty years. There's men down the row'll tell you he was a giant."
**Grimm (small, flat, chalk hand still, and this is the moment he understands the actual problem):** "…And every one of you is *certain*."
**Perra (kindly, finishing his thought, which is what old women do):** "Aye. That's what a memory is, lad. Certain. That's the whole of it."

### P2.2 — The seventh face (bottom third)
**Art:** The Ashwood kitchen, evening: the sketchbook open on the table, **six attempts** and one clean attempt — and **it is not one of the six** (the art team: Maren has drawn *a composition*, an official face — solemn, jaw set, the war-dead's face a realm likes to have). Grimm's hands flat on the table either side of it. The **dusty child** is at the window, outside, still there, now eating somebody else's bread; the reader should love her.
**Maren (flat, a draughtswoman reporting on her own work):** "I've drawn six memories and a *funeral*. This one's the funeral. It's the only face a family keeps, love — you all stand around one day and *agree*, and after that the agreement is the man." *(a beat; she turns the page)* "It's not a lie. It's worse. It's a *decision*."
**Grimm (quiet):** "Then how does a realm *know* anything."
**Maren (and this is the answer that sends him to the last door on the street):** "It doesn't keep faces, boy. It keeps **mouths**. Go and find somebody old enough to have *heard* him."

## Page 3 — *The Box (the drawing that disagrees)*

### P3.1 — The last door (half page)
**Art:** The end of the street, dusk, a small house with a *re-cut* mark over the door — the cleanest nine-strand on the whole street (the art team: somebody has kept this one sharp for a hundred years). **Varda**, old, alone, a **music room** where the tuning-slate still hangs; a keadsake box on the shelf — dwarf work, iron, small, with no ornament but *wear* (her hands have gone to it ten thousand times). The dusty child has finally been allowed inside and is sitting under the table, which the reader will notice is her *processing* position. **Pick** is in the doorway too (he brought them; he is a miner, he is *proud*, he is the reason this scene exists).
**Varda (flat, to Grimm, no preamble — the oldest woman on the street is done with preambles):** "You're the last one, so you'll get the *box*. Everybody else gave you their *opinion*." *(she puts it on the table; she does not open it)* "My da sang at your grandfather's forge the winter before the war. Da came home with a slate and a *grudge*, and the grudge was your family's and it's none of yours." *(a beat)* "Open it."

### P3.2 — The laughing man (half page)
**Art:** **The drawing** — and this is the chapter's key image: not paper; **a slate**, the same size and shape as the one in Echo's hands (the art team: deliberately, exactly the same), and on its *back*, in charcoal, **a dwarf laughing** — head back, mouth open, beard in braids, one hand on an anvil for balance; alive, and *nothing* like the solemn composition on Maren's table. Signed in the corner with one letter: **K.** The reaction panel is the team's: Kaelen leaning in; Lyra's hand at her mouth; **Echo** — who came in behind them and said nothing — going *very* still (the art team: he is holding his own slate at his side, and the reader is invited, without a word, to line up two objects). **Grimm** does not touch it. He looks at it the way you look at a photograph of a person you only ever met as a story.
**Varda (flat, quiet, and the reveal is delivered as gossip, which is how the realm delivers everything):** "A Speaker drew it. Speakers draw the families at the Naming — that's the old job, before the listening took all their time. Your family had a Speaker in it once, boy. One. He'd have been young."
**Grimm (barely):** "…He's *laughing*."
**Varda (and she *laughs*, once, delighted — the cruel kindness of the old):** "They say he laughed like a *hammer*, lad. You've been carrying the funeral face for a hundred years. Nobody in this mountain ever thought your grandfather was *sad*."
**Art note (final frame of the page):** Maren's sketchbook, still open on the table at home, six futures and a funeral — and nobody draws the connection out loud.

## Page 4 — *Two Slates (the same hand)*

### P4.1 — The Hall of Speakers, one more time (half page)
**Art:** The Hall, night, two slates on the bedrock wall's ledge side by side (the art team: this is the chapter's page-4 turn and it must be built as a *lineup*, not a revelation): the **keepsake slate** (a laughing man, signed **K.**) and **Echo's slate** (one repeated stroke, drawn over and over). Same dimensions, same slate, same *hand* — the reader can see it; nobody says it. **Khazri**, in the doorway, **silent** (one frame; he may be drawn but he must not speak this chapter); his eyes are on the two slates. **Echo** stands between them, and the boy is *working something out* in real time, and the art team must let him fail to finish.
**Echo (quiet, to nobody, doing the arithmetic out loud because that is the only way he knows how to do it):** "He drew the families at the Naming. Then he stopped drawing. Then he stopped talking." *(a beat)* "…I've been copying a shape off a slate that had a *face* on the other side of it my *whole life*."
**Pick (from the doorway, flat, the miner's read, and it lands like a dropped tool):** "So what'd the mountain *say*, then? He was out there talking to it, right? Same year the families came in with their plates and their laughing men. So what did it say to *him*."
**Art note:** nobody answers. **Khazri turns and walks out**, and the panel holds the empty doorway — one frame, no SFX, no balloon; the chapter's biggest silence and its cheapest, and the arc will pay for it three chapters from now.

### P4.2 — Viv's addition (half page)
**Art:** The camp, Viv at the board with **Sprocket**, and the board has *changed shape*: not four columns but a **wall with pinned exhibits** (the art team: it is becoming a courtroom display; the reader should feel Viv building a room out of paper and chalk — six sketches, a funeral face, a slate, the festival's book, a copy of the contract plate, the braid-rack's own standard). **Marginalia** is on the cart, drawing the board, because the cart has decided this is the chapter's artifact.
**Viv (the em-dash, fast, flat, a lawyer enjoying herself in the way lawyers enjoy themselves):** "— Update. We are not going to argue with a mine in its own hole in front of a realm that *has no pens*. There's no verdict to win by *speech*. So we don't speak." *(she taps the sketches)* "We **show**. A hundred years of this realm's own memories, out of order, contradicting each other, all *true* — six faces, a funeral, a laughing man signed by a Speaker, and a hole in the middle where the *archive* should be. Nine thousand dwarves are going to sit in that gallery in eight days and look at their own realm's face and *notice what's missing*."
**Kaelen (quiet, the smith's son, reading the board the way he reads a blueprint):** "…Missing what?"
**Viv (and this is the chapter's thesis, and she does not soften it):** "— A name that *answers*. That's what a courtroom is for, Mosaic. We're not trying the mine. We're trying the **silence**."

## Page 5 — *What the Mine Sells (the standing order)*

### P5.1 — The office (2/3 page)
**Art:** The mine's office — and the art team's key direction: there is *no* gothic villainy in it — it is a **shipping office**, tidy, efficient, with a **weighing-scale, bales of braids in packing twine, and a stamp**; the horror is in the *logistics* (the bales are stacked like hay, tagged by shaft and by *year*, and the tags are beautiful). **Skaal** at the bench with the ledger open, cheerful; **Norga** checking a knot; a **clerk** at a standing desk; and on the wall, a **map of routes**, with one line going *west*, off the mountain, marked with a small brass pin: **TERRANIA**. In the corner, **Duelist Molka** with the festival's book, **silent**, watching the bales (she came for the audit — the art team: she is *already* doing her job; she has one frame and no dialogue).
**Skaal (cheerful, flat, showing them the books because they are *legal* and that is the point):** "Forfeit's a *product*, lady. Nobody comes for the ore alone. Ore's a *maybe* — a vein's a hope. Forfeit's *certain*: every braid in that bale was somebody's word, we've got the tag to prove it, and the buyer pays per *promise*. Best customer we've got. Standing order, three years, and they've never once asked for a *discount*."
**Viv (very quiet, reading over his shoulder — and this is the flattest, worst line she has ever said):** "…Who buys a hundredweight of somebody's *broken word*."
**Skaal (turning the ledger, pointing at the line, entirely helpful):** "Counting-house. Terrania." *(he reads it out, the way a man reads a label)* "**The Desk.** Standing order. Signed: **I. Vane.**"

### P5.2 — Three frames at the door (bottom third)
**Art:** **(1)** Viv stepping outside and standing in the cold with the ledger's copy in her hand, not moving (the art team: give her one panel of pure *face* — a lawyer who has just realized her realm has been exporting the idea that a promise is a *commodity*). **(2)** **Molka**, leaving with her book, and *glancing* at Grimm's beard on the way out — one frame, professional, unexplained (the duelist, doing arithmetic of her own). **(3)** The bales: one frame, tight, in the lamplight — and the reader can see, tied to the topmost bale, a **child's braid** (small, bright, ribboned; the tag says *apprentice*, *forfeit*; nobody in the panel looks at it; the art team is not to make it cute — make it *ordinary*).
**Sprocket (flat, from the door, doing the franchise's worst arithmetic):** "…They've got a *customer list*."
**Nereia (quiet, from the dark at the edge of the frame, flask in hand — the markings low tide, and this is the whole of her chapter):** "So does the sea. We call them *songs*." *(a beat)* "It's the same list. Somebody's always selling."

## Page 6 — *The Coat (the rescue)*

### P6.1 — The siege of the Ashwood kitchen (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's comedy set-piece, played completely straight: the **grummpling clan** (Cinder at the back with her arms folded, delighted) has laid **siege** to Maren's kitchen door, because **Tilda tried to wash Pebble's coat**. The coat is hanging on the line, dripping; Pebble is on the *roof of the outdoor privy* with a broom; Maren is at the door with a ladle; the dusty child is in the middle of the lawn, delighted, holding a *flag* made of somebody's tea-towel; Grimm is arriving at a run with Kaelen behind him.
**Cinder (flat, to Maren, as one clan-mother to another, and this is a *negotiation*):** "My boy's coat's on your line, lady. That's the *whole* dispute. There's no *second* complaint."
**Maren (ladle up, unimpressed, the mortal who has survived a hundred years of this family):** "It's *soaking*, it's *stinking*, and I have *smelled* things come out of that mountain that had more manners."
**Pebble (from the privy roof, tiny, hoarse, arms out, and the chapter's most sincere line):** "**IT'S *MINE*.**"
**Bramble (arriving late, taking in the whole scene, and dropping flat into the deadpan that makes her the team's barometer):** "…Nobody touches a grummpling's coat. That's not a joke, that's *law*. I've been carrying three of their *buttons* for a year because they *gave* me them."

### P6.2 — The lining (half page)
**Art:** The kitchen table, night, everyone crowded in (the art team's warm panel — Volume 4's family scene: Maren, Tilda, the child, Grimm, Kaelen, Bramble, Cinder, six grummplings standing on chairs in a ring of solemn attention, and **the coat laid out flat like a patient**). Maren, needle in hand, **undoes the hem** — with permission, ceremonially given (Cinder's nod, on the page, one panel, *that* is the document). And then: **the lining, opened.** Nib's "geode" (a potato, petrified). Chuckle's "geode" (a boot-heel). Nibble's "geode" (a lump of quartz). Boulder-Nib's (a *different* boot-heel). A good nail. A dented spoon. A tooth. A small brass pin. A child's chalk nub. A curl of **hair from somebody's beard** (no comment). Forty centimetres of *hoard*, spread across the table, and every grummpling on the chairs nodding as each object comes out, because every object is *correct*.
And then the **last pocket** — the one sewn shut by hand, badly, by a grummpling — opened last: **empty**, and *padded* — lined with rags against a shape that is not in it, kept soft, kept *ready*. The panel holds on the empty pocket, and the reader gets it before anyone says anything.
**Bramble (quiet — and this is her chapter, and the line is hers because she is the only one who would say it plainly):** "…He's been carrying a *hole* for it. For a year. All that and a hole's the thing he keeps *nice*."
**Pebble (tiny, on the table, hands out, entirely without shame — the faith, stated as a fact):** "Somewhere's a geode. …It's *mine*. I keep the room."
**Cinder (and *this* is the canon 03a beat, paid without a word of sentimentality):** "Pebble's got one idea. It's the best idea in the mountain. I've seen *kings* with worse." *(a beat; to Maren, matter-of-fact)* "Now sew it back."
**Maren (sewing, flat, to the room, and there is not one trace of irony in her voice):** "…I'm putting a *towel* in it. It's a long walk to the festival, and I'll not have him carrying a *hole* with nothing in it."

## Page 7 — *The Door Without a Mark (Roderick's panel)*

### P7.1 — One panel, and the one-way street (half page)
**Art:** The district, night, rain starting. The art team's key frame of the chapter's back half: **one doorway** in the middle of the street where every other lintel carries the nine-strand mark — and this one has been **chiselled off** (the mark's ghost still legible in the stone: a shallow, deliberately-erased scar the size of a hand). Behind the door, a family clearly *lives there* — a lamp, a baby's line, washing. Somebody took a chisel to a family's name above a door, on purpose, and the reader is not told why and will not be. And **Roderick**, standing in the rain with his hand flat on that erased mark (the art team: Ch. 15's cart-edge and Ch. 14's rail, the third of his thresholds — this time the threshold is a *name that isn't there*; **exactly one panel, no dialogue**, and the rain must be drawn *thin*, just enough to run down the stone).
**SFX:** *(rain on stone. Nothing else.)*

### P7.2 — The exhibit grows (half page)
**Art:** The camp under the cart's awning, rain; **Fenn** and **Marginalia** and the **dusty child**, who has followed them all the way home and is now *asleep against the cart's wheel* with the whole team politely ignoring her (the art team: she will matter, and the chapter must not say how; no name for her in this chapter — she is a *child of the district* and stays one). **Grimm** is at the board, and he is doing the thing he does: he has **written the six old women's names on six slips of paper** and pinned them each beside its sketch — a list, an account, *his* ledger, made in his own hand, in chalk, on Viv's wall. **Lyra** watches him do it.
**Lyra (quiet, the elf, and she is the one who gets to name the thing because she is the one who has been keeping an identical account since Ch. 2):** "Six witnesses. You've started an *archive*, dwarf."
**Grimm (flat, not looking up, chalk moving, and this is the chapter's small private victory):** "Aye. It's *mine*, and it's got *names* in it, and every one of them's alive and can be *asked again*." *(he puts the chalk down)* "That's more than my family's had for a hundred years."
**Viv (the em-dash, from her cot, eyes closed, flat as ever, and she is *pleased*, which she will deny):** "— Exhibit F. Six living witnesses and one laughing dead man. …Keep going, Khazdûrin. Court's in eight days."

## Page 8 — *Both of Them (the wall)*

### P8.1 — Maren's wall, after (half page)
**Art:** The front room, morning. **Maren** with the **keepsake slate** in her hands — Varda gave it. She does not put it in a drawer and she does not frame it: she props it on the **empty nails** at the wall's end, *beside* the sketches, and then — the panel the volume has been walking toward since Ch. 14 — she takes **Durin's portrait** (the solemn funeral face, still face-down on the bench since Ch. 15) and **stands it up next to the slate**, so the wall now holds **both**: the man the family agreed on and the man the realm remembers, side by side, front-facing, in daylight. **Grimm** in the doorway.
**Maren (flat, hands on hips, surveying her own wall like a smith surveys a joint — this is her verdict, and it is the kindest thing she has ever said):** "There. Now it's a *wall* and not a *verdict*." *(a beat)* "I've been drawing a stranger for thirty years, love. I'd rather hang two men than keep one of them lying *face-down* like a *debt*."
**Grimm (quiet):** "…The tenth frame."
**Maren (and the answer is the same as last time, and it is no longer an evasion — it is a *standard*):** "Still empty. …When there's a face for it, it goes *up*. Not before."
**Art note (last frame):** the dusty child at the window, outside, watching the two faces go up on the wall, chewing bread that is not hers. Nobody draws attention to it. **Do not name her.**

### P8.2 — Nereia and the cistern (half page)
**Art:** The Cut's water-lamps, and the cistern, and the chapter's most quietly alarming panel: **Nereia** has put her hand on the **old depth-marks** — the whole column of them, cut by hands dead a hundred years, *every one above the waterline* — and beside her the **flask** on the stone, and the crack has *grown* (the art team: it is now two-thirds of the way across the glass; nobody has mentioned it; this is the fourth chapter it has been visible). She is measuring the mountain's water against the marks with a diviner's string. **Morgatha** arrives with the count and stays for one panel without speaking, and then the count — said flat, in her corner, **five… six** — and the reader watches Nereia hear a *number* asked out loud for the first time in four chapters.
**Nereia (flat, the diviner, readings, no drama — and this is the line that makes the clock real):** "The mountain lost a hand's depth of water last month. Marks say it goes *down* a finger a year, steady, forever." *(a beat)* "Last month it lost a *hand*."
**Morgatha (flat, the hunter, corner of the frame, arms folded):** "…Six."
**Nereia (and she *looks* at her, and the reader feels two clocks acknowledge each other):** "Days?"
**Morgatha (flat): "No."** *(and she does not explain, and she walks on, and the panel holds Nereia looking at the count's back — the volume's second untranslated number, and this one is the reader's, now, to worry about)*

## Page 9 — *The Honest Invoice (Drek, at his most reasonable)*

### P9.1 — The mine's office, night (half page)
**Art:** **Drek** alone at the shipping office's window, the bales behind him, the standing desk, the ledger open on the weighing-scale — and the invoice in his hand: the copy Viv read, with the brass-stamped address and the initials **I. Vane**. He has come to *sign off* on it, which he does, in a workman's hand, without ceremony (the art team: this is his chapter-15 deal, kept; he is not hiding anything, and that is the design). **Skaal** waits with the stamp. And then Drek says it — the arc's thesis, in the villain's own reasonable voice, and the reader should find themselves following it *all the way to the last word.*
**Drek (flat, warm, west-country — the theology, completed):** "You want to know what a mine is, Skaal? Not a hole. Holes are *geology*. A mine's a *book*: every shift's a page, every contract's a line, and what comes out of it at the end is *certainty*." *(he signs)* "Stone's the *by-product*. A dwarf pays coin for certainty that a promise will hold. That's the *trade*. And when a promise doesn't hold — well. Somebody's always buying, and the buyer's always *right* to want it, because a broken word is *evidence* that the next one will break too." *(he sets the pen down)* "Your grandfather's generation sold *steel*. Mine sells *terms*. The mountain's not crying about it, boy — the mountain's *asleep*."
**Skaal (cheerful, stamping):** "Standing order's the best thing about this office, boss."
**Drek (flat, looking out at the dark galleries, and this is the last line of his scene and it is *sincere*):** "It's the best thing about it, and it's the *worst* thing about it, and I've not slept properly in nine years, and I do it anyway. …That's the whole of my theology. There's no *god* in it. There's just *books*."

## Page 10 — *What the Realm Owes (Grimm's own audit)*

### P10.1 — The cold room, one more time (half page)
**Art:** The Khazdûrin forge, night — the *same room* as Ch. 14 P10 and Ch. 15's silence, and the art team must draw the change honestly: the **frost is smaller this time** (a hand's worth, at the wall, not the whole face), the **anvil is uncovered and clean** (he has stopped covering it), and on the wall above the anvil, pinned to the family's nine-strand mark, are **six slips of paper with six names in chalk** — the archive, brought home. **Askstone is still wrapped**, in the corner (the rule holds). **Pebble** is asleep in the coal-pot's warm ring, coat repaired and *visibly stuffed*, the pin dangling. Grimm has a **pencil** — his grandfather's? no: it is a *pencil*, a plain one, and he is *drawing*, badly, in Maren's spare sketchbook, with the slate propped in front of him for reference.
**Grimm (flat, alone, talking to a six-note tune of his own — and this is the beat the arc needed: the dwarf using the *street question* on his own wall):** "Six faces and a laugh. Fifty-eight doors. A hundred years." *(the pencil keeps moving badly)* "…You told me to ask, Ma. Nobody said what to do when the *answer's* a room full of strangers."
**SFX:** *(coal, ticking in the pot. It is the warmest sound in Volume 4 and the art team must keep it small.)*
**Art note (last frame):** the beginning of his drawing — a jaw that is *too wide*, copied from the slate, badly, on purpose, and it is the first time in the chapter that anybody *makes* a face instead of remembering one.

### P10.2 — Morgatha, alone with the door (half page)
**Art:** The camp, before dawn; the cart; and **Morgatha** sitting on the cart's step with the count in her corner of the panel and her oath-script forearms bare in the cold — and this is the chapter's only look *into* her, delivered as *accounting*: she is writing, and what she is writing is **not numbers** (the art team: do not show the page; show her hand, and the hand is *slow*, and the reader has never once seen her write). **Kaelen** comes out for water, sees her, does not sit down (the saga's rule: never crowd her) and asks *one* thing.
**Kaelen (quiet, at the barrel):** "What do they count? …You've never said."
**Morgatha (flat, not looking up, and this is canon-grade silence being paid *one notch* — no more):** "…The things I meant to say to Tarkhan."
**Kaelen (quiet, after a beat, and he does not push, and the reader *aches*):** "How many?"
**Morgatha (flat, and she *does* look up, and the number is the chapter's last line on the page and the reason the card will read the way it reads):** "Two hundred and eleven. …I'm at six."

## Page 11 — *Day Eight (what the realm is doing)*

### P11.1 — Four frames, the realm at work (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's *state of the volume* page — day **EIGHT**, cut into the frame's edge, and four frames showing what a realm does when word gets around that a Khazdûrin is *asking old women what his grandfather looked like*: **(1)** the district: three old smiths at a doorway, *arguing* about a beard, and a crowd; the chapter's premise now *spreading* — the street is doing the asking itself. **(2)** The Hall of Speakers: **Echo**, teaching — three children on the floor with slates, copying his shape, and the art team must draw this without comment (the apprentice, doing the only thing he knows how to do: making more people who keep the shape). **(3)** The forge terraces: **Dorin** (the cousin, from Ch. 14) with his sleeve rolled up at the *cold* forge's door — not entering, just standing there with a hammer, which is the most any cousin can do. **(4)** The mine's gallery, deep: **Torvik**, silent, at the rail where the hairline ran, with his **son Urka** below him on the gantry — the reader can see both hands, and one of them is shaking.

**Marginalia (tiny italics, over the same page, and the cart is close to breaking its own rule again):** *"The cart notes that a town three realms away spent a year laying plates so that a road would exist, and that this realm has spent a week *talking*, and that both of them are the same size of act."*

### P11.2 — The count (bottom strip)
**Art:** Three frames, three clocks: **(1)** Nereia, flask in hand, and this time the reader *sees* her do it: **she pours a finger of water out onto the cistern stone** — the flask's level drops, the mountain's stone *darkens and does not steam* (the art team: **this is the chapter's only supernatural beat and it is not a miracle — it is the mountain drinking a finger of water like a mouth**; no glow, no color, no sound; and Nereia's markings go to *attention*, `#d8f3ef`, for one frame, the first time since Ch. 11). **(2)** Grimm's beard, tight: **thirteen**, and beside it, in chalk on the wall, six names. **(3)** Morgatha's corner: **six**, and the panel includes, small, at the frame's edge, the *paper she is writing on*, folded, and one line visible in her hand — the reader may read the first thirty seconds of Volume 5 there and nothing more: *"— I said: 'you'll be back before the thaw.'"*

Then you're *alive*, or you're *thirsty*, and a mountain doesn't get *thirsty*." *(a beat)* "You're not dead. You're not *sick*. You're **empty** — and something's *pouring* into you."

## Page 12 — *The Cart's Board (the send-off; the card)*

### P12.1 — The exhibit wall, at dawn (2/3 page)
**Art:** Viv's board, completed — and the art team's key image of the chapter: **the exhibit**, hung on the cart's side like a show, under the awning, in the frost, at dawn: **six sketches** with six names in Grimm's chalk; **the funeral face**; **the laughing man on the slate**, propped at the center; **the invoice** with *I. ASK THE CONTRACT."** Around it: the team, in ones and twos; the dusty child asleep under the cart with Sprocket's coat and *nobody sending her away*; Pebble on the board's ledge with his coat done up, patched, and slightly too full; and in the corner, **Fenn** with the map, and the map's **forced line, seven pins** — one of them, the twins' road, marked over with a *new* pencil line the reader has not seen before.
**Fenn (flat — the cascade, the saga's recurring close, wearing its shortest coat yet):** "…The face holds at the *street*. The street holds at the *mouth*. The mouth holds at the *memory*. The memory holds at the *slate*. The slate holds at the *hand*. The hand holds at the *chair*. The chair holds at the *hall*. The hall holds at the *silence*. The silence holds at the *ledger*. **Morgatha (flat, from the step, and the number in the corner of the panel is *seven*, and the readers who have been keeping the count will feel it):** "…Seven."

**In the corner where the count lives:** *eight.*

**END OF CHAPTER 16.**

*The street question took a dwarf down fifty-eight doors and left him with six faces, one of them a knee, and a dead man laughing on the back of a slate. It taught this realm what every realm learns eventually and this one had never had to: that a memory is a mouth, and mouths wear out, and a people with no pens will spend a century agreeing about a man they did not see. It went to a shipping office and found the mine's actual product — not ore, not labor: *certainty*, by the bale, to a standing order out of Terrania, which is what the Reforgers buy when they have learned that a realm can be paid for its own unmaking and still call the payment *trade*. It came home to a kitchen and opened a grummpling's lining, and there, under forty centimetres of treasure, was an empty pocket, padded, sewn shut, kept *ready* for a thing that does not exist yet — which is the closest this saga has ever come to showing the reader a *faith*. Eight days. The anvils are down, the mine's floor is swept, the racks are going dark, and a dwarf's own chalk is on the wall: **ask the mountain — ask the street — ask the contract.***
**The record's cards are being cut now, and the count is climbing toward something the reader can finally name.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 16)

- **The tally continues** (P1.1, P11.1): the day count is cut into the frame's edge as in Ch. 15 — **nine** on Page 1, **eight** on Page 11's header frame. Once only, the count may be cut into something that is *not* a frame: this chapter that is the **street's own doorsill** (P1.1, bottom edge — the reader should find it twice and be unsure the first time). **No character mentions it, ever.**
- **Six faces, drawn as six strangers** (P2.1): the chapter lives or dies here. The six panels must be *composed as a lineup the reader can scan*: kitchen / flame-forge / bell-loft / music room / shop counter / schoolroom, and in each one a distinct *face* that shares nothing with the others — quiet, huge-and-laughing, listening-with-head-cocked, mid-song, deliberate, and (Ragna's) **a beard and a knee**. Under each sketch, a caption in **Maren's hand** (the sketchbook is passed around the page like a document, not like a scrapbook). Ragna's panel is the page's heart: play the joke straight, and give the reader one beat to feel it *stop* being a joke.
- **The seventh face is the funeral** (P2.2): Maren's "official" composition must look like the sort of portrait a family commissions from grief — and Maren must *name* it as such ("it's a *decision*"). Do not make it ugly; make it *plausible*. The whole chapter is about how a plausible face eats a true one.
- **The slate** (P3.2, P4.1): the keepsake drawing must be *unmistakably* a different man from Maren's — laughing, head back, one hand on an anvil — signed **K.**, and it must be the **same slate, same size, same stock** as Echo's (the chapter's one visual rhyme; the lettering team should let the two slates share a page and say nothing). **Khazri does not speak in this chapter.** His one frame (P4.1) is a doorway and an exit, and the panel holds the empty doorway afterwards.
- **The bales** (P5.1): the mine's office is a *shipping office* — scale, stamps, twine, a route-map with one brass pin west. The horror is inventory: bales of forfeited contract braids tagged by shaft and year, and **one child's braid with a tag reading *apprentice — forfeit*** (P5.2, third frame). Do not linger; do not make it *cute*; make it *ordinary*. **Skaal is helpful, cheerful, and honest** — he reads the buyer's name off the page because it is written down and this realm has nothing to hide except everything.
- **The standing order** (P5.1, P9.2): purchaser **"The Desk," a counting-house in Terrania, signed I. Vane.** One line, no emphasis, no character reacts beyond Viv's single flat question. **This is a Vol. 9 plant** (canon 03: Irongate Vane, Roderick's brother, the human realm's cell) — do not decode it, do not underline it, and never let anybody in Volume 4 read the initials as a *name*.
- **The coat** (P6.1–P6.2): the siege is *comedy played straight* — a clan, a broom, a privy roof, a tea-towel flag, Cinder negotiating like a diplomat. The lining is the *tenderness*: forty centimetres of hoard every grummpling on the chairs will nod at, and then **the last pocket, sewn shut by hand, opened last, empty and padded.** Nobody explains it. **Bramble gets "he's been carrying a hole for it,"** and **Maren gets the last word** ("I'm putting a *towel* in it") — and neither line may be topped by anyone else on the page. The repaired coat stays *visibly* stuffed for the rest of Volume 4.
- **Roderick — one quiet panel** (P7.1): the district's one **chiselled-off** forge mark, in the rain, hand flat on the erased stone, **no dialogue**, exactly one panel. No explanation of who chiselled it, in this chapter or ever.
- **Drek's invoice scene** (P9.1) must be the volume's most *reasonable* page: he signs without ceremony, and his theology is delivered as **supply and demand** — "a mine's a *book*… we sell *certainty*" — and it must be possible for the reader to follow it all the way to the end. Nobody in the chapter refutes him. Do not add a scare chord; there is no scare chord in this volume.
- **Morgatha's number pay** (P10.2, P11.2, P12.1): this chapter the count receives its first *meaning* — Kaelen asks, once, and she answers with the volume's cheapest sentence and the reader's most expensive: **two hundred and eleven things she meant to say to Tarkhan; she is at six.** From here the count is *read by the reader*, never re-explained. The folded page in P11.2 shows exactly one line of her hand and no more.
- **Nereia's two beats** (P8.2, P11.2): the flask's crack is now **two-thirds across the glass** (fourth chapter of visibility); the cistern's depth-marks *all* above the waterline; and the chapter's one supernatural beat — a finger of water poured on stone that **darkens and does not steam** — drawn *without* glow or color, so it reads as a mouth, not a miracle. Her closing line names the volume's actual villain correctly and nobody notices: **"You're not dead. You're not sick. You're *empty* — and something's *pouring* into you."** (The Empty's grammar — canon 04's Hollow — is here for the first time inside a realm; do not explain it.)
- **Viv's exhibit** (P4.2, P9.2, P12.1): the board becomes a **courtroom display** across the chapter — six witnesses, the funeral, the slate, the invoice, the law page, the tag rubbing, the contract copy — and it must be *built* on the page, panel by panel, so that by P12.1 the reader recognizes it as the thing Ch. 17's court will be fought with. The chalk line "**ASK THE MOUNTAIN. ASK THE STREET. ASK THE CONTRACT.**" is Grimm's, in Grimm's hand, and it is the chapter's thesis: three kinds of witness, one question.
- **The dusty child** (P1.1, P7.2, P8.1, P10.1 cover, P12.1): she is *never named*, *never explained*, has no dialogue, and is drawn in five scenes doing nothing useful. **She is the child from Ch. 15's district** — a child of a street where a name was chiselled off a door. Do not promote her this volume; do not add her to any cast file. She is Volume 5's, if she is anyone's. (Art team: she must *not* be ambiguous-cute; draw her **ordinary** — dusty, bored, eating something that isn't hers.)
- **Rule 6 (Roderick)** — P7.1, one panel, no dialogue. **Rule 4 (chorus)** not used. **Rule 3 (costs drawn)**: the tally, the flask + crack, the cistern marks, the six names in chalk, thirteen braids, the padded pocket, the rack's tags.
