# AURELION · Volume 5 — Chapter 24: "One Throat"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc E: "The One Throat" — the realm's first decision in a year.** Nine stones, one clause with two holes and a witness, a no said with the face up, and a power the captain has been counting her breath against since she was fourteen — spent, once, in front of nine clans, as *credentials*. Then the cutting: nine hundred arms, one knife at a time, and a realm that has to decide what to do about the arms the knife cannot take. And after the law moves, two doors: a surety-hall whose bolt is still leaning inside it, untouched, and a maze whose office an outsider is now holding — which the realm's oldest institution intends to contest, in the realm's newest way: by *reading* for it.
> **Cast:** **The team** — Morgatha (**the clause, the holes, and the arm — the blank patch from Ch. 22 finally cut**), **Viv** (**the amendments; her three terms from Ch. 23 walked into the record; and the word this realm's grammar is missing**), **Ulgan** (**time, and the rest**), **Targa** (the smith who forges the clause's **first mark**), **Horn** (the record), **Maze** & **Taurus** (the maze's claim, brought as a *reading*), **Tauron** (an office to fight for), **Durgan the Unbent** (**03a's line, spent** — the throat changes), **Urzun the Red** (**03a's line, spent** — the vote he resents), **Ghorak** (the stone that says yes without pleasure), **Grom** (the council's question, asked by the code and not by a man), **Khura** (**her no, her witness-line, and the reading — 03b's beat, paid: the one it costs says it out loud**), **Drog** (the debt-line's keeper, and the *carrying*), **Ura** (the healer cutting law into arms by day and mending by night), **Kharza** & **Kha** (the code's youngest students), **Ulrik the Unbroken** (a free man, doing arithmetic), the household of Tarkhan (the nephew, the goat, the child who reads off her own arm), Fenn, Sprocket (**counting arms**), Kaelen (one frame, silent), Lyra, Bramble, Azrael (one line, and it is about oaths), **Nereia** (**no dialogue** — the mended flask, filled and passed down the cutters' line), Marginalia (the cart, at the pass, doing a census), and **Roderick** (**his one silent panel** — and his institution's crime gets a *name*).
> **Locations / threads:** the Ultravote ground and the throat-stone (the vote) → the bowl's floor (the cutting-tables) → the Barrens-forge (Targa's marks) → the surety-hall (a door, a bolt, and a debt) → the maze gate (the claim) → the bowl at night (the drum, and the rest).
> **Arc beat:** The realm cannot decide anything unless every stone is manned, and last chapter the stones came back: so this is the Barrens' **first vote in a year**, and it is a vote on its own soul. Morgatha brings the clause *and its holes* and reads both out, and the realm spends its first hour doing the thing this culture does better than any realm in the saga: **procedure** — the council asks the threshold question (by what standing does an *exile* bring a clause?), and the answer comes out of the realm's own record, because losing in the maze made her the holder of the maze, and the maze is where this realm keeps its law-school. Then the holes get closed, one honest amendment at a time: **Khura's witness-line** — which she cut into the horn herself in Ch. 23 — becomes the clause's lock, because the realm decides that the witness must be *the person the clause costs*, and the code cannot be invoked over a life by anyone who is not willing to pay for saying so; and the second hole — the life *freely given away* — is closed by a limit: the clause holds only where a life is **held against its will**, and a life given stays the giver's, because this realm knows exactly what its clans would read into a permission. Then the vote: stone by stone, faces up, dissents recorded because a lawyer won three terms on a gatepost last chapter and term three says the record reads both ways. It passes **seven to two, no stone silent**, and the two noes are entered *in words* — one of them the house of Tarkhan's, said with its owner's face up, which is the realm's definition of honour. Morgatha's Titanvein shows once, faintly, when her brother's house votes her down, and the realm — which is *made* of endurance — understands exactly what it is looking at, and Ulgan, who keeps the count, says so. **The throat changes:** the Speaker may only voice the assembled will, and the new clause carries a witness-line, and the code does not read its own witness — so Durgan the Unbent steps back from the stone he has held for forty years and holds the horn out to the woman who voted *no*, and **Khura reads the new law aloud to the realm that just made it**, and then Durgan says the line the volume has been waiting for. Then the price, drawn: **the clause is not law until it is carried**, so the realm cuts it into itself — the first mark, into the captain's arm, by Targa's forge, and then nine hundred more, one queue at a time, and the realm discovers two things about its own virtue in a week: that the law arrives as fast as the knives do, and that some arms **cannot take the cut at all**. The clause's last movement is the one that gives the volume its name: **the carrying.** The debt Gorakh cut onto the War-Chief's family cannot be paid by the family, and the clause leaves it unpaid — so the realm takes it: a released surety's debt **comes home to the realm**, which makes the Barrens' one throat the nearest kin of every unfinished debt in Ulgar, and makes the realm that just voted to be gentler *permanently poorer, on purpose*. Khura keeps the debt-horn. The horn says *unpaid, held by the realm*. And inside a warm house whose bolt has never been lifted, a War-Chief who has been held for a year by a word is, as of an ordinance passed at first light, free — and does not go anywhere, because being free is a decision and he is doing the arithmetic, and because there is a cell out there with every ford of the realm on paper and the realm has just taught itself that a life outranks a word, and both of those are true. The chapter ends with the realm's oldest institution filing its claim in the realm's newest way, a duel dated for the coda, the count in the corner at twelve, and a card whose scar finally has the other side of it cut.
> **Motifs advanced:** the throat (**one throat, and the day it changed hands**), the record (**dissent entered as law; a crime *named* before it is punished**), the gift (**the maze given to the loser, and rented back to the realm for a duel**), the count (**nine… twelve, and one stop — and Titanvein, once, at last, held**), the carrying (**a realm that assumes its families' debts and calls it statute**), the reading (**the one it costs reads it aloud**).
> **Not in this chapter:** **Oros's 03a line** and **the four companions** (both held) · the sea's first line (Nereia silent) · Wren's line · Kaelen's pilot uses · the Scar-Reading Chord · the stone-song (no bloom; the mountain is *warm*, and it is a thousand miles away) · the white · the empty inside the mountain · Orvane's garden · Khazur's seal-or-fight · **Gorakh** (absent; his *name* is on the horn and his *crime* is in the margin, and nobody in this realm has met him) · and **no second use of Titanvein** — this chapter spends it exactly once, and the art team must not repeat the hue anywhere else in the volume.

## Page 1 — *What Is Brought*

### P1.1 — The clause and the holes, read out (half page)
**Art:** The Ultravote ground at first light, and the art team's staging rule for the whole chapter: **this is not a crowd scene, it is a *hearing*.** The bowl full but quiet; the stones' own shadows long across the floor; kids up on shoulders because the realm brings its young to law; kettles *covered* — and the art team should notice the realm's tell for a serious day: on a reading day the kettles go; on a vote day they are *put away*. **Morgatha** stands on the bowl's floor with her forearms bare and the **debt-horn** on the throat-stone beside her (Ch. 23: she brought the horn so the record and the word are in the same place), and **Durgan** stands behind the stone with his hands flat on it, and **Khura** stands at the newest stone in the ring (the house of Tarkhan's, cut for the first time last chapter) with her arms folded and her face up. The realm's silence here must be the loud thing on the page: hundreds of people who have come to watch their own law get argued. And the count in the frame's corner, cut small: **nine.**
**Morgatha (flat, to the ring, with the horn under her hand and her arm bare, and this is Ch. 23's promise kept — *"I'll read you both"*):** "— Nine stones. The holding's this: ***the oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code.***" *(a beat; she does not raise her voice and the art team must not draw her raising it)* "And it's got two holes, and I'll say them before anybody else does. **One.** It's got no word for *who* can say a life's at stake — a feud can dress itself up as a life and this holding will open the door for it. **Two.** It's got no word for a life *given* — a man who spends himself gets no ruling out of this, and there's clans in this bowl who'll read that as permission." *(a beat)* "…Third thing's not a hole, it's a *question*. I'm exiled. I'll take that first."

### P1.2 — The council's question (half page)
**Art:** And the realm does the thing it does better than any other realm in this saga — **it asks the procedural question first, and it asks it politely.** **Grom**, of the war-chief council, stands at his stone with his hands behind his back and asks the question *as* a question, in the flat register of a man reading a line off a page he has carried for a year. The art team's direction: **nobody in the bowl is outraged.** Several are relieved. The realm has been *waiting* to be allowed to ask it. Four frames: **(1)** Grom standing; **(2)** the ring's faces, hearing; **(3)** **Viv**, on the floor's edge, already holding the horn's cord — she came prepared for exactly this and the art team should draw her *pleased*, which for her is a flat mouth and both eyebrows; **(4)** **Horn** the carver called forward to *read the entry out of his own record*, because the answer to a standing question in this realm is not a speech — it is a line already in the horn.
**Grom (flat, to the throat-stone, hands behind his back):** "— The council's got one question and it's not about the clause. **By what standing does an exile bring a holding to the circle?**" *(a beat)* "…That's not me being clever, captain. That's the code's own line and every soul here knows it, and if we don't ask it, the *children* will ask it in nine years and we'll have to answer then without you."
**Viv (flat, stepping onto the floor with the horn's cord in her hand, and she has been waiting for this question since Ch. 22):** "— Then let the record answer it. **Horn.** Page four."
**Horn (flat, reading off the horn, no balloon — the lettering team must set this in the record's hand, not the realm's, because it is a *page* and not a speech):** "…*Entry. Walker: the captain. Terms: her three. Result: clause taken at the fourth turn, held at the centre, returned. Loss: hers. Holding: the maze.*" *(he looks up)* "— That's the realm's own rule, chief. **The maze goes to whoever pays for it.** She paid. She holds it."
**Viv (flat, to the ring, and this is her whole contribution to the day in one sentence):** "— Your realm wouldn't let her in a gate. Your realm's law did. **She lost a duel in your house and your house made her a landlord.**" *(a beat)* "…The clause is the head of the realm's own law-school bringing a holding to the circle. That's the standing. If you want to overrule your own maze, do it — but you'll have to write it down, and you'll have to say out loud what your law-school is *for*."

## Page 2 — *Closing the Holes*

### P2.1 — The witness who pays (half page)
**Art:** The first amendment, and the art team's craft page: **Khura's line, cut into the horn at the end of Ch. 23, read aloud for the first time.** Four frames, all close, all on the horn: **(1)** Khura's graver-mark on the horn's side — small, hers, the house of Tarkhan's own; **(2)** Khura standing at her stone, arms folded, letting it be read *by somebody else*, which the art team should draw as the specific patience of a person who has been talked about all morning; **(3)** Durgan's hand on the horn, turning it to the light so the new cut catches; **(4)** the realm's faces doing the arithmetic — because they have just understood what the realm's newest law is going to say about *witnesses*, and it is not comfortable. And the lettering team's rule, invented here for the rest of the volume: **amendments are lettered in the record's hand and quoted by characters in balloons** — the realm has to *say* what it wrote.
**Morgatha (flat, reading off the horn and then straight at the ring — the woman bringing the clause stating the clause's lock):** "— The house of Tarkhan put a line in it, and the line's this: **a life-clause needs a witness, and the witness has to be the one it costs.** Not a chief. Not a council. Not me. Somebody who *pays* for the door being open — because if the witness has to pay, then no clan in this realm is going to stand up and say a life's at stake unless a life's *at stake*." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her NOT looking at Khura, which is how the reader knows she is talking about her)* "That's hole one. It's not closed. It's **locked**, and the lock is a person, and I'm not the one holding the key."
**Ghorak (flat, from his stone, hands open, and this is 03b's *he votes with Morgatha* — but without one note of enthusiasm):** "— Then whose line was it, captain?"
**Khura (flat, from her stone, face up, and this is the first time she has spoken in the chapter):** "**Mine.** …Read it back to me in front of the realm, and then read my *no* next to it, and let everybody stand here and work out what they're looking at." *(a beat)* "— My house is going to vote against this. My line is going to be in it. Both of those are the code. That's what the code's *for*."

### P2.2 — The life given away (half page)
**Art:** The second amendment, and the page's job is to make a *limit* read like a mercy. **Morgatha** states it, Viv letters it, and — the art team's key beat — **Drog**, the debt-line's oldest keeper, stands up and objects *correctly*, because a code with an order has to answer "whose life is first". Four frames: **(1)** Morgatha stating the limit; **(2)** Drog standing, old, slow, unbowed; **(3)** the bowl's silence while an elder asks a child's question; **(4)** **the limit written in the realm's cut script across the frame's lower third** — the art team should set it exactly like Ch. 22's clause line, so the reader recognises the register: *a life held against its will; a life freely given is the giver's, and no clan may invoke it for him.*
**Morgatha (flat, and this is the sentence she rehearsed in a warm room in Ch. 22 and lost twice to a war-chief before she got it right):** "— Hole two. The holding covers a life **held against its will** — and it does *not* cover a life that's given. A man who spends himself stays his own business, and no clan may use my clause to make his spending *speak for him*." *(a beat)* "…So it's not a permission. It's a *fence*. Everything the code said before, it still says. This just goes in **first**."
**Drog (flat, standing, and the realm's oldest voice asks the realm's oldest question):** "— First for *whose* life, girl? A chief's? A stranger's? A wolf's?"
**Morgatha (flat, and she does not look at her father's house, because the answer is not about him):** "— **Any.** That's the whole of it. If it's got to be a *good* life first, then somebody's going to be holding the scale, and I've not walked in here to give this realm one more thing to hold." *(a beat; and the bowl, which has been listening the way a court listens, makes the smallest sound a bowl can make — the sound of several hundred people agreeing without meaning to)*

## Page 3 — *The Chief Who Resents It*

### P3.1 — Urzun's year (half page)
**Art:** **Urzun the Red**, chief of Morgatha's own blood-clan, and the art team's hardest performance in the chapter: a man who has been *right* for a year and hated it. He stands at his stone and does not start with the clause — he starts with the year, flatly, the way a man reads a bill. The art team's craft: **his forearms**, which are the most heavily cut in the realm — every oath of a long life in the same script, and the newest marks not yet faded; the reader should see that this man is the code's *proof* and that he is about to argue against one of its lines. Four frames: **(1)** Urzun standing, sleeves already rolled, which several people notice; **(2)** the surety-hall's roofline in the background of the bowl's frame, drawn small, because it is visible from here and everybody has looked at it for a year; **(3)** his hands, and what they have not done for a year; **(4)** the ring listening *the way they listen to him specifically*, because he is the realm's own argument.
**Urzun (flat, to the ring, and it is a bill, not a speech):** "— The code says a captain's word outranks a blood-debt. My comrade's in a chair in his son's house with a bolt by the door and the realm's word *on* him, and the code has told me for a year to sit down, and I sat down, and I'm *not* sorry I sat down. …So here's what I've got to weigh, stones: a realm where the word holds, and a realm where my brother walks. And I've been weighing it since the frost, and the answer is *bad*, and I'm going to give it anyway." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him looking, once, at the throat-stone and not at the captain)* "…Because the other thing the code says is that a word you only keep when it's *pleasant* isn't a word."

### P3.2 — The vote (half page)
**Art:** And then **03a's line, spent** — and the art team must stage it as an *administrative* act with a face on it: Urzun turns to his stone, puts his hand flat on it the way the realm does for a settled matter, and gives his clan's voice. The bowl's reaction is the page's real subject, drawn in four frames: **(1)** the ring's faces — nobody cheers, several nod, two old ones look at the ground; **(2)** **Ghorak's** stone, hand flat, no words, a yes without pleasure (his clan and Urzun's are the same blood and they have split on this, and neither of them will say one word about it, and the art team should draw them *not* looking at each other); **(3)** **Ulgan's** drum: four beats and a rest, marking the stone's word; **(4)** the count in the corner, moved: **ten.**
**Urzun (flat, hand on the stone, and this is the line, verbatim, unaltered — the chief who changes the vote):** "**The code says a captain's word outranks a blood-debt. Morgatha just** made *the code say the blood-debt outranks the* code. *I vote for the new code. I* resent *it.*" *(a beat)* "— And I'll want that word *resent* in the horn, captain. Not *agreed with*. **Resented.** Any realm that can't write that down is a realm that's lying about how it got here."
**Viv (quiet, at the floor's edge, to nobody, and the lawyer is having the best morning of her life):** "…Four hundred years of legal philosophy and it took a man with a rolled-up sleeve four sentences."

## Page 4 — *The Stones Speak*

### P4.1 — The rite (half page)
**Art:** The vote itself, and the art team's design job: **make an administrative process the most thrilling thing in the volume.** The realm's rite, drawn once so the reader can read every later panel at a glance: a chief stands at their stone, hand flat, **face up**, and gives their clan's word — *yes* or *no* — and the word is given *into the bowl*, not at the captain; the Speaker marks it; the drum gives four beats and a rest; and **the stones speak in the order they were cut** — the oldest stones first, the newest last. Four frames: **(1)** the oldest stone (silver-grey, repointed a dozen times) and its old chief's yes; **(2)** the red-black slab, chisel-worn, a yes; **(3)** a third stone — **and a *no*** — from a clan whose ford-law this clause would reach into: drawn, not named, a middle-aged chief with a bad shoulder who says it without anger and then stands there and *takes the looks*, and the art team must not draw him as a villain, because he is not; **(4)** the bowl's faces, and this is the frame the chapter needs: **the realm watching a no being said and letting it be said.** No shock, no jeering, no hissing. Several people *nod* at him. That is the realm's dignity and it is the thing the Reforgers have never once understood about it.
**Ulgan (flat, calling time, and the realm's clock has a keeper, so the keeper gets the chapter's only running line):** "— *Yes*, first stone, and the drum hears it. …*Yes*, second. …**No**, third stone, and the drum hears *that* too, brother; nobody in this bowl is going to make you stand there by yourself. …Next."
**Azrael (quiet, on the rim, watching a realm vote without one drop of violence in the air, and the fallen angel gets one line and it is exactly his register):** "…I tore an oath off a page, and the page was *paper*, and nobody bled. These people are about to open their own arms for a sentence." *(a beat)* "…I'd like it on the record that I've stopped being sure which of us was serious."

### P4.2 — The newest stone (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's turn, and the art team's careful handling: **the house of Tarkhan's stone speaks last**, because it is the newest stone, and **Khura gives her no with her face up.** Four frames: **(1)** Khura stepping off the line of her household to the stone — her house's young ones behind her at a distance, the nephew holding the child; **(2)** her hand flat on the stone's fresh cut-mark; **(3)** the *no*, given plainly, into the bowl; **(4)** and then — **the frame the volume has been holding since Ch. 22** — **Morgatha**, on the floor, at the exact moment her brother's house votes against the clause that would free her father: and under the skin of her right forearm, along the vein, **a faint red light the colour of an old wound** — `#d1495b`, drawn *small*, *thin*, *under* the skin, **never a glow, never a halo, never outside the arm**, and the art team's absolute rule: **it is the only time this hue appears in the volume.** She is looking at Khura. The count in the corner is visible. And she **counts** — the reader has seen her do it for twenty-three chapters and does not need the caption; the lettering team adds no caption.
**Khura (flat, hand on the stone, to the bowl, and it is a filing with a heart in it):** "— The house of Tarkhan stands at its stone. **No.**" *(a beat)* "…And the record will have the *reason*, because the captain's lawyer put that in the terms and I'll not have it said my house hid behind a word: a man's debt is not a realm's to take, and my brother's is *unpaid*, and I'll not pretend it's finished to make anybody's morning easier." *(and then, to Morgatha, in exactly the same tone, which is the point)* "…No hard feelings, captain. You'd have done the same, and worse, and better."
**Ulgan (flat, and the realm's oldest woman is looking at the captain's arm, and she says the chapter's other great line):** "— There's the vein. …*Look* at it, brothers. That's the realm's own, and she's had it under a *count* since she was fourteen years old, and she's got it lit in front of nine clans and she's counting. **We're voting on a holding about lives.** Would you rather it was carried by somebody who *hasn't* got a full tank and a lid on it?" *(a beat; and the drum, four beats and a rest, and the bowl's answer is not a cheer — it is the sound of several hundred people standing up)*

## Page 5 — *The Stop, and the Reading*

### P5.1 — The stop (FULL PAGE, one panel, no dialogue)
**Art:** The chapter's single stop, and the volume's rule holds: **when the count stops, that is the panel.** One frame, **full width, full height, no dialogue, and no count in the corner.** The image: **Khura**, leaving her stone, walking down the rim into the bowl with the **debt-horn** in both hands — not as a surrender, not as a triumph: as a *filing*, the way this realm carries things it means. Behind her, the nine stones and the whole realm standing; ahead of her, the throat-stone, and **Durgan** with his hands on it; and on the bowl's floor, between them, **Morgatha**, whose arm is bare. The art team's craft notes: **no Titanvein** (the hue must not appear anywhere near this panel — the vein has had its one moment on P4.2 and it is *over*), no SFX, no speed lines, no dramatic angle. Shoot it flat, at eye level, from the rim, the way the realm is seeing it: **the woman the clause costs, walking to the stone to read it.** The single most important detail: Khura's face is *up*, and her hands are *steady*, and the horn is being carried the way you carry somebody else's mail — carefully, and without ownership.

### P5.2 — *Read it, Khura* (half page)
**Art:** The reading, and the reserved canon beat paid: **the one it costs says the new law out loud.** Staged as a *transfer of the throat*, in four frames: **(1)** Durgan's hands coming off the throat-stone — the first time in forty years the man has let go of it, and the art team should draw his hands and not his face; **(2)** Durgan turning the horn in his hands and holding it **out**, across the stone, to Khura; **(3)** Khura taking it; **(4)** Khura reading — lettered plainly, in balloons, and the art team must not decorate it. Behind her, drawn without a caption: the realm standing, and — the detail that costs the art team nothing and buys everything — **the two chiefs who said no are standing too**, because the realm stands for a reading, and dissent and disrespect are different things here.
**Durgan (flat, hands off the stone, and this is procedure — not an honour, not a concession, and the art team must not draw it as one):** "— The circle has spoken. **Seven voices yes. Two no. No stone silent.** The holding carries." *(a beat; and then, because this is why the realm keeps a man like this at this stone)* "…But the holding's got a witness-line in it now, and the code does not read its own witness. I've held this stone forty years and I'll not start *bending* on the last clause of my life. …**Read it, Khura.**"
**Khura (flat, taking the horn, looking at the realm, and reading a law she has just voted against — line by line, exactly as the record has it, and the lettering team sets each line in the record's hand as she says it):** "— *The oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code.* **Where a life is held against its will;** where a life is given, it stays the giver's, and no clan may speak for him; **the witness is the one it costs**, and their name goes in the horn; and **a debt this holding releases comes home to the realm.** …*Seven yes. Two no. No stone silent. Entered.*" *(a beat, and the art team must let her hold the horn up so the ring can see the cut)* "— There. It's read. It's *mine* to have read, and I want the record to say that too."
**Morgatha (quiet, from the floor, and it is not gratitude and not triumph; the art team: she is looking at her father's house's stone and not at the law):** "…Aye."

## Page 6 — *The Throat Changed*

### P6.1 — Durgan's line (half page)
**Art:** And the line the volume has carried since Ch. 22, spent in the plainest type in the chapter — and the art team's instruction is that **the frame must not celebrate it.** Four frames: **(1)** the horn back on the throat-stone, Khura's hand leaving it; **(2)** the bowl's faces, standing, watching Durgan, who is still a man with his hands at his sides; **(3)** Durgan's face, and this is the only close-up he gets in the book, and it is not joyful — it is a man who has just watched his office change shape and has decided, on the spot, to be *for* it; **(4)** the realm's own answer: **not a cheer.** The realm does the thing this realm does, and the art team invented it in Ch. 22 and must reuse it exactly: **the bowl repeats the clause back**, out of time with each other, hundreds of voices, reading it off the *air* and off each other's arms, the way they read grievances into a stone.
**Durgan (flat, from the throat-stone, hands at his sides, and this is 03a's line, verbatim, unaltered):** "**The Ultravote is one throat. Today the throat** changed. *That's not a* coup. *That's the* code *talking.*" *(a beat)* "…Read it back, brothers. It's yours now, and it's got a hole in my office the size of a house. I'd rather say that out loud than let you find it in nine years."
**Viv (quiet, at the floor's edge, and this is the lawyer's own amendment finally paid — she wins the chapter's quietest prize and the art team should draw her with her arms folded and her eyes shut):** "…Term three. *The record reads both ways.* You wrote it on a gatepost last week and you didn't know you were writing today. **Two noes in the horn, in words, with reasons.** The realm's law has got a *dissent* in it now, and in nine years there'll be a clan that wants to know why the house of Tarkhan said no, and the horn will tell them, and it will not say anything *unkind*, because that's what a record is for." *(a beat)* "…That's the first decision this realm has taken in a year, and it took it without one drop of blood. I'm going to remember that on days when I'm not this happy."

### P6.2 — The first cut (half page)
**Art:** The price promised in Ch. 22, drawn — and the art team's rule is absolute: **it is a *procedure*, and it is *hard*, and nobody in it is having a good time.** The realm's own benches down at the bowl's floor where the cutting-tables have been set up (planks, lamps, knives, boiled cloth, **Ura's mender's kit** at the end of the row, because this realm is not stupid); **Targa**, of the Barrens-forge, with the mark-chisel; and **Morgatha** first, because she brought it, sitting on a bench with her arm out on the **throat-stone** and the realm watching. **END OF CHAPTER 24.**

*The realm that keeps its law in its arms has a parliament that cannot raise its voice unless every stone is manned, and by the end of the last chapter every stone was manned, and so this chapter is what a realm looks like when it finally gets to *decide*: they ask the procedural question first and they ask it kindly; they let a chief who has been right for a year tell them the answer is bad; they let an old man ask whose life comes first, and they get told *any*, and the sound several hundred people make when they agree without meaning to is the loudest thing in the bowl. The clause passes seven to two with no stone silent, and the two noes go into the horn *in words, with reasons*, because a lawyer won three terms on a gatepost the week before and one of them said the record reads both ways — and then the realm does the thing that makes it unlike every other realm in this saga: the Speaker steps back off the stone he has held for forty years, because the new law carries a witness-line and the code doesn't read its own witness, and the woman who voted *no* is the one with the standing to read it *aloud*. That is the whole Barrens in one morning: honour is not agreement, it is *attendance*. Then the price, which this realm collects in skin — nine hundred arms, one queue at a time, eleven of them too thin to take the cut, and a solution invented on the floor's edge over bread: put the *first word* on them, small, because a sentence is a sentence if you know where it starts. And then the amendment that gives the volume its name: a debt the code used to hang on a family now comes home to the *realm* — nobody forgiven, nothing cancelled, the one throat carrying what no house can, which means the Barrens has voted, in writing, with knives, to be permanently poorer and permanently cleaner, and has invented insurance and paid for it in forearms. A War-Chief who has been lawfully held for a year is free by ordinance and stays in his son's house doing arithmetic, because the arithmetic is about *fords*, and there is a cell out there with every one of them on paper, and because the realm he was held by has just been told in writing that a life outranks a word. And at the pass, a cart that has been refused entry at nine realms' borders files one finding and stays where it is: **the realm is the only thing in the world that can be made stronger by being made to carry.***
**The record's cards are hide now, and the scar that nothing was written on last chapter has the other side of it cut, and the count in the corner is at twelve.**
