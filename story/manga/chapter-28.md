# AURELION · Volume 5 — Chapter 28: "The Founder"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc E: "The One Throat" — the volume's antagonist sits down at a table with the woman whose brother he used, and the sentence he has been carrying for eleven years is said out loud by the old man who taught him to see.** Gorakh the Scarred comes back from the far side of the wound: a cyclops in a plain coat with a sampler's hands and a season of grey on his boots, and he comes back to a stockade where a nineteen-year-old has spent two weeks reading his clauses to his own clerks, where a filing is waiting on his signature, and where — three days behind him on the same road, having walked east since Chapter 23 with one sentence he has been holding for eleven years — comes **Oros the Old Eye**, who taught him to see the wound and who has not been able to talk to him since the day the family was lost. The chapter's structure is a **table**, and the volume's whole method: no one fights, nothing explodes, and every knife in it is a *sentence said politely in a room with four chairs and no bench*. Gorakh answers the complaint by **conceding it and then some** — he enters restitution for the third crossing out of his own funds, which costs him more than any punishment the court could have handed down, because the camp's money is the survey's money and the survey is the only thing he has left. Then he does the thing the volume has been walking toward since Ch. 22's horn: he tells Tarkhan's sister the truth about her brother — not the killing (that was another cell, and he names it) but the *use*: he moved a dead man's debt onto the dead man's house **because the Barrens had the only procedure in nine realms that could write a law in a week**, and he needed a realm to invent a word for the wound, and it did, and the clause that has now been read at nine doors and entered in a foreign court's book exists **because he made a hostage out of a war-chief to force it into being**. He does not apologise for the aim. He apologises for nothing at all, exactly once, in seven words, and the count stops. Then **Oros's line** — 03a's, spent verbatim at last — is delivered by an old sage who walked three hundred miles to say eight words to a man who used to be his student, and after it the founder asks the realm for the only thing he has ever asked anybody for: **a reading that isn't his own** — a reader at the camp, permanently, "so that the wound has a witness who isn't *mine*" — and Morgatha, whose brother is dead because of this man, does not forgive him and does not refuse him, and takes the request home to the realm in her pocket, where Chapter 29 will answer it.
> **Cast:** **Gorakh the Scarred** (**the founder; the best of us; the man who used a family to make a realm change its code**), **Oros the Old Eye** (**03a's line, spent** — the teacher, and the chapter's knife), **the registrar** (the founder's teacher's apprentice; thirty years of his books), **the gate-clerk**, **the sampler** (**Gorakh's surviving daughter — drawn, never named, and never given a line about her father**), the ward's old cyclops (both hands wrapped), the **camp's clerks** and the **troll surgeon**, **Morgatha** (**the table; the question she finally asks; the two fingers**), **Viv** (who drafts the answer to a filing she came to win and leaves with something *harder*), **Kharza** (who reads the clause at the founder of the first cell), **Fenn** (who gets what he came for, and one thing he did not), **Sprocket** (the column's second entry, and the first one that costs something), **Azrael** (one line, and it is the chapter's mirror: the angel who broke one law for a good reason, looking at a man who kept every law for a worse one), **Bramble** (one line, about roads), **Kaelen** (**one silent frame**), **Nereia** (**no dialogue** — the well, the flask, and the markings), Marginalia (the cart, filing its *forty-second* complaint, and withdrawing it), and **Roderick** (**his one silent panel** — his brother's name on a paper in a camp at the edge of the world).
> **Locations / threads:** the camp at the edge of the world — the gate, the hearing room (a table, four chairs), the map-room, the ward, the stockade's north corner, and the grey beyond all of it.
> **Arc beat:** This chapter has to do four jobs and it must do them all *quietly*, because the volume's whole argument is that the loudest thing in the world is a person agreeing with you. **Job one:** the founder's answer to the filing — and his answer must not be a defeat for the realm; it must be *worse than a defeat*, a concession that gives the realm everything it asked for and a thing it did not want: **restitution out of the camp's own purse** (which pays the survey's wages for a month) and a letter, entered, reading ***this camp was wrong at the third crossing, and it was wrong lawfully, and the two are the same problem***. **Job two:** the conversation with Morgatha. It must not be a duel and it must not be a confession scene; it must be a *reckoning between two professionals*, in which Gorakh gives her the truth she has been walking nine hundred miles for and then declines to be forgiven, because his canon is not villainy — it is the code taken to its logical end: the wound is real, the realms will never move, so a man moves them, and a man who moves them has to be willing to use whatever the realms love. He used her house. He did it with a lawful ask and a clean hand. He would do it again for the same reason and he says so, and then he says the only apology he has, and it is not for her brother. **Job three:** **Oros.** The teacher arrives on the same road, signs in at the same gate, and spends one page and eight words on eleven years. It is the volume's turning point for the *cell*, not for the realm: the first cell's founder is not defeated in this chapter; he is *read at*, by his own teacher, and the reading is the whole of the rest of his life. **Job four:** the request. Gorakh asks the realm for a **reading post at the camp** — not a treaty, not an alliance, not a pardon: "the wound should have a witness who isn't mine." It is the most dangerous sentence in the volume, because it is *reasonable*, and because it would put the Barrens' newest law on the shore of the grey for the rest of the series. The council's answer, the realm's answer, and Morgatha's answer are **Ch. 29's** — the coda — and this chapter ends with the filing signed, the debt of the third crossing paid, and a captain standing at the stockade's north corner looking at the hole in the world, deciding.
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**restitution entered; the column's second entry; a signature in the right place for the first time**), the door (**a camp that asks instead of takes; a witness who isn't mine**), the reading (**the teacher's eight words**, **the reader at the founder's table**), the count (**twenty-five… twenty-seven, and the volume's fourth stop**), the carrying (**a realm asked to carry a wound**).
> **Not in this chapter:** **Titanvein** · the stone-song / any bloom (`#e07a3f` zero) · **the four companions' identities** are paid *emotionally* and never named (rule 7: **no new named characters**, drawn only) · the sea's first line (**Nereia silent**; the sea stays Vol. 6's) · Wren's 03a line · Kaelen's pilot uses (**one silent frame**) · the Scar-Reading Chord · the white · the *empty* inside the mountain · Orvane's garden · Khazur's seal-or-fight · **the actual killers of Tarkhan** (named as **the fourth cell**, and left *uncaptured* — the hunt is not this volume's) · the twins' letter · and the Re-Oath vote.

## Page 1 — *He Comes Back Polite*

### P1.1 — The sampler (half page)
**Art:** The far side of the camp at first light, and the art team's first shot of the founder — **and the rule for this entire chapter: he is drawn small, and tired, and *working*.** Gorakh the Scarred: a cyclops in a plain grey coat with a sampler's straps and a **long-handled scoop**, coming in off the grey with four others, and the reader's first impression must be *not a general*: a man in his sixties, one eye, deep scar across the brow, hands like a stonemason's, and — the art team's key detail — **a strip of cloth sewn over his coat pocket, exactly the way the troll menders sew a clamp into a garment**. He walks the last hundred paces of the grey with his scoop over his shoulder, and behind him the party sees, for the first time from *inside* the camp, four people come off the wound carrying sample-boxes, and one of them — **a young cyclops woman in a sampler's coat, drawn, never named** — walks *past* him without a word and goes into the ward, and **the founder stops walking for one heartbeat and then carries on**, and the art team must draw that and nothing else. The count in the corner: **twenty-five.**
**The gate-clerk (flat, out of his booth, and the only character in the chapter who treats the founder like weather):** "\u2014 Founder. Filing's on your desk \u2014 Ulgar, three crossings, complaint abated by the registrar, and there's a *reader* in your map-room that's been at your clerks for two weeks and has half of them standing up when she talks." *(a beat)* "…She's got a horn and a rolled sleeve and she's *nineteen*. You'll like her. I do."

### P1.2 — The map-room (half page)
**Art:** The founder in his own map-room, and the art team's staging rule: **this is his home and it should read as one.** The layered-hide maps on the wall; the newer sheet; a table with the founder's own working copy of the same country, in pencil, going down onto the grey *by the yard*; sample-boxes on shelves with wax seals and dated tickets; **a kettle**; and on the windowsill, facing the wind, a row of **eleven small stones**, each with a name cut in it, and the art team must let the reader see exactly four of the names and no more. He comes in, puts the scoop on its rack, washes his hands, and reads the filing standing up. Four frames: **(1)** the scoop going onto its rack (the art team must draw the rack: it has eleven other scoops on it, all of them *worn out*, and he does not throw them away); **(2)** the filing, read; **(3)** the founder's face, and the art team's craft is that his reaction to the Barrens' filing is **relief**, not anger; **(4)** and the arrival, unannounced, at the map-room door — the gate-clerk behind her, the camp's clerks getting up, and **Kharza**, in the doorway, with a travel-horn on her back, nineteen years old, and the founder looking at her for a long moment before anyone says a word.
**Gorakh (flat, to the doorway, and this is the first line out of the volume's antagonist's mouth and it must be *ordinary*, because that is what makes him frightening for the next eleven pages):** "\u2014 You'll be the reader. …Sit down, then. I've read your clause. Six times: three in the copy they sent up the road and three from the chart at the foot of a ward bed, and I'll tell you what I think of it in a minute, and I'll tell you where it's *wrong* in a week, because if a nineteen-year-old's going to sit in my map-room for a fortnight, we may as well be useful to each other." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him pouring tea, because he does, and because it is not a tactic)* "…Everybody in this camp's been standing up when you talk. That's *new*. I'd like to know why."

## Page 2 — *Signatures*

### P2.1 — The answer to the filing (half page)
**Art:** The hearing room — the table, the four chairs, no bench — and the chapter's first legal movement, and the art team's rule: **it happens with the party present and the registrar's two clerks standing at the wall.** Four frames: **(1)** the founder reading the registrar's draft answer, pencil in hand, and the reader must see him strike things out; **(2)** the restitution figure, lettered in plate-type: **the third crossing's fee returned, doubled, plus the survey's own toll-roll for the crossing, out of the camp's funds**; **(3)** **the registrar's** face, because this is the page's real subject — the man who keeps the books doing the arithmetic and finding it *hurts* (the art team: he looks at the founder the way a book-keeper looks at a man who has just spent a month of wages); **(4)** the signature block, and the blank line from Ch. 27's Roderick panel finally filled — the art team must draw the same device, the same blank line, and **the founder's own hand putting his name on it**, with his title in the smallest possible lettering: **Gorakh, of the first cell**.
**Gorakh (flat, signing, and this is his register — a man dealing with paper the way a mason deals with stone):** "\u2014 Return the fee doubled, and put the survey's crossing-roll in as a line item, and I'll write the letter myself. …Say this: ***this camp was wrong at the third crossing, and it was wrong lawfully, and the two are the same problem.***" *(a beat)* "…Your realm's complaint is correct and I'm not making a *gesture*. I bought a ford because a company had it and I wanted the well-drilling boats through; I'd have done it again and I'd have *paid* again."
**Viv (flat, across the table, and the lawyer has spent two weeks being impressed and has been hating every minute of it):** "\u2014 You're giving us the case. Costs, restitution, an apology in writing, and a line in your own books. …Why?"
**Gorakh (flat, and this is the answer that tells the reader exactly what kind of antagonist this is):** "\u2014 Because *you're* the first realm that's ever come here with a **reader** instead of an army, and I've been standing on that grey for a hundred years waiting for one. I'll pay a month of the survey's wages to keep a *witness* on my doorstep, and if you think that's generosity, advocate, then you've not understood what I'm *for*."

### P2.2 — The word in the horn (half page)
**Art:** And the page's second movement: **Viv reads Gorakh the margin-word**, and the art team's rule is that this must be *flat*, and that the founder's reaction must be small. She has carried a copy of the debt-horn's margin (the word *the lawful hand*, entered under Gorakh's name) since Ch. 24, and she reads it to him: four frames: **(1)** Viv producing the copy, and the lettering team must set the word in the **record's cut hand** as quoted text, the way this volume always quotes the horn; **(2)** Viv reading it, plainly, and adding the statute's state: *entered as a note, not yet voted; a crime needs its own circle; the Barrens has promised to hold the first circle of the Nine for it when the road comes home*; **(3)** the founder's face — and the art team must draw **no satisfaction**, only a man of sixty listening to four words that describe his whole life in another realm's grammar; **(4)** **Kharza** behind Viv, and her two sentences, which are the chapter's first hard blow, because a nineteen-year-old says them without mercy and without cruelty.
**Kharza (flat, from behind Viv, and the reader has been waiting for this since Ch. 23's margin):** "\u2014 That's the word, Founder. *The lawful hand.* Nine words for a thief in my realm's grammar and *none* for you, and my realm spent a week and nine hundred arms inventing the one you're standing under." *(a beat)* "…And I'll say the other half, because Viv's too *polite* and the captain's too angry and Sprocket's got a column. **We wouldn't have needed the word if you'd knocked on a door and told us about the wound.** You're paying for the *crossing*. You've not paid for a *year of my captain's father in a chair*, and you've not offered, and I've read your books for two weeks, Founder, and I know exactly why: because you don't think it *counts*." *(a beat, and the art team must draw the room's silence)* "…That's the whole case, then. Everything else is arithmetic."

## Page 3 — *The Fourth Cell*

### P3.1 — What happened to Tarkhan (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's factual page, and it must be delivered **by the registrar**, not by the founder — the art team's decisive choice: the founder does not get to narrate his own crime. The registrar brings the *records*, because that is his office and because he has known what is in them for nine years and has never once been asked. Four frames: **(1)** the registrar laying three sheets on the table: a cell-log, a muster-roll, and **a death-notice from another cell**, and the lettering team must set them in the plate-type register; **(2)** the log entry: **the fourth cell**, operating out of a mine-road east of the pass, which went *off the sheet* nine years ago — no charts, no samples, no chain of custody, **and the first cell has been filing against them ever since**; **(3)** the founder's hands, flat on the table, and this is the art team's craft: he does not move them for the rest of the page; **(4)** and the registrar's own line, which is the chapter's honesty, delivered in his civil-servant register and therefore landing like a hammer. **And one final frame, at the door behind the registrar's shoulder, and the art team must give it no emphasis at all: RODERICK** — not entering, not speaking, holding one sheet out at arm's length for the registrar to take — the fourth cell's **last sighting report**, and on its witness-line, drawn plainly enough to read, a clerk's signature block: **I. VANE** — and nobody in the frame looks at it, and nobody in this chapter ever mentions it.
**The registrar (flat, reading from the muster-roll, and the camp's book-keeper is the one who tells the truth because he is the only man in the room whose job is the truth):** "\u2014 Nine years ago the fourth cell went off the sheet: no chart, no sample, no report, and they killed for the first time in the second spring. …They took a Barrens orc off a road for his *goods and his coat*: the first cell has the whole list. Captain — **the men who killed your brother were not ours, and they were ours *once*, and we've been hunting them for nine years with paper**." *(a beat)* "…And I'll say the other thing myself, because he won't: the *debt* that came home to your father's house came home because *he* asked for it — **he had no duty to, and he needed a lever, and your realm was the only realm in the world that could make a law in a week.** He wrote the ask in his own hand. I typed it. That's my part. I've kept it nine years and I'll answer for it in front of anybody you like."

### P3.2 — "Why" (half page)
**Art:** And the founder answers, and the art team must shoot it as **three frames of a man explaining arithmetic to colleagues, then one frame the reader will not forgive him for.** No raised voice, no chair pushed back, no monologue: **(1)** the wall map with the wound on it, and the founder's pencil *tracing the survey line* while he talks, because he cannot stop working; **(2)** his argument, lettered as a *list* because that is how he thinks: *nine realms, four hundred years of treaties; the wound is a mile wide and growing by a finger a decade; no realm will move while its own house is in order; therefore* **a realm must be made to write a law it did not mean to write**; **(3)** the specific: **the Barrens had the Ultravote — one throat, nine stones, a procedure that can invent law in a week** — and the realm would never have used it on the wound, because the wound is not a clan's grievance, **so the hole had to be opened from inside the realm's own honour**: a hostage nobody could legally release, held by a house with a grievance, in a code that could not answer itself; **(4)** and the frame the art team must not soften: **the founder's face when he says what he says next.**
**Gorakh (flat, pencil moving, and this is the canon sentence — *the code, taken to its logical end*, and it must be delivered without relish and without apology):** "\u2014 I did not have your brother killed, captain. I've never killed anybody and I've never ordered it and I'd have kept him alive if it were mine to keep. **I used him.** He was dead, and a dead man's debt is a *lever* in that realm, and I took the lever, and I asked a room for a rule, and a girl of twenty answered it with grain and a word and nine hundred arms." *(a beat, and the pencil stops)* "…And here is the part you came nine hundred miles for, so I'll say it plainly and I'll not dress it. **I did it because your realm, of all nine, was the only one where a law could be born in a week — and the wound does not have a week's patience or a century's.** Every realm in the world will be arguing about that hole in the ground a thousand years from now. I needed *one* realm to have a word for it. Yours has. It cost you a brother before I ever touched your house, and it cost your father a year in a chair after, and if I had it to do again I would be *looking* for another way and I would not find one, and I've been looking for *eleven years*." *(a beat)* "…I'm sorry for the year. I'm not sorry for the lever, and I'll not lie to a woman who's been counting something in the corner of the room since she sat down."

## Page 4 — *The Stop*

### P4.1 — The fourth stop (FULL PAGE, one panel, no dialogue)
**Art:** The volume's fourth — and last — stop, and the rules the art team has obeyed three times must now be obeyed completely: **one frame, full width, full height, no dialogue, no balloons, no captions, no count in the corner, no Titanvein, no bloom, no SFX, and no dramatic angle.** The image: the hearing room in the camp at the edge of the world — the table, the four chairs, the registrar's three sheets, the pencil down, the kettle gone cold — and **Morgatha**, sitting across from the founder of the first cell, very still, with her own arm on the table and the realm's new clause cut into it in four lines, in the light of a camp lamp; **the founder's hands flat on the table**; **Viv** upright beside her with the copy of the horn in her fist; **Kharza** behind, sleeve rolled; **the gate-clerk**, in the doorway, forgotten, holding a message nobody is reading; and outside the window, drawn *only* as a band across the top of the frame, **the grey** — because the art team must make the reader feel the thing the whole chapter is about: *the wound is why this is happening, and it is not in the room, and it will outlive every person at that table.* The single most important craft note: **Morgatha is not crying, not shaking, and not reaching for anything.** She is a captain doing the arithmetic she has done since Chapter 3, and the panel is the silence in which it comes out *equal on both sides and wrong*.

### P4.2 — What she asks (half page)
**Art:** The words after the silence, and the art team's rule: **she does not ask for an apology and she does not give one.** Four frames, all close: **(1)** her hand flat on the registrar's sheets, straightening them — the realm's gesture for a settled matter, but *on somebody else's records*; **(2)** the question, and the lettering team must set it plainly: **who. Give me the fourth cell**; **(3)** the founder's answer: he gives her **everything he has** — the nine years of paper, the list, the last known road, and the one thing paper cannot buy; **(4)** the two of them, standing, and the page's last frame is **the two-fingered touch, inverted**: not her touching his record, but **his hand, flat, on the corner of her copy of the horn** — and the art team must not let it read as warmth; it reads as a man *swearing on a document he is not permitted to be named in*.
**Morgatha (flat, straightening his sheets, and this is the volume's hardest line and it is said without a tremor):** "\u2014 Then we're done with the *why*. …You've told me my brother was a lever. I've read the code since I was fourteen and I know what a lever is and I know the realm was *bent* right up to the moment I signed it straight again, so here's what we'll do, Founder. **Give me the fourth cell**, on paper, with the nine years of it, every road and every name you've got, and I'll not ask you for one word about my father or my house or the year." *(a beat)* "…And I'll take your *reading*, because you asked for it and because my realm answers that now. That's all you get from me. There's no forgiveness in this room, and I've not come for any, and if you've got a *mind* that wants one you can take it up with my father, who is sitting in the sun in a coat he hadn't worn in a year, and he'll be *kinder* than me." *(a beat, and the art team must letter the last line small, because it is the volume's whole thesis about what the realm has become)* "…Sign the answer, Founder. Then sit down and eat something. You're grey to the *wrists* and somebody in this camp's got to go back out there."
**Gorakh (flat, and the man's single piece of unguarded speech in the entire volume, delivered to a table, not to her):** "\u2026Paper is not going to catch nine years of men on a road, captain. But I'll give you the paper, and I'll give you the *one* thing it can't buy, which is the truth about how they hunt: **they read**. They read the same rules the realm does, and they use them, and they've been nine years ahead of me because I keep trying to *stop* them with rules and they keep *arriving* with them." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him looking at Kharza for exactly one panel)* "…You brought a reader nine hundred miles. So did they. That's the whole of what I know."

## Page 5 — *The Old Eye*

### P5.1 — A man at the gate with a stick (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's turn arrives by *road*, exactly the way it should: at the gate, in the afternoon, a **very old cyclops** with a stick, a lamp, a satchel, and no appointment, who has been walking east from the old-forges since Chapter 23 and is *not* surprised to have arrived. **Oros the Old Eye**: the art team's design must be a *lifetime* older than Gorakh — white-eyed, patient, one good hand, a coat gone soft with age, and the lamp he carries is the same kind that hangs in the map-room of the old-forges (so the reader recognises the *trade* before the *man*). Four frames: **(1)** the gate-clerk, out of his booth, and this time — for the first time in the chapter — **the camp stops working**: clerks at the windows, the troll surgeon at the ward door, samplers in the yard; **(2)** the registrar, at a run from the map-room, arriving breathless, and the art team's craft: the book-keeper's face tells the reader everything about who this old man is to the founder; **(3)** the founder, coming out of the hearing room, stopping dead in the yard with his coat still on one arm; **(4)** **the walk across the yard**, drawn in one long horizontal panel: the old man and the founder, thirty paces apart, nobody in between, the camp silent, and **Oros does not hurry** and the founder does not move.
**The gate-clerk (quiet, to the party, in the yard, and the clerk is the reader's surrogate):** "\u2026That's *Oros*. The Old Eye. Founders' teacher — the one that taught the first four of them to sight a line." *(a beat)* "…He came here once, nine years ago. He got to that chair — the one the founder's in — and he turned round and went home without saying a word, and the founder has *never once* mentioned it, and *everybody in this camp knows the date*."

### P5.2 — Oros's line (half page)
**Art:** And the line the volume has held since Ch. 23, spent at last — **03a's line, verbatim, unaltered** — and the art team's instructions are absolute: **no music, no framing for grandeur, no close-up before it and no reaction shot after it for four panels.** Two men in a camp yard, one holding a stick and a lamp, one with a coat over his arm, and eight words. Four frames: **(1)** Oros, stopping three paces away, and looking at the founder the way a mason looks at a wall; **(2)** the founder saying *"Master"* — and the art team must draw the word as small as a word can be drawn; **(3)** Oros speaking the line, and the lettering team must set it in the plainest type in the chapter, no emphasis on any word, **because the sentence has no emphasis: it is a statement about teaching**; **(4)** and the yard after it: the camp clerks at their windows, the ward door, the samplers, the party, the grey beyond the stockade's north wall, and the two old men standing in the middle of it, **not touching, not reconciled, not done**, and nobody in the frame knows whether the founder has just been forgiven, indicted, or *transferred to another class*.
**Gorakh (flat, and this is all he says first):** "\u2026Master."
**Oros (flat, unhurried, exactly the 03a line, verbatim and unaltered — and this is the volume's knife):** "**I taught you to** see *the wound. I never taught you to call the* patient *the* doctor." *(and then, because a man who has walked three hundred miles has one more sentence and it is *his own*, and the art team must letter it as a separate small panel)* "\u2026I've got a chair in your map-room and a fortnight to spare, and I'd like to see the soundings. Not the charts. The *book*."

## Page 6 — *The Book*

### P6.1 — Ninety-nine years of soundings (half page)
**Art:** The map-room, that evening, and the chapter's *centre of gravity* — and the art team's staging rule: **the least dramatic page in the volume, and the one the reader must not be able to put down.** Two old cyclopes at a table with **the survey book**: a huge, cloth-bound, ruled ledger, one hundred years of daily soundings on the wound — depth, colour, drift, temperature, and, in the later years, a column the founder added himself: ***what the grey did today***, with entries in a man's hand that get shorter and stranger as the decades go by. Oros reads it with a lamp and a lens; Gorakh sits across from him with his hands flat; and nobody else in the camp comes in, and the art team must draw the *doorway* with the whole camp's silhouettes in it, keeping away. Four frames: **(1)** the book open, and legible sample entries, from year one to year ninety-nine; **(2)** Oros reading the last twenty years, and — the page's turn — **stopping at one entry**; **(3)** the entry itself, and the art team must set it exactly: *year ninety-seven, day two hundred and ten: it moved four fingers overnight. The maps are all wrong. I have not told the camp. — G.*; **(4)** the two old men, and Oros's question.
**Oros (flat, finger on the entry, and the sage's question is the whole chapter's hinge):** "\u2014 Ninety-nine years of this, and you never sent a *copy* to anybody. Not to the old-forges. Not to the courts. Not to a single realm on this road. …Why not, boy?"
**Gorakh (flat, and the answer is not an excuse, and the art team must draw him the way he has been drawn all chapter — a working man at a table):** "\u2014 Because I *did*, Master. Year eleven: I sent the first copy to the old-forges and they filed it under *disputes*, because the wound was a family's land to them and the survey was a claim. Year nineteen: I sent the second to the Hundred Courts and it came back with a *fee* attached and a request for the surveyor's licence. Year thirty-three: the Silk. They *catalogued* it." *(a beat, and the art team must draw his hands turning palm-up, flat, on the table, and nothing else)* "…So I stopped sending copies and started *arriving* with them. **END OF CHAPTER 28.**

*The first cell's founder came back from the far side of the wound in a plain coat with a scoop on his shoulder, and the volume gave him eleven pages and did not let him win a single one, and did not let anybody defeat him either, which is the harder trick. He conceded the complaint and paid restitution out of the purse that pays his samplers, because nine realms had filed against his camp and the tenth had sent a *reader*; he told Tarkhan's sister the truth, which was not that he had killed her brother but that he had *used* him — a lever, taken from a dead man's debt, because the Barrens had the only procedure left in the world that could invent a law in a week, and because the wound does not wait for a century of argument. He did not apologise for the lever; he apologised for the chair; and the woman who had carried that count for six years sat across from him in a room with four chairs and let the count stop. Then an old man with a lamp and a stick walked three hundred miles to say eight words to the student he raised, and the volume's whole argument about teaching came down to a statement about who gets called the doctor. And the reading happened — not in a bowl, not in a tavern, but in a stockade yard, with menders and samplers and clerks holding their caps, because the realm's youngest reader stood up on a sample-box and read her realm's newest law to the people who had been standing on the edge of the world's wound for a hundred years and had never once told anybody. The answer the party left behind is the smallest and most dangerous thing in the volume: not an alliance, not a treaty, not a reading-post on the shore, but a **clerk's desk outside a gate** and a rule rewritten to fit the world — the reader may refuse, because a reading that cannot be refused is a door, and a reader who may refuse is a wall. In the founder's case, which rode up the road on a cart, is a hundred years of soundings and the names of four people in the front, because a survey is only as good as the people who took it, and nobody in nine realms had ever asked who they were. The grey moved four fingers overnight, one year ago, and the man who wrote that down did not tell his own camp. The party walked back up out of the seam with both of those facts in the same bag.*
**The record's cards are hide, and the newest one has, cut small below the law-lines, a mark with nothing under it that is the exact shape of a road on a forearm.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 28)

- **The founder must be drawn small, tired, and working — the whole chapter depends on it** (P1, P6). Gorakh the Scarred: a cyclops in his sixties, one eye, a scar across the brow, a plain grey coat with a **mender's cloth sewn over the pocket**, a sampler's straps, and a **long-handled scoop** that lives on a rack with eleven worn-out predecessors, none of them discarded. He pours tea without being asked, cannot stop working with a pencil while he talks, and reacts to the realm's filing with **relief**. **Never draw him with a throne, a smile, a monologue pose, or a raising of voice; he is a man at a table and that is the horror.**
- **The chapter is a *table*, and every knife in it is a sentence** (P2\u2013P4). The hearing room: **a table, four chairs, no bench** (contrast the seam court's bench, Ch. 27). His answer to the complaint is **restitution out of the camp's own funds, doubled, plus the survey's crossing-roll, plus a signed letter*** — ***this camp was wrong at the third crossing, and it was wrong lawfully, and the two are the same problem*** — and the registrar's book-keeper's face must show the *cost* (a month of the samplers' wages). The **blank signature line** first drawn on Roderick's Ch. 27 panel is **filled here by the founder's own hand**, title lettered as small as possible: *Gorakh, of the first cell.*
- **The truth about Tarkhan is delivered by the registrar, not the founder** (P3.1) — a copy-ledger, a muster-roll, and a death-notice from **the fourth cell**, which went off the sheet nine years ago (no chart, no sample, no chain of custody) and killed for the first time in the second spring. The registrar's own confession — *he asked for the debt to be moved, I typed it* — must be drawn flatly; his register is civil-servant and it is the only truth-telling office in the camp. **The fourth cell is named and left uncaptured**: three weeks ago four of them signed into the **old-forges** map-room as *pilgrims from the Rift*, asked for the holdings of the Barrens and the seam, and were given a reading and left. **Their pattern, canon from here:** *they take nothing anybody didn't hand them.*
- **The founder's justification must be an argument, lettered as a list, and it must be *right about the wound*** (P3.2): nine realms, four hundred years of treaties; the wound a mile wide, growing a finger a decade; no realm moves while its own house is in order; therefore a realm must be made to write a law it did not mean to write — and the Barrens had the **Ultravote**, a procedure that can invent law in a week, and the wound is nobody's clan grievance, so **the hole had to be opened inside the realm's own honour**. His canon line: *"I did not have your brother killed … I used him."* No apology for the lever; **one apology, seven words, for the year** — and the art team must not let the panel linger.
- **The stop (P4.1) is the volume's fourth and last, and it obeys every rule**: one frame, full width and height, **no dialogue, no count in the corner, no Titanvein, no bloom, no SFX**, flat from the room at table level. Everything in it is *ordinary* — a cold kettle, three sheets of paper, four chairs, the gate-clerk forgotten in the doorway — and the one thing that must be legible is **the band of grey across the top of the frame**, the art team's whole thesis in a composition: *the wound is in the window and not in the room.* **Morgatha is not crying, not shaking, not reaching.** She is doing arithmetic that comes out equal on both sides and wrong.
- **Oros's line is 03a's, verbatim and unaltered, and it must be delivered in a \u200bcamp yard with a stick and a lamp** (P5.2): *"I taught you to see the wound. I never taught you to call the patient the doctor."* \u2014 plus eight more words of his own (*I'd like to see the soundings; not the charts, the book*). **No music in the framing, no close-up before, no reaction shot for four panels after**; the camp's silence is the reaction. **Canon: Oros stays at the camp** \u2014 a chair, a lamp, the survey book, and eleven years of a student to argue with \u2014 and he appears in **one** panel thereafter in this chapter (watching the signing from a doorway) and is not drawn again.
- **The survey book, and the entry that matters** (P6.1): one hundred years of daily soundings on the wound \u2014 depth, colour, drift, temperature, and the founder's own column, ***what the grey did today*** \u2014 with entries that shorten and strange as the decades pass. **Year ninety-seven, day two hundred and ten: *it moved four fingers overnight. The maps are all wrong. I have not told the camp. \u2014 G.*** Viv's summary is the coda's brief: *the grey moved four fingers overnight and he did not tell his own camp.*
- **What the realm asks for, and gets, must be *smaller* than the offer** (P7\u2013P8): Viv's five-clause document, lettered legibly in her hand so the reader can argue with it \u2014 (i) one reader and one clerk; (ii) **the clerk's office is the deed-register, first: no deed, no door**; (iii) ***the reader may refuse; a reading that cannot be refused is a door; a reader who may refuse is a wall***; (iv) **the post is paid by the realm, not the camp, and the survey's books are open to the realm's clerk for as long as the post stands**; (v) **the post is not an alliance, and the realm's answer to the first cell's crimes stands unanswered until a circle hears it.** Kharza's objection (*are we wrong, then?*) and Viv's correction (*a reading may not be refused at a **door**; a clerk is not a door \u2014 a counter can be bought*) is the volume's final piece of law and must be drawn as a lawyer thinking in front of a nineteen-year-old.
- **The realm's first seal, canon from here** (P8.2): the Barrens has no seal \u2014 **Morgatha presses the debt-horn's own worn mouth into the wax**, so the realm's mark on its first treaty with anybody is **the impression of a horn**. Draw it, and use it in every document after this one.
- **The final reading is in a yard, and it must not be applauded** (P9.1): the camp has **no hall**, so the reading happens between gate and ward, with clerks, samplers in grey-booted coats, the troll surgeon with his hands in the wash-water, four ward-patients carried out in chairs *because the old cyclops with the wrapped hands asked to hear it*, and the **samplers picking up their boot-caps and standing** at the end, and the **clerks closing their ledgers**, which nobody in that camp has ever done during a reading. Kharza's addition to the law is hers, not the clause's, and the lettering team must set it as a separate small panel: *"you've all been standing on the edge of my realm's worst debt for a hundred years, and not one of you ever told us. \u2026Now you've got a reader. Start with the book."*
- **Kharza's court-work must be paid off the page and not by her** (P9.2): two clerks bring the survey's older ledgers and ask whether the realm will read *those* too; **Kharza doesn't know and says so**; the clerk says *then we'll ask your clerk* \u2014 the realm's procedure **working without her**, which is the whole of Vol. 5's institutional argument. The samplers then gather at the north corner at dusk and read **their own log entries aloud**, awkward and private, for the first time in a hundred years.
- **Azrael's line is the chapter's mirror and must be the cleanest thing in it** (P9.2): *"I broke one word, on a page, for a town \u2026 he's what I'd have been if I'd had the paperwork. \u2026It's not a comfort. It's a warning, and I don't know who it's for."* **No glow, no halo, no wings, ever.**
- **The fourth cell, on paper** (P10): the founder's nine years \u2014 a strip-map of the mine-road east of the pass in the layered-hide style; **eleven dated sightings**, each with the founder's pencil margins (*no sample, no chain, no chart*); four confirmed members; two captured and lost to a Silk court; and **one recent report, three weeks old**: four of them signed into the **old-forges** as *pilgrims from the Rift*, asked for the holdings of the Barrens and the seam, were given a reading, and left. **Morgatha's response is drawn, not spoken**: she reads the entire file in order, then **cuts the fourth cell's road into her own forearm** below the clause \u2014 small, plain, a *note* and not a law \u2014 and leaves **one horn-sliver** on the table. No dialogue on the page; the only lettered text is the file's tab: **FOURTH CELL \u2014 OPEN \u2014 ELEVEN SIGHTINGS \u2014 CUT OVER?** (the last two words in her hand).
- **Rule 6 (Roderick)**: P3.1, **exactly one panel, no dialogue** \u2014 at the hearing-room door, holding a page out of the founder's nine years: the fourth cell's **last sighting report**, and on it, drawn plain and legible, a **witness-line filled in by a clerk whose signature block reads I. VANE**, because the fourth cell's paper has been witnessed by Roderick's brother's office, and **nothing in this chapter explains it, and nobody in the frame comments.**
- **Rule 7**: **no new named characters.** Gorakh, the registrar, the gate-clerk, the troll surgeon, the old cyclops with wrapped hands, the two clerks, the samplers and **the founder's daughter** (the sampler in the grey-booted coat) are all drawn and **she is never named and never given a line about her father**. The **four** who read with him are paid *emotionally only*: two on the far shore of the grey, one in the ground at the far camp, one in his yard with a sample-box \u2014 and their **names are written in the front of the survey book**, which the realm now owns, and the volume never reads them out.
- **The count and the card.** Corner counts: **twenty-five** (opening) → **twenty-seven** (P7.2) → **twenty-eight** (P9.1) → **twenty-nine** (P10.2) → **thirty** (P12.1). **One stop: P4.1.** The card (P12.2) is the seventh **hide** card; its own movement is **a fourth line cut below the clause-lines on the card's left edge, unconnected to the clause, with nothing under it** \u2014 the exact shape of the road the captain cut into her arm (P10.2), and **no document ever connects them.** Caption: ***Here meet the named.***
- **What Ch. 28 must not do** (reservation list, checked): **no Titanvein**; **no stone-song / no bloom** (`#e07a3f` zero, notes included); the **sea's first line** stays Vol. 6's (**Nereia has no dialogue at all**; she is at the well with her flask and her markings); **Wren's 03a line** unspent; **Kaelen gets one silent frame** (P9.1, at the back of the yard, coat open \u2014 and P12.1's single backward glance) and **no line**; the Scar-Reading Chord unspent; the white never restaged; the *empty* inside the mountain; Orvane's garden; Khazur's seal-or-fight; the **twins' letter** (Fenn's column stays empty); the **Re-Oath vote**; and **no fight** \u2014 the fourth cell is not found in this chapter, and the founder is neither defeated nor redeemed.
