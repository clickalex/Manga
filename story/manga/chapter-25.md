# AURELION · Volume 5 — Chapter 25: "The Walk for the Holding"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc E: "The One Throat" — the duel that decides a school, and the choice the volume was built for.** Two minotaurs walk the maze for the holding: one names an *office* at the gate, the other names a *school* — and by the realm's newest procedure (a lawyer's three terms, cut into a gatepost four days ago) the words they choose decide what they win. The bigger man loses by taking the flattering clause three times, and the realm's rule gives him exactly what he asked for, which is a *classroom*, and the roar that goes up under the well-lip is the volume's first cheer and it is for a *teacher*. Then dusk, and the gate, and the realm does the thing it has been putting off since Volume 1: it reads Morgatha's own exile out loud, tells her the word she asked for ten years ago is in the horn's margin, and offers her her stone back. **Her answer is the Chapter and the volume's turn** — she refuses the stone, refuses to be read as finished, keeps her brother's oath *unfinished* on her own arm when the realm offers to carry it, and takes the road: because a law is only a law where it has been *read*, and because a crime without a circle is still a lawful hand. What the realm keeps is the war and the fords; what it *sends* is the thing a realm of cut arms can send — **readers** — one to walk with the Nine, and a belt of them posted at every crossing that once let a stranger in with a courteous ask.
> **Cast:** **The team** — Morgatha (**the choice; the stone refused; the oath kept**), **Viv** (whose three terms govern a duel now, and who says so), **Ulgan** (the clock, and the delegation's consent), **Kaelen** (one line, and it is the kindest thing anybody says to her), **Fenn** (**his ledger's fourth movement**: he closes the Barrens' page with forty-one days on it and a column kept empty on purpose), **Sprocket** (auditing a school), **Bramble**, **Azrael** (one line, and it is about *oaths kept badly for good reasons*), **Nereia** (**no dialogue** — the markings point, and the reader knows where), Marginalia (the cart, released, and filing its last dispatch), and **Roderick** (**his one silent panel** — at the well-lip, watching a realm make a crime out of his institution's best habit). **The Barrens** — **Tauron** (the centre held), **Taurus** (**the loser, and the realm's first schoolmaster**), **Maze** (the keeper, calling turns), **Horn** (the record), **Ulrik the Unbroken** (**a coat, a road west, and arithmetic finished**), **Khura** (the horn's keeper, and one line that is not forgiveness), **Durgan the Unbent** (the office, and it keeps books now), **Urzun the Red** (the stone held out), **Grom · Ghorak · Drog** (the council's asks), **Ura · Targa · Ripple · Flow · Dart · Kharza · Kha** (the belt of readers, and the one who walks with the Nine), **One-Eye** (a room that cannot refuse, and a clerk in the doorway), and the household of Tarkhan (the nephew, the goat, and a child with a slate).
> **Locations / threads:** the maze's **well-lip** (the whisper-loft, where the realm watches the centre from above) → the centre chamber (a duel, a giving, and a school's first day) → the gate at dusk (the council's two asks, and a choice) → the surety-hall's door (a bolt, and a bracket) → the pass at dawn (the reading-off, and two roads).
> **Arc beat:** The duel is the chapter's *foreground* and its argument: the maze's rule is that everybody loses, the loss is carved, and the maze goes to the loser — and Ch. 24 added a lawyer's three terms to it, one of which is *the walker names what was lost, in words, and it goes in the record*. So when the two minotaurs stand at their gates and name their stakes out loud, Tauron says *an office* and Taurus says *a school* — and the realm's newest law, four days old, quietly makes the *words* the stake. Taurus is the better fighter on the day and wins every exchange he can reach; the maze then takes him apart with his own appetite, three times, using the code's own contested clauses as bait, and he arrives at the centre's outer ring standing inside a wall that says *the holder answers*. Tauron — who has been walking quietly, reading, since before Taurus was born — is already at the bare wall with his hand on the claim-stone, and does the only unkind thing he has ever done in this book: he offers the fight anyway. Taurus looks at the wall, and at his own hands, and concedes **in words, on the record**, because the realm has taught him in a week that the words are the thing. Then the giving, and the realm's whole philosophy in one afternoon: the winner takes the **centre**; the loser receives the **maze** — which is to say, the school — and the man who filed a claim because he wanted the fight stands in the lamplight with nine hundred faces over his head and gets a *classroom*, and grins, and takes it, and the roar under the well-lip is the loudest sound in Volume 5. The chapter's second movement is the **council at the gate**: the realm asks its captain for two things — to lead nothing, and to take everything back — the war-party (west, to the fords, with her father in the coat he has not worn in a year) and the *stone* (her clan's captaincy, and the exile read out loud at last: she asked the circle, ten years ago, to name a crime its grammar did not have, was refused, and answered by cutting her own sentence into her own arm — *I will stand in the circle the day the circle has a word for him* — and left). The realm now has the word, in a horn's margin, put there by a vampire lawyer four days ago, and it thinks that is close enough. **It is not close enough for her**, and this is the choice: the note is not a law; a crime needs a *circle*; and a realm that has just taught itself the value of being readable has not yet learned the second half — that a law is only a law where it has been *read*. So she declines her stone, declines the realm's offer to finish her brother's oath into the record, keeps the unfinished clause on her own arm exactly as it has been for six years, and takes the road: the Nine east toward the sea, the war west to the fords, and between them the thing this realm can actually export — **readers**. One walks with the Nine: **Kharza**, the code's youngest student, carrying a travel-horn cut by the realm's own carver. The rest are posted at the crossings — nine doors, nine arms, one per clan, beginning with the map-room that cannot say no, where a **clerk** now sits in the doorway and the oldest rule in the volume gets its answer: *a reading may not be refused* — so the realm will be *read at*, first, politely, at every door from now until the Re-Oath.
> **Motifs advanced:** the gift (**the maze goes to the loser; the stone goes back to the giver; and a realm's greatest export is its readers**), the record (**a *refusal* entered as the archive's first chosen-unpaid entry; a dissent; a note**), the door (**the request-shelf gets a clerk; the bolt goes back on its bracket**), the count (**twelve… thirteen, one stop, and the rest-bar's first notch**), the reading (**the realm's farewell is a reading; the law travels as people who can be read**).
> **Not in this chapter:** **Titanvein** (spent in Ch. 24; barred here and for the rest of the volume) · the stone-song (no bloom; the mountain is a thousand miles away) · **Oros** (off-page entirely — his 03a line waits for Gorakh's chapter) · **the four companions** · the sea's first line (**Nereia silent**, and her markings do the work) · Wren's line · Kaelen's pilot uses · the Scar-Reading Chord · the white · the *empty* inside the mountain · Orvane's garden · Khazur's seal-or-fight · **Gorakh** (a name on a road, never a person on a page) · and **the Re-Oath vote itself** — promised, dated a realm away, and not held.

## Page 1 — *Two Gates, Two Words*

### P1.1 — The well-lip (half page)
**Art:** The maze on the morning of the walk, and the art team's new architecture for it, drawn once and reused: **the well-lip.** The centre chamber has a **roof-shaft** — the maze is cut down into red rock, so above the bare-walled room there is a ring of standing-room on the surface, the **whisper-loft**, reached by a stair from the gate, with nine hundred people on it and a rope rail, looking down; the shaft's edges are cut with the names of champions, oldest first, *and the newest cut is not a champion's name* — it is a clause, and beneath it, two strokes (the holes), because Ch. 23's walk is now part of the building's face. Down in the lamplight, the centre: the bare wall, the claim-stone at its foot, a lamp, a bucket, a rag. Up on the lip: the realm — kids with their legs through the rail; the grandmother and her dough (fourth appearance, still no name); the eleven with their wrist-words; the nine clan chiefs; **Durgan** with his hands behind his back, because there is nothing to voice yet; and the team at the rail with the best view, because **Morgatha holds the maze** and a holder gets a rail. And the chapter's framing device laid in on the first page, cut in the corner: the count — **twelve.**
**Bramble (flat, at the rail, to the reader's question, which is a fair one):** "— They built a *theatre* over it. …Three hundred years on nine realms' roads and I've never met anybody who put the school *under* the audience and the fight *inside* the law. I'd call it mad if it hadn't just spent a week carving a clause into nine hundred forearms without one man bleeding for it." *(a beat)* "…I *like* it here. That's the worrying part."

### P1.2 — The words at the gates (half page)
**Art:** The two gates at opposite ends of the maze — and the art team's staging rule: **do not draw them as opponents yet.** Four frames: **(1)** **Tauron** at the north gate, in the plain coat he wears for duty, a single cloth wrap on his hands, no weapon; he is old, he is unhurried, and the reader should notice that half the fighters in this realm have learned the walls from him; **(2)** **Taurus** at the south gate, stripped to the waist, taped knuckles, both shoulders oiled, and the art team's detail: he is *laughing* — he is having the best week of his life; **(3)** **Maze** at the slot between them, with the horn on his knee and **Horn** beside him going over the day's cut, and — the new thing, invented four days ago and cut into both gateposts where the queue can read it — **the walker's term**: *the walker names what he risks, in words, at the gate, and it goes in the record*; **(4)** the two gateposts, lettered legibly, because the reader must be able to go back and check.
**Maze (flat, between the gates, and the keeper is running a duel the way a registrar runs a wedding):** "— Walkers. Rules. One: the walls are *read*, not fought; you may carry a blade and you may not use one instead of reading. Two: the champion never leaves the centre; today there is no champion, so the centre's the *prize*. Three: everybody loses, the loss is cut, and the maze goes to the loser. …Four, and it's the captain's lawyer's, and it's the newest line on this gate, so read it slow when you walk past: **name what you're risking, out loud, and the horn keeps it.**" *(a beat)* "…North gate."
**Tauron (flat, no theatre, and the words have been waiting in him since Ch. 23):** "— **An office.** I keep the centre. If I lose it, I lose the thing I've done with my hands for forty years and I'll still walk out of here with the maze, because that's the code, and I've *read* the code."
**Taurus (loud, cheerful, hands open, and this is the page's hinge and nobody in the frame knows it yet):** "— **A school.** The labyrinth's law-school's been taught by nobody for a season. Whoever walks out of here holds it. …I want the *centre*, and I'll take the school with it, and I'll tell you the truth, keeper: I've wanted to *beat* him since I could hold a fist."
**Viv (quiet, at the rail, above them, and the lawyer's face does the chapter's first piece of work without moving — the art team: both eyebrows):** "— He named it. …Under my third term. **Nobody in this realm has understood yet what we did to it.**"

## Page 2 — *The Reading-Match*

### P2.1 — Two ways through a building (2/3 page)
**Art:** The walk, drawn as the chapter's set piece and the art team's best chance of the volume to make *reading* thrilling: **two men moving through the same law in opposite directions**, cut against each other panel for panel, above/below. Four pairs, and the reader should be able to track them without a caption: **(1)** Tauron stops at the first contradiction — the third holding against the fifth — and reads it *twice*, mouth moving, one hand on the wall; Taurus is thirty strides ahead, and the art team must show what he does at the same turn: he reads it once, shrugs — *fine* — and takes the *shorter* clause, because the shorter clause is the one that lets him move. **(2)** Tauron takes the long way round a clause about **guest-right** (the wall's own words: *the guest may not be turned from the fire*), because the short way's corridor is cut with a clause he does not accept; Taurus takes the short way and is *right* about it, and the walls let him, and that is the maze's cruelty: it is not unfair, it is *exact*. **(3)** **The well-lip**: seen from above, the two walkers are two moving points in a cut-out page, and the realm is reading them the way the realm reads everything — out loud — with old fighters calling the turns from memory and kids getting the clauses wrong and being corrected by the eleven. **(4)** Two frames of the maze's *floor*: the cuts in it. The art team's device, and it must be invented here: **the maze's floors carry the code's *exceptions*** — the clauses where the code contradicts itself are cut into the floor at the turns, worn smooth by ten thousand feet, so a walker who looks down learns what the wall above him is arguing about. Tauron walks with his eyes *down*. Taurus walks with his eyes *up*, at the exits.
**Maze (flat, calling from the rim of the well, and the keeper narrates nothing and judges nothing):** "— North's at the fifth turn and he's reading. South's at the third and he's *fast*. …Call it, brothers; that's what the rail's for."
**Azrael (quiet, at the rail, one line, and it is his register and it is about oaths kept badly for good reasons):** "…He's going to lose to a *wall*. I broke a vow on a page and it took me two hundred years to understand the page wasn't the thing holding me. This one's about to find out in an afternoon."

### P2.2 — The flattering clause (half page)
**Art:** And the turn, three times, drawn as three temptations and three easy doors: the art team's rule is that **nothing supernatural happens.** The maze does not glow, does not speak, does not punish. It simply *offers* — the corridor is there, the clause over it is genuinely in the code, and taking it costs exactly what the code says it costs. Four frames: **(1)** *the hungry may take what the closed hand holds* (the ford-law) over a corridor that is warm and downhill and leads toward the centre — and Taurus takes it, and the corridor is *right*, and he gains forty strides; **(2)** *a debt may be read at the stone* over a passage with a *stone* in it, and Taurus reads his own champion's claim out of habit and steps past — correctly; **(3)** *the word answers for the man* over a corridor whose floor-cut says, small, in the exceptions: *where the man answers for the word* — and Taurus, flushed, four strides from the centre-ring, reads the floor, *sees it*, decides the exceptions are for clerks and lawyers, and takes the corridor anyway, because he is winning and the corridor is there; **(4)** **the centre-ring's outer corridor**: the walls here are the oldest work in the maze, cut by hands in a hurry with a war on, one line to a clause, and the line that runs the full length of the passage at eye height reads — legibly, and the art team must set it large and plain and *not* dramatic — ***the holder answers***. And Taurus walks into it with his shoulders, because it is a corridor and he is a fighter, and the floor under his feet drops four fingers into a worn channel and the channel's cut says the rest of the sentence, which is *…for what he holds.* And the corridor's far end is a wall. The art team must draw the *dead end* as exactly what it is: a wall with ten thousand handprints on it, and a man standing in front of it with his chest heaving and his palms flat on stone, in a room that does not care, hearing, from around the corner, the lamplight of the centre, and one pair of flat feet, arriving, unhurried, on schedule.

## Page 3 — *The Offered Fight*

### P3.1 — Tauron at the bare wall (half page)
**Art:** The centre chamber, in the shaft's light, from *above* — the art team's key shot of the duel, and it must be the reverse of every arena panel in the franchise: looking **down** a well at two old fighters and a stone, with nine hundred faces around the rim. **Tauron** has his hand flat on the claim-stone and he is *not* posing: he is standing exactly as Ch. 23 drew him, arms folded, breathing normally, with the wrap still on his hands — because he read seven turns and never threw a punch. **Taurus** comes in through the centre's door, hands down, no rush; the two of them in the lamplight; and the realm above not making one sound. And the chapter's most important line, which the art team must not decorate: Tauron *offers* the fight.
**Tauron (flat, and this is the only unkind thing he has done in twenty-five chapters, and it is delivered without unkindness, which is worse):** "— You named a school. …You'll get it; that's the code and I'd cut my hand off before I argued with it. But you came here for *me*, and you're four fingers from a wall, and I've got all afternoon. **Do you want the fight, or the lesson?**"
**Taurus (flat, breathing hard, hands at his sides, and the art team's rule: no shame, no comedy — just a man doing arithmetic in a corridor):** "…The fight. …But I'd lose it *here*, with the wall behind me, and in nine years some kid's going to stand on the lip and hear how I took the short corridor." *(a beat)* "— So: **I yield.** In words. On the record. Wasn't the wall. Was the *reading*." *(a beat, and then, and this is where the realm decides it loves him)* "…And when I'm teaching, that's the first thing I'm teaching."

### P3.2 — The carve, and the giving (half page)
**Art:** The giving, staged as **procedure and then joy** — and the art team must sequence the page so the *paperwork* comes first and the *roar* comes last. Four frames: **(1)** **Horn** at the rail above with the horn on his knee, cutting while Taurus is still speaking — the realm's archival voice, unchanged in three chapters (*one line per statement, and the date, and the words he used*); **(2)** **Maze** at the mouth of the centre, declaring it flatly into the shaft with the realm listening; **(3)** **Tauron**, putting his hand back on the claim-stone — the office resumed, and the art team must draw him taking *no* pleasure in the moment, because he has held this stone for forty years and the stone does not care who holds it; **(4)** **Taurus**, and the realm's rule delivered straight to his face — and then the roar: **nine hundred people on a rock lip, cheering a *schoolmarm*.** The art team must draw the lip's reaction as *delight*, not irony: they are not laughing *at* him. In this realm a man who reads badly and says so with his own mouth is a **man who paid for the lesson**, and they have been raised on that the way other realms are raised on kings.
**Maze (flat, into the shaft, so the realm hears it):** "— **Walked: two. Result: the north walker holds the centre, and the south walker declares his own loss at the last turn, in words. Loss: cut. And the code's second gift stands: the maze goes to the loser.** …Taurus. The school's yours. The lesson's yours. Take the lamp."
**Horn (flat, cutting, and this is the realm's archive speaking — the third of the marks the whole realm will read tomorrow):** "— I've got your words, both of you. …North said *an office*. South said *a school*. Listen, brothers, and I'll read it back: **the man who asked for the school got the school, and the man who asked for the office got the office, and the realm didn't have to give either of them anything it didn't already have in writing.**" *(a beat)* "…That's your new law, is what that is. Four days old, and it just settled a fight that ten thousand losses couldn't."
**Morgatha (flat, from the rail, as the holder, doing the last piece of paperwork herself, and the art team must draw her *not* looking at either man — she is looking at the two gateposts and the newest line on them):** "— The holding's transferred. …I gave you the maze in the fourth turn and I've been holding it since, and I've done exactly one useful thing with it: put a lawyer's terms on a gate." *(a beat)* "…Tauron. It's yours. **Cut the second gatepost the same way and let the next fighter read his own terms before he lies to himself.**"

## Page 4 — *The First Class*

### P4.1 — The school, day one (half page)
**Art:** The maze's centre chamber, three days later, and the art team's most charming page of the volume: **a law-school in a well.** Benches carried down the stair; a board the size of a door with the maze's whole disputed-code map chalked on it; a lamp hung from a hook; and **Taurus** — shoulders still taped, standing where Tauron stood, *terrible* at this and *trying*, with the realm's youngest up on the lip with their chins on the rail. Four frames: **(1)** Taurus mid-lecture, having just gotten the fifth holding wrong, being *corrected by a nine-year-old on the rail* — and the art team must draw him taking the correction *well*, which is the whole of his arc in one panel; **(2)** **Kha** — 03b's youngest student — at the board with the chalk, drawing the ford-law's exception the way the code's own cut script draws it, taking over from the schoolmaster and being *right*; **(3)** the class itself: two kids, three veterans, a river-caste troll mender sitting in the back with a toolbox (he is *learning the code* because his bridges keep getting argued about), and **one of the eleven** — the old woman with paper arms — who has the first word of the new clause on her wrist and has come to learn what the rest of it says; **(4)** **Tauron** in the *background* of the frame, at the claim-stone, arms folded, listening to Taurus teach, and — the art team's detail, never remarked on — *writing nothing down*, because he has all of it, and always has.
**Taurus (flat, mid-lesson, and the realm's newest schoolmaster is being corrected and taking it):** "— …Right. **Kha's right and I'm wrong.** Third holding doesn't beat the ford-law; it goes *first*, and there's a difference, and the difference is a *building* we're all standing inside. Now say the difference out loud, all of you, because a rule you can't *say* is a rule you're going to break by accident, and then somebody's going to have to hold your *house* for it."
**Sprocket (flat, at the back of the class with a slate on his knees, auditing the realm's newest institution on its first day, and the franchise's record-keeper has found his register at last):** — Attendance: twenty-six. Corrections accepted from the gallery: two. …I'm entering it as *taught*, keeper. Your realm's got a school now, and somebody in this party is going to have it **written down that it happened** — on the day, with the names of the children who knew better than the master.
**Maze (quiet, at the back of the class, to Viv, and this is the keeper's whole contribution to the volume's argument):** "…Four days ago he wanted to hit me because I wouldn't let him fight on a Tuesday. Look at him. …Captain's lawyer: your terms made a *schoolmaster* out of the most impatient man in the Barrens, and you did it with three sentences on a gate, and I'll be reading them out to my own grandchildren."

### P4.2 — One lesson for the road (half page)
**Art:** And the page that *pays the chapter's next movement*: the nine walkers who will be posted at the crossings, in the school, on the lip, taking the realm's one crash-course in *being read at* — because the realm's newest institution is not an army, it is a **reading post**, and the art team must draw it as *training*, not ceremony. Four frames: **(1)** nine people on a bench with horn-slivers in their laps — **Ura** (healer, forearm still bearing the fresh cut), **Targa** (smith, mark-chisel on a belt-loop), **Ripple** (river-caste mender, big hands, tiny sliver), **Flow** (river-fisher, and the art team's joke: he brought food), **Dart** (goblin courier, up on a bench-back, feet swinging, and he is the only one who is *faster* than the training), and four more drawn and unnamed — one per clan, and **not one of them a fighter**; **(2)** **Durgan** teaching the one thing the Speaker can teach: *the office does not argue* — read the ask, read the code, read the *reason* into the horn, and then say the realm's word *whole*, and never once raise your voice; **(3)** **Ulgan** walking the line with her fist-drum, teaching them the time — *four beats and a rest* — because a realm's answer has a *rhythm* and a reader who rushes gets answered over; **(4)** **Kharza**, at the end of the bench, the youngest of them, with her travel-slate, being handed — and this is the page's last frame and the chapter's quiet promise — **the travel-horn**: a smaller horn, new-cut, the clause and its witness and its limit and its carrying on its face, made by **Horn's** own hands for a journey, and the art team must draw the carver handing it over *without ceremony*, because it is a **tool** going to work.
**Durgan (flat, to the nine, and the realm's office speaking to its new clerks):** "— You'll be asked, at every door you sit at, by somebody with good manners and a lawful ask. …You'll have three things and no more: the words, the reason, and the *right to say no to a room*." *(a beat)* "…And when they ask you what gives you the right, you'll say: **the debt that room handed a stranger came home to nine hundred arms, and the realm read it, and the realm has decided it will be read at, from now on, politely, forever.**"

## Page 5 — *What the Realm Asks*

### P5.1 — The gate at dusk (half page)
**Art:** The maze gate at dusk, and the art team's staging rule: **this is the volume's last big staging, so it must be built like the first one** (Ch. 22's debt-line, Ch. 23's gate) — the same slot, the same horn-slab above, the same grandmother's dough, the same lamplighter starting his shift *behind* the meeting, and the queue gone home. Present, in a loose half-ring: the council (**Grom**, **Ghorak**, **Drog**, **Durgan**, **Horn**, **Maze**, **Taurus** fresh from class); the team along the wall; **Khura**, apart, with the horn on its thong; **Urzun the Red**, with something in his hand that the art team must *not* reveal in the wide shot; and — walking down the pass-road, unhurried, in a **coat** the reader has never seen him wear, because it is a war-chief's coat, hanging now on a man it fits — **Ulrik the Unbroken**, out of doors, out of the pass, out of the year. And **the bolt**: not here. The bolt is behind him, leaning inside his son's doorframe, and the art team's rule for the whole page is **nobody mentions the coat.**
**Ulrik (flat, warm, at the gate, to his daughter, the first thing a free war-chief says):** "— I walked down. Four miles. I stopped twice, for the view, and once to be *rude* to a goat that came at me, and I'll tell you the truth: I've been out of that house for six hours and my knees have been out of it for a year." *(a beat, and then, flat, because he is a war-chief and the arithmetic is done)* "…And I've been doing sums, and the sums are: **the fords.** Nine crossings on that map. Every one of them's a room with a keeper in it and a courteous rule in it, and every one of them's a door into my realm. So the realm's going to want to put something at those doors, and I'm the man who's been sitting in a chair for a year thinking about doors."

### P5.2 — The stone, and the exile, read out (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's legal page, staged as **a public reading** — because that is how this realm humiliates nobody and settles everything. Four frames: **(1)** **Urzun**, stepping forward with the thing in his hand: a **clan stone** — the small one a captain carries, the same size as the one the reader saw in Ch. 22's ring, and the art team must draw it *cut with a name*, and the name is *hers*, and it has been cut there for ten years; **(2)** **Durgan**, reading her exile out of the horn — and the art team's craft: this is the first time the *record* holds anything about her, and the reader should feel the cold of it, because the realm has kept her sentence in horn for a decade the way it keeps everything; **(3)** the terms, lettered in the record's hand across the lower third, so the reader can check them: *exile, self-pronounced, at the circle, entered by her own request* — *condition: **the circle will have a word for the hand that keeps the code and takes a realm**; on that day the exile ends and the stone is hers*; **(4)** the realm's faces on the rim above the gate, listening, and — the art team's detail — several of them *already know*, and have known for ten years, and have said nothing, which is why the queue at the debt-line never once asked her for her name.
**Durgan (flat, reading, and this is the realm's voice at its most careful):** "— Ten years ago this captain stood in the bowl and asked the circle to name a thing the code had no word for: a hand that keeps every rule and uses them, and takes a realm with them, and owes nothing. The circle looked in its horn, and the horn had no word, and the circle said so — *truly*, stones; we did not lie to her, we said *we have no word* — and she said: **then I'll not stand in a circle that can't read what was done to my house**, and she cut the sentence into her own arm and walked out that door." *(a beat; and then the office does the thing offices can do, and the art team must not make it triumphant)* "…Captain. Four days ago a vampire lawyer put a word in this horn's margin, under the name of the cyclops who did it. It is a *note*, not a law. **It is also the word.** The council's read the condition. The condition's met. …The stone's yours."
**Urzun (flat, holding the stone out, and this is the volume's bluntest man being exact):** "— It's your clan's. You were the best captain this realm had and the code made you a *stranger* in it for ten years and I voted for that, and I'd vote for it again, because it's the code and I'm the man who keeps the code even when it costs me my own captain." *(a beat)* "…But the code's got a new line now, and I don't have to be that man any more, and I've been *waiting* to not be him, captain. Take the stone."

*The realm that keeps its law in its arms put two men in a building that is its own code and let them argue with it, and the argument was decided by four sentences a vampire lawyer cut into a gatepost four days before: the walker names what he risks, in words, and the record keeps it. One named an office and got an office. One named a school and got a school, which is a loss, which is a gift, and the realm cheered because it has been raised on the idea that a lesson you paid for is a lesson you own — so the second-best fighter in the Barrens teaches the code now, badly, in a well, with a nine-year-old correcting him, and will be the best teacher the realm has had, and the whole thing cost nobody a drop of blood. The same week, the realm read a captain's exile out of the horn it has been keeping her sentence in for ten years, told her the word she asked it for is now in the margin of that horn, and held her stone out to her. She looked at the shape of it and said the truest thing anybody has said in Volume 5: a note is not a law; a crime needs a circle; and a realm that has just learned to be *readable* has not yet learned the second half — that a law is only a law where it has been *read*. So she gave the stone back, and when the realm offered to take her brother's unfinished oath off her arm and put it in its horn — the kindest thing this culture can do, the whole of its new clause in one gesture — she laid her arm across the horn's mouth and said **no**, because the realm's care would have finished a sentence her brother never got to finish, and because the one other person at that gate who has said no to this realm this week said it for the same reason about her own brother's page. The horn got a new line out of it and the line is the volume's: **a thing can be kept and unfinished at the same time.** Then the road forked. West: four hundred arms and a war that is going to be conducted out of chairs, because the realm's answer to a crime that used courtesy was to put readers in every room and be read at, forever, politely. East: a cart, a young reader with a travel-horn, and a company of nine who carry a law that will not fit on paper and a horn that has never once been read in a realm with a sky the wrong colour. And at the top of the pass, a woman with an unfinished clause on her arm said the fourteenth thing to her brother, and the corner of the frame said fourteen, and the record's newest card was already being cut.*
**The record's cards are hide, and one of them has a rest-bar with a single notch in it, and nobody in the story ever explains what the notch is for.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 25)

- **The walk is a *reading-match*, and the art team must not draw it as a fight** (P2–P3). No blood, no gore, no impact frames, no wire-fu, no SFX except feet and breath: the duel's drama is **two men reading the same building differently**, cut panel-for-panel against each other. Tauron reads the walls *and the floors* (the new device: the maze's **exceptions** are cut into the floors at the turns) and takes the long way round; Taurus reads *exits*, and is fast, and is **right** twice — the maze is not unfair, it is **exact**, and it never once glows, speaks, or punishes. The turn: the flattering clause (*the hungry may take what the closed hand holds*), taken three times, ending in a corridor whose wall reads ***the holder answers*** and whose floor finishes the sentence (*…for what he holds*) and whose far end is a wall with ten thousand handprints. **Tauron's offered fight** (P3.1) is the only unkind thing he does in the book and it must be drawn *without* unkindness. **Taurus's yield is spoken, in words, on the record** — Ch. 23's yield-procedure, applied — and then the code's gift: the winner holds the **centre**, the loser receives the **maze (the school)**, and the realm's roar is **delight, not irony**: this realm believes a paid-for lesson is property.
- **The new architecture must be drawn once and reused: the well-lip** (P1.1) — the centre chamber's **roof-shaft** and the ring of standing-room above it (*the whisper-loft*, a stair from the gate, a rope rail, the shaft's edges cut with champions' names and, newest, Ch. 23's clause and its two strokes). This is how a realm watches a walk, and it gives the volume its best wide shot: **nine hundred faces looking down a well at the law.** The maze's **four gates** (north/south by the two walkers; walls' hands differ by gate), the gateposts carrying **the walker's term** (Viv's, four days old), and the centre's **claim-stone** are established here.
- **Viv's three terms now govern duels** (P1.2, P4.1) — and the realm *hasn't noticed it yet*, which is the joke and the plot: the walker names what he risks, in words, on the record, so the words become the stake. Viv's line at the rail (*"He named it. Under my third term."*) must be set flat and small. **The second gatepost gets cut** (P3.2, Morgatha's last act as holder). She transfers the holding to Tauron **on the page, as paperwork**, and the art team must draw her looking at the gateposts and not at the fighters.
- **The school's first day** (P4.1–P4.2) is the volume's charm-shot and must be warm without being cute: benches, a chalked board, a lamp on a hook, **Taurus being corrected by a nine-year-old on the rail and taking it well**; **Kha** (03b) at the board; a river-caste mender in the back row with a toolbox, because his bridges keep getting argued about; **one of the eleven** (the old woman with the first word on her wrist) learning what the rest of the clause says. **Tauron listening at the claim-stone, taking no notes.** The **nine readers** are trained here — **not one of them a fighter** — by **Durgan** (the words and the right to say no to a room) and **Ulgan** (the time: four beats and a rest); **Kharza** receives the **travel-horn** from **Horn**, without ceremony, because it is a **tool going to work**.
- **The choice must be staged like a stop, and the stop must be staged like nothing** (P7.1, P8.2, P9.1). P7.1's four frames deliberately let the reader believe she takes the stone **for one panel**; the count in the corner reads **thirteen** and does *not* stop. **The stop (P8.2) is the volume's third**: one frame, full width and height, **no dialogue, no count in the corner, no Titanvein, no bloom, no SFX**, shot flat from the rim — **her right forearm laid across the mouth of the debt-horn**, Khura's hand on the other side, Durgan with the graver, and the one craft note that matters: **Ulrik is the only person in the frame who is not anxious.** The reasoning comes *after* the silence (P9.1), flat, and it is the volume's thesis stated as family: putting her brother's oath in the horn would make it read **settled**, and Khura — who said *no* at a stone this week for exactly that reason — is the only other person at the gate who knows what that means. **What the horn gets instead** (P9.2) is the archive's first **refusal**, lettered legibly: ***one debt, held unfinished, by the choice of its holder; the realm may not finish it; the realm may carry nothing of it but the reading***, and the realm's answer is the **palm-out debt-acknowledgement** on the whole rim of the gate — the volume's courtesy, paid in one frame.
- **The war's shape is *furniture*** (P6.1–P6.2): Viv's warning is the chapter's argument — *a war-party without a circle* — and the realm's answer is the thing this culture would actually invent: **readers posted at the crossings**, one per clan, **beginning with the map-room that cannot say no**. **One-Eye's line turns the room's oldest rule one degree** (*a reading may not be refused — so you may put whoever you like in my doorway*) and he speaks the room's consent **and Oros's silence**; **Oros is not drawn in this chapter at all** (his 03a line waits for Gorakh's chapter). **Ulrik's coat** is the volume's last image of him as a war-chief: draw the old campaign-braid on the shoulder, let somebody in the frame notice it, and **never remark on it**. His war is *chairs, nine doors, and a realm answering a crime with furniture.*
- **Rule 7:** the nine readers use **existing 03b names** — **Ura, Targa, Ripple, Flow, Dart** (the goblin courier who canonically carries the first Re-Oath petition) — plus **four drawn-and-unnamed, one per remaining clan**; **Kharza** (03b, the code's youngest student) is the one who walks with the Nine, with **Kha** staying to assist the school. **No new named people** anywhere in this chapter. **Nereia has no dialogue at all** (her beat is the compass-markings at the pass, P10.1, and the sea remains Vol. 6's). **Kaelen gets one line at most and it is the kindest thing in the chapter** — keep it flat. **Bramble** and **Azrael** get one line each, both about oaths, both in their own registers. **Marginalia** gets **one** note (P10.1) and it is the cart's last dispatch from the pass: a defeated complaint, and the cart *going*, because it was always going to go.
- **Rule 6 (Roderick)**: P6.1, **exactly one panel, no dialogue** — small, at the edge of the lamplight, reading the map with **his hands at his sides**, and he must appear **nowhere else on the page**, and the panel must not tell the reader whether he is reading his institution's route, his brother's signature, or the four carved marks on the paper that nobody in the frame touches.
- **The card and the tells** (P12.2): the fourth **hide** card, format unchanged, the accumulated marks continuing — and two new things, both promised earlier and neither explained: **the refusal** (one small cut, deliberately unjoined to the series) and **the rest-bar's first notch** (the tell planted in Ch. 22 P12.2, now paid). Count in the corner: **twelve… thirteen… fourteen.** Caption: ***Here keep one unfinished.*** — the series' **one deliberate break** of the “Here … the named” pattern, for the volume's refusal (audit fix, Ch. 26 pass: the card had repeated Ch. 8's caption).
- **What Ch. 25 must not do** (reservation list, checked): **no Titanvein** (spent Ch. 24; barred for the rest of the volume) · **no stone-song and no bloom** (`#e07a3f` zero) · **Oros off-page** (mentioned by One-Eye only) · **the four companions** unnamed and untouched · **Gorakh** a name on a road and never a person on a page · **the sea's first line** (Nereia silent; the markings point and that is all) · Wren's 03a line · **Kaelen's pilot uses** · the Scar-Reading Chord · the white · the *empty* inside the mountain · Orvane's garden · Khazur's seal-or-fight · **the Re-Oath vote itself** — it is *promised*, entered in the horn, dated a realm away, and **not held in this volume.**
