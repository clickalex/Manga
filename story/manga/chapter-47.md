# AURELION · Volume 8 — Chapter 47: "The First Number"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc H: "The Gray Wings" — the count's fourth and fifth days, and the day the terrace's blank line gets a figure in it.** Ch. 46 opened Seraphel and taught the reader its single instrument: the realm's law is **worn**, and the realm keeps itself with a **count** — a number with no pronouns, cut in pairs into a post, read out to anybody who walks past, and never, ever naming anybody. Ch. 47 is the chapter where the instrument is picked up and used for something other than keeping. On the fourth morning the Orders open an **annex to Schedule I** — an *enumeration* of the realm's fallen, beginning at **the Edge** — and the realm's own machinery does what good machinery does: it counts. Nobody in this chapter is cruel. The clerks are polite, the tally-boards are new, the Archon is kind, the quarter at the rim hands up **its own ledger** so that the number will be *theirs* before it is the terrace's — and the terrace enters it, thanks them, and the schedule has its first figure: **one hundred and forty-one.** Standing against that, on the same board, in the same hand, is the other line the realm has decided this week: **the matter of the statute of Venn — the close of the ninth day of the count.** Same paper. Same hand. The chapter's whole argument is that they are the same paper.
> **Cast:** **The road party** (nine, the box, the cart **Marginalia**; **Azrael Venn** — *who reads his own file in a room with no counter, is thanked by a child for a crack, and spends the chapter finding out that the door in front of him only opens one way*; **Viv** — *who reads the annex as a lawyer and finds the blank that has not been filled*; **Bramble** — *who asks the realm's oldest clerk the only question she has ever asked any realm, and gets a bookkeeper's answer, and gets a better one from a quarter*; **Lyra** — *who knows what a blank in a law does, because her realm has been paying for one since Ch. 38*; **Morgatha** — *who has watched a code learn who to cut and says so once, to a wall*; **Nereia** — *whose clock gets nine days of rain out of a sky that is not her sea*; **Kaelen** — *one sentence, from the step, with his boots on the box*; **Fenn** — *a layer of the map that is a number and not a place*; **Grimm**, **Sprocket**, **Roderick** — *the axle, the notes, and one still panel*). **Seraphel:** **Wing-Count** and **Wing-Count-Young** — the trade, the post, and a question from a changeling that the elder answers out of his own sixty years and not out of the law; **Purge-Adjudicator Seraphine** — who reads the first figure off the terrace's board and asks the Archon every question except the one that is hers; **Archon Seravon** — who receives a ledger from the rim with perfect courtesy and enters it, because entering is what a desk is for; **the ledger-woman of the Edge** — unnamed, drawn, and the chapter's hinge; a **school of the two-winged** on a field-trip to the count; and one **Carved-Order gargoyle** on the parapet, motionless, drawn once again and mentioned by nobody.

> **Arc beat:** Ch. 47 must do five things. **One — the instrument turned.** The realm's count, which has only ever been a way of *keeping*, is turned on the realm's own people: the Orders open an enumeration under Schedule I, beginning at the Edge, and the art team's rule is that the whole thing must be drawn as **paperwork** — new boards, polite clerks, an ordinary day, nobody raising their voice. **Two — the file.** Azrael reads his own file in the reading-room and the mechanism of the ninth day is set out flat: the statute as it lies · the standing · and **the offer** — a statute may be **re-cut whole at the one's own word**, entered as a new undertaking and not as a correction; a statute broken in ignorance is mended at the counter, and a statute broken *knowing* is a choice, and *a choice is answered, not mended*. He does not decide in this chapter. He finds out what the door is. **Three — the quarter's move.** The Edge, which has kept its own count in the lee of a knee-high wall for three years and never once offered it, sends its ledger up to the stand — so that the number will be the quarter's before it is the terrace's — and the terrace receives it and **enters it**, and the schedule has its first figure. Her beat is the chapter's warmth and it may not be treated as a rescue: nobody in Seraphel does her a wrong to be corrected, and she does not ask to be saved. She asks to be *written down*, and she is — by hand, at the rim, in a book the terrace has never asked for. **Five — the near-question.** Seraphine reads the first figure off the board and asks the Archon everything: the arithmetic, the schedule, the method, the word. She asks *what* four times and does not ask *why* once, and the chapter must end her page on her hands and not on her mouth.
