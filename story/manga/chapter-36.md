# AURELION · Volume 6 — Chapter 36: "Sung True"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc F: "The First Song" — the Re-Oath, sung.** The sea's law is exact and everyone in Thalassa has always said so out loud: **a law binds only what is sung true.** Nobody in four hundred years has had to find out what that means. Now a thousand of them are standing in eleven courses of coral with one no at the roll, the roll is open, and the crown is a witness — not a source, not an engine, **a witness** — and the Tidesong Crown has asked the realm its one sentence, the same way the Everbloom asked Sylvaris, in the same lettering with no tail on it: ***…Give me your part.*** And the realm of the deep cannot do it. Not because anybody refuses, and not because anybody is wicked: because *the sea's parts are secrets.* That is the whole grief this volume has been walking toward: for as long as anyone can remember, every merfolk has kept their part of the first song to themselves, sung it alone, underwater, where nobody could hear them get it wrong — and a realm that keeps its parts secret cannot, in one low water, sing them where a thousand others can hear. So the Re-Oath takes **three low waters**, and the chapter is all three: the first attempt collapses at the ninth course with the notation fraying out of the water; the second gets further and stops dead at the **no**, because a no has no note in an oath until a judge says it does; and the third one works, and it works **because of everything the first two failed at** — the children who learned their parts in public at a fence rail, the fifty-nine who were paid to sing other people's words and have to put their slips down, the old ones whose fifty-year solos do not fit and who have to *drop* them, and one old woman at the roll whose part is the word *no* and who sings it, alone, at the water, before a thousand voices take it up *with* her instead of over her. Two chords are struck and the chapter is built to hold both: **the Oathflame Choir** — Viv and Azrael's one page, read at the water and taken up in song, a contract bound by a chorus — and then **the Chorus-True**, Nereia as the *first* voice and a thousand behind her, which is her Chord, and which she has been carrying since Ch. 3 without knowing the first thing about it: the first song was never a solo. And when it binds, the crown is what it has always been: **a witness, not a source** — and the realm of the deep, which has spent four volumes' worth of law on the idea that a thing must be *said* to exist, sings its consent where a thousand people can hear it, which is the only reason any of it counts.
> **Cast:** **The road party** (nine; **Nereia** — *the first voice, and the chapter is hers: the chord struck, the solo dropped, the flask set down and handed back*, **Azrael** — *who reads the one page at the water and takes the Oathflame Choir up with her, and who has one dry line about what a no is for*, **Viv** — *the page itself: the legal basis of a song, drafted at a rock with no paper*, **Morgatha** — *who counts the parts and finds the one nobody can hear*, **Kaelen** — *who goes furthest into the water in his boots and stays dry from the knee up, because the realm's stone has to stand somewhere*, **Fenn** — *who draws the chord as a line through every mark he has ever cut*, **Kharza** — *who cuts the oath's one page into the crossings' slats*, **Sprocket** — *the entries*, **Bramble** — *who asks the question that gets the third low water* ) + **Marginalia** (the cart on the shingle; > **Arc beat:** Ch. 36 must do five things and none of them with a miracle. **One — the ask.** The crown asks its one sentence (*…Give me your part*) and the realm **fails at it twice**, and the failures must be drawn as *craft* failures, not moral ones: a thousand merfolk who have sung their parts alone for their whole lives, discovered in public; the notation frays out of the water at the ninth course on the first attempt and stops dead at the refuser's place on the second. **Two — the no gets a note.** Pearl's finding is the chapter's hinge and it is the second law the deep learns from the land in two chapters (*the record reads both ways* was the first): **an oath that cannot be sung with a no in it is an oath over somebody — and the deep does not swear over people.** The mechanics: the no is sung *first*, alone, at the water, and the chorus takes it up **with** her and never over her, which is the only way *sung true* has ever meant anything. **Three — the two chords.** **The Oathflame Choir** is struck exactly as canon says it is struck — Azrael and Nereia, a contract bound by a chorus, two people and one page — and then **the Chorus-True**: Nereia as the first voice and a thousand behind her. And the chapter's last movement must belong to Nereia: she sets the flask down on the roll and steps back so the thousand can be the first voice — and somebody hands it back to her, because an office is not a thing you can put down by putting the cup down.

## Page 1 — *The Ask*

### P1.1 — The crown as witness (half page)
**Art:** And the volume names the thing it has been singing toward, and the art team's rule for it is the strictest in the book: **a crown is a witness, not a source, and nothing about it may look like power.** The setting: the reef at the first low water of the three; eleven courses of coral with the thousand standing in them; the **roll** on its stand at the north point with the **refuser** beside it, hand flat, exactly where Ch. 35 left her; the **bone chair** on the knots' stone with Maelis in it and Song-Part at her shoulder; the tide-guard along the rim, tridents covered; the **nine dry witnesses** on the chimney ledge with **Kaelen's crossing-stone** at the ledge's foot; **Marginalia's plate** on its rope, dry; and the company's clerk at the ledge's edge, hands empty, where Ch. 35 put him. What is new: **the Tidesong Crown** — and the art team must draw it as *the oldest object in the reef and the least interesting thing in the panel*: a low, unornamented circlet of four hundred years of **coral and drift-bone**, worn at the inner ring's front on a plain stand of the same stone as the roll, **no gems, no light, no glow, nothing that shines** — a witness's chair, not a throne. Four frames: **(1)** the wide reef, from outside the last ring, with the crown small on its stand and the thousand drawn as *texture*, not as faces; **(2)** the crown, close, and the art team must draw the **wear on it**: four hundred years of hands, one side polished, the bone gone smooth, a hairline crack mended with a coral graft nobody ever mentions; **(3)** **Coral**, at the roll, opening a new column with her chalk, and the reader must be able to read its heading: ***the parts***; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **the crown's one sentence**, cut into the air above it in the sea's ring-script — the chapter's whole ask — and the art team must letter it **smaller than every other piece of writing on the page, with no tail on the ellipsis**, because the Everbloom asked Sylvaris in precisely that lettering and the rhyme is the point: ***…Give me your part.***
**Pearl (flat, to the thousand, and the judge states the law of the ritual in four sentences, and none of them may sound like ceremony, because in this realm ceremony is a *procedure*):** "\u2014 ***The crown has asked. Enter the ask in the roll at full length, and then let it stand there and be read by anybody who doubts it.*** \u2014 The law of the ritual, for the thousand and for the record: the crown is **not a source.** It has no power in it, it never had, and the day this realm treats it as an engine is the day this realm has failed its own statute. The crown is a **witness** \u2014 it has sat at the front of this reef for four hundred years and watched, and what it is asking now is to be able to say, honestly, that it watched the realm *choose.* \u2014 **The ask is: give me your part.** Not your voice: your **part.** The piece of the first song you have been carrying since you learned to carry anything, the one you have been singing alone, underwater, where nobody could hear you get it wrong. Sing it where the others can hear. That is the whole of the ritual, and it will take three low waters at the rate this chamber is going, and I will tell you now: **there is no punishment in this reef for a part that does not fit.** There is only the count, and the count is honest, and it is going to be honest about every one of us for three days."

### P1.2 — The first attempt, and where it frays (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's first failure is drawn as **weather, not as blame**, and the art team's rule: **the collapse happens in the notation, not in the people.** The thousand take the ask up: the outer rings first, then inward, each course singing its part *out loud, in public, for the first time in any of their lives* — and the art team must draw that as a *texture change*: in the first four courses the notation comes up clean and thin (the volumes' land-singing grammar does not apply here; this is water, and water holds a chord when it can); by the sixth course the lines are arriving **out of step**, drawn as overlapping waves with their edges out of register; at the ninth course the chord **frays out** — the notation thinning, shredding, and going quiet — and the page must end in a **silence that the art team draws as an absence of lettering over the last two courses**, with the water still and eleven hundred faces doing nothing at all. Four frames: **(1)** the outer courses taking the ask, notation rising, and the reader must be able to see that it *works* for four courses; **(2)** **an old merfolk** in the sixth course (drawn, unnamed, and old in the way the sea makes old) singing with her eyes closed and her part *wrong*, drawn kindly, because she is not being punished; **(3)** the ninth course, and the notation coming apart — the art team must draw the *fray* the way Ch. 35 drew land-singing, but here it is nobody's fault and nothing to do with air; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Coral**, chalk stopped, writing the only thing the roll can write: a ruled line with **nothing on the far side** (the volume's oldest device, and it must be used here without comment).
**Coral (flat, at the roll, and the scribe's entry is the chapter's statement of the problem, and it is a *diagnosis*, not a reproach):** "\u2014 Entry: ***the first taking, first low water: four courses true; two courses out of register; three courses frays out; the ask not met.*** — And my note, which goes in beside it in the small hand, because the roll is not a place for comfort and it *is* a place for facts: **this is not refusal and it is not inability. This is a habit.** Every one of us learned our part alone, because singing it where somebody could hear was how you got laughed at, and we have done it that way for four hundred years, and last evening a thousand people tried to do in one hour what none of us has ever done in his life: *hold a tune next to somebody else.* \u2014 The reef is not disappointed. **The reef is** ***inexperienced.*** It is a different and much more repairable thing, and I would like the parliament to proceed on that basis."

## Page 2 — *The One Page*

### P2.1 — A rock, and the answer is a sentence (2/3 page)
**Art:** And the chapter's legal heart arrives on the dry side, and the art team's rule: **Viv drafts on a wet rock with no paper, and the page she produces must be lettered to be read.** Setting: the chimney ledge's dry shelf above the water, evening of the first day; the crossing-stone at the ledge's foot; Kaelen's boots in the water below the shelf (**water to his knee, dry from the knee up**, because the realm's stone has to stand somewhere); Marginalia's plate on its rope; Bramble sitting on the stone's edge with her shoes off eating something; the shore's lamplighter coming down the stair; and **Viv and Azrael**, working: Viv with her sleeve rolled and her finger in the wet stone writing the *shape* of the thing, Azrael sitting on the ledge with a page — **the real page**, the one piece of paper in the chapter — because he is the only person in this party who has ever kept an oath's paperwork. The page itself must be drawn in three states across the panel's frames, and its final state must be legible: **one page, one column, six short lines**, which is all a chorus of a thousand can hold. Four frames: **(1)** the ledge, from the water's side: Azrael, Viv, the stone, the boots below; **(2)** Viv's hand in the wet stone, drawing the *structure* — and the art team must draw the structure and not the words: the ask, the answer, the no, the part, the witness, the count; **(3)** **Azrael** writing, and the art team must draw him writing *well*, without hesitating, the way a professional does the one job he has been doing since before he was thrown out of anywhere — and no glow, no halo, no wings; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the one page, held up between them against the last of the light — and the reader must be able to read **all six lines**, because this is not a prop, it is the chapter's law.
**The one page (lettered in full, in the record's plainest hand, and it is what the Oathflame Choir will bind — six lines, and the art team may not add a seventh):** "*The realm of Thalassa gives its parts. — **One.** Each voice gives the piece of the first song it has been carrying, and gives it out loud, where the others can hear. — **Two.** No part is refused for being broken, and no part is refused for being small. — **Three.** The no of one voice stands: it is sung first and it is sung with, and never sung over. — **Four.** The crown is the witness and not the source; nothing here is owed to the crown and nothing here is owed for it. — **Five.** The parts belong to the voices that carry them: the realm may sing them and may not keep them, and may not sell them, and may not give them away. — **Six.** What is given here is given to be sung, and it is given where the water can hear it. — *The realm of the Barrens, its second register, and the roads: read aloud at the crossings, daily.*"
**Viv (flat, at the rock, working, and the advocate's line is the legal basis of a song and must be delivered as a *correction of the ritual* rather than as a speech):** "\u2014 The crown asked for a *part*. It did not ask for a promise, and it did not ask for an oath of loyalty, and it did not ask anybody to give anything **away** — and I want that in writing, this afternoon, before a thousand people start singing, because a realm that improvises a ritual in public is a realm that is going to invent a **debt** by accident, and my realm has spent a hundred years learning how much that costs." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her still working while she says it, because Viv talks and works at the same time and always has)* "…So: the parts are *theirs*. The realm may sing them, and may not keep them, and may not sell them — **that one's for the company's benefit and he'll be glad of it, wait and see** — and the no stands, and it's sung first, and it's sung *with*: no oath in nine realms gets to be sung *over* somebody, and if the sea's law can't manage that, then the sea's law is about to learn a rule out of a *gatepost* for the second time in two chapters." *(a beat)* "…Azrael, is it a page a chorus can hold? Because if it needs a second page, it's not a song any more, it's a *brief*, and I've written a hundred of those and not one of them ever made anybody sing."

### P2.2 — Bramble's question, which is the third low water (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's mechanism gets asked by the person whose job in this saga is asking, and the art team's rule: **the question must be asked with food in hand and no solemnity whatsoever, because the answer is the chapter's turning point.** Setting the same ledge, later; the lamps; Bramble with her shoes off and both feet in the warm shallow of the shelf, the cart drawn small on the shingle behind her, the plate on its rope; and — the art team's one indulgence — **the refuser**, drawn up on the shelf's far end, sitting with her own back to the reef and her wages still folded in the slip, **listening to all of it and saying nothing**, because she has not been asked anything yet. Four frames: **(1)** Bramble, the ledge, the shallows, the crew working behind her; **(2)** Bramble's question, drawn with her mouth full and one finger up, and the art team must letter it *in a caption* because her mouth is full: *so if she says no and the oath can't have a no in it, and she's one of the thousand, then what's the count supposed to say?*; **(3)** **Viv** and **Azrael** looking at each other, and the art team must draw the exact half-second of two professionals realising they have been drafting six lines around the one line that matters — **no balloons on either of them**; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the **refuser**, at the far end of the shelf, and the reader must be able to see that she has heard every word and has not moved, and the count in the corner, in Morgatha's hand, for the first time this chapter: **nine hundred and ninety-nine parts given; one withheld; the count holds.**
**Azrael (flat, at the ledge, and the fallen angel's line is the chapter's second-best sentence and it must be said to Viv, quietly, with no balloon tail at all):** "\u2014 …The no's not a hole in the page. It's a **line of the page.** — Viv. Every oath I've ever seen sworn over somebody was sworn *over* them because the room was afraid of a pause. That's the whole history of the thing. The pause is the only honest note in it \u2014 it's the one everybody else is standing on." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him looking at the woman at the end of the shelf and *not* at Viv)* "…Ask her to sing it. Not to agree. **To sing the no.** And then let the thousand find out whether their part is worth anything when it has to sit next to a voice that isn't going to say yes."

## Page 3 — *Thirty-Six Slips*

### P3.1 — Eleven stations close at full pay (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's dry-side machinery runs again, and the art team's rule: **the company does the decent thing on paper, in advance, and everybody can read it.** Setting: the shore road at dawn of the second day; the eleven stations' boards turned; the company's pier office with its stove; the **stand-off book** open on the counter with a fresh page; Marra's boat at the pier with her chalk board showing **eleven crossings today** and full boats; the free slat with its queue of nine, and the realm's own reader reading the day's slip and then reading the **oath's one page** aloud after it, because the crossings have been told to carry it. Four frames: **(1)** the pier board, relettered a fourth time — the art team must draw the old letters under the new paint *again*, now in three places, and the new lettering must be legible: ***CLOSED FOR THE OATH. CARRIAGE RESUMES AT THE FLOOD. \u2014 SUNG ASKINGS: HELD. WAGES: POSTED AND PAID. \u2014 READINGS AT THE WATER: FREE, AND NOT OURS TO SELL.***; **(2)** the stand-off book, close, with one page for the three days and a single line lettered: ***all stations closed at the sea's call; fifty-nine singers released to their realm; wages paid for the days as worked; no charge carried to any asker; askings held in order.***; **(3)** the fifty-nine station-singers coming down the shore road with their bags, and the art team must draw them still in their company coats, because they *are* company singers and the saga is honest about that; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **the slips** — a basket at the station counters with thirty-six asker's slips still in it, held in order, and the art team must letter three of them, because each one is somebody's brother, somebody's boat, somebody's debt, and holding them is a cost the chapter must draw.
**The company's clerk (flat, at the pier, to the fifty-nine, and the antagonist's line is a *supervisor's* line and it must have no sentiment in it at all, which is what makes it land):** "\u2014 Wages are posted and paid for the three days, and I've written *released to their realm* in the stand-off book in my own hand, and I want you to hear why, because I'd rather you knew than guessed: it's not a kindness and it isn't a gift. **A gift is what you give somebody you've decided is poor**, and you're not poor and I'm not your uncle. It's the same arithmetic as the last time. An hour where my singers are standing in the sea is an hour they're not at my counter, and I pay for **hours** \u2014 that's the whole company, it always was, and if the sea needs you for three days then the sea has hired the three days and the company's the one that pays, because the company's the one that made the schedule." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him handing over a *list* and not a speech)* "…Thirty-six askings held in order, first-come, first-served, and I'll not have one of them pushed ahead of another for money while you're gone. **Somebody's brother is third in that basket.** I read them all last night, and I'd rather have read them in a court than in a basket, and that's the closest I'll come to saying anything about it."

### P3.2 — The children of the shore teach the parts (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's mechanism for *why the third low water works* is planted here and must be drawn as the smallest thing in the chapter, which is where this saga always puts its load-bearing beams. Setting: the shore road at morning, and the fence rail of Ch. 35; a dozen children of the shore — fisherfolk's kids, the lamplighter's child, two in merfolk scale that does not fit them yet — sitting on the rail and on a wall, and **one old merfolk** from the first attempt (the one who sang with her eyes closed and got her part wrong) teaching **four notes** to the rail's children, and then the children teaching **four other children**, and the art team must draw the relay in one horizontal panel: **the same four notes, handed along the rail like a bucket.** Present: the realm's reader at the slat with the oath's page in her hand, reading it aloud to the queue, and the queue half-listening because the rail has the better music. Four frames: **(1)** the rail, the children, the old one, the relay; **(2)** the notes, drawn as the sea's notation **out of water** — frayed at the edges, thin, and *passing from hand to hand*, lettered four times down the panel; **(3)** the reader at the slat with the page, and the queue, and the art team must draw a fisherwoman reading the six lines over the reader's shoulder and *getting to the fourth one*; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Bramble**, at the fence, with a mouthful of bread, being taught four notes by a nine-year-old and **actually trying** — and the notation above her correctly drawn with no craft in it at all, because she is a changeling on a shore and cannot sing, and the chapter's whole argument is that the part she gives is the *trying*.
**Kharza (flat, in the crossings' register, in the record's cut hand, and the realm's youngest reader files the day the way her realm files everything):** "*Submitted to the horn with the week's slips: the sea's oath was read aloud at nine crossings today after the welcome, in order, and at two of them it was read by children who had learned it standing on a fence rail, and the reader at the edge post asked for it twice and then asked for the fourth line to be read again on its own, which is not a complaint against the reading, it is a *question about the fourth line*, and I have written to the sea asking what the fourth line does to a voice that has already given its part away. \u2014 Also: the company's stations are closed and the wages are paid and thirty-six askings are held in a basket in order, and I have written that down too, in the register's plain column, because in a hundred years somebody is going to want to know whether the company behaved, and the answer is going to be on a slat on a road, in my hand: **it did.** \u2014 k.*"

## Page 4 — *The Second Low Water*

### P4.1 — The taking, and the pause (2/3 page)
**Art:** And the second attempt gets further than the first and dies in the same place it died before, and the art team's rules are exact: **the chorus is better and the notation is better and the stop is identical**, because the reef has the same hole in it. The setting: the reef at the second low water; the thousand in the courses, drawn now with a *difference the reader can find*: they are standing **shoulder to shoulder**, closer than on Page 1, because the two days of relays on the shore have changed how they stand; the crown on its stand; the roll with Coral; Pearl at the ring's edge; the tide-guard; the nine on the ledge; the clerk at the ledge's edge with the stand-off book under his arm; and **Mnemothe** at the knot with his eye open, drawn once on this page and once on Page 10, and never explained.
