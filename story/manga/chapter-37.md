# AURELION · Volume 6 — Chapter 37: "The First Song"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc F: "The First Song" — the volume's finale.** Everything has been sung: the parts are given, the covenant is re-bound, the count stands at a thousand with one no inside it and not under it. What is left is the thing the whole volume has been *not* doing — the song itself, four hundred thousand years old, one line of it gone quiet for a year and a day, going back down the water to the place where the world keeps its anchor. And the finale's discipline is the discipline of all four volumes that came before it: **nothing in the deep is drawn, nothing in the deep is voiced, and nothing in the deep is named** — no whale, no shape, no god, no face, no glow. The waking happens on this chapter's pages the way weather happens: as *pressure*, as a tide-clock's float above a mark, as the reef going still in one panel and every lamp dimming at once, as a song sung by a thousand people *for* somebody instead of *at* somebody, and as a story that the sea has been telling itself since before it could hear itself begin being **handed over, in public, to an audience, by the only creature that can hold a listener and let them go.** And because this is a finale and Volume 6 is a volume about law, the chapter closes its ledgers: the sea's year of unboundness, entered as a debt in a horn nine hundred miles west, is read out, answered and filed; a contract is spent and the paper given to the water; a tide-guard's captain gives the realm's army its first order in the form of a bar of music; two sirens of the law-court duel over the last article and settle it in a duet, and the winner sings the first verdict in her career that is not a cage; and Nereia — who lost the first song, who carried a line alone for a year and a day, who was flat by a quarter-tone in front of a whole realm and kept going — gets the thing she has been owed since Chapter 3: not her song back, and not a healing, and not a miracle, but **an answer.** It comes up the tube the way the thread went down it, and it is not a sentence; it is the first line, going home toward the shore this time, and the keeper's hand is flat on the shell when it arrives, and the count in the corner reads the only number Volume 6 has ever really been keeping.
> **Cast:** **The road party** (nine; **Nereia** — *the volume's arc pays out: the answer, the flask, and the line about doing it with them*, **Morgatha** — *who reads the sea's debt out and files it, and still gets no answer about her son*, **Kaelen** — *who gets to see a bridge hold*, **Fenn** — *who finishes a map the way a map should be finished*, **Viv** — *whose contract reaches its term, and who says the word the Barrens' code does not have*, **Azrael** — *who lets the page go into the water and says nothing about it*, **Kharza** — *who cuts the closed year into the crossings' slats*, **Sprocket** — *the entries*, **Bramble** — *who asks the old woman for the card* ) + **Marginalia** (the cart, on the shingle, plate on its rope, dry) · **Captain Roderick Vane** (**his one silent panel** — an awl, a mark above the highest mark, and one word written on the day's heading) · **the chorus-parliament** (03b: **Pearl** — *who dockets the year*, **Coral** — *who closes the page and writes the last line of the column*, **Shell** — *who calls the closing*, **Tide / Current / Stream** — *who finally get to sing a first line they did not have to teach*, **Song-Part** — *whose hands carry a voiceless ruler's part into the only song that has ever really been hers*) · **the Thalarch Maelis** (voiceless; > **Arc beat:** Ch. 37 must do five things and the last one is the only one that matters. **One — the answer arrives without being a character.** The deep's reply is drawn as *pressure and consequence*: a thread of current with no direction; the shell-tube carrying water; the reef going still; the lamps dimming once; the tide-clock's float above its highest mark; and a hundred and eighteen shore-people hearing rain that isn't raining. **The art team may not draw a shape in the water at any point in this chapter, may not letter a voice, and may not put the word *Leviathan* anywhere except in these production notes.** **Two — the waking is spent here and nowhere else.** The volume's reserved beat pays in this chapter: it wakes, it hears itself remembered, and it goes back to sleep, and *that* is the ring's growth — canon from Vol. 0: the reef grows its ring the year the realm sings the song back to the thing it belongs to. All of it must be told through the reef, the roll and the people, because the deep is unpicturable by rule. **Three — the ledger closes on both sides.** The Barrens reads out the sea's year of unboundness — the debt entered in Ch. 31, *to be read aloud at each circle until the reef is told to have grown its ring* — and closes it; the horn learns a new word it did not have (**a story**, which this realm's code has no entry for, because a code of bindings has no grammar for a thing that must be *spent to be kept*); and the reef-roll writes the same year out from the wet side. **Four — the law finishes its own business.** The guard is ordered into a chorus by a bar of music; the contract reaches its term and its paper goes into the water like the company's answers did; and the law-court's two sirens duel over the last article before the Nine are released, so that the realm's release from sea-law is sung as **a duet in which the second voice disagrees** — *that's not a rebellion, that's the law* — and is decided in **the first verdict Aurelia Vox has ever sung that is not a cage.** **Five — and this is the chapter.** Nereia does not get her song back, does not get healed, does not get a miracle: she gets **an answer**, up the tube, at the far edge, in the presence of a man whose whole life is a board of notches — and the answer is not for her, and that is the point. She says the line the volume has been building to since Ch. 3: *it was never mine and it was never a solo, and we did not do it alone — we did it **with them.*** The count in the corner reads **a ring — and a rest**, the flask goes back on its hook full and never counted again, and the last page of the volume is a wide panel with the water in it, because the water has been in the saga since Chapter 3 and nobody has ever once been allowed to draw it properly.
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**a debt closed; a story entered; the roll's year docketed**), the reading (**the last reading of the volume is the sea's own first line, read aloud by nobody — heard**), the door (**the first line goes home; the audience is the door**), the count (**a ring — and a rest**), the carrying (**a contract spent; a story spent; a song carried by a thousand and handed on**).

## Page 1 — *The Morning After*

### P1.1 — The reef at first light, and the year's column (2/3 page)
**Art:** And the volume's last chapter opens on the least dramatic morning in its run, and the art team's rule: **nobody is celebrating, because in this realm celebration is what you do when there is no work.** Setting: the reef at first light, the day after the oath; the eleven courses **almost empty** — a few hundred still about, tidying the ways a village tidies; the coral's shallow water; the knots' stone; the crown still on its stand at the inner ring with **two tide-guard beside it** (its escort home, as Ch. 36 promised); the roll on its bench at the north point with **Coral writing**; the chimney ledge with the **cart**, the plate on its rope and the crew's gear packed and stacked; the **crossing-stone** at the ledge's foot; and the shore-folk gone home except four children asleep against the cart's wheel, drawn without comment. Present for the crew: Nereia, wet to the shoulders and not dried off, sitting on the ledge with her feet in the shallow; Azrael with the **contract's page** in his coat pocket and one hand on it; Kaelen holding a plumb-line over the ledge's edge for reasons the chapter never explains; Fenn asleep sitting up with his board on his knees; Viv with the hearing's notes; Morgatha with the tally; Bramble eating; Sprocket writing. Four frames: **(1)** the reef at first light, wide, from the chimney's height, with the **empty courses** and the water flat; **(2)** the roll, close: **Coral closing a column**, and the reader must be able to read the last three lines she has just cut — ***the parts: given*** / ***the covenant: re-bound, in the realm's voice*** / ***the year's debt: stands in the horn of the Barrens until the reef is told to have grown its ring***; **(3)** the crew on the ledge, drawn as **people at the end of a job** — no speech, no tableau, a mule's hoof, a boot being mended with cord; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the **water beyond the chimney's mouth**, drawn from the ledge, **one line and one stroke of light, and nothing in it at all** — the panel that has ended every chapter of this volume and that the chapter is about to make a liar out of, which the art team must set up *without* foreshadowing and without a ripple: exactly the same water as Ch. 35's last page. And the count in the corner, in Morgatha's hand: **a thousand — sung true.**
**Coral (flat, at the roll, to nobody in particular, and the scribe's line is the chapter's first movement stated as *bookkeeping*, which is how this volume signs all its love letters now):** "— Year docketed: the parts given, the covenant re-bound, the count at a thousand and one of them a no, the contract entered and standing. **And one line that is not closed.** — The reef keeps its books the way the reef keeps everything, which is in rings and in ink, and I am obliged to write down that we are **sitting on a debt that is not ours and is not the realm of the Barrens' either**: the sea spent a year unable to bind, the Barrens entered that year in its horn as something **owed**, and by the terms of that entry the debt clears on the day the horn is *told the reef has grown its ring.* — The reef's rim has not grown a ring since the quiet began. I have checked it twice with a boat this morning. So the year's entry stays open on two sides of a world, and this scribe would like it understood by every voice still on this reef that **nothing about the last three days has undone the year.** We bounced a covenant off a silence that is still there. That is a great deal and it is not the whole of it, and the roll does not say things are finished when they are not."

### P1.2 — The thin line (1/3 page)
**Art:** And the volume's turning point arrives as **a line of water**, and the art team's instruction is the most important in the chapter: **it must be smaller than the reader expects and quieter than they can bear.** Setting: the chimney's mouth, within the hour; Nereia on the wet step with her feet in the water; the tide-guard on the stair above; Whalesong's post drawn **far off at the frame's edge as a smudge of lamp** (the art team may place it and must not zoom); the crew behind her on the ledge; and the *thing itself*: from the water beyond the last ring, a **thin line of current** — drawn as a single travelling crease on a flat surface, no shape under it, no direction to it, nothing following it, going **west along the reef's face** and away. Four frames: **(1)** the water's surface, wide, with the line crossing it *diagonally toward the top-right of the panel, which is west*, and the reader must be able to see that nothing is under it because the art team tints the water's depth the same as everywhere else in the frame; **(2)** Nereia's feet in the shallow and the line passing **six inches from her ankle**, and the art team must draw the hairs on her arms and no other cue; **(3)** her face, and the art team must draw her **not looking at the reader, not looking at the crew, looking down the line**; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Coral, chalk stopped**, and the line she has begun to write and does not finish, and the reader must be able to see it: ***note: a current out of season, no—*** — and the count in the corner, in Morgatha's hand, newly made: **a thread — and a rest.**
**Nereia (flat, on the wet step, and it is the first thing she has said all morning and it is not a speech, and the art team must give it **no balloon tail at all**):** "— That's not a tide and it's not the reef's draw and it's not the meltwater, and I've been reading water since I was nine. — It's **aimless**, and everything in the sea has a *reason*, and the only thing that has ever moved water without a reason is—" *(a beat, and she does not finish the sentence and the art team must draw her stopping, because the words for it are not in the chapter and never will be)* "…Coral. *Don't write it down yet.* Just — put the chalk down for a moment and **listen**, because if I'm right, we've got about an hour, and if I'm wrong, I've made a fool of myself in front of a scribe, and I'd like the roll to record that I'd rather be wrong."

## Page 2 — *The Far Edge*

### P2.1 — The post, and a mark that is not a tally (half page)
**Art:** And the volume's farthest place gets the chapter's quietest page, and the art team's rules for the post are unchanged from Ch. 30–35 — **the shelf, the mast, the tube, the shell trumpet, the lamp, the notch-board — with one difference that the reader must find without being told.** The board: three hundred and sixty-one notches and the cut one and the rest, and on its **edge**, in order, the marks the reader has been watching for six chapters: **a line, a line under it, four marks, and a rest** (Ch. 32), **a stroke going up** (Ch. 35) — and, new, the thing the chapter is actually about, which the camera must reach last: Whalesong has cut **the sea's own notation, one symbol of it, small, at the end of the edge-marks** — not a tally, not a stroke, not a numeral: **a note.** Present: **the post's helpers** (two krakenkin, drawn, unnamed, on the stair with a crate); the smoke; the rain that is not raining (the art team must draw the crew on the stair **looking up**, because the sky is doing something that the post's own chapter-canon has always refused to explain); and Whalesong himself, older than the last time, standing up. Four frames: **(1)** the post, wide, from the water: the shelf, the mast, the tube, the lamp, the board — and the rain that is not raining drawn as **light with water in it** and nothing else; **(2)** the board, close, with all six edge-marks legible, the new note last; **(3)** Whalesong's hand on the shell trumpet **and his other hand flat on the tube**, and the art team must draw the **water on the tube's shoulders** — condensation in a place that has been dry for a hundred years; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the **ledge at the reef**, far away, drawn in the post's own frame as a *smudge of lamp and three figures* — because the two ends of this chapter are about to be one event, and the art team must connect them with a panel and not with a line.
**Whalesong (flat, standing at the board with the knife not in his hand, and the keeper's line is the chapter's first true fact and it must be delivered the way he has delivered everything for a hundred years: as a job description):** "— It's moving. — I want the record exact, because I've kept this board for a hundred and some years and the board has never once flattered me: **the water in the tube is coming up.** Not fast. Not much. It's *tide* water at the wrong hour of a day that isn't the equinox, and the shell's cold at the mouth and warm at the shank, and there is **a current running along the reef's face toward the shore** that was not there when I came on watch and will not be there when you come back with the boat." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him not looking at any of it and looking at the sea)* "…I've cut three hundred and sixty-one notches for silence and one stroke for a road and four marks for a sentence. — **I've got nothing on this board for what's happening now, and I've decided that's a fault in the board and not in the water.** Bring the knife. I'm going to put a *note* on the edge; and if anybody ever asks you what the note means, you tell them the truth, which is that a man who listens for a living finally had something to write down and used the only grammar he could reach."

### P2.2 — "…again" (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter says the word the volume has been holding in a doorway since Ch. 32, and the art team's rule is that **it is said by the wrong person in the right room and must not be set up as a revelation.** Setting: the post's shelf, noon, the light with water in it; the two krakenkin on the stair with the crate set down and forgotten; the tube's shoulders still wet; Whalesong at the board; and the sound — drawn in the panel's *air*, in the notation the saga uses for land-singing, **fraying at the edges and coming from below**, so the reader understands that the post is hearing through rock what the reef is hearing through water. Four frames: **(1)** the shelf, and the sound arriving as a low, wide band of thin notation across the lower third of the panel; **(2)** the krakenkin, and the art team must draw the two of them **not moving** and one of them reaching for the other's arm; **(3)** Whalesong, and the art team must draw **what he does not do**: he does not smile, does not weep, does not lift a hand; he stands at the board with the knife **back on its hook**, because the note is already cut and there is nothing to add; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the board's edge, close, with the four marks, the rest, the stroke going up and the new note, and below them — the art team must add this and never explain it — **a single drop of water on the shelf's stone, no bigger than a thumbprint**, which is the only water in the post that did not come up the tube.
**Whalesong (flat, and the keeper says the chapter's word and it is the same word the founder said in a doorway in Ch. 32, and the art team must letter it identically: an ellipsis, then the word, then a full stop — and nothing else on the page may use that lettering again):** "— …**Again.**" *(a beat, and the art team must draw him turning away from the board to the stair and picking up the crate, because the post has a schedule and the schedule does not stop for the end of a silence)* "…That's not the first line. Don't you write that down. That's the *sea* doing something it does every few thousand years and doesn't explain to anybody, and I've spent a hundred years being the man who is **allowed to notice** and not the man who is allowed to *know*, and I'm not going to spoil it at my age by having an opinion." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him already three steps down)* "…Boat in an hour. Tide's wrong and I'm going anyway; tell the reef the post is coming, and tell them to put a **chair** out, because a man my age does not stand through other people's reunions."

## Page 3 — *The Answer, Inside the Tube*

### P3.1 — The tube's mouth, and the listeners (2/3 page)
**Art:** And the chapter's central image is **a shell with water in it**, and the art team must resist every instinct they have. Setting: the Deep-Listening post, the shell trumpet's mouth, close, on its stand at the tube's head; the seawater that has come up the tube standing **in the trumpet's bowl**, a hand's depth, clear, unlit, unremarkable; the shelf behind it out of focus with the lamp and the board; and — the page's subject — **the listeners**: Whalesong's hand flat on the bowl's rim from the left of the frame; the two krakenkin at the shelf's edge, one with a tentacle-tip in the water of the bowl and one with both eyes shut; and, at the frame's right edge, arriving on the stair in wet coats with the boat's gear still on them, **Nereia**, **Song-Part**, **Coral** — the sea's three readers of water who came ninety miles to put their hands in a puddle. Four frames: **(1)** the bowl, close: the water, the shell's interior, the light; and the art team must draw **the surface of the water in the bowl as absolutely flat**, so that the reader knows something is wrong with it; **(2)** the hands: Whalesong's flat on the rim, a tentacle-tip in the water, Nereia's beside it, Song-Part's beside that — **four touches, no more, and no balloon**; **(3)** the water in the bowl, and the art team's instruction for the whole saga complies here: **the water in the bowl begins to carry the first line** — drawn as **melody and faint ripples, never as words** — a line of the sea's notation rising *out of the water into the air of the shelf*, thin, going up, and the reader must recognise it as **the thing Ch. 30 forbade: the first line, rendered as music and not as text**, drawn in the panel's air and not on any page of anybody's book; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Nereia's face** — and the art team must draw the tears **as water**, which for a merfolk out of water is the joke the volume has earned, and must draw her hands **still flat on the rim and not raised**, and must put **no balloon near her at all**; the balloon on this page belongs to Coral and it is one line.
**Coral (flat, at the shelf's edge, chalk in hand and chalk not moving, and the scribe's line is the chapter's only piece of dialogue on this page and it must be small):** "— For the roll, and I will write it exactly as I say it and no finer, because I have been a scribe for nineteen years and I have never once had to describe this: **the first line came up the tube in seawater and stood in a shell on a shelf in a room with four people in it.** It is not in a book. It has not been in a book since the quiet began. It is in **water**, on a shelf, in front of a fisherman's bowl, and it is the same line — I have heard it sung at the reef for nineteen years and I have heard it *not* sung for one, and I am telling you with my hand on the tablet that this is **not a copy.** — Nobody write this down for the horn yet. It isn't the realm's to give away and it isn't mine to write: **it's the sea's, and it came to us, and we are going to have to decide what to do about that** — this afternoon, in a proper room, with the crown as witness, because Volume 6 has taught this realm exactly one kind of manners and it is *read it out loud where everybody can hear, and then put it where it belongs.*"

### P3.2 — What Nereia doesn't say, and what she says (1/3 page)
**Art:** And the volume pays its arc in **four small frames and no music**, and the art team's rule: **this page is two people and a bowl.** Setting: the shelf, minutes later; the light with water in it gone thin; the two krakenkin sent down the stair with the crate; Whalesong at the board with his back to the room, deliberately, which the art team must draw as *manners* and not as coldness; **Song-Part** kneeling at the bowl with her hands in her lap; and Nereia on her knees in front of it with her hands on the rim. Four frames: **(1)** the bowl, and the water **still flat** with the notation hanging in the air above it and the fray no longer at its edges — the art team's one change in the entire chapter's visual grammar: **the line is not fraying any more**, and the reason must never be explained; **(2)** Nereia's hands on the rim, and the art team must draw the flask at her hip and her hand **not** going to it, which is the panel's whole content; **(3)** **Song-Part's** hand reaching past her to the bowl and **setting one fingertip in the water** — and the reader must understand, without a word, that a voiceless ruler's keeper is putting a part in, and that the part being put in is **not hers** (the art team must draw the same fingertip-position as Ch. 36's P9.2, the office's own habit); **(4)** and the page's last frame: the notation over the bowl, and **two lines in it** where there was one — and the count in the corner, in Morgatha's hand, newly made and small: **two lines — and a rest.**
**Nereia (flat, kneeling at the bowl, and the volume's first voice gets the chapter's thesis and it must be short and it must not be about her, and the art team must draw her hands flat on the rim the entire time):** "— Eleven years old, and they gave me a line and told me to carry it, and I believed them, and I carried it in the dark where nobody could hear me get it wrong, and it stopped, and I spent a year and a day deciding that the stopping was **mine** — my fault, my line, my failure, mine to fix. — And then a hundred and eighteen of us walked a coast road singing it badly, and fifty-nine people put somebody else's slips down on a reef, and an old woman sang a *no* into the middle of it and stayed, and a thousand people who had never once sung their parts where anybody could hear them sang them anyway, a quarter-tone out, on purpose, in public, for three low waters." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her looking at the water and not at anybody)* "…It hasn't stopped being the first line. It's stopped being **mine.** It's a **story**, and the sea tells it to whoever's listening, and it just told four people on a shelf with their hands in a bowl, and the reason I'm not singing right now is that **singing it would make it about me again.** — Coral. Write this one down for the horn, because the realm of the Barrens is going to want the exact words and I want them to have them: **we did not do it alone. We did it with them.**"

## Page 4 — *The Flood at the Wrong Hour*

### P4.1 — The gate, and a tide-clock that is wrong (half page)
**Art:** And the volume's biggest physical event is drawn as **a morning of the wrong arithmetic**, and the art team's rule: **no spectacle, no surge, no wall of water — a tide that is twenty minutes early and four feet higher than the marks, on a coast that reads its own tide-clock at breakfast.** Setting: the sea-gate at noon, the drowned arch, the stair, the tide-flats, the stockade, the free slat with its queue of nine, the company's pier below with its stove lit; the **tide-clock's float** riding **above its highest mark**, drawn so that the reader can see the marks and the float and the difference; and, on the pier, the company's clerk standing on the counter's chair with his boots off because the water is over the boards, and his board still up. Four frames: **(1)** the gate, wide, and the *water*: at the top of its rise, flat, no foam, no wave, the arch's crossing clear where the crossing should be under two feet of tide; **(2)** the tide-clock, close: the float, the marks, the day's date on the slat, and the art team must draw **the marks themselves as the chapter's evidence** — a year's worth of pencil and cut lines, and one ink line above them all, new; **(3)** the company's pier: the water over the boards, the chair, the boots, the board, the clerk; and the reader must be able to read the board: ***WE CARRY. *It took one morning of the wrong tide, a bowl of seawater on a shelf, three hundred and sixty-two notches, one ring of coral a hand's breadth wide, and four hundred years of a realm that had kept its own song in pieces.
