# AURELION · Volume 6 — Chapter 35: "The Chorus Counts Itself"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc F: "The First Song" — the Re-Oath of Thalassa is called, and the sea's oldest law runs into the newest employer in the world.** The sea's law is exact and has always been exact: **the Chorus-True is a thousand voices, and the Re-Oath is sung by all of them or it is not sung at all.** Nobody has ever had to test it, because until this year nobody in Thalassa had anywhere else to be. But the realm spent a year quiet — the first song had stopped reaching the surface, the chorus had nothing to sing, the reef's ring did not grow, and the offices of a chorus with no song are offices with no work. So the year the sea went silent, the shore filled up with a company selling askings, and the company needed voices: honest contracts, good wages, posted rates, nine feathers a sung asking at eleven stations along the coast. **Fifty-nine of the Chorus-True are on that payroll.** The count comes up nine hundred and forty-one. What follows is not a fight and not a rescue, and it must not be either: the contracts are lawful, the wages are good, the work is honest, and the company's clerk has already been told once by a court what he may and may not carry. The chapter's work is the scribe's work: **to define a shift and a voice as two different things**, so that a chorus member may be a paid singer and still stand in the Chorus-True — because a shift is work and a voice in the chorus is a person, and the sea's own finding three says *person* means a person. And then the chapter's heart, which is nobody's to argue: one of the fifty-nine comes home and **refuses**, out loud, on the record, with her wages folded in her hand — she sang a year at empty water and she will not swear the realm did not do that — and the sea's law has to answer her. Its answer is the volume's deepest exchange: **a voice present by refusal is present** — the doctrine the Barrens cut into a gatepost in Chapter 23 (*the record reads both ways*), adopted at last by the oldest law in the world, written into the reef-roll with the realm's own article cited beside it. The sea learns a land-law at the bottom of the water, the count comes to a thousand anyway, and what Ch. 36 has to sing is a chorus that is finally, honestly, *all there.*

## Page 1 — *The Call*

### P1.1 — The reef's wet chamber, and the call (half page)
**Art:** And the volume's oldest music comes back on, and the art team's rule: **the call is not a summons, it is a *counting-in*.** Setting: the reef's wet chamber above the water — the ribbed stone, the **reef-roll** on its stand, the **bone chair** empty beside it (the Thalarch is on the shore, and the chair's absence must be drawn as *furniture missing*, not as a symbol), the shell-lamps, and the **three voices** — Tide, Current, Stream — standing in the water at the chamber's lip with **Coral** at the roll and **Shell** at the tide-step. The call itself: canon-obedient and unmagical — the sea's call is **sung**, three parts, down the shell-roads, and the art team must draw it *travelling*: a single continuous panel of the call going out along the reef's channels and up the shore-road's tidal culverts, drawn as ring-script moving through water and **thinning into the air at the shore-line**, where the sea's voice cannot follow. Four frames: **(1)** the wet chamber, the roll, the empty chair; **(2)** the three voices taking the call, and the art team must draw the **count's first sound** — three notes, in the sea's notation, low, unwritten, because the call is older than the roll; **(3)** the call travelling: reef channels, the chimney, the tide-culverts, the shore; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **the shore-line**, and the sea's call arriving *as water-sound only*, and above it, on the road, the realm's own apparatus catching it: a free slat, a lamp, and a reader with her head up.
**Coral (flat, to the roll, entering the call, and the scribe's line is the chapter's whole legal situation in four sentences):** "\u2014 Entered: **the Re-Oath of Thalassa is called.** The roll is open for the count, the count is the whole of the law, and the law is the old one and there is no newer one: **the Chorus-True is a thousand voices, and the oath is sung by all of them or it is not sung at all.** — I will say the rest of it here, at the roll, before we begin, because I will not say it later where it can sound like an excuse: it is a year since the first song stopped reaching the surface, and in that year this realm has had **no work in it for a chorus.** The reef did not grow a ring. The Shell sat. The offices of a chorus with nothing to sing are offices with nothing in them, and a voice that has nothing to sing and a family to feed goes where the wages are. — Scribe's note, entered: ***the count begins at nine hundred and forty-one.***"

## Page 2 — *Nine Hundred and Forty-One, and Fifty-Nine Gaps*

### P2.1 — The rings, and the gaps (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's central device arrives, and the art team's rules about it are absolute for the rest of the volume: **the chorus assembles in the reef's own rings** — the memory-reef's concentric growth-rings, one ring a year, acres of coral, and the chorus standing in them at low water, ring by ring, in the order of the count — and **fifty-nine places stand empty**, drawn as *water and bare coral*, at the ring's sixth and seventh courses, and the reader must be able to **count the gaps** in a wide panel if they try. Present: **Coral-Kin** and the krakenkin tending the reef's rim (the rim grown since Ch. 32 — canon continuity); **Mnemothe** at the knot at the ring's centre, eye open, saying nothing; **Pearl** at the ring's edge with the count; **Shell** at the tide-step calling; the **tide-guard** along the outer ring with tridents covered; the **reef-roll** on its stand at the ring's north point; and the **Nine** on the dry side of the reef's chimney ledge as witnesses, in the composition the reader knows. Four frames: **(1)** the reef from above at low water: the rings, the standing chorus, **and the gaps**, with the rim's new pale line at the outer course; **(2)** Mnemothe at the knot, and the art team must draw the **whole ring and not a creature** — the elder attends; **(3)** the count being called: Shell calling a course's number, and the chorus answering in the sea's formal grammar, and the art team must draw the answering as **waves of notation** moving ring by ring; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Morgatha**, on the chimney ledge, in the dry party's line, **counting** — and the art team must give her the corner: the chapter's count is drawn from here, and it is drawn **as her tally**, because the reader has had this device since the Ulgar and it belongs to the person who cannot stop using it.
**Morgatha (flat, on the ledge, to Viv, and the war-chief's line is the chapter's method stated by the only person on the reef who counts for a living):** "\u2014 Nine hundred and forty-one. — My realm counts things, that's what we are, we cut a tally in a horn every time a thing happens and we've been doing it for nine hundred years and there is a number in there for the day my *brother* went out. So I'll tell you what I know about counting that the sea's about to learn the hard way: **a count isn't a measure of what's there. It's a measure of what you're willing to be seen missing.** Nine hundred and forty-one looks like a lot of chorus. It's not. It's a *hole* with a chorus round it, and every one of us on this ledge can see the edges." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her looking at the sixth ring)* "…Fifty-nine. Right. Where are they?"

### P2.2 — Where they are: eleven stations, fifty-nine voices (half page)
**Art:** And the answer is a *payroll*, and the art team's rule: **draw it as honest work in good weather, because that is what it is.** The setting: the eleven sounding-stations along the coast that the company keeps for its **sung askings** — the board from Ch. 33 (*ASKINGS AT THE SEA-GATE. WRITTEN OR SUNG.*) made real: a stone stair down to the water at each, a counter, a stamped rate, a queue, an **asker's slip with the question written on it**, and a **singer** standing at the water's edge singing somebody else's sentence out over the tide. The art team must draw the *work* precisely: the singer's stance (professional, unhurried), the slip held flat, the little brass bell the station rings when the asking is carried, and the **receipt** stamped at the counter — ***ASKED. CARRIED. HEARD. \u2014 not ours, not held*** (canon from Ch. 34, and the honest terms of the trade). And the art team must draw the other half of the truth: **the water does not answer.** Not once, in any of the eleven panels; the sea is silent for paying customers exactly as it is silent for everybody, and the chapter must not editorialise about it. Four frames: **(1)** a station, wide: the stair, the counter, the queue, the singer, the water; **(2)** the slip, close and legible: *my brother's boat went out in the spring; I want to know if the deep has him* — and the singer reading it, and the art team must draw her face **kind**, because she is; **(3)** the bell, the stamp, the receipt, and the queue's next asker stepping up; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **the roster** — the company's shift-slate, cut with fifty-nine marks, pinned at a counter's edge, with the clerk's own note in the margin legible: *singers, contracted to the company; singings, at the company's call, at the waters named; wages posted; terms by the week.* And the count in the corner, in Morgatha's hand: **nine hundred and forty-one.**
**The company's clerk (flat, at a station counter, to Viv, and the antagonist's opening position is not a defence, it is a *statement of fact* and he must deliver it without a shred of triumph):** "\u2014 Fifty-nine, and I'll give you the roster before you ask, because you'll get it anyway and I'd rather be the one who handed it over. — Read the language with me, advocate: *singers, contracted to the company; singings, at the company's call, at the waters named; wages posted; terms by the week.* That is the whole instrument. I did not write it to catch a chorus; I wrote it because a business needs to know when its people are in. — And I'll say the part that will make you dislike me: **your realm's quiet year is my recruiting office.** A chorus with no song to sing is fifty-nine trained voices standing idle on a shore with families, and I pay posted wages for work they are good at. If you want to argue that, argue it with the *year*." *(a beat)* "…But I've been in one of your courts. So go on and argue. I'll read it out in front of whoever you like, and I'll do it standing up."

## Page 3 — *A Shift and a Voice*

### P3.1 — The contract, read aloud on the shore (2/3 page)
**Art:** And the chapter's legal page, and the art team's rule: **the realm's habit is now the whole coast's habit — everything is read aloud, so the argument is a reading.** Setting: the shore road below the free slat, at the chapter's noon: a table borrowed from the company's waiting room (canon: they have furniture now, and the detail should sting *and* be pleasant), the contract open on it, the roster (the shift-slate, with its fifty-nine marks) beside it, the free slat's reader in the front row of the audience with the day's slip still in her hand, the shore's people gathered the way this realm gathers for anything (nine, then nineteen, then forty), Marra's boat tied below, and the ship's bell from the pier ringing the hour like it does. Four frames: **(1)** the table, the contract, the roster, the crowd — and the art team must draw the crowd's *grandmothers and children* in the front row, because this is the saga's economics and the saga's economics happen in front of children; **(2)** the clerk reading his own contract aloud, in full, at the realm's custom, and the art team must draw him **reading it well**, because he is good at his job; **(3)** **Viv**, taking it apart: standing, no notes, and the art team must draw the page's key action — she lays the contract down beside the **roll's call slip** and puts one finger on each, and the reader must see that the two documents name **two different waters**; **(4)** the crowd, and one detail the art team must place: **a station-singer** — drawn, unnamed, still in her work coat, off shift, standing at the town's edge of the crowd and *listening*, because it is her contract being read.
**Viv (flat, at the table, and this is the chapter's thesis, delivered as a working advocate's close reading — short sentences, no rhetoric, every clause turned over once):** "\u2014 Clause two. *Singings, at the company's call.* — Whose call is the Re-Oath? Not the company's. The roll's. It came by shell, by tide-guard, by the sea's own three voices, and the company has never sung in its life. — Clause three. *At the waters named.* — Name me the waters in the call, clerk. *(and the art team must draw him looking at the call slip and not answering)* The call names the **reef**, and the reef is not on your roster; it is nobody's counter. — And clause four, the one I've read four times because I wanted it to be worse than it is: *wages posted; terms by the week.* **A week.** Not a voice, not a person, not an office: **a week of singings.** — So here is the finding I am going to ask a court to write, and I am asking you to write it first, in your own hand, at this table: **you bought shifts. You never bought a chorus place, because a chorus place was never yours to be offered** — and the reason is the sea's own finding three, the one your employers' charter ran aground on eleven days ago: *person* means a person. **A shift is work. A voice in the Chorus-True is a person.** You may buy the first for nine feathers and post the rates on a board. The second is not for sale at any price and never will be, and if you disagree, then say so now, at this table, in front of the reader, and we will go and stand in a dry room together and find out."

### P3.2 — The clerk's arithmetic, and carriage entered (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's mercy, second movement, and the art team's rule: **the concession is a sum, done on paper, by the antagonist, in front of witnesses.** The clerk asks for the contract's own paper, turns it over, and does the arithmetic of a stand-off: fifty-nine singers, one hour, wages posted by the week, and the week's terms are **hours worked** — and an hour at the reef is not an hour at a station, so the honest entry is: **the hour is worked and paid.** He writes it out, reads it aloud, and signs it; and then he goes past what anybody asked him for, because this saga's antagonists are *professionals*: he asks the sea to enter the company's sung askings in the reef-roll **as carriage** — a trade named in the sea's own record — because he stood in the sea's court eleven days ago and heard the court say the word *carriage* out loud, and he wants the trade in the book where his clerks can point at it, and he will pay for the entry the way his station pays for a stamp. Four frames: **(1)** the clerk at the table with the contract turned over and a pencil, and the sum drawn as a working man's arithmetic; **(2)** the stand-off slip, lettered: ***stand-off, entered: fifty-nine singers; one hour; wages paid; the hour is worked; the waters are the reef, at the sea's call, and not the company's***; **(3)** the clerk reading it aloud and signing it, and the art team must draw the crowd's reaction — **not applause**: the realm's habit after a reading is silence, and then one person in the front row nodding; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Viv and the clerk, both of them standing**, and the art team must draw the exact second after the thing is settled: two professionals, a table, a paper, and no anger anywhere in the panel — and the count in the corner, still **nine hundred and forty-one.**
**The company's clerk (flat, at the table, writing, and the antagonist's arithmetic is the chapter's second-best line and it must be delivered as a *correction of his own boss*):** "\u2014 Six feathers an hour, posted, times fifty-nine, times one hour: three hundred and fifty-four feathers, and I will enter it as **worked**, because it *is* worked — an hour where my singers are standing in the sea is an hour they are not at my counter, and the company pays for hours, that's the entire company. — And now the other thing, and this one's mine and not the board's, so don't tell the board." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him writing the second entry in a different hand, smaller, at the foot of the page)* "…*Carriage, entered: the company's sung askings, carried to the water at posted rates; the water owes nothing; the company holds nothing.* — I want that in the sea's own book. Not because it saves me anything. Because in a hundred years there'll be two boards on this shore and a woman with a brush will be deciding which one to believe, and I'd like the book on the *wet* side to have said **what we are** in our own words, while I'm alive to make sure of it." *(a beat)* "…Also, if the court's inclined: the fares. My singers buy their own seats home. I've put the hour's wage in their hands and Marra's posting the rate on her gunwale, and I've told her that if she discounts one feather for any of them, the company will hear about it and think it's a *gift*, and a gift is what people give to somebody they've decided is poor. She says she'll not discount. She also says she's raising the rate tomorrow, and I believe her."

## Page 4 — *The Recall*

### P4.1 — Slips, stamps, and full boats (half page)
**Art:** And the recall is staged as **paperwork and getting up early**, and the art team's rule: **no parade.** The setting: dawn at the pier and the shore road; the company's eleven stations' rosters coming in by runner; the stand-off slips being stamped at the counters; **Marra's boat** at the pier with her timetable chalked on the board (six crossings, **full**), her second-boat rival tied up and unused with its crew standing around (the art team must draw them *helpfully* — they take passengers too, at the same rate, and the chapter must not make them villains); the free slat's queue letting the singers go ahead of them; and the crowd at the pier **not cheering**, because the realm's habit for anything that matters is to stand and watch it happen quietly. Four frames: **(1)** the pier at dawn: two boats, a queue, chalk, rosters; **(2)** a station counter with the stand-off being stamped, and the art team must draw the singer's own hand taking the **wage** and folding it into a pocket, and then *separately* buying a ticket — the two actions must be two panels, because the whole dignity of the page is that they are two transactions; **(3)** Marra at her gunwale with her bucket and her chalk, and the reader must be able to read her board: ***rate: posted. no discounts. no gifts. children pay half.***; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **the runner** — the company's youngest board-clerk, drawn, unnamed — going up the coast road with the stamped slips in a satchel, because the count is being *filed* as well as called.
**Marra (flat, at her gunwale, to a customer who has just tried to pay for nine singers as a kindness, and the selkie's line is the chapter's ethics in three sentences):** "\u2014 No. — You can buy your own seat and you can buy your neighbour's if you like, that's your business and the company's. You cannot buy *theirs*, because they've got wages in their pocket this morning for the first time in a year and you'd take the morning off them and call it generosity. — Now: rate's on the board, no discounts, no gifts, children pay half, and the chap with the second boat behind me is sailing at the same time at the same rate and if you'd rather go with him I'll *tell you* which shoal to avoid, because I'm not a *pirate*, I'm a *service*." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her spitting over the side and grinning all at once)* "…Nine singers to the stair, four to the breakwater, and the old one —" *(a beat)* "— the old one says she'll walk. Whatever. Let her walk. It's eleven miles and the tide's wrong and she's the fifty-ninth and I'll not be the one to hurry her."
