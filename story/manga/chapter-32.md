# AURELION · Volume 6 — Chapter 32: "Then Come In"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc F: "The First Song" — the deep answers the answer, and the sea invents the one thing its law has never had: an ear.** Nine days after the report went home, the Nine stand on the tide-flats at the **flood**, which is the hour the sea-gate is drowned and the hour nobody in Thalassa has ever tried to hold a session — and at the top of the drowned stair, in the swell, the chorus-parliament convenes **the flood session**: the first session in the history of the deep held not to *bind* but to *listen*. Because that is the law the sea has just learned about itself: at low water the Shell holds a voice; at flood the water holds one — **the Shell is a mouth at low water and an ear at high.** What the ear hears is the answer to the note a keeper sang down a tube at Ch. 31's end: the deep does not repeat the first line. It sings the line **after** it — the first song's **second** line, which nobody in four hundred thousand years has heard, and which the sea can only sing **half** of, because the rest is in a key the water does not have. And what the second line *is* — the sentence the oldest law in the world says after it has said *nothing that is heard is lost* — turns out to be an invitation four words long, and the whole volume turns on it: ***"Then come in."*** A law, and then a door. What the chapter then does with those four words is the reason Volume 6 exists: the sea **cannot write** — a song is *heard* or it is lost, and Coral's whole office is singing things back — so for the first time in the history of the realms the deep **asks the land to do the one thing the land is for**. *Keep it.* Not hold it, not own it, not file it: **keep** it, in the realm's own hands, in the realm's own script, read aloud at every circle — because a sentence in a key nobody has, held by the only species that writes things down, is the first law in nine realms that needs *both* a singer and a reader. And the chapter's second movement is the answer to that ask, happening everywhere at once, because the realm of the collar has been waiting nine hundred miles for a law it can serve: **the flood reading** — at the flood hour, once a day, at every post and every door and every well, the realm reads something aloud, because that is what it has instead of a voice.
> **Cast:** **The road party** (nine; **Nereia** — *the keeper-Above's first act, and the flask emptied into the sea*, **Viv** — *the first person in the saga asked to write something down instead of arguing it*, **Kharza** — *who says the fifth article out loud before anybody has written it down*, **Kaelen** — *one plain question*, **Fenn** — *the tide-clock at the top mark, drawn*, **Sprocket** — *the sixth entry*, **Azrael** — *the chapter's closing line about ears*, **Bramble** — *doors that say yes*, **Morgatha** — *the count's rest at the top of the water*) + **Marginalia** (the cart, on the flats, unimpressed by the sea — again) · **Captain Roderick Vane** (**his one silent panel** — a lead-line, and the depth read by hand, as the water comes up the pier) · **Ferry-Captain Marra** (03a; the boat *launched* at last, and one line about never having been so glad to be wrong) · **the chorus-parliament** (03b: **Tide**, **Current**, **Stream** — three voices; **Coral** the scribe; **Pearl** the judge; **Shell** the herald) · **the Thalarch Maelis** (03a: voiceless; carried to the stair-head in the swell; her chair **set on the third step** and her hand given for a vote) · **Song-Part** (03b; the keeper who has carried a ruler's part for eleven years and hears, at last, a line that needs *two* voices) · **the memory-reef** (**Mnemothe**, **Coral-Kin**) · **the Deep-Listening post** (**Whalesong**: the phrase carved into the edge of a notch-board) · **the realm** (the nine posts' readers, unnamed; the school's second term, unnamed; **the deputy** at the edge; **the gate-clerk**) · **the founder** (***"…Again."** — his only word in two chapters*) and **Oros** (**the one who reads to an empty chair, on purpose**).
> **Locations / threads:** the tide-flats and the drowned sea-gate at flood → the stair-head, in the swell (the flood session) → the Deep-Listening post at dawn → the memory-reef (a lip grows) → nine posts, one hour (the flood reading) → the school in the well → the camp (the well, the gate, the map-room) → the **second mark** → the flats again, and the flask.
> **Arc beat:** Ch. 32 must do three things and it must do them in the saga's own grammar — *institutions, not miracles.* **One — the ear.** The sea's law grows by one clause, and the clause is an *absence* that the sea noticed about itself: sessions at low water bind; nobody has ever held a session at flood *to hear what the water is carrying.* Pearl enters it; the tide-guard carry the Thalarch's chair up the stair; the roll takes a **tenth notch** — and the notch is the first one in the roll's history that was not cut for a *law*, but for a **listening**. **Two — the second line.** The deep answers the note with the line *after* the first line, which is the whole hook paid: not an explanation, not a voice, not a creature — **a sentence**, four words, half of it in a key the sea cannot finish. And then the ask: *write it down.* The realm of readers is, for the first time in five volumes, asked to do the thing it does **by somebody who cannot** — and the chapter must let that land as the honour it is, because the sea has just told the land what the land is *for*. **Three — the flood reading.** The realm's answer is characteristic and enormous and costs nothing: at the flood hour, every door in the realm reads something aloud. Nine posts. The school's lower step. The camp's gate. The map-room, where an old sage reads the survey aloud to an empty chair while a man in a grey coat stands at the back and listens, and says, when it is done, the only word he has said in two chapters — *…Again.* The chapter ends with the sea and the land holding the same four words by different means: the sea singing half of them, the land writing all of them, and neither of them able to do it alone.
> **Motifs advanced:** the reading (**the deep's sentence, read aloud at a post**), the door (**a law that ends in an invitation; doors that say yes**), the record (**the roll's tenth notch; the horn's first entry of a foreign *sentence*; Sprocket's sixth entry**), the count (**a rest at the top of the water**), the carrying (**a song that needs a singer and a writer**).
> **Not in this chapter:** **Titanvein** (unbarred, unstaged) · the stone-song (`#e07a3f` zero) · **the Leviathan** (*never drawn, never voiced, never named as answering*; the chapter's one permitted near-miss is Whalesong's sentence that the sound is **not one voice and not a shape**: *"that's not the whale. That's what the whale is standing on."*) · **Aurelia Vox's duet-verdict** (*still held — and the chapter deliberately does not use her, because the verdict that frees the team from sea-law is a later chapter's business*) · **Elune** and **Coraline** (held) · **the fourth cell** (unmentioned) · **Irongate Vane** (unmentioned) · **the twins' letter** (Fenn's column stays blank) · **Wren's 03a line** · **Kaelen's pilot uses** · the Scar-Reading Chord · the white · the *empty* inside the mountain · Orvane's garden · Khazur's seal-or-fight · the Re-Oath vote · and **no new named characters** (rule 7): the posts' readers, the school's class, the samplers, the tide-guard, the krakenkin and the carriers are drawn and unnamed.

## Page 1 — *Flood*

### P1.1 — The flats, and a boat that is finally waterborne (half page)
**Art:** The tide-flats at the flood, and the art team's first payoff: **Marra's boat comes off the wagon.** The flats at high water are a moving sheet of shallow sea with the road's line still visible under it; the wagon stands hub-deep; the boat is in the water at last, tied to the cart's tail with the same rope that has crossed two volumes, and **Marra** is standing in it with a pole, grinning like a person who has been right about something for nine hundred miles. The gate itself is **drowned** — the arch's top two feet above the surface, the tide-mark column under water, and the reader must *see* the marks go under, because the column is the chapter's clock as of this page. Four frames: **(1)** the flats at flood, wide, the road's line under water and the boat launched; **(2)** the wagon hub-deep and the mule *furious* about it; **(3)** the gate, drowned, with its column of marks under the surface and only the top marks dry; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the party on the **pier of rock** beside the gate, and **Nereia** at the edge, markings *attention*, and her flask in her hand — and the art team must draw the flask **full**, and her not drinking.
**Marra (flat, from the boat, pole in hand, and the selkie's register is a schedule coming true):** "\u2014 Nine days parked at low-water marks and *this* is the hour the whole thing's been waiting for: **flood.** Gate's drowned, stair's full, and the Shell's got four feet of water in it — which means nobody's going down and *anything* that wants to come up can." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her poling the boat in a circle, delighted and professional at once)* "…Right. My boat's on water, my bucket's on the gunwale, and I've been telling you for a week the sea's got a *timetable*. Everybody be polite: **we're guests at high water and there's no rulebook for that.**"

### P1.2 — The stair-head (half page)
**Art:** And the first strange thing of the chapter, staged *flatly*: at the head of the drowned stair, in the swell, the **parliament is coming up** — not swimming past, not surfacing to stare: **walking up the steps**, three tide-guard first with their tridents *still covered*, then **Song-Part** with her arm through the Thalarch's, and then — the image the art team must make the chapter's signature — **the Thalarch's reed-and-bone chair, carried up the stair by four merfolk and set down on the third step, where the water stands at a hand's depth**, and Maelis, voiceless, sitting in it, in the swell, with the tide running around the legs of it. Four frames: **(1)** the tide-guard coming up the stair, tridents covered, water up to their chests; **(2)** the chair carried, level, the bearers' faces careful; **(3)** Maelis seated on the third step; **(4)** and the page's last frame, from behind the party: **Coral** the scribe wading up with her coral tablet **held over her head in one hand**, and the tide-clock's float visible out through the drowned arch, resting at the **top mark.** In the corner of the frame where the count lives: **eleven.**
**Coral (flat, shouting over the water, dripping, and the scribe's complaint is the chapter's funniest line and it must be delivered with total dignity):** "\u2014 If anybody asks: this has never been done. Sessions are at *low water* — that's not a custom, that's a **wall**: the Shell floods to the fifth course and you can't hear a verdict through moving water and *nothing in the roll says a session can be held at flood.*" *(a beat, and the art team must draw her tablet, held up, dripping off her elbows)* "…And then Pearl did the arithmetic on what we *heard* at dawn, and the arithmetic says **bind at low water, listen at high**, and the arithmetic is going to be in that roll by the end of the hour whether or not the roll likes it."

## Page 2 — *The Session That Has Never Been Held*

### P2.1 — A session with an ear in it (2/3 page)
**Art:** The flood session, and the art team's staging rule: **the tide is the room.** The session is held on the drowned stair: the parliament stands in water from the waist down on the steps; the three voices stand above the waterline on the stair-head's stones; the Thalarch's chair is on the third step; the tide-guard hold a **rope line** across the stair's mouth so that nothing floats away — including, as the art team's joke, **Marginalia's plate**, which the cart has sent down for the occasion and which the tide-guard have tied to the rope out of respect. And the composition's whole argument is the water: **at the top mark the stair is not a room, it is a *throat*.** Four frames: **(1)** the wide staging, from above: the stair, the ring of stones, the water, the chair on the third step, the nine on the gate's pier above; **(2)** **Pearl**, standing waist-deep, and the tide going *past* her, convening the session — and the lettering team must set it in the ring-script, in the sea's own formal grammar; **(3)** the three voices taking the chord that *opens* a session, and for the first time in the saga the art team must draw a chord **going out** — the lettering's lines drawn *away* from the singers, not around them, because the session's purpose is out there; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the water, and the art team must leave three-quarters of the panel as moving water — **nothing in it, no shape, no suggestion** — with the top two marks of the tide-clock dry above it on the arch, and the reader alone with the hour.
**Pearl (flat, convening, and the judge of the deep does the whole new thing in one breath and the lettering must show the *roll* being amended as she speaks):** "\u2014 ***Convened: the flood session. First of its kind; entered as such. Purpose: not to bind. The Shell is a mouth at low water. At flood it is an ear — and the roll's first entry in four hundred thousand years that was cut for a* listening ***is cut today.***" *(a beat, and the art team must draw her turning to the party on the pier, and the sea running between them)* "…Nine, on the stone. You brought the note; we sang one back down the tube nine days ago on a keeper's judgement, and the water's been *answering* since — a finger at a gully, a finger at a well, and last night, at the post, a **phrase.** If the deep has something to say to the *surface*, then the surface should be in the room — and today the room is *this*, so stand where you're standing and don't fall in."

### P2.2 — Kaelen's question, and the answer's shape (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's exposition is *given* to the plainest character in the cast, because the saga's rule is that the reader's question is asked by whoever has the least patience for ceremony. Four frames: **(1)** Kaelen, on the pier, coat hood back, asking his question — and the art team must draw **all the sea's officials turning to look at him at once**, which is the joke: nine hundred miles of law and the smith asks the obvious thing; **(2)** Coral answering, drawn as a diagram: she wipes a slate and draws — **a line, then a line under it** — the first line and the *next* one; **(3)** the tide-guard, listening; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Whalesong's marks**, arriving — because the post is a day's swim from the gate and the parliament sent for the board, and the page's last panel must be the **notch-board's edge**, carried up the stair by two krakenkin, with four cuts on it that are *not* notches: a **line, a line under it, four small marks, and a bar**.
**Kaelen (flat, and the mortal smith asks the question the reader is holding, and it must be asked without any cleverness at all):** "\u2014 Sorry. One thing, and then I'll be quiet: is it a *word* or a *tune*? Because a tune I can hum and a word I'd have to be *told*, and everything you've done since we got here has been *tunes*, and everything we do is *words*, and I'd like to know which kind of thing we're standing in front of." *(a beat)* "…That's the whole question. It's not a *deep* question. I fix things for a living."
**Coral (flat, chalk in hand, and the scribe's answer is the volume's premise in three sentences):** "\u2014 *Both*, and that's the trouble and it's your trouble now. **The law is a tune.** The *first line* is a tune — that's why it survived four hundred thousand years, because a tune can't be filed, only *carried*. But the thing coming up that tube since dawn isn't a tune, smith. **It's got *syntax*.** Something down there is putting words in an order — and the sea can *hear* the order and can't *write* it, and every scribe in this realm is about to have a very bad morning about that, so *stand by.*"

## Page 3 — *The Post, at Dawn*

### P3.1 — What came up the tube (half page)
**Art:** And the Deep-Listening post, at dawn, nine days of nothing after the note, and the art team's instruction is that the whole page must be **one man in a place where a man has been for a hundred years**, and everything that changes is *small*. The shelf; the mast; the tube; the notch-board with its three hundred and sixty-one notches, the cut one, and now — on the board's **edge**, where he has never cut anything — four fresh marks, carved, the wood still pale. Four frames: **(1)** dawn on the shelf, the tube, the man; **(2)** **the moment**: the tube's mouth, and the faint haze from Ch. 31 blooming into a **thread of moving water** — drawn as a single clean line of current going *up* the shell's throat, against the tide, and the art team must draw no sound, no glow, no shape, nothing else; **(3)** Whalesong's hand, flat on the shell, and his face — and the rule the art team must obey absolutely: **no wonder on it.** A hundred years of manners does not do wonder. It does **attention**; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the knife, the edge of the board, and the four marks.
**Whalesong (flat, to the tube, and the listener gives the chapter's exposition and it must be *technical*, because that is how he has stayed sane for a century):** "\u2014 Nine days. Then this. — Not a note: a **phrase.** Four marks' worth, and the *last* one is a bar — a *rest*, and I'll tell you what that means because it's the only music I've got: it means the phrase **expects an answer**, and the answer's not mine to give, because the answer's supposed to come from a **second voice**, and I have been one voice at one tube since the day they gave me the job." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him carving the fourth mark very carefully)* "…And I'll say the other thing once, to you and to the water, and then I'll not say it again. I've listened all my life. **That was not one voice. That wasn't a shape either.** Whatever is down there, it isn't the whale — **it's what the whale is standing on.** I've cut the marks. Take them up the stair."

### P3.2 — The walk up the stair with a piece of wood (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's humility beat: **two krakenkin carry a piece of wood nine hundred feet up a drowned stair** because the sea's own archive is an animal and the only *object* in this realm worth carrying is a hundred-year-old board with cuts in it. Four frames: **(1)** the krakenkin on the stair, the board held level between them, tide-guard clearing the way; **(2)** the board passing hand to hand up the steps — and the art team must draw every merfolk on the stair touching it once, the way a queue touches a coffin; **(3)** Coral, wading to meet it with her eyes closed, memorising the marks before she has even looked; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the board set down beside the Thalarch's chair on the third step, and **Maelis** — voiceless, in the swell — putting **one hand flat on it**, and the art team must draw her chair's legs in the water and her hand on the wood and nothing else.
**Song-Part (flat, to the party, and the keeper's line is the chapter's ache, said twice a century and never resolved):** "\u2014 She can't sing it and she can't read it, and she's the only one of us who was *there* when the first line was first sung — so she does the thing she can do with a sound: **she holds it.** That's her hand on the board. That's the whole of the Thalarch's part in this, and I've carried her voice for eleven years to be the mouth of it, and today the mouth gets to *listen*." *(a beat)* "…Sing it when you're ready, scribe. She'll keep it steady."

## Page 4 — *Then Come In*

### P4.1 — The chorus hears it (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's — and Volume 6's — central page, and the art team's brief: **the second line must be heard, not seen, and the page must be built so the reader hears it arrive.** Sequence in four beats on one page: **(1)** Coral, on the third step, singing the four marks back — a phrase in the ring-script with a **bar at the end**, sung *up* at the drowned arch, the sea's formal "who is it"; **(2)** the water in the arch, and the tide-clock's top mark, and **the answer**: drawn as the sea's own lettering, rising through the arch from below — and the lettering team must set it in a *different* ring-script weight than the sea's, the way a hand differs from a *print*, so that the reader can see at a glance that this is not the chorus singing; **(3)** the three voices catching it — Tide, Current, Stream — and the art team must draw the **failure** first and honestly: they take the phrase and *lose* the middle of it, the chord going thin in the same panel, because half of it is in a key the sea does not have; **(4)** and the page's last frame, wide, from above the arch at the top of the flood: the chorus in the water, the nine on the pier, the drowned gate, the flats, the boat, and the whole sea around them drawn as **light and one line**, and — the art team's one permitted liberty all chapter — **the moon's reflection as a single stroke on the water pointing out through the gate**, because the reader should have one thing to look at that is not anybody's face.
**Tide (singing, then flat, and the chorus-lead's failure report is the most important sentence of the volume):** "\u2014 ***Nothing that is heard is lost*** — that's the first line, and under it —" *(and the art team must draw the chord taking, and then the break)* "\u2014 *and under it there's a second line and* **we can't finish it.** Four words of it come up clean and the rest goes somewhere we don't have **keys** for. Three of us, four hundred thousand years of practice, and we are standing in the water singing a **hole.**" *(a beat, and the art team must draw the three of them looking up through the arch at the nine)* "…So here's what we've got, and it's the whole of what we've got: the deep has answered the answer, and the answer is **four words and a door**, and the rest of it belongs to whoever can *write things down* — which, as of about an hour ago, is **your problem.**"

### P4.2 — The second line, given words once (half page)
**Art:** And the four words are set on the page — and the art team's rule is *identical to Ch. 30 P9.1* and must be obeyed to the letter, because the saga gives these sentences to the page exactly once each: the sea's **ring-script** on the top line and the mortal **plain type** beneath it, no emphasis, no italics, no ornament, and around them the flood: the stair, the chair on the third step, the board, the chorus, the nine, the rope-line, Marginalia's plate tied to it, and the water climbing the arch. Four frames: **(1)** Coral, chalk on slate, writing the four words *in the sea's script* for the first time in history — and the art team must draw her *shaking*; **(2)** the slate held up to the Thalarch, and Maelis reading it, and Song-Part's hand on the chair's arm; **(3)** **the four words**, large, alone in their frame, ring-script above plain type; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the party on the pier — and the art team must draw each of the nine with one reaction and no balloons at all: Kharza's hand flat on the slate she has carried since the Barrens, Viv already *writing*, Fenn's hat in his hands, Sprocket mid-entry, Morgatha's count stopped in the corner (the corner shows the count and then **nothing**), Azrael's hands in his pockets, Bramble crouched at the pier's edge staring straight down into the water, Nereia's markings *rising*, Kaelen looking at his own hands.
**Coral (flat, reading her own slate aloud to the whole sea, and the moment the volume has been walking toward since Ch. 3, delivered as *procedure*, which is why it lands):** "\u2014 ***Nothing that is heard is lost.*** *…**Then come in.***" *(a beat, and the art team must draw the tide moving, and nobody moving)* "…That's the second line. That's *all* of the second line that comes up. The law and the door, and the door's four words long, and — Pearl, **write this down or tell me to write it down**, because a law that ends in an invitation can't be *bound* and it can't be *held* and it can't be **sung back** by anybody who hasn't got the whole key, and I have been a scribe for nineteen years and I am **not** going to be the person who loses the second line of the first song because our entire realm has no **pens.**"

## Page 5 — *The Ask, and the Lip*

### P5.1 — The reef's lip grows (half page)
**Art:** And the reef, ninety feet under, at the flood — the art team's rule: **the reef does this without anybody watching it happen**, and the chapter shows the aftermath, which is stronger. Four frames: **(1)** the memory-reef from above, through its chimney, at flood: the rings, the dark knot at the centre, and the outermost lip — the thin, low, level one from Ch. 30 — **now carrying a fresh, paler line of growth along its whole rim**, the first growth in a year; **(2)** **Coral-Kin**, tentacles moving along the new rim the way a gardener's hands move along a hedge, and the art team must draw the *care* in it; **(3)** **Mnemothe**, at the centre, and his eye on the new line — no line of dialogue necessary, and **none is given**; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the reef-roll beside the bone chair, above the water, in the wet chamber — Coral's tablet hung on its hook, and on the horn strip, **one more notch; and beside it, cut in a way the roll has never been cut, a small square hole**: the sea's first entry that is not a note.
**Pearl (flat, from the third step, reporting to the party, and the judge's summary is the volume's institutional spine):** "\u2014 Two entries and I'll read them out so nobody has to ask me twice. **One: the flood session is entered as an office** — at the top of every water, from now on, the sea stops and *listens*, four times a day, and any realm on the shore may bring a question to it. **Two:** the deep's answer is **heard, and not held.** A thing sung true cannot be unsung — that's ours — but this thing we cannot *sing*, so we cannot *keep* it, so we are going to do the only thing four hundred thousand years of law has never once required: **we are going to give it away, to the only people in the world who can put it down and pick it up again.**" *(a beat, and the art team must draw the square hole in the roll)* "…That's your notch, nine. It's cut square because it isn't a note. Take it home, read it aloud, and **come back and sing it to us** — because the sea intends to learn the rest of its own second line off *you*, and I'd rather be honest with you about that than polite."

### P5.2 — The write-ask (half page)
**Art:** And the ask itself, in the sea's grammar, and the art team must stage it as **a negotiation with a blank**: Maelis, in her chair on the third step, in the water, and **Song-Part** speaking with her hand; the sea's newest request; and Viv — who has been standing on a rock arguing with the world for five volumes — being asked to *type*. Four frames: **(1)** the Thalarch's hand, lifted, and Song-Part at her shoulder; **(2)** the ask sung, in ring-script, and the lettering team must set it *once*: ***keep it. write it. read it where the water cannot hear and bring it back where the water can.***; **(3)** Viv's face — and the art team must draw the exact second an advocate realises she has been handed the first *drafting job* of her life that has nothing to do with winning; **(4)** and the page's last frame: Viv, dripping, on the pier, with the horn's copy open across both hands and a pen she has been carrying since Nocturne and never once been asked to use *like this*.
**Viv (flat, to the sea, and the em-dash is intact and the volume's whole thesis arrives in her mouth):** "\u2014 Four hundred years of argument, nine realms of tribunals, a war, a company, a founder and a *collar*, and the first client who ever asked me to write something down **without a single clause about who signs it** —" *(a beat, and the art team must draw her looking at the blank page)* "…is the *ocean*. Fine. Yes. **Keep it, write it, read it where the water can't hear, bring it back.** — But I want one line of my own in there, and it's going to be the line that matters, and you're all going to have to live with it: *the sea may not be bound by anything written down; the sea may be* **answered** *by anything a person has written down, read aloud at its water.*" *(a beat)* "…Say yes and I'll write it in ink, and then I'll need three hundred miles of road and about nine different rectangles of paper, because **this is not a document, this is a** ***route.***"

## Page 6 — *The Flood Reading*

### P6.1 — One hour, nine doors (half page)
**Art:** And the realm's answer — and the art team's rule for this page is the one the whole saga has been building: **the realm of the Barrens answers a law of the sea with a habit, not a law.** The composition is a single horizontal strip of **nine narrow panels in one row**, one per post, each drawn at the same height and the same hour with the sea's own *flood* in the background only in the ones that are near water — and the reader must see the same act nine times: a reader standing at a door with paper, reading aloud, and somebody listening. Four frames: **(1)** the strip of nine (post one: a Barrens crossing, dry, two clerks; post two: a gully post with water up the beach...); **(2)** two of the nine drawn larger — the **edge post**, where the reading is done by the **deputy**, standing, with the **reader's chair empty behind the desk and the collar still on its nail** (the office is a day east of here and the whole queue can see it: *a deputy may hold a door; a deputy may not sit as a reader*); and a post inland where the reader is an old man who has been at that door for forty years; **(3)** the clerk's register at one of them, and the entry the reader must be able to read: ***flood reading · first of its kind · read aloud: the deep's second line (four words) · entered***; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the **ninth** post — the edge — the queue, the well behind it standing with two chalk-marks and a third one *fresh*, and the deputy reading the day's deed aloud with the empty chair at his shoulder, and nobody in the frame looking at the chair, which is how the art team must show that everybody is.

**END OF CHAPTER 32.**

*It took one hour at the top of the water and four hundred thousand years of law, and what the sea got out of it was an ear. Up on the land, the realm answered a sea's law the way it answers everything — with a habit and no statute, and a couple of lines in a register: at the top of every water, at nine posts and a school and a camp, somebody stands at a door and reads out loud. - **The chapter's whole structure is the tide, so the tide must be in the frame on every page** (Ch. 30's rule, harder): Page 1 the **flood**, gate drowned, column under water; Pages 2–5 **the top mark**, the stair a throat, the chair on the third step, the rope-line; Page 6 the **flood hour** inland; Pages 7–8 the water beginning to fall; Page 9 **low water**, the column dry at last; Page 10 the next flood; Page 11 the second low water; Page 12 **low water again, and the arch clear.** The **tide-mark column** is the volume's clock and must be readable at a glance in every panel that shows the gate.
- **The flood session is an institution, not a miracle** (P2.1). Draw it as *procedure in water*: the parliament waist-deep on the steps, the three voices above the waterline on the stair-head stones, the tide-guard holding a rope line across the stair's mouth, **the Thalarch's reed-and-bone chair carried up and set on the third step** (a ruler enthroned in the swell), **Coral's tablet held over her head**, **Marginalia's plate tied to the rope out of respect**, and the tide going *past* everyone. Pearl's convening is lettered in the sea's ring-script as always, and the art team must show the **roll being amended as she speaks**. Canon: **the Shell is a mouth at low water and an ear at flood; sessions bind at low water and listen at high.** The **tenth notch** in the roll is **cut square** because it was not a note.
- **The second line is given to the page once, exactly like the first** (P4.2): ring-script on the top line, the realm's plain type beneath, no emphasis, no ornament. ***Nothing that is heard is lost. Then come in.*** The chorus hears it and **fails honestly** (P4.1: the chord goes thin; the middle is in a key the sea does not have), and that failure is canon and permanent: **the sea will always sing only half of its second line**, and the other half must be *written*. **Never explain the deep.** The one permitted sentence is Whalesong's, and it must not be repeated: *that's not the whale; that's what the whale is standing on.*
- **The ask is the volume's spine** (P5.2): the sea — via Maelis's hand and Song-Part's voice — asks the land to **keep the sentence: write it, read it where the water cannot hear, bring it back where the water can.** Viv's condition becomes the instrument's first two articles and the whole document is lettered legibly on P9.1: **one** the sea may not be bound by anything written; **two** the sea may be answered by anything written, read aloud at its water; **three** the keeper-Above may not hold an answer (read at the water, released); **four** the second line is kept in the record, sung in part by the sea and read in full on land, answerable by any person who can read; **five** *nothing that is heard is lost, and nothing that is written is heard until somebody reads it out loud* — Kharza's clause, and the saga's own definition of a reading.
- **The flood reading is the realm's answer, and it is a habit, not a law** (P6): nine narrow panels in one row, one per post, the same act nine times at the same hour; a register page headed **READINGS, NOT LAWS**; and the rule stated by **the deputy** at the edge post, standing beside a chair he may not sit in: *it doesn't bind anything; that's the point of it.* **Canon: the realm now reads something aloud at the top of every water at nine posts, the school, and the camp** — cheap, daily, and permanently. The school's slate goes on the wall **at the same height as the refusals** (Taurus's instruction: the wall is for sentences too).
- **The map-room beat is the chapter's heart and must be shot with no ceremony at all** (P7): Oros reads the survey's **first year** aloud at the flood hour, one year a day, at the founder's own request; the second chair is empty; the founder stands **in the doorway**, coat and scoop, half-lit, with **the grey drawn as a band of blank paper through the gate behind him** — and **no balloon in his panel, and his face not drawn.** His one word, from the doorway, is the chapter's last spoken beat: ***"…Again."*** Oros's answer is to reach for the *next* volume — no reply, no look, no music. **Rhyme, deliberate and never remarked on: Whalesong said the same word into a tube in Ch. 30 P12.1.**
- **Nereia's office works here for the first time** (P8.1): she reads the second line from the horn's copy **down at the drowned arch** and the sea answers upward; the two scripts appear **side by side in one panel** with the seam visible; her markings take a new pattern, **wide slow rings — the pattern of listening** — established here as hers forever; and the **flask ends its four-volume career as a deadline and starts its new one as an office**: she pours the last measure of grove water into the sea at the top of the flood and has it refilled *with flood water* — hollow to the office, not to her thirst.
- **Rule 6 (Roderick)**: P8.2, **exactly one panel, no balloon, no caption, no count, nobody watching** — the **lead-line** off the pier's seaward face, depth read **by hand**, the rope's fathom-marks **worn because he tied them himself three volumes ago** (Ch. 5), and the gate's throat drawn as a dark mouth in the band of light with nothing in it. The gate-clerk's supper-table footnote is the only comment, and it must stay deadpan.
- **Bramble's page (P10) is comic relief with a hook under it**: her **child's map of doors** (ticks, crosses, one **YES**), the gate-clerk asked to spell a word she wrote herself, a second YES added at the gate during the flood hour, and her mask's tag **wet from the sea and still illegible** — the saga's withheld name, played for a laugh again and never explained.
- **Kha arrives at the post on this chapter's Page 10** and the arrival must be drawn as the smallest possible event: a case on a desk, **the collar lifted off its nail and put on for the first time**, a chair sat in, a register handed over like a kitchen. Her first reading at the post is the deep's four words, read standing **on the desk's cross-bar** so she can be seen over it, and the queue applauds the way queues do: **one man stamping a boot.** Nobody comments on her age; **she never once looks eleven on the page**, only small.
- **The card (P12.2) is the eleventh hide card, Volume 6's third.** Its own movement is a **square hole punched clean through the hide** — matching the roll's square notch (P5.1) — so the reader sees the **white page beneath** through the leather: the saga's oldest image for a thing that is not there yet, and its newest image for a door. Nine notches along the lower edge; corner count **eleven… seventeen — and a rest**; the cart's margin-line, in tiny italics, is *“says 'then come in.' rude, honestly. i was already in.”* Caption: ***Here open the named.*** And the gate's new stone-cut **READ HERE**, cut beside four hundred years of tide-marks, is the chapter's last image of the realm being itself.
