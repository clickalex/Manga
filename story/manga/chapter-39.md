# AURELION · Volume 7 — Chapter 39: "What a Promise Is Worth"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc G: "The Oath-Eater" — the trial of oaths ends, and the realm reads its own clause.** Sixty-one thousand two hundred and four demons have stood at a lectern and said out loud what their promises were worth to them, and the queue is still standing in the street. On the fifth morning the Court answers the question Cindral signed: *which letter is warm.* The answer comes the way this realm's answers always come — through an instrument, a reading, and an archive — and what it finds is worse and better than a hole. The Contract's own clause says ***the Contract holds while there remains one who keeps it***, and the realm has spent eight years reading the second line of that sentence (*and if there is no one, the Contract is void and none are bound*) as though it were a fact about the world, when it was a **test** about the realm: a question a drafter left in the wall to find out who would keep a promise after the office that witnessed it walked out of the door. The realm has been keeping its promises the whole time. Four thousand two hundred and six of the trial's promises were **guaranteed on a name that was chiselled out of an archive field**, and every one of them has been kept — quietly, un-witnessed, for eight years — by cooks and road-clerks and ferrymen who did not know they were keeping anything, because in Emberfall keeping your word is not a virtue, it is **hygiene**, and nobody had told them the law had stopped watching. And at the ash gate, seven hounds have been keeping the same clause on their collars since the day their master left, fed by a bath-house, filed as *void-adjacent* — and when the Court finally reads a collar properly, the metal answers, because a collar's text lights for a **kept** promise and this realm has just read out sixty-one thousand of them in public. The void was never a hole. It was a **vacancy**, and the realm has been standing in it all along, and the man who voided the Contract is about to be **summoned** — not as an accused and not as a debtor: as a **party**, to come and hear what was kept on his name.
> **Cast:** **The road party** (nine; **Viv** — *who finds the number, and asks the Court for the one thing no law of this realm provides: a reading, every morning, until her name is hers again*, **Bramble** — *who asked the hounds the only question that ever worked*, **Kaelen** — *who reads a collar the way he reads a hinge*, **Morgatha** — *who has been keeping a promise out of contract for eight years herself and says so in four words*, **Fenn** — *the volume's ledger-voice, and the party's map now has a jurisdiction on it*, **Azrael** — *who reads the whole testimony and takes none of it personally*, **Nereia** — *who asks the realm the question its law has never had to answer: what do you call a promise kept with nothing holding it*, **Lyra** — *whose vote is carried and who gives the pack the one word the Court can accept*, **Grimm** — *who cuts the field in the archive stone*, **Sprocket** — *the entries* ) + **Marginalia** (the cart, at the ash gate, plate on its rope) · **Captain Roderick Vane** (**his one silent panel** — the arrivals' ledger open at the gate, one outsider's entry, and a captain who writes it in his own log and closes the book, because it is not his jurisdiction) · **the Fire-Court's Seven Archdukes** (03a: **Ashavane** — *who corrects the realm's oldest misreading and files the correction*, **Cindral** — *whose signed question is answered, and who enters the field that cannot be restored*, **Pyrethra** — *who witnesses the reading and then says the sentence the whole volume has been waiting for*, **Sulfakar** — *whose ledger of eight uncollected years becomes a schedule*, **Emberlyn** — *whose eight years of soup become law*, **Nigravox** — *whose self-ban was on a misreading, and who is asked by the Court to say so*, **Vesperfire** — *who stands for the first time in the chapter and says nothing at all*) · **Draft** (03b;
