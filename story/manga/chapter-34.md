# AURELION · Volume 6 — Chapter 34: "Carried, Not Kept"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc F: "The First Song" — what the sea's answer is worth, argued in a room with a dry sand floor.** The company has been buying askings at eleven feathers since the sea answered, and the sea-gate's queue runs nine days deep. Then the realm of the Barrens does the one thing no factor in nine realms can price: it reads the deep's second line **aloud, for free, at nine crossings, in public, every morning** — and the company's counsel appears at the shore with a writ and a charter and a very good argument. So Volume 6 gets its first courtroom, and it is the sea's own: **the Shell, at low water**, the chorus-parliament in session, the Thalarch in her chair, Pearl judging without a gavel, and the company's clean-handed clerk on the sand, singing nothing because he cannot sing, reading everything because he can read. What the chapter is *about* is the difference between a **trade** and a **theft** — the company says the realm took its market; Viv says the realm never sold, and that the thing the company is complaining about losing is the *price on the air*. What the chapter *does* is the saga's real work: it defines an institution, on the record, in front of witnesses. **Four findings** come out of the Shell — carriage may be priced and the water may not; an answer is not property; *person* means a person; and the ledger is released — and the last one costs the company every answer it has banked since it started buying askings, read aloud at the water once by its own clerk and given to the tide, with the receipts kept, because the receipts were always the real business and nobody in the trade had ever dared say so. Rule 6 is a captain in an antechamber with a charter, marking one word with his thumb. Kaelen re-sews the ledger's spine with a sailmaker's stitch. Bramble is *furious* that a reading is free. And at the end, a clerk who has been selling the sea's answers for two years stands in a free queue at a slat on the coast road, with a question of his own, because the court's fourth finding has just told him the only thing he can be at the water: **a person.**
> **Cast:** **The road party** (nine; **Viv** — *counsel, and the chapter's whole engine: the publication argument, the four findings pursued line by line, and one private line about clients*, **Morgatha** — *who decides what a realm pays for*, **Kaelen** — *a smith's eye for a seam, and a stitch*, **Nereia** — *the keeper-Above reads the release at the water; the flask never counted again*, **Kharza** — *who reads the second line into the sea's record*, **Fenn** — *who finds his own coastline on somebody else's wall*, **Azrael** — *one dry line that names the whole trade*, **Bramble** — *who tries to pay and cannot*, **Sprocket** — *the entries*) + **Marginalia** (the cart, on the shore, plate tied to a tide-guard's rope for the duration of the hearing, out of respect) · **Captain Roderick Vane** (**his one silent panel** — an antechamber, a charter, a thumb on one word, and the book left open, nobody watching; he does not speak in this chapter and has no other panel) · **the chorus-parliament** (03b: **Pearl** the judge — *the four findings*, **Coral** the scribe — *who writes a gap in a chord*, **Shell** the herald — *who announces a suit by tide and object*, **Tide / Current / Stream** — *three voices, unable to sing one word*) · **the Thalarch Maelis** (voiceless; in her chair on the Shell's dry floor; **votes by handing Song-Part her hand**) · **Song-Part** (carries the Thalarch's part and her vote) · **Ferry-Captain Marra** (03a; a **timed service** now, two seats empty at the top of the tide, and one line about rivals) · **the company's clerk** (drawn, unnamed, returning from Ch. 33 P6: courteous, clean-handed, and the chapter's antagonist-by-profession) · **a register-officer of Terrania** (drawn, unnamed; certifies a charter and keeps no opinions) · **the tide-guard**, **the lamplighter**, **the shore queue**, **the free queue** (all drawn and unnamed).
> **Locations / threads:** the reading-road west (the summons) → the coast, and the company's pier office → **the Shell**, at low water (the first suit under the second article) → the antechamber above the tide-line → the tide-flats, and the water → the coast road's free slat → the shore at the next low water, and the card.
> **Arc beat:** Ch. 34 must do three things and it must do them without a miracle and without a villain. **One — the argument.** The company's case is *good*: the realm's free readings have destroyed the value of the askings the company has been selling, and the company built the road the realm's readers walk. Viv's answer is not a trick and must land as a *thesis*: the sea sold nothing, ever; the company's trade was never in answers; and *a sentence read aloud to nobody who pays is not a loss, it is a **publication*** — and a publication is what the deep has been asking for since Ch. 32, in its own grammar, in the second article, in *writing*, because the sea cannot write. The court must hear both arguments fully, and the company must be allowed to be **right about the road** — which is why the realm's own decision (Morgatha's, not the court's) matters: *a realm pays its carriers and never pays its witnesses.* **Two — the four findings.** The rulings are the chapter's canon and must be legible on the page, in order, and each must be a *definition* rather than a punishment: **carriage may be priced, the water may not** (a company may sell a seat, a stamp, a walk, a receipt; no person may be charged for a reading of the second line, or refused one) · **an answer is not property** (nothing heard at the water may be held, banked, bought or sold; a receipt — that a question was asked and heard — may be kept) · ***person* means a person** (the instrument's second article reads *anything a* **person** *has written down, read aloud at its water*; a charter is not a person, so no company may hold the sea's offices — reader, ear, keeper — and no office may be bought, sold or inherited) · **the ledger is released** (every answer the company has banked since it began buying askings is read aloud at the water once, in order, by its own clerk, and released; the receipts stay). **Three — the cost, drawn.** The release is the chapter's set-piece and must be drawn as *labour and loss and no gloating*: a ledger torn along its fold, two columns separated by a clerk's own hands, the answer columns carried to the water in a basket by the tide-guard, and the receipts left standing as a book — a spine Kaelen re-sews with sailmaker's thread because that is what the party does for people it has just beaten. And the chapter's last beat must stay small: the clerk, off duty, in the realm's free queue, with one asking of his own, and the answer he gets is not new — it is the same four words the realm has been reading aloud at nine crossings since Ch. 33, which is the whole point of having made them *public.*
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**the four findings entered in the roll; the ledger's receipts; a gap written down**), the reading (**the hearing as a series of readings; the ledger read aloud and released**), the door (**the sea's articles as doors; *carried, not kept***), the count (**readings ten through fourteen, and a rest**), the carrying (**the chapter's title: what may be carried and what may not be held**).

**Viv (flat, at the cart's tail, reading the writ aloud to the party, because the realm's habit has become hers and the art team must show it):** "\u2014 *The company will appear by counsel.* — Right. Nobody sings the verdict. Nobody's going to like all four of them."

### P2.2 — The charter, and one word the chorus cannot sing (half page)
**Art:** And the chapter's first reading into the record, and the art team's instruction for the whole hearing: **every document is read aloud, and the sea sings it back into the record, and where the sea cannot sing, the reader must see the chord fail.** The register-officer reads the charter: the company's grant, lettered to be legible in part — *\[the register\] grants to the company the carriage of goods, wares, letters and **questions** upon the eastern caravan road and the shore roads, together with the weighing, stamping and registering thereof; and the discovery, salvage and possession of all wrecks, finds, and **discoveries** of the deep.* Four frames: **(1)** the register-officer reading from the charter, and the art team must draw the *seal* hanging from it, and the officer's hands exactly as careful as a mender's; **(2)** the three voices taking the charter up — and the art team must draw the **chord as a shape**, going up the chamber, letters rising; **(3)** **the gap**: the chord thins to two voices and then to none at the word **discoveries** — drawn as the ring-script *stopping*, mid-line, with a blank the width of the word, and the reader must be able to see the hole in the lettering; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Coral**, chalk stopped on her tablet, and a line of the record's hand with nothing in it but a ruled gap — and **the clerk's face**, which the art team must draw reacting to the sea's failure and not to the court's.
**Coral (flat, to the court, and the scribe's observation is the chapter's first evidence and it is *not* an accusation):** "\u2014 For the record, and I'll say it plainly because everybody in this room heard it: the chorus **cannot sing the charter**. It took it up — goods, wares, letters, questions, the weighing and the stamping, all of that sings — and at the word *discoveries* the chord went to nothing, twice, at the same place, and I have written the gap down at its width because that is what the roll does with a thing the sea will not say. — I don't know what it means. The sea doesn't *say* what things mean; it sings or it doesn't, and in nineteen years of scribing I have watched it refuse **four** words, and every one of them was a lie *somebody was being paid for.*"

## Page 3 — *The Claim, and the Road*

### P3.1 — The company states its loss (half page)
**Art:** And the antagonist gets the chapter's best-written argument, and the art team's rule: **stage it as arithmetic, not as menace** — the clerk reading from his own ledger figures, the court listening, and the reader must be able to follow the sums: *askings placed: one thousand and sixty; fees taken: eleven thousand six hundred and sixty feathers; askings placed since the realm's readings began: two hundred and nine, against six hundred in the same season last year.* The art team must draw the numbers **as a chart in the ledger's own ruled columns** and not as a metaphor, because the chapter's whole point is that the company's case is real. Four frames: **(1)** the clerk reading, the ledger open on the bench, and his hands *clean*; **(2)** the columns, close and legible; **(3)** the party along the wall, listening — and the art team must draw **Viv** with her eyes shut, which is how she listens when something is well-made; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the clerk closing the ledger, and the art team must draw the sound it makes in the chamber, because it is the only loud thing in it.
**The company's clerk (flat, courteous, to the court, and this is the chapter's strongest case and it must be delivered with no sneering at all):** "\u2014 The company's position, in order. **One.** The deep answered the realm of the Barrens, and the realm read the answer aloud at nine crossings, in public, daily, free. **Two.** Questions are the company's trade — the register's charter grants us the *carriage of questions* on these roads, and we have carried one thousand and sixty of them to this water at a posted and posted-and-published rate. **Three.** When a sentence is read aloud at every door in a realm, an asking that pays eleven feathers to hear the sea say something becomes an asking that pays eleven feathers to hear the sea say **the same four words the reader down the road says for nothing.** Our askings are down by two thirds in a season. — The company does not say the realm stole the answer. The company says the realm **gave it away**, and that giving a thing away is **carriage**, and that carriage on these roads is ours by charter, and the register's stamp on that charter is not a decoration." *(a beat)* "…We are not asking the court to stop the reading. There is no court anywhere that could. We are asking the court to say what the company may sell — because after this season, **nobody knows**, and a trade nobody can define is a trade that dies, and eleven clerks on this shore who did nothing wrong will be on the road by winter."

### P3.2 — The road the realm rode free (half page)
**Art:** And the company's second barrel, and the art team must draw **the road itself as evidence**: a panel showing the eastern caravan road as the company built and keeps it — the drain-cut, the weighbridge at the seam-port, the stamped waybills in their box, the posts with their boards, the graded ford approaches — and over it, in the same panel, drawn faint as a route-line, **the nine crossings the realm's readers now walk** — the reader must see with their own eyes that the realm's free readings run **on the road the company paid for.** Four frames: **(1)** the road, cut and posted, and the route-line over it; **(2)** the weighbridge and a waybill, close; **(3)** the company's road-clerks drawn *working*, four of them, unromantically, in the rain; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the clerk setting the road-ledger on the bench beside the askings-ledger, and the count in the corner: **eleven.**
**The company's clerk (flat, and the second half of the case is the part Viv must concede, and the art team must draw the concession *on her face* — one frame, two seconds, no balloon):** "\u2014 And here is the thing the realm's own advocate is too honest to skip, so I will say it for her: **the realm read our sentence on our road.** Thirty-one years of drain-cut; nine weighbridges; the graded approaches to the bought ford; the posts at the crossings, the slats, the lamps, the ink in the boards — the realm's readers walk on company planks, captain, and read the sea's free words above company stamps. If the realm says the readings are not our business, then let the realm say what part of the **carrying** is the company's — or sell the road to the Barrens for a price, and I'll carry the deed home myself."

## Page 4 — *Who Was Refused*

### P4.1 — Viv's answer, in the sea's grammar (2/3 page)
**Art:** And the chapter turns, and the art team's rule: **Viv argues standing on the dry sand inside the ring's hearing, without notes, and the page must look like work rather than like a speech** — her hands doing what they do, the room's two columns of light moving as the tide drops above them (the shafts' light moves *inch by inch* across the sand; the art team must draw it in every panel of the page, because the court's clock is above them and outside the chamber). Four frames: **(1)** Viv standing, and the art team must draw the **four objects** she sets on the sand in a line, because they are her argument: the horn's copy (the realm's record), the crossing's slat-slip (the week's readings), the punched card from the cart (the door), and a **blank sheet**; **(2)** the chamber, wide, from the shaft's height, so the light's two columns are visible on the sand; **(3)** Pearl listening and **Maelis's hand moving** — two fingers, on the chair's arm, once; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the blank sheet, close, on the sand, and the clerk looking at it, and the count in the corner: still **eleven.**
**Viv (flat, and the chapter's thesis, delivered as an advocate's *inventory* — the register she has used since Ch. 22, and the art team must draw her building the argument on her fingers):** "\u2014 Three questions, and I'll ask them in the order the court thinks in. **One. Who was refused?** Nine crossings, one season, tens of thousands of readings: the realm's files record **two** refusals — a household that would not have the sea read at the ford where their brother went under, and a man who would not have it read at his own door — and in both cases the reader walked twenty paces and read the words to the road, aloud, at the same volume, and filed the refusal with reasons, *respecting it.* **Not one person in a season was refused the sentence or charged for it.** **Two. Who was charged?** The company charges four feathers for readings at its posts; the realm's posts charge nothing; and when the company put a price on readings at the third crossing in Ch. 31, my colleagues did not fight it — they walked twenty paces down the road and read for free, and the realm's answer to a company was a **queue**. **Three.** —" *(a beat, and the art team must draw her crouching to lay the blank sheet flat on the sand beside the other three objects)* "— **Three: who was cheated?** Not the company. *Nobody sold these words.* The deep gave the second line at the flood, in the water, to nine persons, unasked, at no price, and then — this is the part in the article, and it is in *our* words, because the sea cannot write — **the sea asked the realm to keep it.** The instrument says the sea *may be answered by anything a person has written down, read aloud at its water.* That's the licence. It was given to **persons**, at the **water**, for **free**, in **writing**, by the only party in this room that owns the sentence — and the company's charter, which I have read three times since dawn, grants them the *carriage* of questions. **Carriage.** They're not accused of anything. They're just in the wrong sentence."

### P4.2 — The week's readings, read into the record (half page)
**Art:** And the realm's evidence is **the slips**, and the art team's rule: **this page is a reading, and the reading is performed by the party's youngest reader, and the room listens to a child the way it listened to a charter.** Kharza reads the week's slips aloud into the record — Coral singing each line back as it is entered, which is how the sea records anything — and the art team must draw the slips as **the same slats the reader saw in Ch. 33**, so the reader recognises them: *nine crossings read; two refusals filed with reasons; fifty-four heard; nineteen children; one crossing asked for it twice; one in the old tongue.* Four frames: **(1)** Kharza with the slips, standing inside the ring, and **Coral** opposite her, taking each line up in song as it is entered — the art team must draw the two of them as a **duet of office**, not as a performance; **(2)** a close on the singing: the ring-script of the record forming *above the slips*, so the reader sees a filing happen by voice; **(3)** the company's clerk, listening, and — the art team must be honest — **he is not bored**; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **Maelis's hand**, out of the chair's arm, flat, once — the Thalarch's vote-by-hand given for an *entering*, which the chapter must not explain and which the reader will remember from Ch. 30.
**Kharza (flat, reading, and the realm's youngest reader reads her own week into the sea's record, and the art team must letter the slips exactly as Ch. 33 lettered them):** "\u2014 Entry for the record, read from the crossings' slips and sworn as the realm swears things, which is by reading them out loud where somebody can hear: *nine crossings read daily; two refusals, filed with reasons, both respected; the reading given on the verge, twenty paces on, both times, and heard; fifty-four souls heard the sentence that week; nineteen of them children; one crossing asked for it twice and was given it twice; one asked in the old tongue, and the reader learned the old tongue's four words in a night and read it so.*" *(a beat, and the art team must draw her looking up at the three voices)* "…That's all of it. Nothing in it was bought and nothing in it was refused, and if the court wants the number that matters, it's this one: **in one season, nobody was ever turned away from those words.** Not once. Not for a fee and not for an enemy."

## Page 5 — *The Word in the Charter*

### P5.1 — The antechamber (**rule 6 — one panel, silent**)

**END OF CHAPTER 34.**

*It took eleven days, one writ, one charter, two hundred and nine answers, and a court with no gavel, and what came out of the Shell was four findings and a trade redefined rather than broken. The company's case was real and the chapter says so: they built the road, they kept the weighbridges, they carried a thousand and sixty questions to the water at a posted rate, and when the realm of the Barrens read the sea's sentence aloud at nine crossings for free, the asking trade fell by two thirds in a season. The realm's answer was real too, and Viv made it in the sea's own grammar: nobody was refused, nobody was charged, and nobody sold anything, because the deep gave the second line at the flood, in the water, to nine persons, at no price, and then asked the realm to keep it — and the instrument's licence says *anything a* **person** *has written down, read aloud at its water*, which is a sentence with a word in it that no charter can occupy. The charter said **carriage**, and a captain of Terrania read it in an antechamber and left it open on a page he chose and told nobody, and the realm's advocate walked in and found the word already under somebody's thumb. So the court found four things and cut them into the roll to be sung: carriage may be priced and the water may not; an answer is not property; *person* means a person; and the ledger is released. The ledger was torn along its fold by the clerk who had read all two hundred and nine answers twice, the answers were read aloud at the shaft's mouth in order and given to the tide, and the receipts were kept and stamped, because the receipts were always the actual business. Kaelen re-sewed the spine with a sailmaker's thread because a realm that takes a holding does not take a man's work. The realm's own finding wasn't the court's and was cut on the coast road the next morning: the Barrens will contract eleven clerks and pay for their mornings, will pay nothing for words, and its readers will never sell an askings — *a realm pays its carriers and never pays its witnesses.* The company's clerk painted a new board with the old letters showing through it: **we carry; the sea does not owe** — and stood in his own company's queue with a question of his own, and then paid nine feathers for a seat, and went out to the drowned stair at the top of the water and read the four words aloud himself, because they are free and a person is allowed to read. The water did not answer. It has never answered on demand, and the instrument never promised it would, and the clerk left a slip behind him in his clean registered hand that the realm filed as a record of work: *asked, carried, heard — nothing owed, nothing held.* At the Deep-Listening post a hundred years of one voice at one tube ended this season; at the reef the chorus began to assemble; and the sea's Re-Oath, which is a thousand voices or it is not sung at all, has just discovered what every institution in Volume 6 has been discovering one at a time: that a law can define a trade, a door can define a person, and a payroll can define a chorus — and only one of the three of those can be subpoenaed.*
**The record's thirteenth card is hide, and its own movement is a stamp and a ruling: a company's mark struck once in ash and soot — imperfect, because a stamp struck on hide always is — and a single clean line cut across its face with the finding under it. Carriage may be priced; the water may not.**
