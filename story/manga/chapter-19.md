> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc D: "The Mountain-Ask" (the last item of the year — nine hundred lines ruled and blank for a century — and a mine, a book, a hammer, six old women's memories and one mortal's mouth: the witness is called, and this time the stone answers)**

> **Locations / threads:** the Court Floor at dawn (the last leaf) → the low stone table and the raw wall → the galleries and the terraces → the mine's mouth (**Bastion**) → the cistern → the fifty-eight (frost coming off the lintels) → the deep gallery at night (**nine hundred names**) → dawn, day three.
> **Arc beat:** The year has one item left in it and it is not a reading. The Grand-Anvil opens it, the realm stands up in its good coats, and every dwarf over thirty in that gallery knows exactly how this goes — because it has gone this way for a hundred years: nine hundred lines, the stone asked, the stone silent, the year judged *kept*, everyone home for supper, the Speakers' chairs empty. This year a nineteen-year-old unwraps his grandfather's hammer, lays it on the anvil that has waited a hundred and eleven years to be named, and asks. Nothing happens, for one panel longer than the realm can bear — and then the frosted palm-print on the anvil's face *melts*, and the century-old coal in a clay pot goes bright all at once, and the rock begins to sing: not a tune, not a voice — a *list*, nine hundred lines long, read very slowly by something enormous, in a language of shapes that nobody in this realm can read any more. **Khazri speaks** — the Mountain-Speaker's first words in forty years — and they are exactly the words the bible has been holding for him: he didn't lose his hearing; the mountain stopped talking; there's a difference, and it's the difference between a dead ear and a dead kin. Then the realm finds out what it has been keeping on a boy's slate for fourteen years (the mountain's own mark), what the Chord of the Everburn and the Hundredfold is for (a stone-song read by a mortal), and what six old women in the front rank were actually carrying in their sketchbooks (the memory that goes *with* the name). **The first line of the last page fills: Durin Khazdûrin.** A mortal woman with the only pencil in the mountain writes it into the book, and then the pencil goes hand to hand through nine thousand people, chasing the light down nine hundred lines. The witness testifies, and it testifies for the *hands* — the roof-fall's four become four names, the forfeitures become people, the clause that was read past is read out — and the mine's own anvil is the one line where the light does not come, because a mine has hands and no *hands that wore it*, and Drek says so out loud, about himself, without flinching. A twenty-metre warden walks down from the pass and says the sentence he says once a year, and for the first time in a hundred years the page behind it is not blank. At dawn the keeper writes the year's word — one word, in the book's own hand, a word the book has never carried: **answered** — and the hammer stays lying open on its own family's anvil, and the pot has one coal in it, and the realm goes up the hill to light its forges.
> **Motifs advanced:** the stone grammar (**the stone-song, at scale, on the surface, as a bloom — `#e07a3f`, spent at last**), the Chord (**the Mountain-Ask: Everburn + Hundredfold — the anvil between two hands, and its own hue, *first-heat*, twice, both inside the act**), the witness (**the witness testifies; and it testifies for the hands**), the record (**nine hundred names in pencil; the mark on the boy's slate; the year's word: *answered***), the door (**a line with no name on it**), the kept thing (**a century of coal spent to one coal; a hammer left open on an anvil**), the count (**Morgatha twelve → thirteen; the naming-line, 1 → 900**), the lamp (**the flask is gone; the realm reads by its own light now**), the empty (**the mine's anvil: no hands; the Deep Cut, pointed at by machinery, and now *lit***). **Not in this chapter:** the geode; the forge's relight; Grudka's braid-adoption; the forge-duel; Torvik's line; the sea's first line; Kaelen's scar-lean (Vol. 5's door stays shut); the white (gone); Kaelen's second and third pilot-line uses; what the mine is going to do about nine thousand contracts with no plates on them — that is Chapter 20's.

## Page 5 — *The Speaker*

### P5.1 — Forty years (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's second-largest image and the one the art team must not decorate: **Khazri steps off the wall.** The Mountain-Speaker has stood at that stone through a ledger, a funeral, an objection, a year of reading and a century of silence — and now he takes one step out of the arc of the chairs and *speaks*, forty years after the last time, into a mine full of light, in front of nine thousand people, and the art team's direction is: **do not draw him as a prophet. Draw him as a man clearing his throat.** His hands are at his sides; his voice is bad — cracked, unused, the voice of a man who has talked to no one since he stopped being able to tell anybody anything. Behind him: the two slates, still on the eighth chair's seat. The realm's reaction is *the realm's*: the room must be drawn as nine thousand people hearing a voice they have never heard — and, the art team's key detail, **nobody in the gallery moves toward him.** They are too well-mannered to crowd a Speaker, which is exactly how he was able to do forty years of it.
**Khazri (hoarse, flat, cracked, forty years of nothing behind it, and this is 03a's line, verbatim, unaltered, and it must be lettered plainly — no tail, no falling type):** "**I didn't lose my hearing. The mountain stopped talking. There's a difference, and it's the difference between a dead ear and a dead kin.**"
**Khazri (a beat; and then, because a man who has spoken once does not have to be careful any more):** "…That's it. That's what I've had in me since the lamps went on in this hole. You can all stop asking my *slate*." *(a beat)* "It's talking **now**. Sit down, the lot of you, and *listen* to it, because I've heard it before and you haven't."

## Page 7 — *Nine Hundred Lines*

### P7.1 — The pencil goes hand to hand (2/3 page)
**Art:** The naming at scale, and the art team's key invention for the page: **the assembly line of memory.** The light comes up through the stone table, one line at a time, slow — and the **pencil** chases it. **END OF CHAPTER 19.**

*The realm asked the stone a question for the nine hundredth year and, for the first time since the Cut opened, the stone answered — and what it had to say was not a sentence, not a voice, not a miracle: it was a *list*, nine hundred lines long, of hands. ## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 19)

- **The reserved colour is spent, once** (P4.1, P9.1). `#e07a3f` — the stone-song, the mountain's money colour, held since the under-roads' single ember-line in S1 (Ch. 10 P9.2) — appears in this chapter **at scale, on the surface, as a bloom**, exactly as [character/mosaic-nine.md](../../character/mosaic-nine.md) reserved it. Two conditions the art team must not break: **(1)** it is **a *bloom* of *rings*, not a beam** — the light comes up the *hairline* and travels the stone's own grain in concentric widening lines, the mountain *writing on itself*, and it is never a firework, never a halo, never a glow-aura on a person; **(2)** it appears **on rock only** — not on the book (the book's page light is the Chord's hue, see below), not on any skin, not on any anvil's *name*. In Ch. 20 the mountain is *warm*, not lit: **do not restage the bloom.**
- **The Chord: the Mountain-Ask (P6.1–P6.2).** Canon 04: **Everburn (dwarf) + Hundredfold (human)** — *the mountain consents; a stone-song read by a mortal.* Geometry: **Grimm's two palms flat on the anvil's face** (the Everburn half — his family's hundred-year fire, spent; the hammer lying across the iron) and **Kaelen's right hand flat on the open page at the edge of line one's blank** (the Hundredfold half — the mortal who says things back), with the low stone table — *the mine's own rock, under the book* — carrying the light between them. The reader must be able to draw one straight line from the hammer to the page. **The Chord's own hue for Volume 4 is *first-heat*, `#f0d9a0`** — a pale warm gold, Vol. 4's and no one else's — and it appears **twice in this chapter, both inside this single act**: (1) the blank of line one lettering itself in light, (2) the wet hairline going gold for one frame where it passes the gallery. It is **metric, not a glow** (Ch. 6 P6.1 / Ch. 13 P8.1's grammar). **The mountain's writing does not stay:** the light fades and the pencil writes underneath it, and that is the chapter's entire theology — *a stone can say a name once; a person can keep it.*
- **The mark on the boy's slate (P5.2) is the mountain's own hand, and it is not translated.** Echo's slate (Ch. 14–18: one stroke, repeated, fourteen years, unreadable) is the *same mark* that comes up through the stone at line one. **Do not explain it, do not have anybody name what the mark "means", and do not let Echo read it** — he recognized it and he still cannot read it, and that is *fine*, because the reading is Kaelen's job and the copying was Echo's, and the realm has needed a copyist for four hundred years without knowing it. Khazri wrote that mark for him before he stopped speaking; nobody in the chapter says so out loud; the reader has the two panels side by side and that is all they need.
- **Khazri speaks — once, verbatim, and then he is allowed to be a person** (P5.1). 03a's line is spent here, unaltered, lettered plainly, no tail, no falling type, no halo, **and the art team must draw him as a man clearing his throat, not as a prophet.** His second and third lines are short and practical (he tells the realm the mountain is talking and to sit down and listen). **After this chapter he may speak, sparingly; he does not make speeches.** The realm's reaction is the room's, not his — and nobody crowds him, which is how he lasted forty years.
- **Bastion's sentence is spent here and nowhere else in Volume 4** (P9.1). Canon 03a: *one sentence, once a year.* Twenty metres of granite, kneeling at a mine's mouth that has **no door** — Ch. 17 P6.2's threshold used for the first time, unremarked. The panel is the fewest words in the chapter and must be the heaviest: letter him alone on the page, in the giant's plainest type. Then **the mountain answers the sentence** (the light standing on the surface, one frame, from outside — the only frame in the saga where the bloom is seen *from a distance*).
- **The mine's anvil stays blank (P7.2).** One line out of nine hundred gets no light, and it is the mine's own working anvil, because a mine has hands and no *hands that wore it*. **Drek says the truth about himself, in public, plainly, with no self-pity and no performance** — and the chapter must not punish him for it or reward him for it. His eyes go to the **machines**, the Ch. 15 P6.1 tell, paid: Viv is the only person who catches it, one line, and the *depth* he logs at P8.2 is the volume's last seed. Do not underline any of it, and do not resolve it in this volume.
- **The costs are drawn, not said** (P8.2, P11.1): **the century's coal burns down to one coal** (Bjorn carried that pot for a hundred years; he gets four words and no eulogy, and the art team should let the reader find the pot's inside in one frame and no other); the **cistern** is a finger lower and Nereia refuses to say it out loud in a happy room; the **Deep Cut's lower gallery is lit** for the first time, with the bore-heads swung and no hand on a lever; and **the great-forge stays dark** — the page's rule is *daylight only in that doorway*, because **the forge's relight is Volume 4's coda, not this chapter's**, and the reader wanting it is the point.
- **The pencil is the chapter's second protagonist** (P6.2, P7.1, P10.1, P12.1). Line one is written by **Maren Ashwood**, left-handed and hurried. After that the pencil **travels hand to hand through nine thousand people** — miners, mothers, children lifted up, the six old women each signing her own family's line — and the art team must draw the **mine's clerks with their pens and ink standing three steps away, doing nothing, twice**, because the realm's record is not going to be written by the mine.
