# AURELION · Volume 7 — Chapter 40: "One Who Keeps"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc G: "The Oath-Eater" — the office and the vacancy: three days of readings, and the form this realm has never written.** The trial is over and the realm has gone back to work, which in Emberfall means three days of *reading*: the findings carried out of the chamber and read aloud **where they were kept** — four gates, sixty-one stalls, a barrow, a bath-house, and one doorstep on the fifth lane. And the Court has one item of business it can no longer put off: the vacancy is filed, the eighth chair has no holder, and the realm has to decide out loud **who keeps its law.** It has no form for this. It has never written one, because it has never needed one — Emberfall fills an office **by the First Contract**, and renews every seat at a Re-Oath, and the office that now stands vacant is the office that keeps the Contract. The discovery belongs to the word-forge and it is this realm's oldest disability said plainly: **Emberfall can read every promise ever made in it, and it cannot read a blank** — a blank is not a promise, the air over a blank is empty, and four thousand years of clerks have walked past their own missing signatures and filed a finished page. So the realm's first new form in four thousand years will be **cut into stone instead of hanging in gold**, read aloud at the opening of every session, and it will have, by design, no place to put a name: *filled by: the clause · held by: whoever keeps it · field for a name: none · kept by: reading.* And on the ninth day, while the realm drafts it, an outsider with one eye and a long-handled scoop walks into the word-forge's office and files for a document — **a measured drawing of the archive's fourth page** — which a realm whose rule is that nothing is withheld from a reader grants him in public, enters on a slate chip, and reads out at the gate with the rest of the day's business, while nobody in the party notices anything at all. ## Page 3 — *A Blank Is Not a Promise*

### P3.1 — The demonstration (2/3 page)
**Art:** And the chapter's mechanism is drawn as **a piece of good work with a hole in it**, and the art team's rule: **the clerks have built something beautiful, and the horror is that nobody in the room can see what is missing except the man with the pins.** Setting: the Fire-Court's chamber, the eighth day's afternoon; the iron table cleared except for the clerks' **demonstration tablet** — a full-width slate with a hundred ruled fields, the best piece of drafting the chamber has produced in a generation, and the room's gold hanging off it in a hundred legible promises, so the tablet reads, at a glance, as *finished law*; **Cindral** at the table's head with the lower archive's register; **Draft** at the tablet with brass pins, a rule and a chalk-line, working; the archdukes; the parties; **Viv** on her feet; the gallery standing along the walls; and the eighth chair and lamp at the frame's edge, where they have been moved to, because the room's business is about them now whether anybody says so or not. The chapter's visual hinge must be unmistakable in one frame: **the field where a name would go, ruled, empty, and producing nothing at all, while the promise hanging over every other field in that slab is plain as weather.** Four frames: **(1)** the tablet, wide, the hundred promises; **(2)** the one field, close, with Draft's rule laid across it and his pins marking the corners and the empty air above it drawn *as air*; **(3)** the room's faces, drawn by the light they are all reading by, all of them seeing nothing; **(4)** and the page's last frame: Draft's white tablet, the finding written in his own hand and the four words ***nothing to see*** isolated at the top of it, underlined twice, the way a man underlines a thing he is about to make a whole realm read out loud.

**Viv (flat, on her feet, at the parties' table, and the advocate's question is the chapter's demand and it must be asked like a lawyer asking for a document and not like a hero asking for a miracle):** "— The vacancy is filed. The chair is empty and the realm is not going to re-oath for two days and the office cannot wait for a re-oath anyway, because an office that waits for the realm to re-bind itself is an office that stands empty while the realm is unbinding, which is the sentence this entire volume has been about. — So: **which form fills it.** — Not the procedure. The *document.* — One page. Two pages. Whatever the realm uses. — I have stood in four realms' courts and every one of them had a piece of paper for this, and the ones that didn't had a man with a cousin. — Clerk: bring it."

**The Court's clerk (flat, at the table, fifty years in the lower archive, and the archivist's answer is the chapter's engine and it must be delivered as *shelving*, because for him it is a fact about shelves):** "— There isn't one. — I'll say it in the order I'd say it to a new clerk, and then I'll show you what my clerks built this morning. — One: this realm has forms for a promise, a witness, a penalty, a receipt, a collar, a crossing, a doorstep and a guest. — Two: this realm has **no form for filling an office.** An office here is not filled by a person, it is **held as a term of the Contract** — the seven seats are renewed when the realm re-oaths, all of it, in one room, at once, and that is the procedure; that is the *whole* procedure; there has never been another one, because there has never been a vacancy. — The eighth office was established on the day this realm was written, by the hand that wrote everything else that holds it up, and we have not needed a form for four thousand years for the same reason a wall doesn't need a door: nothing has ever tried to come through one."

**Draft (flat, at the tablet, and the forge's finding is the chapter's mechanism and it must be given as *measurement*, because the djinn has spent thirty years being the reason this realm's drafts work):** "— One field in a hundred. — Look at it. — Your clerks wrote a form. It's good work; it's the best drafting I've seen out of this chamber, and I want that on the record before I take it apart, because my office exists to take things apart and it grieves me when the thing is good. — Here is the field. Ruled, sized, squared, the depth of every other field on the slab. — And **there is nothing over it.** — Your law hangs in the air, chair. Every promise made in this realm becomes legible the moment it's spoken and stays legible while it holds, and the whole realm has organised four thousand years of government around *being able to read it* — clerks, gates, receipts, collars, witnesses, me. — **A blank produces no text.** A blank is not a promise. It is a *place* a promise would go, and air does not read places. — So a form with a field in it has been lying on this realm's tables since the founding day producing nothing over the one thing it wanted, and every clerk who ever read one did exactly what any reader in this city would do: they read the promises, saw the field, saw no text, and concluded *nothing to see.* — That is not a missing form, chair. That is four thousand years of missing **signatures**, and nobody in this realm has ever been able to see one, because an unsigned field in Emberfall looks exactly like a finished page."

### P3.2 — The eighth page (half page)
**Art:** And the find is drawn in the dark, and the art team's rule: **the realm's answer has been shelved for four thousand years and the shelf is dusty, not sacred.** Setting: the lower archive, the eighth day's evening, lamps in brackets, the Contract's leaves in their shallow cases laid along one wall like roof-slates; the archivist (**drawn and unnamed**: short, old, chalk on both cuffs) carrying the case with the lamp held low, because this is a room where you do not raise a light to a page; **Cindral** with the register and the brass pins borrowed from the forge; **Draft** at his shoulder with the chalk-line; and the page itself, drawn **rising** in the frame — the fourth leaf of the First Contract, the clause in it, the margin, the drafter's small marks, and — beside the clause, in a working scribe's hand, in white chalk pressed into the surface **and never rubbed out** — the annotation this volume has already shown the reader once: ***intentional.*** Also in the frame, small, at the shelf's end: the **bowl** with the chipped tokens in it, and a clerk's hand not touching it. **Cindral (flat, in the archive, holding the lamp low, and the clause-keeper's horror is professional, quiet, and one hundred and eleven years old):** "— I have kept the clauses for a hundred and eleven years. — Every register in this realm comes through my hands. I have read this page aloud in four sessions of this Court and I have signed three of its copies. — Here is the establishment of the eighth office. In the founding hand.
