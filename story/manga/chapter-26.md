# AURELION · Volume 5 — Chapter 26: "The Road East"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc E: "The One Throat" — out of the Barrens, and out of the medium.** The team walks the caravan road east with a law they cannot store: the clause is cut into nine hundred arms and cannot be carried anywhere, so the realm has sent the only thing it can send — **a reader**, and the readers work in the first mile. On the road they meet the thing every other realm in the worlds keeps instead of skin: **paper** — a caravan's ledgers, a guild's books, a weighbridge's manifests — and the realm's youngest student of the cut script has to learn what paper is *for*: it travels, it copies, it can be read by anyone, and it **lies**, because nothing on paper has to pay for being read. The chapter's argument is the volume's, turned outward: a law that travels as text is a rumour; a law that travels as **a person who has paid for the right to say it** is a law. So the realm's law crosses its first border the only way it can — read aloud, in a tavern, by a nineteen-year-old, with the record's copy entered as *a reading and not a law* — and something worse and better happens than anybody planned: a stranger asks to be **read into the book**. And on the road, a fallen angel who broke one oath on paper two hundred years ago and has been quiet for nine chapters asks the reader the question the whole arc was built to make him ask: **what does your realm do with a hand that keeps every word?** The answer costs him something: the realm's rule is that a witness is the one it *costs*, so the travel-horn takes the second entry in its short life — a debt nobody witnessed, entered at its holder's own asking, to be read in the circle when the circle comes. Then the road, the sea-smell, and the card.
> **Cast:** **The team** — **Kharza** (**the reader; her first border; the ledger's first entry**), **Azrael** (**the unwitnessed oath, entered; the question he has been carrying since Vol. 1**), **Viv** (the lawyer who defines what a copy may say about itself), **Fenn** (the map gets a symbol that has never existed: a *law*, drawn), **Sprocket** (the ledger, and the counting of days), **Morgatha** (the unfinished entry and the witness rule), **Kaelen** (one frame, one line), **Nereia** (**no dialogue** — the markings, and the salt), **Bramble** (**one line, and it is about roads**), **Marginalia** (the cart, on its ninth realm's road, filing the complaint it has waited a volume to file), and **Roderick** (**his one silent panel** — a manifest with his own signature on it). **The road** — **Caravan-Lead** (human, and unimpressed by law), **Caravan-Gnome** (the caravan's scribe, and Kharza's first teacher in ink), **Caravan-Orc** (guard; the mountain of a man who reads the clause twice), **Caravan-Vamp** (healer; night-shift, and the one who asks the real question about paper), **Marra** (03a's selkie ferry-captain, met on the road — **one line, and it is about water**); **the caravan**, and **Seam-Tavern** (the halfling who keeps the tavern where the Nine meet, and who asks for the first *reading* east of the pass). Plus **Quick** (goblin courier, with the west's first dispatch and the swarm's waybills) and **Khura's sliver** (a horn-fragment in Kharza's satchel, cut with the *reason* — the realm's first postal article).
> **Locations / threads:** the Barrens' eastern border post (a chair, a lamp, a sliver, and a rule) → the caravan road and its ledgers → the weighbridge at the seam-port of **Dorno's Cross** (the guild's books, the Reforgers' eastbound freight) → **Seam-Tavern** → the road at dawn, with salt in the wind.
> **Arc beat:** Vol. 5 spent five chapters making a realm change its own law in writing that is not writing; this chapter takes that law *out of the realm* and finds out what it weighs. Ch. 26's job in the season is to be the **hinge chapter** — the last easy one before the road gets serious — and to do three structural things. **First: the reader's first border.** The realm's nine reading-posts begin working, and the caravan road gives Kharza her first ask: a cartel's factor who wants the Barrens' toll record and gets **read at** instead of refused — and the realm's whole new institution proves it can run on nothing but a voice, a reason, and an arm. **Second: paper.** Every other realm keeps its law in books, and the caravan's scribe trades Kharza a lesson for a reading: ink travels, ink copies, ink can be *read by anyone* — and ink does not have to pay for anything, which is exactly the hole the Barrens spent a volume closing. So the chapter's investigation is practical and lawyerly: what can a copy of the clause honestly *say about itself*? Viv's answer becomes the realm's first export document — a copy that declares it is **a copy of a reading, made by a named hand, on a named day, at a named door, and not a law** — and Kharza's travel-horn carries the *reason* (Khura's sliver) because a rule without its reason is precisely what got the realm hurt. **Third: Azrael.** Quiet for nine chapters, he asks the question the arc exists to make him ask — and the realm's rule about witnesses turns out to fit him exactly: he has carried an oath nobody witnessed for two hundred years, and the travel-horn will take it, unwitnessed, unpaid, unfinished, entered at his own asking. It is the second entry in the new horn, and the first one that is *voluntary*. Between those three things, the volume's promise is restated in a realm that keeps nothing: **a law travels as a person who has paid for the right to say it.** The chapter ends with the sea-smell in the wind, the card, and the road going on.
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**a copy that declares itself; a reading and not a law; the travel-horn's second entry**), the door (**a reader posted; a tavern that asks; a weighbridge that says what it is**), the gift (**a caravan's scribe teaches for a reading; a stranger asks to be read in**), the road (**the ninth realm's road under a cart that has been waiting a volume to complain about it**), the count (**fifteen… eighteen, and the corner keeps them because they are still hers**).
> **Not in this chapter:** **Titanvein** (spent in Ch. 24; barred for the rest of the volume) · the stone-song and every bloom (`#e07a3f` zero, in art and in notes) · **Oros** (off-page; his 03a line is still held for Gorakh's chapter) · **the four companions** · **the sea's first line** (**Nereia silent** — the salt arrives as *smell and markings*, and her first words are Vol. 6's) · Wren's 03a line · the Scar-Reading Chord · the white (never restaged) · the *empty* inside the mountain · Orvane's garden · Khazur's seal-or-fight · **Gorakh himself** · the twins (Fenn's column stays empty; **no letter arrives in this chapter**) · and **the Re-Oath vote** (promised, dated a realm away).

## Page 1 — *The First Post*

### P1.1 — A chair, a lamp, a sliver, a rule (half page)
**Art:** The Barrens' eastern border, first light, and the art team's key invention for the chapter: **the reading-post is a piece of furniture.** Not a gatehouse, not a watchtower — a **chair**, a **lamp**, and a **post** cut with the realm's script (the newest line on it, cut this week: ***A READING MAY NOT BE REFUSED — NOR HANDED BACK***), and beside the chair a **sliver-box** (a clay pot of horn-slivers, like the map-room's request-shelf reduced to what one person can carry) and a **travel-horn** on a strap over the back of the chair. Standing beside it: the realm's **second reader** — not **Ura** (who has the first post out west), but **Flow**, the river-caste fisher from 03b, and the chapter's running joke is that the realm has staffed its newest institution with the two least likely people in Ulgar: a fisher and, further along the road with the team, a nineteen-year-old. **Fenn** is on the cart, already drawing the post; **Kharza** is standing *behind* the chair, with the travel-horn on her back, and the art team must draw her the way she is: nineteen, in a coat cut for a bigger orc, with a satchel of slivers and a slate, *looking at the horizon* — and the team is on the road, and the Barrens ends two hundred paces behind them. The count in the corner: **fifteen.**
**Flow (flat, from his chair, mouth full of breakfast, and the realm's newest civil servant has found his register):** "— Morning. You'll be the eastbound party. …Room's read your clause at me four times already and I've only been sat here since dark, so if you want a *reading* you can wait your turn like the *cart's* going to." *(a beat)* "…Fenn. Draw the chair *empty*, would you? It looks better empty and I'm not going to be sat in it forever."

### P1.2 — The first ask (half page)
**Art:** The post at work, and the art team's staging rule: **it must be ordinary.** A cartel's factor — a goblin with a ledger-book, 03b's **Quick** in his courier's role for the *cartel*, not the swarm — arrives from the east with **a lawful ask** in writing: a stamped paper from a Hundred Courts' guild, demanding the Barrens' toll record "per the terms of the caravan compact". Four frames: **(1)** the paper, held out, and the lettering team must set it in **stamped plate-type** (the saga's established register for Terrania's bureaucracy: the exact opposite of the realm's cut script); **(2)** Flow reading it, slowly, with a finger, and the art team must draw the moment his face changes — he is not insulted; he is *bored in a professional way*; **(3)** Flow turning the paper over and finding the realm's answer, which the reader already knows: *a lawful ask may not be refused* — so Flow does not refuse; **(4)** and then the realm's actual move, drawn as **four panels of an arm**: Flow rolling his sleeve, pulling the travel-horn around, and reading **the clause, the witness, the limit, the carrying, and the reason** at a goblin with a stamped paper — out loud, in the road, in front of the caravan, and then handing the *sliver* over so the factor can keep the reading in horn, because ***a reading may not be refused — nor handed back.***
**Flow (flat, reading from his forearm, and this is the realm's whole new doctrine delivered by a fisherman):** "— So. *The oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code*, where a life's held against its will; a life given stays the giver's; the witness's the one it costs; and a debt the holding releases comes home to the realm. That's the reading. This is the horn it's cut in. This is your copy." *(he puts the sliver in the goblin's hand)* "…Your paper says you may *ask*. It's right. Ask's free and *reading's* free. The *record's* not — you'll be wanting the tolls, and the tolls are mine to keep, and I'll read you the tally the same way every time you come, and one day you'll bring a paper that says *give*, and I'll read you *this* instead, and we'll both be doing our jobs." *(a beat)* "…Good morning."

## Page 2 — *What the Caravan Keeps*

### P2.1 — Ten wagons, and a book (half page)
**Art:** The caravan road, midday, and the art team's new visual world: **the road east is *administrated*.** Ten wagons; a mule; a banner with a seam-port's mark; and on the lead wagon's box, in the shade, a **gnome with a book the size of a door** — **Caravan-Gnome** (03b, the caravan's scribe), with ink, a sand-box, a ruler, and three pigeon-holes of paper, working while the wheels turn, because a caravan that cannot show papers at the next bridge does not eat. Riding around him: **Caravan-Lead** (human, grizzled, bored, the man who has done this road two hundred times), **Caravan-Orc** (the guard — a mountain in leathers and a *very good* cook), **Caravan-Vamp** (the healer, dozing under a parasol against the sun, awake at night), and a **faun** with a lute who has not yet played a note in the chapter and will get the last one. The team walks with them: the cart (Marginalia's cart is on its *ninth realm's road* and has filed a complaint about the *road surface*), Fenn's map open on the cart's tailgate, Kharza walking beside the lead wagon looking up at the book.
**Caravan-Lead (flat, to Viv, chewing, and the caravan's lead has one rule and it is not a law):** "— You'll be the ones with the horn. …Right. Road rules: we don't stop for arguments, we don't take sides, we don't *carry* anybody's law. You read at whoever asks; you don't read at *my* people without their *ask*; and if a bridge-keeper wants papers, your party's my party, so you'll stand there and look respectable while the **scribe** does the talking, because on this road *paper* is the voice and I've never once seen a horn win an argument with a **stamp**." *(a beat)* "…No offence meant."

### P2.2 — The book, and its price (half page)
**Art:** That night's halt, the fire, and the chapter's central lesson staged as **an apprenticeship across a fire**: **Caravan-Gnome** (312-year-old Fenn's *younger* colleague and total opposite: ink-stained, fussy, ~90 years old, wearing spectacles) shows Kharza the book. Four frames, and the art team must make the book *beautiful and dangerous*: **(1)** the book open, and its pages are a *ledger*: names, weights, tolls, dates, and — the detail Kharza cannot parse — *corrections*, entries struck and re-entered with a note in the margin; **(2)** Kharza's face, doing what the reader has watched this realm do for five chapters: **reading**. She reads the same entry twice and finds the lie: a weight adjusted *after* the stamp; **(3)** the gnome's reaction — he is *pleased*, not defensive: a girl who can read a false entry in a strange hand on her first day is not a girl he has to explain things to; **(4)** the trade: he asks, formally, for a **reading** — his own — and offers the only thing this chapter's economy has: **teaching**.
**Caravan-Gnome (flat, spectacles up, and the gnome's line is the whole chapter's thesis in his own register):** "— You've read it. Good. Now here's what your realm doesn't know and mine can't stop thinking about: **ink doesn't have to pay**. I strike a weight, I enter another in the margin, I stamp it, and the road believes me — and I'm an *honest* man, and I've done it twice this year, and both times I was *right*." *(a beat)* "…Your people cut a law into an arm. Mine write it in a book and then *argue about the book*. I'd say yours is the stronger kind, and I'd say it because I've been on this road forty years and I've seen a *stamp* walk a caravan into a war." *(a beat)* "…So: teach me the horn. I'll teach you the ink. Fair?"
**Morgatha (quiet, from the fire's other side, not looking up, and this is the volume's antagonist-of-ideas having her own thought aloud):** "…Aye. And when the ink lies, who pays?"

## Page 3 — *A Copy That Says What It Is*

### P3.1 — Kharza writes it wrong, three times (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's *craft* page, and the art team's register invention: **the clause in ink is wrong and the reader must be able to see it.** Four frames, each a sheet of paper, and the lettering team's rule is that **ink is a different hand than the cut**: three attempts, each more lawyerly than the last, each *worse* — **(1)** Kharza's first try, in a big, careful, childlike hand: *"The oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code"* — and Viv takes it away without comment; **(2)** the second try, with the four parts (holding, witness, limit, carrying) in a neat list, and Viv stops her at the witness line, because the witness line requires **a person's name and a person's cost**, and on paper a name is just a word; **(3)** the third try, in which Kharza has written, at the bottom, in her own words, *"I read this at the Barrens' east post, and my name is Kharza, and I'm the one who pays if I read it wrong"* — and *this* one Viv picks up. And the page's turn: **(4)** Viv's eyes over the top of the sheet, on Kharza, and the lawyer has just been *taught* something by a nineteen-year-old.
**Kharza (flat, frustrated in the specific way of a good student, hunched over paper with an ink-stained cuff):** "— It's *worse* on paper. The horn's got the *words* and the *witness* and the— it's got *everything* on it, and if I say it out loud wrong, souls hear me do it. On paper I can write the clause nine times and not one of them *costs* me anything, and that's exactly what the cyclops did." *(a beat)* "…Viv. How do you put a *cost* in ink?"
**Viv (flat, taking the third sheet, and the lawyer's answer is the chapter's first big win):** "— You don't. You put it **around** it. You write down what was *read*, by *who*, to *whom*, *where*, *when* — and then you write, in your own hand, that this page is a copy of a *reading* and **not a law**, and you sign it. A copy that tells the truth about itself is evidence. A copy that doesn't is a **rumour**, which is what the Barrens calls a clause with no witness." *(a beat)* "…Congratulations. You've just invented the realm's first **document**."

### P3.2 — The page that travels (half page)
**Art:** The document itself, drawn full-frame, and the art team's rule is that it must look **plain and permanent**: a half-sheet, ruled by the scribe's ruler, with the realm's cut script *quoted* inside it; three signatures at the foot (Kharza's hand; Viv's hand; and the gnome-scribe's stamp, in wax, which the lettering team must set in the *stamped plate-type* register); and the line that does the work, lettered plainly: ***this is the copy of a reading. It is not a law, and it was never carried by one.*** Around it, four small frames of the document being handled — folded into Kharza's satchel, shown to Caravan-Lead, stamped by the scribe, and read by **Caravan-Orc**, who is the chapter's surprise: the guard reads it *slowly*, and then *twice*, and then he starts, quietly and forever after, doing something the art team must plant here and never explain: **when the clause is read aloud anywhere near him, he puts his hand on his own arm** — not his heart, not his sword; his **arm** — because he was born in the Barrens and left it thirty years ago and has just been handed back the thing he left.
**Caravan-Orc (flat, handing the page back, and the guard's two sentences are worth more than the chapter's whole argument):** "— Read it again at the halt. Slow. …I know the words. I've just not heard them with a *road* under me."

## Page 4 — *The Angel Asks*

### P4.1 — Azrael's question (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's heart, staged as **two people walking at the tail of a caravan, one behind the other, in the dust, at evening**: **Azrael** behind the last wagon, hood down (he has been wearing the hood less since Ulgar), and **Kharza** beside him with the travel-horn on her back. The art team's rule for the whole page: **do not draw a single glow, halo, wing-shadow or holy light** — the saga's law (no race is the monster; no angel is the light) applies, and this is a conversation between a nineteen-year-old and a two-hundred-year-old liar with a good heart. Four frames: **(1)** Azrael asking the question; **(2)** Kharza not answering immediately — the art team must draw her *thinking*, which for this character means she looks at his *hands*, because the realm reads hands; **(3)** Azrael telling it, flatly and briefly, for the first time on the page since Vol. 1: the town, the order, the parchment, the tear; **(4)** the two of them stopped, the caravan going on ahead of them, and neither moving.
**Azrael (quiet, and this is his whole arc opened in one question, asked of the only person in the party who has never once been told what he did):** "\u2014 Your realm's got a word in its horn now for the hand that keeps every rule and takes a realm with it. …What's it got for the hand that **breaks one**, and keeps every other word for two hundred years, and can't put the broken one *down*?" *(a beat)* "I tore an oath off a page. A page, Kharza. It was written in ink by people I loved, and I read it every year, and one year the reading wasn't enough to save a *town*, and I put my hand through the seal. And your realm—" *(a beat, and he stops, because he has never said this part out loud)* "— your realm would have made me **keep it in my arm**. In my own hand. Where I'd have to *read it every time I raised my hand to help anybody.* …I've spent two hundred years being *glad* it was only paper. And I've spent two hundred years *knowing* that's the wrong answer, and I've never once been able to explain why."

### P4.2 — Kharza's answer (half page)
**Art:** Kharza's reply, and the art team's direction: **she does not comfort him and she does not forgive him.** She does what the realm does — she takes his question *literally*, and then she reads at him, and the page must make the reader feel the difference between a *reading* and a *lecture*: it is the same words, and it is aimed at a specific man. Four frames: **(1)** her rolling her sleeve in the dusk, deadpan, because this is work; **(2)** the clause read aloud — and the lettering team's rule: her voice is drawn as **ordinary**, no lettering effects, because the realm's readings have never once been dramatic; **(3)** the *reason* read after it, from **Khura's sliver** — the realm's first postal article — and the reason is not a sentence about laws; **(4)** Kharza's own two sentences at the end, and the art team must draw her looking at the horn's face, not at him.
**Kharza (flat, reading off the sliver, and this is the reason the realm cut in Ch. 23, carried out of the realm for the first time):** "\u2014 *Because a rule you can't say is a rule you're going to break by accident, and then somebody's going to have to hold your house for it.*" *(a beat; she hands him nothing, and closes her sleeve)* "…That's my realm's *reason*. Not *nobler*, just plainer." *(a beat)* "And as for your hand — I'm nineteen, so I'll say the thing the war-chief'd make me say at a stone instead of the kind thing. Your realm wrote your oath on paper so it could be *carried*. Mine cuts them in arms so they can't be *put down*. …You want to know which one *works*? Ask a man who's been carrying his for two hundred years. You've been *paying* the whole time, angel. Your paper just never told you *what for*."

## Page 5 — *Dorno's Cross*

### P5.1 — The weighbridge (half page)
**Art:** The seam-port of **Dorno's Cross**, and the art team's design brief: **a place where nine realms' roads all agree to be boring.** Not a bazaar, not a wonder — a **weighbridge**: a long roof on posts, a platform with iron scales, a wall of pigeon-holes full of paper, four clerks, a stool for the bridge-keeper, and one gate, and beyond the gate the road goes east and gets *wider* because other realms' roads are feeding into it. Every realm's mark is carved on the gateposts — including, new this month, **the Barrens' reading-post**, and the art team's detail: it has been put up on the **east** side of the gate, in the shade, with a chair that nobody is sitting in yet because the realm's eastbound reader has just arrived with the party and is *looking at it*. The caravan queues. The paperwork happens. And this is the chapter's one piece of pure world-building joy: **the team watches a bridge-keeper win an argument with a stamped paper**, and Viv — who has spent five chapters watching a realm with no paper do law better than any realm she has seen — gets to watch the thing she has always wanted: **a bureaucracy being *skipped***, because the realm's reader has no papers at all, and the bridge-keeper cannot find a category for her, and finally marks the gate-book, in ink and in the plate-type register, with the phrase the art team will use for the rest of the volume: ***NO PAPERS — READS.***
**Caravan-Lead (flat, watching the gate-book, and the chapter's driest joke arrives without any character acknowledging it):** "— Two hundred trips, and I've bribed that man twice, and a girl with a *horn* just walked through him. …I'm not asking. Whatever your realm is, I don't want to be holding it when it *changes hands*."

### P5.2 — The Reforgers' freight (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's turn to plot, staged as **an ordinary queue**: ahead of the team at the weighbridge, six wagons with plain boards — a freight company with a **clean, unstamped, unremarkable manifest**, and the art team's direction: **do not draw the Reforgers' mark.** No sigil, no black plate, no uniforms. Just freight: **crates of ledgers**, by the shape and the number, and a *clerk* in a plain coat, and — the detail the reader will remember — **the crates are stamped with a small, single, horizontal cut**, the same mark Fenn's ledger draws at the top of a page. Six frames: **(1)** the queue; **(2)** the manifests being stamped; **(3)** **Roderick**, and this is his single silent panel (rule 6): he has taken a manifest off the stack — no dialogue, no caption — and he is reading the bottom of the page, where a standing order's line is printed, and the line carries **his own name**; **(4)** the crates going through the gate east, and **Viv**, watching the crates and *counting them*, because this is what she does; **(5)** Viv's face; **(6)** the six wagons going east on the wider road, drawn small, from behind, with the Barrens' new reading-post chair in the foreground *facing the wrong way*.
**Viv (quiet, to Morgatha, doing arithmetic out loud in the manner of a woman who has learned this realm's habits):** "— Six wagons. All paper. They're not burning anything and they're not *hiding* anything: they're **moving a records office** east, through a bridge-keeper, with a manifest, and every single step of it is *filed correctly*." *(a beat)* "…Your realm taught me a word for that last week and I put it in a horn's margin and it hasn't been voted, so I'll say the other thing I've got. They're not outrunning your law. They're outrunning the *four days* it takes to read it."

## Page 6 — *The Tavern Where the Nine Meet*

### P6.1 — Seam-Tavern (half page)
**Art:** **Seam-Tavern** (03b: the halfling who keeps the tavern where the Nine meet), and the art team's design rule: **it is a tavern *at a seam*, so it is nine taverns overlaid.** One room, one counter, and nine different ways of sitting in it: a Nocturne corner with lamps turned down at the far end; a Khazadûm bench built for dwarves and a raised table for cyclopes; an elven long-table where a high elf and a wood elf can eat without looking at each other; a water-trough cut into the floor for selkies and merfolk; goblin rafters; a demon's high stool by the fire; a gargoyle's perch on the porch; and, new — the art team's detail, drawn exactly once and never remarked on — **a plain chair by the door, with a nail beside it**, because the tavern-keeper heard there was a realm now that reads at doors, and left the chair for whoever came. And this is the chapter's set piece: the realm's law gets **its first reading in another realm**, and it happens in a tavern, at the request of a halfling, in front of maybe thirty people from nine realms, at a table that has been cleared of supper.
**Seam-Tavern (flat, wiping the counter, to Kharza, and the halfling's request is the whole of the chapter's plot in one sentence):** "— Word came down the road ahead of you: there's a realm east of the pass that reads at people. …Well. This is nine realms' front door and I've been keeping it thirty years and I've had *three* fights on this floor over *debts nobody could prove*. So: **read it at my room.** And if it's rubbish, I'll say so, and you'll eat standing up."

### P6.2 — The reading, in a room of nine realms (half page)
**Art:** The reading itself, and the art team's craft: **this is the volume's device turned inside out.** In the Barrens, readings are drawn as **a field of forearms, read from behind and above**; here, in a room where nobody's law is written on anybody, the art team must draw the *listeners* — **hands, mugs set down, a demon's ledger open because he came to check it against his contract, a selkie's arms folded, an elf's chin on a fist, goblins in the rafters quiet for once, a gargoyle's head down from the porch, the tavern-keeper with a cloth stopped mid-wipe** — and one chair by the door that stays empty. And the last frame must be the thing the chapter promises: a **young carter**, orc-descended, thirty years out of Ulgar with a Barrens accent gone soft, gets up, and asks a question the lettering team sets small and plain — ***may I be read in?*** — and Kharza, nineteen, does not have a rule for it, and does the thing the realm would do: she takes the sliver out of her satchel, and cuts nothing (no horn for it, no right to cut), and instead does what Viv taught her on Page 3: **she writes it down** — the name, the day, the door, the ask — on paper, in ink, and reads it back to him, and says the sentence that closes the chapter's argument: *"It isn't a law out here. It's a reading. When somebody carries the word east, your ask goes in with it."*
**The carter (flat, and this is 03b's caravan-orc payoff arriving by a different door — a man who left and has been forty years oddly quiet about it):** "\u2014 I left the Barrens at sixteen and I've a family here and I've never once made them hear about it. …My mother's house closed a ford with the Drog's people, and it was *code*, and I've been *ashamed* of it for forty years without a word for the shame. If your realm's got a horn that *says* things now — **say it.** Say: *his ask is entered.* …That's all. I don't want it *settled*. I want it *read*."

## Page 7 — *The West's First Dispatch*

### P7.1 — Quick, and the waybill (half page)
**Art:** The road east at dawn, and the chapter's plot movement arrives at a run: **Quick** (03b goblin courier) catching the caravan *from the west* — dust, a bag, a horn-sliver, and a **waybill stamped by the swarm**, and the art team's rule: the swarm's paperwork is **stamped plate-type** (Terranian register) *because the goblins carry for everyone and read nothing*, and the chapter's joke is that the realm's reader has to read a courier's stamp. **END OF CHAPTER 26.**

*The realm that keeps its law in its arms has spent five chapters changing its code, and this chapter takes the change out onto a road where nobody can read forearms at all, and the answer to "how does a law travel" turns out to be the answer this whole volume has been circling: **it doesn't.** A law that cannot be carried as text has to be carried as a *person who has paid for the right to say it*, which means the realm's newest export is a nineteen-year-old with a chisel and a satchel of horn-slivers, and its newest product is not a statute but a habit: read the ask, read the reason, say the realm's word whole, and never refuse a reading. Along the way the party meets paper — the thing every other realm keeps instead of skin — and the youngest reader in the party finds out that ink has one enormous advantage and one enormous flaw: it travels, and it does not have to pay. So Viv invents the realm's first document, and it is deliberately weak: a copy that declares itself a copy of a *reading*, with names, days and doors on it, and a line saying plainly that it is *not a law*. On the same road, a fallen angel who has been carrying an unwitnessed oath for two hundred years asks the reader a question, and the realm's rule about witnesses — the line a hostage's sister cut into a horn to punish a war — turns out to fit him exactly: the witness is the one it costs, so he names his cost aloud, in front of witnesses, and the travel-horn takes its second entry, and the man who was never forgiven becomes the first person *read* rather than pardoned. And behind it all, on the western roads, the realm's war continues to be conducted out of chairs: two crossings taken without a blow, a toll nobody could prove gone, and four hundred arms discovering that the most frightening thing they can do to an enemy who uses courtesy is **sit down in his room and read their own arm at him.** The road drops toward a band of water that no one in the party is looking at yet, the map has three dots on it for a law that has been read three times, and a column on a waybill stays blank because a 312-year-old mapmaker is still counting days.*
**The record's cards are hide, and the newest one has two dots and a line between them that are not in the key, and nobody in the story will ever say why.**
