# AURELION · Volume 7 — Chapter 41: "The Door in the Page"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc G: "The Oath-Eater" — the twelfth morning: the largest promise this realm can make, and the sentence nobody in the room wrote.** Eight years without a Re-Oath end at dawn, and Emberfall does what it has always done with a great thing: it does it at a gate, in a queue, with clerks. The realm re-oaths **by signature** — the only realm in the nine that signs itself — and because a spoken promise hangs in this realm's air in contract-gold until it is kept, the twelfth morning's page goes up at the ash gate under the gate-law that tells strangers their oaths are cheap, and by noon there is a whole realm's worth of gold standing over one leaf of soft black stone. The Court reads the First Contract aloud from the **leaves** for the first time in eight years — not from the wall's reading-copy, which is what this realm has been reading at gates twice a week for four thousand years — and the order of the day is the order the volume has been building: **the ninth field is read first** (a blank line, an outsider's name reserved until it is hers again), the parties enter as **witnesses and do not sign**, the calling is read at noon in the middle of the signing, and the last oath signs last. And in the middle of the largest promise this realm can make, a sentence gets read aloud that nobody in the room wrote — because the fourth leaf of the founding contract has one property no reader in four thousand years has ever looked for, and the realm's newest reader finds it the way the realm has just learned to find anything: with a finger, under the line. ***The sentence hasn't got a floor.*** The clause that made the Contract void was read as a hole in a sentence. It is a **hole in a page**: the only text in the founding contract cut clean through the slate, invisible from above (because a realm that reads by sight reads the top of a letter and never its depth), and impossible to mistake at the right hour, against the right lamp, when the light comes **through** the words. The realm has spent four thousand years reading a wall. Today it read the leaf, and found the door — and then found out, in the same morning, what its own honesty had been keeping that door open for: not a man, not a claim, but the plain arithmetic of a rule it refuses to change (*nothing is withheld from a reader*), an office that is held by reading, a lamp entered to nobody, and a call posted at the gate that it has now read aloud twelve times without an answer. The Nine witness. The realm signs. And the door, being cut and not written, cannot be shut.
