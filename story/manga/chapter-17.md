# AURELION · Volume 4 — Chapter 17: "The Eve"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc D: "The Mountain-Ask" (the seven days, compressed — a mine closes its own books by law; a year's worth of somebody's word leaves west in a cart; eight chairs are carried down a mountain to face a wall; an anvil goes home on its heir's shoulder past fifty-eight doorways that all reach out and touch it; and a realm that has answered its own silence for forty years opens its mouth to answer it again — and stops)**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Grimm (**the anvil**), Kaelen (**the wrapped hammer**), Lyra, Morgatha (**the count — nine, eleven**), Nereia (**the lamps**), Azrael, Viv (**the procedure**), Bramble, Fenn (the cascade, *early*, because the chapter's last word is not his), Marginalia, Sprocket. **The support** — **Roderick** (his one quiet panel: the mine's mouth, which has no door). **The stone realm** — **Duelist Molka** (**her one spoken line of Volume 4: the keeper's question**), **Mountain-Speaker Khazri** (**silent — and this chapter he *moves***), **Echo** (the slate; the word that does not get said), the **eight chairs** (seven carried by miners, one carried by the Speaker himself), **Maren Ashwood** (one line, at the anvil), **Tilda**, **Pebble** (**asleep in an anvil's hollow; the coat, repaired**), the **grummpling clan** (a vigil, rationed), **Grand-Anvil Khazur** (the book's reader, rehearsing), **Orik Khazur**, **Stonehand Drek** (**the eve's honest statement**), **Skaal**, **Norga**, **Pick · Urka · Torvik** (silent, at the rail), **the fifty-eight** (hands, reaching), **the dusty child** (**under the eighth chair**), nine thousand dwarves (**seated — on their own anvils**).
> **Locations / threads:** the Cut's approach roads (the realm filing in) → the rack-walk (the year closed by lamp) → the shipping dock (the last consignment west) → the Hall of Speakers (the chairs taken up) → the terraces and the fifty-eight (**the anvil's walk**) → **the Court Floor** (the deepest gallery, nine hundred anvils in rows, the exhibit at its mouth) → the cistern and the water-lamps → the deep gallery at dawn (**the Asking**).
> **Arc beat:** Seven days become this chapter, and the frame's tally does the running. The mine closes the year *by its own law* — the keeper walks the racks and shuts the lamps one at a time, and the shift-horn sounds over a mine where nobody moves, because there is no shift: the year is unread and the realm is a court. A cart of forfeited braids leaves west at dawn, unstopped and *documented*, because Viv has decided that the buyer's realm keeps records and Vol. 9 will want them. Eight chairs come down the mountain — seven carried by miners whose own braids are scored, one carried by the Mountain-Speaker alone at night, and set in an arc facing the raw wall where the hairline runs. The Khazdûrin anvil walks home down the fifty-eight: the coal pot riding it, the street laying hands on it as it passes, a dwarf carrying his grandfather's anvil and a boy carrying a wrapped hammer behind him. On the Court Floor, nine thousand dwarves sit down on nine hundred anvils, and Viv teaches a realm its own courtroom in one afternoon — the first time in the saga's history that a people find out they have *law*. Drek makes the mine's statement, and it is true: the mine has kept every promise it ever made. Viv answers that the mine is not on trial; the *oath form* is — and the one witness the court has never once called is the mountain itself. Then the eve's rite: the keeper opens the book, asks the year's question, and the realm — which has answered its own silence with the same four reassuring words for forty years — opens its mouth to say them, and *stops*. Nothing is answered. Everything has changed.
> **Motifs advanced:** the count (**the tally's seven days in the frame's edge; Morgatha's nine → eleven**), the account (the year **closed by lamp**, not by writ), the door (**a mine has no door, because a door would make it a place you could shut**), the witness (**nine hundred anvils as the jury, and the one witness uncalled**), the kept thing (**a pot of coal riding an anvil; a wrapped hammer; two slates on a chair's seat**), the empty (**the eighth chair, stood away from; a formula that does not get said**), the lamps (**lit by a diviner who was never asked**), the record (**the card: *Here read the named***), the ink-and-stone (**the exhibit, hung on the mine's own bolts**), the flask (**cracked, and being spent on somebody else's dark**). **Not in this chapter:** the stone-song; the Ask; Bastion's sentence; any line at all from Khazri; the geode; the golems; what the hairline is for; the trial itself.

## Page 1 — *The Seven Days (the tally runs)*

### P1.1 — The approach roads, compressed (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's first page is a **four-frame strip that eats a week**, and the tally in the frame's edge does the running: **SEVEN** on the first frame, **SIX**, **FIVE**, **THREE** on the last (the art team: cut the marks *deeper* as they fall — this is the one page in the volume where the tally moves fast enough for the reader to catch it moving). **Frame one:** the Cut's approach road, filled — families walking down with bread, bedrolls, children, and *anvils on hand-carts*; the whole realm coming to a mine, not to work. **Frame two:** miners at the shaft-mouth being *turned away* from their own shift — not by guards: by a **closed lamp** (the rack-lamp at the shaft's head, shuttered); men standing in a crowd, hats in hands, reading it. **Frame three:** the work, turned around: the same miners building **rows** in the deepest gallery — anvils set in graded ranks, families' marks forward, aisles left between the ranks, so that the floor reads as *seating*; a foreman with a chalk-string laying it out like a nave. **Frame four:** **the exhibit** at the gallery's mouth, and this is the joke the chapter opens on and does not explain: the mine has built it a *frame* — iron, bolted into the rock on the mine's own bolts, roofed, lit — because if there is going to be an exhibit in Drek's hole, it will be a *well-made* exhibit. **Viv**, standing in front of it, arms folded, looking at the craftsmanship with an expression the art team should make impossible to read.
**Viv (the em-dash, flat, to nobody — and this is the whole chapter's politics in one line):** "— He hung my evidence. *Level.*"
**Marginalia (tiny italics):** *"The cart notes that the mine is helping the case, and that the cart has met villains before and none of them ever *carpented*."*

### P1.2 — The hollow anvil (bottom strip)
**Art:** Three frames of small warm things in a big cold week: **(1)** **Pebble**, asleep **inside the hollow base of an anvil** (dwarven anvils are waisted at the base; a grummpling fits; the art team: this must look like the most comfortable bed in the world and it must be *Pebble's own idea* — his blanket is a mine rag, his pillow is a boot-heel). **(2)** The **grummpling clan's vigil**, rationed: Cinder, sitting on an upturned crate with her back to the gallery, feeding her clan *on a schedule*, because they will be in this mine for eight days and she has done the arithmetic; the clan, entirely content, in a circle. **(3)** **Khazur**, in a side gallery, alone, **rehearsing**: the Grand-Anvil with the festival's book open on a barrel, reading the year's first lines out loud to a wall, twice, slowly, correcting his own *care* — the art team's key small image of the chapter: the most powerful dwarf in the realm, practising so as not to make a mistake.
**Khazur (to the wall, dry, catching himself, flipping back a page):** "…No. 'Settled' comes *after* the witness. Again."

## Page 2 — *The Year, Closed by Lamp (the racks go dark)*

### P2.1 — The rack-walk (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's first great silent sequence. **Molka** walks the **braid-racks** — the mile of rails, a thousand tags deep, the mine's whole ledger of promises — with **one rack-lamp in her hand**, and at each rank she **closes the lamp's shutter** (a small iron disc, turned: the art team must design the gesture so the reader learns it by the third one). Rank by rank, the mile of rack goes *dark* behind her, and the tags stay exactly where they are. She is not confiscating anything; she is not punishing anything; she does not look angry. She is **closing the year**, because the book says the year's business cannot be conducted while the year is unread — and in this realm *that is law*, and it is the first time in forty years that anyone has needed it. **Drek** stands at the far end with his arms folded, watching his own mine get shut by a rule in a book, and he is not fighting it, and the art team must make his face *satisfied*.
**Drek (flat, to no one in particular, watching the last lamp close — and this is the volume's most dangerous line):** "Good. Book's shut. Now nobody can buy a thing until the realm's looked at it. …What do you think a *year's reading* is worth, to a mine?"
**Sprocket (flat, from the gallery mouth, board under one arm):** "…That's the first time a villain's ever said something I'd have charged for."

### P2.2 — The horn (bottom third)
**Art:** Three frames, and the page's real ending. **(1)** The mine's **shift-horn** sounds at the shaft-mouth, the way it has sounded twice a day for forty years. **(2)** Nine thousand dwarves hear it — in the galleries, on the stairs, on the approach roads — and **nobody moves.** No hats come off, no ropes are taken up, no ropes are put down. The art team: draw the *people* — a staircase full of dwarves standing still with a horn in the air over them. **(3)** The horn stops. One frame of the horn, alone in its frame, at the top of the mine, with nobody's hand on it and no hand coming.
**SFX:** *(the horn. Then, one panel, no SFX: the largest room in the mountain, and no sound in it.)*
**Fenn (flat, in the margin, from the cart — the map maker's log, and it is the shortest he has written all volume):** *"Day six. The horn called nobody. I have never in three hundred years drawn a *silence* that had a shape like a *shift*."*

## Page 3 — *The Last Cart West (documented, not stopped)*

### P3.1 — The shipping dock at dawn (half page)
**Art:** The dock outside the Cut: a single cart, **bales of braids** in twine (Ch. 16's product, now leaving), a **carter** (unnamed; a *human*, from the Seams — the art team: keep him ordinary, tired, professional; he does not know or care what is in the bales, and that is the point), and the mine's clerk with the consignment slip. Loading it, in the frame: **the child's braid** from Ch. 16 P5.2, on the top bale, tag legible. Watching: **Viv**, **Fenn**, **Sprocket**, **Bramble**, and — small, at the frame's edge, sitting on a fence rail with her heels drumming — **the dusty child**. **Drek** is *not* there, which is the mark of the page: he has nothing to hide, and nothing to say, and he has already signed.
**Carter (flat, professional, to the clerk — the only dialogue the cart gets, and it should be mundane):** "Terrania. Nine days, barring the ferry. Sign says the *buyer* fetches the *bills*, so I'm just the road."
**Bramble (quiet, on the rail, to Viv, watching the child's braid go up onto the cart — and this is her chapter line, and it is very small):** "…Somebody's going to have a *very* neat desk."
**Viv (the em-dash, flat — the decision stated once, and the reader must be able to disagree with it):** "— Let it go. I can't stop it, and I shouldn't: it's the *best* exhibit we've got. A cart of severed oaths on a road to a realm whose whole religion is *paperwork*. Let them file it. Let them *stamp* it." *(a beat)* "Vol. 9's problem."
**Fenn (dry, without looking up from the map, making a mark on the western route):** "You'll note I'm not the one who *said* that out loud."

### P3.2 — Three frames (bottom third)
**Art:** **(1)** Sprocket, chalking the board: a new row, **"FORFEIT: EXPORT"**, with a number, and *no total* (the art team: the column stays open). **(2)** Nereia at the dock's water-trough — the miner's water — pouring from her flask into the **trough**, watching it go down, saying *nothing* (the art team: this is her chapter's first beat and it must be under-noticed by every character in the frame; only the reader sees her spend her own clock on somebody else's thirst). **(3)** The dusty child, hopping down off the rail and walking *into the mine*, past a guard who does not stop her, because she has been in his mine three days already and he has stopped noticing her.
**Marginalia (tiny italics):** *"The cart notes that nobody in this realm has asked the small dusty person for a name, and that she has not offered one, and that the cart intends to keep noting this until somebody does."*

## Page 4 — *Eight Chairs (carried down a mountain)*

### P4.1 — The chair-bearers (half page)
**Art:** The Hall of Speakers, morning of the fifth day. The **eight stone chairs** are being taken up — and the art team's key direction: **the miners volunteer**, and they volunteer in a particular way: seven of the eight chairs are carried by **miners** — six of them scored through (the reader can see the tags in their beards; one of them is **Pick**) and the seventh the man who *tied* the knots (**Norga**, the braider, who has done hundreds of these by hand and carries a chair with both hands and no expression at all). They handle the chairs like *reliquaries* — because they are: eight chairs is the realm's whole listening, and it has been sitting empty on a mountain above a mine for years. **Echo** walks alongside with the two slates in a satchel; **Khazri** walks *behind* everybody, and does not take a chair, and nobody asks him to.
**Pick (flat, cheerful, carrying a chair worth more than his contract — and this is the chapter's thesis arriving in a miner's mouth, uninvited):** "Nobody in my family ever got to *sit* in one of these. But carrying one down? That's an *arm*. I'll take the arm."
**Echo (flat, correcting him, because the apprentice is the law's memory):** "Seven are *empty*, Pick. The eighth is *his*. You're carrying the *silence's* chair."
**Pick (a beat; then, without any change of tone):** "…Still an arm. Watch the step."

### P4.2 — The eighth chair, at night (half page)
**Art:** The mountain's shoulder at night, and the page's key image: **Khazri carries his own chair down the mountain, alone.** No lamp (he knows the road), no escort, no ceremony — an old dwarf with a stone chair on his back, walking down a switchback, with the mine's lights below him like a lake of embers. In the panel's *edges*, drawn but never foregrounded: **two of the team** watching from above (Lyra and Fenn; the art team: nobody follows him, nobody helps him, and this is the only look any of them get at the Speaker's face all volume). And in the last frame of the page, down on the Court Floor: the chair set in the arc, and — the art team must draw this as *quietly as possible* — the **seven empty chairs face the gallery's raw wall**, and the eighth chair is set **half a step back** from the line, by Khazri, who then goes and stands at the wall itself instead of sitting.
**Fenn (quiet, in the margin, watching him go down):** *"He's not carrying it. He's *bringing* it. There's a difference and I've spent eleven days trying to draw it."*
**Lyra (quiet, on the switchback above, to Fenn, and she does not explain — she has been keeping an account of her own since Ch. 2):** "He's taking it to the only wall in this realm that might still be *saying* something."

## Page 5 — *The Anvil's Walk (fifty-eight doorways, fifty-eight hands)*

### P5.1 — The cold room, the last morning (half page)
**Art:** The Khazdûrin forge, morning of the fourth day. The **anvil** is uncovered, **hoisted** — and the art team's key direction: it is not being *hauled*; it is being **dressed**: the family's nine-strand mark wiped clean, the horn polished by hand, the base wrapped in the old harness leather, and — the detail that must be in the frame and must never be pointed at — the **clay pot with the century's coal set into the anvil's base-hollow**, lashed there, *riding the anvil down*. **Grimm** with the hoist rope; **Kaelen** behind him with **Askstone in its cloth** (the hammer has been wrapped for four chapters; the reader will now get it: it is the only thing in this room that is not being *carried open*). **Maren** with her coat sleeves rolled, doing the last thing she does before a family's piece goes out: she wipes the anvil's face with a cloth, once, and puts the cloth in her pocket — the art team: she does not give it to anybody, she keeps it.
**Maren (flat, stepping back to look at it — a Seam-woman's verdict on a hundred years of silence):** "There. It's *clean*. Whatever it says down there, it says it *clean*."
**Grimm (quiet, hoisting, not looking at her, a son):** "…Ma. If it doesn't—"
**Maren (cutting him off, the way she has cut him off since he was nine, and this is the sentence she has been holding for three chapters):** "Then it doesn't, and you'll have carried your grandfather down a mountain in front of *fifty-eight doors*, and this street will have watched you do it. That's not *nothing*, boy. That's a *funeral that got done right*."

### P5.2 — The fifty-eight (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's money page — **the anvil's walk**, drawn as a *procession one street long*: Grimm carrying his grandfather's anvil on a shoulder-yoke; Kaelen with the wrapped hammer; Bramble; Lyra; the grummpling clan; the *dusty child* (walking backwards in front of it, the way children do); and the **street** — all fifty-eight doorways of it, opening one after another as the anvil comes, and *every doorway's people coming out to lay a hand on it*. Not a salute; not a blessing: **hands on iron**, one at a time, the way you touch a person, and each one doesn't stop the walk. The art team's key detail: in the middle of the procession, the **one doorway with the mark chiselled off** — and its door opens too, and a hand comes out, and lays on the anvil, and goes back in. **Nobody says anything about it. Roderick is in the frame, at the edge, and it is not his panel.**
**Art note (the panel the page is for):** **Old Varda**, at the end of the street with the keadsake box under her arm, does not touch the anvil with her hand — she touches it with the **slate**, the laughing man's back, once, and then walks back inside. No dialogue. The art team: the slate's face must be *toward* the anvil for that frame, and toward the reader, or this panel does not work.
**Tova (calling down the street from her forge door, cheerfully, to the whole procession, and it is the sound of a realm changing its mind out loud):** "Khazdûrin! When you've heard it, come BACK and tell us what it *said*! Forty years of guessing is enough!"

## Page 6 — *The Court Floor (the mine becomes a room)*

### P6.1 — Nine thousand dwarves, nine hundred anvils (2/3 page)
**Art:** **The Court Floor**, evening of the third day, from above and *long*: the deepest gallery, and in it the realm's anvils in ranked rows (Ch. 15's haul, finally doing the thing it was hauled to do) with **nine thousand dwarves seated on them** — on their own families' anvils, marks forward, aisles between. The art team's key direction: this must be *legible as a court* — the eight Speaker chairs at the head, in their arc, facing the raw wall where the hairline ran; a **low stone table** at the center with the **iron basket of ninety-one cut braids** on it; the **braid-racks going dark down the gallery's far end**; the lamps in clean rows; the **exhibit** at the mouth with its mine-built frame; and, threaded through all of it, the ordinary mess of a place nine thousand people have been living in for three days: bedrolls, kettles, children asleep across anvils, a washing line between two ranks, an old woman selling soup. It must be *warm* and it must be *enormous*, and the reader must be able to count the emptiness at the head of the room (the eight chairs, the dark racks, the wall).
**Azrael (dry, standing with the team, looking at a realm on its own furniture, and the fallen angel gets the line about *church* because nobody else is allowed to):** "I've stood in four cathedrals. …This is the first one that's *honest*. There's no altar. There's just the *books*, and everybody's *sitting on their own.*"
**Sprocket (flat, board under one arm, and he has finally found the right number for the occasion):** "Attendance: *everybody*."

### P6.2 — Roderick, at the mouth (bottom third — his one quiet panel)
**Art:** The mine's outer mouth, night, and the art team's key direction for **Roderick's single panel**: he stands in the *doorway-shaped* gap at the top of the Cut, and **there is no door in it** — no frame, no gate, no hinge; the mine is a hole with a road through it, because a mine that could be shut would have to be *somebody's* to shut. The captain's hand is flat on the *side* of the opening, on the rock, not on a frame that isn't there. No dialogue. Exactly one panel (the art team: fourth threshold in five chapters — a river, a mine-rail, a road, and now a doorway that is *only rock*; the rule is that he is never given two).
**SFX:** *(wind down a hole. The mine's own draught, which never stops, and which the art team should have been drawing since Ch. 14: the Cut breathes.)*

## Page 7 — *Viv Teaches a Realm Its Own Law (the afternoon of the second day)*

### P7.1 — Procedure, out loud (2/3 page)
**Art:** The Court Floor, and the page is a *lesson*: **Viv** standing on a low crate with the festival's book open (the book is Khazur's; he lent it, which is itself an event), and — the art team's key direction — **the realm listening like a class**, because nobody in the mountain has ever been told *how their own court works*. Rank after rank of dwarves, sitting forward; children lifted onto shoulders; the miners' scored braids visible everywhere; **Echo** taking notes on a slate (the realm has no pens — *he is the pen*); **Khazur** standing to one side with his arms folded and a small, dangerous, entirely genuine *pride* on his face; **Drek** at the rail above, listening.
**Viv (the em-dash — fast, flat, precise, and this must be *enjoyable* to read, because the realm is enjoying it):** "— Here's the shape, and I'll give it to you once. **One:** the keeper opens the book and asks the year's question. **Two:** the year is read out loud — every entry, in order, and the realm hears it. **Three:** *any* dwarf present may stand and say **'I object'**, and then the realm hears *that*, too. **Four:** when the year's read, the book's last item is the **naming of the anvils**. **Five:** the year's word is judged." *(a beat; she turns a page)* "That's it. That's your law. It has been sitting in this book for four hundred years and you have used it to *count iron*."
**A miner in the third rank (cheerfully, standing up — an unnamed voice, and the art team should give him an ordinary face and a good beard):** "Can I object *now*?"
**Viv (flat, delighted, not smiling — and this is her best line of the volume):** "— No. But hold it. Hold it *tight*. You're going to want it *sharp*."
**Art note (the page's last frame):** the realm *laughs* — one full-drawn panel of nine thousand dwarves laughing in a mine, and it should be the warmest panel in Volume 4, and it should be uncomfortable, because the reader knows what the laughter is going to cost.

### P7.2 — The mine's statement (half page)
**Art:** **Drek**, on the Court Floor, standing where the realm can see him (the art team: not on a dais — on the floor, at the front of a rank, the way a man stands in his own workplace). No papers (there are no papers). He speaks, and the realm *listens politely*, because he is a mine-owner and this is his hole and everybody here has taken his coin at least once. **Viv** stands opposite with the book. **Khazri** at the wall behind them both, silent.
**Drek (flat, west-country, unhurried, and *entirely truthful* — this speech must be the most reasonable thing anybody says all volume):** "I'll not argue the *charge*. You'll read the year, and the year will say what it says. So I'll say mine, and then I'll sit down. This mine has kept **every promise it ever made**. Every contract, every wage, every widow-stay, every shaft closed when it went wrong — *kept*. The realm's own book has got it in *writing*, which is more than the realm's got for anything else in it." *(a beat; he looks around the ranks, and he is not lying and not posturing)* "If this court finds against me, it finds against a *form* — against the words on a plate — and not against a single broken word of mine. I've never broken a promise, and I've never made one I couldn't keep, and there's not a dwarf in this gallery who can say otherwise, because it's *true*."
**Viv (the em-dash, quiet, into the silence he leaves behind, and this is the volume's turn: she concedes the whole argument to win the case):** "— He's right. The mine's not on trial." *(a beat; she closes the book on her thumb, which the art team should frame as the *only* sound in the panel)* "The **oath form** is. Nine thousand promises were sworn to a witness that has never once been asked to testify — and this court has got exactly one remedy in it, and it has never been used, and it is sitting in the *book*, in the *first rank*, above the counting." *(she looks up, at the wall, at the chairs, at Khazri standing in front of it)* "The mountain's the only witness we haven't *called*."
**Art note (final two frames):** the realm going quiet — not hostile: **thinking**; and **Khazri**, at the wall, with his back to the room, and the panel gives him one small motion and no face: his hand comes up and rests flat on the stone.

## Page 8 — *Two Slates on a Seat (Khazri's word, which is not a word)*

### P8.1 — The eighth chair (half page)
**Art:** The eve of the first day, the Court Floor emptying for the night. **Khazri** walks down the arc to the **eighth chair** — the one he carried down and set half a step back — and **Echo** is there, with the two slates in his satchel: the laughing man signed **K.**, and the apprentice's single stroke. And Khazri — silent, and this is his only *action* of the volume — **takes both slates and lays them flat on the seat of the eighth chair**, side by side, facing out. Then he steps back from the chair, and goes and **stands against the wall** — which is, the reader will now understand, *where he has been standing for forty years.* One panel of the two slates on the seat (the art team: the laughing man face-up, the apprentice's stroke face-up, both toward the room). One panel of the chair. One panel of the man.
**Echo (quiet, to the back of the man at the wall, and the apprentice asks the only question he has left — this is his chapter, and the art team must not make him cry):** "…You're not going to *sit*, are you."
**Echo (next — and this is the beat that tells the reader exactly what the arc's turn is going to need: not a memory, not a drawing, not a chair, but a *word*):** "…I know the *shape* of it. I've got it *right*. Fourteen years, and it's *right*, and I can't *read* it." *(a beat)* "Say *that* back to me."
**Art note (the page's last frame):** **Khazri does not turn around.** The panel holds: an old dwarf at a wall, a boy at a chair, two slates on the seat, and the whole empty hall behind them. **No dialogue. No SFX. This is the closest the volume comes to the Speaker's voice, and it is a *silence with a shape*.**

### P8.2 — The dusty child under the chair (half page)
**Art:** Later that night: the Court Floor asleep — ranks of dwarves on their anvils under the dim lamp-rows, the ordinary snores and small sounds of nine thousand people. And at the head, in the arc of chairs, **the dusty child** — who has been in this mine for a week and belongs to nobody and has been fed by everyone — is **asleep on the floor under the eighth chair**, curled against its leg, with one hand out and **palm flat on the cold floor of the gallery, exactly the way Khazri's hand was flat on the wall earlier that day** (the art team: this is the page's quiet rhyme — do not point at it; the lettering must not comment; the reader finds it or doesn't). And in the frame's edge, one more ordinary thing, drawn without emphasis: the **lamp-rows**, and at their end, **Nereia**, going along the wall with the flask, filling the miners' water-lamps one by one; the level in the flask is low; the crack is the whole way across the glass now; her markings are at their dimmest and she has told nobody anything.
**Marginalia (tiny italics — and this is the third chapter in a row that the cart has tried to be funny here, and this time it does not):** *"The cart notes a small person asleep under the chair of a man who cannot speak, holding the floor like a hand holds a wall. The cart does not know why it finds this difficult to draw. The cart has drawn it four times."*

## Page 9 — *The Ask He Actually Needs (Grimm and Kaelen, the night before)*

### P9.1 — Two smiths and a wrapped hammer (half page)
**Art:** A side gallery, night, one lamp; the **Khazdûrin anvil** set down for the night with the **coal pot** still lashed in its base (a small warm glow at the anvil's foot, which the art team should let the reader keep noticing the way the characters can't stop noticing it); **Grimm** sitting on the floor with his back to the anvil, and **Kaelen** across from him with **Askstone in its cloth** laid across his knees; **Pebble** asleep in Grimm's lap, coat repaired, *visibly stuffed*, one hand on the pocket. This scene is the volume's heart, and it must be quiet, and it must be *undramatic*.
**Grimm (flat, a workman stating a problem, and this is the whole character in six sentences):** "Here's what I've got. Six women's memories. A drawing on the back of a slate. A hundred-year-old anvil my family never named, and a mountain that's said nothing to anybody since the Cut opened." *(a beat)* "And a *Speaker's* chair with two slates on it, one of which is a *laughing man* and the other's a *shape* nobody can read. So here's my ask, and I want you to hear it *properly*."
**Grimm (next — and this is the request the whole volume has been walking toward):** "Tomorrow, the realm reads the year, and then it *names the anvils*, and when it gets to *mine*, the law says the name has to be *answered*. So if it says **nothing** — if the stone's got no voice for a dead Khazdûrin on the day the realm's actually listening — I want you to **say the name anyway**. You've done it for a forest and a king and a *door*. I've got six witnesses and they're *alive*, and the man's *sad* face is hanging on my mother's wall next to a *laughing* one, and if the mountain keeps its mouth shut tomorrow, I'd rather my grandfather's name went into the year out of **your** mouth than not at all."
**Kaelen (quiet, and the answer must be *careful*, because the Name-Return has rules and this saga never breaks them):** "…A name's not a cure, Grimm. It's a *witness*. If I say him, I say him *with* what the realm remembers — the six, the slate, the street — and it won't sound like a mountain. It'll sound like a *street*."
**Grimm (flat, immediate, and this is the line):** "Good. He was a *street*."
**Kaelen (after a beat; and then, plainly, which is how the saga makes its promises):** "Then I'll be standing where you can see me. And if it goes quiet, you'll hear me say **Durin Khazdûrin**, and the whole realm can hear it with you. …And if the mountain *does* answer—"
**Grimm (finishing it, and the reader's spine should go cold, because the whole arc is standing here):** "Then nobody'll ever know which of us it *meant*."

### P9.2 — Lyra and Bramble, above (half page)
**Art:** The gallery's upper rail, the two of them watching the lamplit floor below (Lyra with her rings hand on the rail — the art team: count them, one more than Ch. 13, no comment; Bramble with the scarf, the tag *not* shown). This is the chapter's *team* beat: the two who notice things, noticing the same thing.
**Bramble (flat, quiet, watching Grimm not count his braids for once, because he has stopped counting anything this week):** "…You know what's *really* in that hole? Not the mine. Not the bickering. There's nine thousand people down there who've spent a *week* getting ready to listen to a *rock*." *(a beat)* "That's either the bravest thing I've ever seen or the saddest, and I've decided I'm not going to choose until tomorrow."
**Lyra (quiet, the elf, and she answers with the thing she has spent thirteen chapters learning):** "It's neither. It's a *council*, and it's been *waiting* — they've got the room, the book, the chairs and the year. What they haven't got is one *voice* to answer with." *(a beat)* "…The elf realm had to learn the same lesson. Ours came with an inheritance. Theirs has to *arrive*."
**Bramble (after a beat, dry, the mask giving nothing and the voice giving it all):** "…So we're doing *therapy* for a *mountain*."
**Lyra (and she *almost* smiles — the art team: one crack in the wall, and it is the only one in this chapter):** "Mm. Get in line."

## Page 10 — *The Lamps (what a diviner spends)*

### P10.1 — The cistern, at night (half page)
**Art:** The Cut's cistern, and the art team's key reveal of the chapter's back half: **the water-lamps along every gallery wall** — hundreds of them, each a small glass with a wick floating on water — and the reader has been *walking past them* for four chapters without being told: they are **hers.** **Nereia** at the cistern with the flask, filling the lamps one at a time; the flask's crack running the whole way across; the cistern's level **below the oldest depth-mark on the wall** (the art team: the marks are Ch. 15's; this chapter the water is *under* the lowest one, and it is not commented on). **Morgatha** arrives, corner of the frame, the count moving on the panel's edge (**nine → ten**), and this time she *helps*: she takes a lamp and holds it while Nereia pours. Neither of them remarks on it. It is the only kindness between the two of them in the volume and it takes four panels and no words.
**Nereia (flat, the diviner, counting aloud as she fills — and the reader should do the arithmetic with her):** "One hundred and six. …One hundred and seven." *(a beat; not looking up)* "Your turn. Watch the wick, it drowns if you're *generous*."
**Morgatha (flat, doing it, and then — the hunter, at a cistern, asking the thing nobody has asked out loud):** "Your flask's *broken*. How many days have you got left in it?"
**Nereia (and this is her chapter's last line, and it must be delivered *entirely flat*, the way she delivers weather):** "Two. Maybe three. …I'll not be needing them all. The realm's got a *year* to read and I've got a *desk* to sit at and a *cracked* flask, and I've decided I'd rather the lamps burned *tonight* than I burned *interesting* tomorrow."

### P10.2 — The realm's own water (bottom third)
**Art:** Three frames, and they are the chapter's *thesis in images* and must not be captioned. **(1)** A lamp on the gallery wall, burning; behind it, out of focus, a rank of sleeping dwarves. **(2)** The **cistern marks** in close: forty years of cut lines, and the water *under all of them*. **(3)** **Nereia's hands**, capped flask, wet, empty; and the tin cup she has been drinking from all week, chained to the cistern's pipe by the mine, small, ordinary.
**Azrael (dry, from the mouth of the gallery, watching her come up with the empty flask — the fallen angel's line, and he has earned it since Ch. 11):** "The sky-court used to say this about the sun: *it goes down so the field goes on*." *(a beat)* "They were lying. They meant: *it can't help it, and neither can you.*"
**Nereia (flat, walking past him, capping the flask — and this is the door to Vol. 6, and it must stay shut):** "Not yet. The song's not *mine* to spend on a mountain. It's *theirs* to ask for." *(a beat)* "Tomorrow, I'll be sitting at the *desk*. If the realm's got a question for the sea, it can ask it *properly*."

## Page 11 — *The Last Night (the cascade, early)*

### P11.1 — Fenn's cascade, at the mouth of the Cut (2/3 page)
**Art:** The team at the mine's mouth at night, the Court Floor's lamps below them like a city, the whole mountain's weight above (the art team: the composition is Ch. 13 P12.1's *from above*, one realm further: last time a grove, this time a *hole full of people*). **Fenn** with the map; **Marginalia** with the cart (the cart is *inside a mine*, which is the volume's quietest joke and must be drawn completely straight — a cart, a mine, chalk marks, the whole absurdity); **Sprocket** with the board, columns and columns; the Nine in the frame in ones and twos; across the valley, faint, the **braid-line's** carved pillars; and in the far distance, one silhouette the art team may draw *once* and never again this chapter: **Bastion**, patient, at the pass, facing the mine.
**Fenn (flat, the map maker — the cascade, delivered *early* on purpose, and the reader will feel the chapter turn under them when it ends the way it ends):** "…The book holds at the *keeper*. The keeper holds at the *question*. The question holds at the *year*. The year holds at the *entry*. The entry holds at the *word*. The word holds at the *hand*. The hand holds at the *braid*. The braid holds at the *account*. The account holds at the *court*. The court holds at the *chairs*. The chairs hold at the *wall*. The wall holds at the *stone*. The stone holds at the *silence*. The silence holds at the *room*. The room holds at the *realm*."
**Fenn (next, quieter — the honest version, pen down, which is the tell):** "…For now, the *realm* holds. Nine thousand dwarves are going to sleep on their own anvils tonight so that tomorrow they can hear a *year* read out by their own law, in a hole, over a vein, with the one witness nobody can subpoena standing *forty feet* above their heads." *(a beat)* "I've drawn nine realms, girl. This is the first court I've ever seen that's *nervous*." *(a beat)* "And the map's got **six** pins left. The twins still haven't written."
**Marginalia (tiny italics, and this is the last line of the cart's for the volume's first half — it must be small and it must hurt):** *"The cart notes nine hundred anvils under nine thousand people, eight chairs, one wall, and a boy asleep under the eighth. The cart would like the record to know that it has already drawn tomorrow, and that it has drawn it blank."*
**Morgatha (flat, from the dark at the frame's edge, and the number is **eleven**, and the reader — who now knows what the count counts — will feel the ground move):** "…Eleven."

### P11.2 — The last lamp (bottom strip)
**Art:** Three frames. **(1)** The lamp-rows along the sleeping ranks, every one burning (hers). **(2)** **Khazur**, alone at the low stone table, laying out the year's first page — one page only (the book is a book; a year is a book; he is *practising the first line* and his lips move and nothing comes out). **(3)** **Grimm**, sitting against the anvil with Askstone's cloth **beside** him now (Kaelen left it; the art team: the hammer is within reach of the dwarf it belongs to, still wrapped, on the night before the day — and nobody takes it out). **The anvil, the coal pot, and the dwarf, in one frame, at the bottom of the deepest gallery in the mountain, and nobody says anything at all.**
**Sprocket (flat, tiny, from the aisle, and this is the chapter's last joke before the silence):** "Nine thousand on the floor. Nine hundred anvils. Six hundred lamps. …One wrapped hammer. I'm going to need a *bigger* board and I'm not *sorry*."
**SFX:** *(coal, ticking in the pot. Under everything. The last sound in the chapter that is not a room holding its breath.)*

## Page 12 — *The Asking (the eve rite — dawn of the first day)*

### P12.1 — The question (2/3 page)
**Art:** **The Court Floor at dawn.** Every lamp lit; the racks dark to the gallery's end; nine thousand dwarves standing at their anvils (the art team: *standing* — the realm rises when the book opens, and that must be drawn as a *sound*, ranks and ranks of it, without one word of SFX); the eight chairs in their arc facing the raw wall; the two slates on the eighth chair's seat; two slates' worth of man standing at the wall with his back to the room; the anvil at the head of the middle aisle with a **coal pot burning at its foot**; a dwarf at its shoulder, and behind him a boy with a wrapped hammer, and behind him a street's worth of witnesses, and behind them, a team of nine who have never once been given a kingdom. **Duelist Molka**, at the low stone table, with the festival's book open, **and she opens her mouth — for the first time in Volume 4 — and asks the one question the book has ever permitted its keeper to ask.** The art team: her single balloon in the chapter, and the largest type on the page, and it must be lettered *plainly*, the way a person says a thing they have said forty times.
**Molka (the keeper of the book — the year's question, and Volume 4's only line from her until the forge-duel):** "**Who speaks for the mountain?**"
**Art (the page's held panel — the whole chapter, and the whole volume, in one silence):** the realm — nine thousand mouths — **starts to answer.** And the answer is old and it is theirs and it was made for exactly this hole in the year: for forty years, since the day the Cut opened and the Speakers went quiet, the realm has replied to that question with four words, and the words are not a lie, and they are not a comfort either: **"Stone waits."** The art team must show it *starting* — rank after rank drawing breath, a child lifted to a shoulder with her mouth already open, **Echo** in the front rank with his chin up and the word *Stone* visibly on his lips, **Khazri's** shoulders at the wall — and then the balloon **does not come**. No dialogue. No SFX. **Nine thousand dwarves with their mouths opening on a word they do not say.**
**Art note (the final frame):** the **coal pot** at the anvil's foot, in close — ticking, burning, *alive* — and above it, out of focus, the thing the reader has not been allowed to see properly since Ch. 13: **Grimm's hand is not on the wrapped hammer.** He has left it wrapped and he has left it *down*, and his fists are at his sides, and the last panel of the chapter is a dwarf standing in front of his grandfather's anvil with his mouth shut, in a room where nobody will say what they always say, on the morning of the day the realm reads its own year out loud.

### P12.2 — The end card (bottom third)
**Art:** Plain page — **carved**, the fourth stone card (the border interlace now a *series* with 14–16; the chisel-work should be at its finest, and the art team should add, unobtrusively, in the card's carved border, the *shape of the eighth chair* — the reader will find it or not). Carried beside the words in chalk, rubbed: **Wren's down-stroke, Fenn's circle-with-a-tail, the horizontal, Bark's cut ring, the unfinished braid — and, new, a sixth mark, drawn in *charcoal*, not chalk: a laughing man's face, three lines, signed K.** (the art team: the chaos of a *slate* on a *stone* card is the joke and the point). In the corner where the count lives, cut: **eleven**.
**Caption (the record — cut, not written; the twelfth card, and the fourth of the stone cards):** **Here read the named.**
**Small, bottom:** *Next: Chapter 18 — the reading. The book opens, the year comes out of it one entry at a time, nine thousand dwarves sit as jurors in their own mine — and the first objection in this realm's recorded history is raised by a miner with a scored braid who has never once been asked what he thought.*
**In the corner where the count lives:** *twelve.*

**END OF CHAPTER 17.**

*The eve did everything an eve can do. A mine shut its own books by a rule out of a four-hundred-year-old ledger and found out that a closed year is the most valuable thing it has ever had on the shelf. A cart of somebody's severed word went west, documented, toward a realm that files everything. Eight chairs came down a mountain — seven on the shoulders of miners who had signed the thing those chairs exist to judge, and the eighth on the shoulders of a man who has not spoken since the Cut opened and who set it half a step out of line. An anvil went home past fifty-eight doorways, and fifty-eight hands came out to meet it, including one hand out of a door with its family's mark chiselled off it. Nine thousand dwarves sat down on their own anvils and were taught their own law in an afternoon by a fifteen-year-old vampire with an exhibit and a grudge, and laughed, and the laughing was not a mistake, and the villain of the piece stood up in his own hole and told the truth about himself, and was not contradicted, because there is nothing in his ledger that is broken. Two slates were laid on an empty seat by a man who will not sit in it. A diviner spent her last two days of water on six hundred lamps and went to bed with a cracked flask. And at dawn the keeper of the book asked the question she is permitted to ask once a year, and this realm — which has answered its own silence every year for forty years with the same four words — opened its mouth and did not say that stone waits, because a realm with an anvil's worth of names in front of it had finally found one thing it could not pretend was patience.*
**The record's cards are being cut now, and for the first time the tally in the corner is not the only count in the book.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 17)

- **The tally runs fast, once** (P1.1): the frame-edge day count moves **seven → six → five → three** on the chapter's opening strip (the only page in Volume 4 where the marks fall *within* a page), reaches **one** in P11.1's edge, and the card's corner carries the count. The marks are cut *deeper* as they fall. **No character ever mentions the tally.**
- **The year closes by lamp, not by writ** (P2.1): Molka's rack-walk is a *silent* sequence — one gesture (shutter, turned), learned by the third repetition, repeated down a mile of racks, with the tags left exactly where they are. She is not confiscating, punishing, or angry: she is executing a line in a book. **Drek's two lines here are the volume's most dangerous dialogue** — he is *pleased*, because a closed year is an auction. Do not draw him as scheming; draw him as *satisfied*, which is worse. **Molka gets exactly one spoken line in Volume 4** (P12.1's question) and this page gives her none.
- **The horn that calls nobody** (P2.2) is the chapter's thesis in one device: twice a day for forty years the shift-horn has assembled a workforce; today it assembles a *court*, and *nobody moves*. Three frames. One SFX. Then a panel with no SFX at all, which the art team must keep a full rank of people in — the size of the room is what makes the silence loud.
- **The consignment is let go, deliberately** (P3.1): Viv's decision must be *readable as a decision*, not as a failure, and it must be possible for the reader to disagree with her. The carter is ordinary — a tired Seam human who does not know what is in the bales; the child's braid from Ch. 16 P5.2 is on the top bale with the tag legible; nobody stops him. Sprocket's new column stays **without a total**. Nereia's trough beat (P3.2) must be drawn so small that only a re-reader notices she is spending her own clock on the mine's thirst.
- **The chairs** (P4.1–P4.2): seven carried by **scored miners** — one of them the man who tied the knots (Norga) — and the eighth by **Khazri alone, at night, with no lamp and no escort**. Nobody helps him; nobody follows him; two of the team watch from above and get no face from him. The chairs are set in an arc facing the gallery's **raw wall**; the eighth is set **half a step out of line** and its owner then *stands at the wall* instead of sitting. **Khazri has no dialogue in this chapter, or any chapter of Volume 4 so far.** The slates on the seat (P8.1) are his only *act*: the laughing man face-up, the apprentice's stroke face-up, both toward the room.
- **The anvil's walk** (P5.1–P5.2) is the chapter's money sequence: the anvil **dressed** (mark cleaned, horn polished, base wrapped, **the coal pot lashed into the base-hollow to ride it down**), **Maren's cloth put in her pocket** (she keeps it; she does not hand it to anybody), and the fifty-eight doorways where **hands come out and touch the iron without stopping the walk** — including the doorway with the mark chiselled off, which must be drawn *in the middle of the procession*, briefly, and never remarked on by a single character. **Varda touches the anvil with the slate, not her hand**, and the slate's face must point at the anvil in that frame.
- **Kaelen carries Askstone** (P5.1, P11.2): the hammer stays **wrapped** — it has been wrapped since Ch. 14 and the reader should now understand that this is a *character state*, not a mystery: the dwarf who owns it is not ready to be seen asking. Kaelen offering to carry it is what two smiths say to each other. It is within Grimm's reach on the last night and he does not take it out. **The hammer opens in the arc's finale, not before.**
- **The Court Floor is a room and must be drawn as one** (P6.1): nine hundred anvils in graded ranks, nine thousand dwarves on their own family's furniture, aisles, the eight chairs, the low stone table with the **iron basket of ninety-one cut braids**, the racks dark at the far end, the exhibit at the mouth **in the mine's own iron frame on the mine's own bolts**. Thread ordinary life through it: bedrolls, kettles, children asleep across anvils, a washing line, soup. **The realm is not solemn; it is *moved in*.**
- **Viv's lesson** (P7.1) must be *enjoyable*: five numbered rules, an unnamed miner's cheerfully rude question, and a **full panel of nine thousand dwarves laughing in a mine**. This is the saga's formula being shown to the audience *as procedure* — the realm's own law is the weapon — and the laughter has to land as warm before it lands as terrible. **Drek's statement (P7.2) is entirely true and must not be undercut by a single drawing choice**: he has kept every promise; the mine is not on trial; **the oath form is**; and Viv concedes his whole argument to make her case. Her last line ("the mountain's the only witness we haven't called") is the volume's thesis and must be the quietest line on the page.
- **Roderick — one quiet panel** (P6.2): the mine's mouth, which has **no door** — a hole with a road through it — hand flat on the rock beside the opening, no dialogue. Fourth threshold in four chapters (river, mine-rail, road, doorless mouth); the rule is one panel and no more.
- **The diviner's lamps** (P8.2, P10.1–P10.2, P11.2): every water-lamp in the Cut is **hers** — she has been filling them since Ch. 15 and nobody asked her to; the chapter pays it as *reveal*, not as announcement. The flask's crack now runs the **whole way across the glass**; the cistern sits **below the lowest old depth-mark**; she counts as she fills and she tells Morgatha the truth flatly ("**two, maybe three**"), then changes the subject to the *realm's* reading. **The sea's first line is not sung in this chapter or this volume** — her last line explicitly reserves it to the realm that will have to *ask*.
- **Morgatha's count** (P10.1, P11.1): the corner numbers move **nine → ten → eleven**, and by now the reader knows what they count (Ch. 16 P10.2: two hundred and eleven things she meant to say to Tarkhan; she was at six). The chapter must never re-explain it. Her single act of kindness in the volume — holding a lamp while Nereia pours — takes four panels and **no dialogue about it**.
- **Grimm's ask** (P9.1) is the emotional spine and must be played *small*: two smiths on a floor, one lamp, a sleeping grummpling, a wrapped hammer on the wrong man's knees. Grimm asks Kaelen to **say Durin Khazdûrin aloud** if the mountain stays silent — and the *rules of the Name-Return must be respected in his refusal-and-acceptance*: a name is a witness, it is spoken *with* the memory the realm supplies, and it will sound like a **street**, not like a god. **Grimm's "Good. He was a street"** is the volume's best line and belongs to him. The chapter's last panel (P12.1) must *withhold* the payoff: the hammer still wrapped, the hands empty, the mouth shut.
- **The Asking** (P12.1): the keeper's question is the **only** thing Molka says in Volume 4 until her duel — "**Who speaks for the mountain?**" — and the realm's answer formula is **"Stone waits."** (the four words this realm has answered its own silence with since the Cut opened). **This chapter the formula is not spoken.** Show it *starting*: ranks drawing breath, a child's open mouth, **Echo** with *Stone* visibly on his lips — and then **no balloon, no SFX, one large panel of nine thousand people not saying a thing.** Do not give the withheld words to anybody; do not have a character name what just happened; **the realm's first objection (Ch. 18) is the consequence, and it must arrive in Ch. 18, not here.**
- **Rule 6 (Roderick)** — P6.2, one panel, no dialogue. **Rule 4 (chorus)** not used. **Rule 3 (costs drawn)**: the tally, the flask and its crack, the cistern marks, the coal pot, the scored braids, thirteen braids, the sealed lamp-rows going dark.
- **The cascade moves early** (P11.1): Fenn's litany closes at "the room holds at the *realm*" and the chapter's *actual* last word is a silence — the pattern break is deliberate and the lettering should not compensate for it by widening the final panel's type.
- **What Ch. 17 must not do** (reservation list, checked): no stone-song; no vein-light (`#e07a3f` does not appear — the only warm light in the chapter is **coal in a pot**, which is the point); no Ask; no Chord; **no line from Khazri, Bastion, Grudka, or Torvik** (Torvik is drawn twice, silent, at the rail); the golems stay lit windows only; **the geode is not found** (Pebble sleeps *inside* an anvil and his pocket stays empty); the sea's first line stays unsung (Nereia explicitly reserves it); Wren's line, Rootdeep, Thale's hand, Lyra's Crown-Touch, Bramble's tag, Pippin's Mosaic, Kaelen's 2nd/3rd pilot uses, Orvane's garden, the white — all untouched; **no new named characters** (the carter, the clerk, and the miner who asks his question stay unnamed; the dusty child stays unnamed and is *not* promoted — she is asleep under a chair, and that is all she is, this volume); and **the trial does not begin: the chapter ends on the eve's silence, and the reading is Ch. 18's.**
