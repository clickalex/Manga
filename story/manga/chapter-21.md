# AURELION · Volume 4 — Chapter 21: "The Anvil's Warm"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc D: "The Mountain-Ask" — THE VOLUME 4 CODA · four small things, one day: an old man's century of shifts ends and he wakes up anyway; a proud clan carries fire across the terraces into a forge it never owned and a woman ties a braid into a boy's beard; a grummpling's one idea is found in the crawl-space of the room it was always under, and a pocket that has been padded for a hole for a year finally fits; and a realm that has spent a month learning to sign its own name decides what to do with the one line in the year that nobody wrote.**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Grimm (**the forge, and the hand on the anvil**), Kaelen (silent — his volume ends at a doorway), Lyra, Morgatha (**the count — fifteen**), Nereia (**the cistern — one silent frame, no line**), Azrael, Viv (**the clause, and the pencil she refuses**), Bramble (**the coat — and the volume's last note**), Fenn (the map, the six pins), Marginalia (the cart — her last note in the mountain), Sprocket. **The realm** — **Forgemother Grudka Emberhand** (**03a's line, spent; the braid**), **Duelist Molka** (**the keeper — the realm's first election**), **Grand-Anvil Khazur**, **Echo** (reads the realm's new objection aloud), **Norga** (the knot, untied), **Ragna** (the realm's first objector, and its fourth line), **Maren Ashwood** (the hinge; the wall; the tenth frame), **Tilda Ashwood** (the wick), **Bjorn** (wordless — his century ends on this page), **Bjornka** (one line), **Cinder** (silent — her line was spent in Ch. 16), **Pebble** (the room), **Nib · Chuckle · Nibble · Boulder-Nib · Qrunk · Peb** (the clan), **Stonehand Drek** (one silent frame), **Roderick** (**his one quiet panel**), **Torvik · Urka · Pick · Skaal** (in the crowd), and **the dusty child** (never named, never explained, and now asleep under a coat).
> **Locations / threads:** the fifty-eight at night → the flame-forge's doorway → the cold room (the Khazdûrin forge, opened) → the terraces, and the fire carried down → the forge's first working day → the crawl-space under the floor → the rim house (the kitchen door) → the Court Floor (the election; the objections page) → the road west.
> **Arc beat:** The coda. Nothing here is a crisis; everything here is a *consequence*, and the volume pays its four withheld things in one movement. **A coal goes out:** the pot Bjorn fed for a hundred years is gone into the First Fire (Ch. 20), and the old man's shift — the shift his whole century was built around — simply ends, with no ceremony, because a realm with a public fire has no need of a man with a pot; he wakes at the feeding hour anyway, and walks up the terraces to stand in the crowd of dwarves who have come to *look* at the fire. **A braid goes in:** Grudka Emberhand arrives alone at the Khazdûrin forge's door with nothing in her hands, says the sentence 03a has been holding for her — *the mountain's a relative; so's a forge; so's a kid who can't hear his own grandfather's face* — and the flame-clan carries the First Fire down the terraces in a pot to light the cold one in the middle of the great-forge. Then **Norga** unties the mine's contract braid (the knot she tied careful, in Ch. 15, on a boy who was on the books) because the plates are void and the knot is no longer a word, and **Grudka ties the flame-clan's braid into Grimm's beard** — thirteen braids in, thirteen braids out, and the reader who has been counting all volume is given the one change in the count that matters. **The pocket fits:** the grummpling clan has been living in the Khazdûrin crawl-space since the frost left, digging toward the thing Pebble has been carrying a padded hole for since Ch. 16, and **Nib** breaks through into a geode that is *warm* — and grummpling law is a law of giving, so it goes into the coat with the seam, and Maren's towel comes out, and Pebble says the one thing he has ever said about it. **And the blank line:** the realm holds its first election (a knock, and the keeper's name goes in the book — **Molka**, whose palms are bound), and then the new keeper has to rule on the one line in the year that nobody wrote — the mine's own anvil, blank since Ch. 19 — and the realm's answer is the volume's whole law in one clause: **the line waits, and the waiting gets a year, and the realm keeps a blank on purpose.** The team leaves west the next morning (Morgatha's count reaching **fifteen**), Roderick gets one panel at the erased mark he has been standing at since Ch. 16 — and this time the street has light in it — and the volume's last page is wordless: a forge that has been cold a hundred years, nine hundred still-cold forges around it, a fire in the middle, and a small dusty person asleep under a grummpling's coat.
> **Motifs advanced:** the kept thing (**the pot on the shelf; the fire carried one last time — and now carried *in***), the braid (**the count paid: 13 → 13, the mine's knot out, the flame-clan's in**), the name (**a realm that signs itself decides to leave one line blank, on purpose, with a year beside it**), the door (**every forge in the realm is a door that has to be *taken* fire, one at a time, for months**), the geode (**warm; unexplained; in the pocket that was padded for it**), the count (**Morgatha: fifteen**), the pocket (**a hole kept nice, filled, and a hole kept, deliberately, in a book**), and the cart's last note. **Not in this chapter:** the stone-song's bloom (the mountain is warm, not lit — never restage `#e07a3f`); Bastion; the sea's first line; Kaelen's second pilot use; the tenth frame's face; the dusty child's name; the mine's machinery and the depth-log (Vol. 5's); Khazur's seal-or-fight; the geode's *why* (never explained, in this volume or any).

## Page 1 — *The First Night Off*

### P1.1 — The kitchen, at the hour (half page)
**Art:** The fifty-eight, deep night, and the art team's key direction for the page: **draw the absence of a chore.** Bjorn's kitchen — small, clean, one lamp, a chair, a table, and a **bare patch on the shelf** where a clay pot stood for a hundred years (the ring it left in the wood, drawn once, unremarked). **Bjorn**, dressed, awake, sitting at the table at the hour he has got up for since he was a young man: the feeding hour, when somebody in this house walked two rooms over and fed a neighbour's fire. He is not doing anything. His hands are on the table. In the second frame, hip height, unemphasised: he has reached, without looking, for a pot's handle that is not there — the same reach he made a hundred thousand times, drawn identically, and the art team must let the reader *see* it land on nothing. And across the table, **Bjornka**, fifteen, in a blanket over her nightclothes, awake at the same hour with the same nothing to do.
**Bjornka (quiet, matter-of-fact, a kid reporting a fact about her own body):** "…I woke up anyway."
**Bjorn (and the old man says nothing — the art team's job is that he *does* nothing: no line, no sigh; one panel of a dwarf sitting in his own kitchen at the hour, at a table, being *finished*)**
**Art note (third frame):** out of the kitchen window, up the terraces, **the flame-forge's doorway**: lit, and — this is the page's release — *busy at midnight*, a dozen dwarves standing in it in their coats with their hands in their pockets, and nobody going in or out. The art team: this has become a *place people go*.

### P1.2 — The doorway crowd (half page)
**Art:** The flame-forge doorway, late: the realm's new habit, drawn as pure ordinary dwarfishness — five dwarves and a grandmother standing in a doorway looking at a fire the way people look at the sea; a boy with a bucket he has clearly brought for no reason; two miners on their way to a shift, standing a minute longer than they should; a woman who has brought a *chair*. And **Bjorn** in the middle of them, with **Bjornka** at his elbow, not saying anything to anyone. The fire inside is **honest hearth-orange** (the art team: **no `#e07a3f`, no bloom** — this is fire, and the realm made it out of a pot), and the Forgemother's clan keeps it, and the panel must hold the volume's real point: *they all came because they can.*
**Dwarf at the front (a stranger, to nobody, comfortable, chewing):** "…Third night running. I've got a fire at home, you know."
**The grandmother (flat, not looking away from it):** "Aye. So've I."
**Art note (last frame, and it is the beat that closes the volume's pot):** **Bjorn's hands**, both, empty, hanging at his sides — and the tile above his head where the forge's old fire-hook hangs, where a *pot hook* has hung for a century and is now holding an ordinary lamp; and the art team must NOT have anyone notice, give him any dialogue, or point at it. He stands in the doorway for one more panel than the others and then goes home.

## Page 2 — *The Cold Room, Swept*

### P2.1 — A family at work (half page)
**Art:** The Khazdûrin forge, morning, and the Art Direction Rule for this location, inverted: **this room has been drawn dark and hoarfrosted since Ch. 14 — draw it *swept*.** The shutters open; the floor clean; the century's frost **gone**, not melted in a puddle but *simply not there* (the art team: no water, no drips — the stone is dry and the room smells of stone); the anvil's face **scrubbed bare of a hundred years of rust** and oiled; a **new wick** in the forge-lamp and **tallow** on the bench; the bellows re-leathered. **Grimm** at the anvil-block with a scraper; **Maren** with a broom and her sleeves rolled past the elbow; **Tilda** (the young aunt — the art team: Tilda is Maren's *sister*, and must be drawn as the much younger one, not as Grimm's sibling) up on the block polishing the lamp-glass with her cuff. And **Askstone** on the bench where a smith's hammer lives, unwrapped, oiled, ordinary — the volume's withheld object now simply a tool in a room.
**Maren (flat, sweeping, not looking up, and this is the whole mother in one line):** "Your grandfather would have had a fit about that floor. He kept it worse."
**Grimm (quiet, scraping, and he gets one word because it is all he has):** "Aye."
**Tilda (bright, defensible, holding up the lamp-glass):** "…It was black. It's *clear* now. That's *better*."

### P2.2 — The crawl-space (half page)
**Art:** The chapter's first comedy beat, played straight as always: **the grummpling clan is living in the Khazdûrin forge.** Since the frost went, the crawl-space under the floor and the old vent in the base of the chimney have become — by grummpling logic — *accommodation*: a lantern the size of a thumb, bedrolls made of gloves, a chimney-vent hung with drying mushrooms, six grummplings coming and going through a hole in the base of the wall that has clearly been *improved* with a chisel that belongs to Grimm. **Cinder** (clan-mother) sits on the anvil-block's edge with her arms folded, watching her people the way a farmer watches weather, **completely silent** — the art team's note: **Cinder's canon line was spent in Ch. 16 and she does not speak in this chapter at all**; her silence is pride, and the reader who remembers what she said about the best idea in the mountain should feel it land in the last page. And the one cold thing in the room, drawn plain: **the hearth** — grey, swept, empty, laid with kindling nobody has been able to light, because a family that has been cold for a hundred years has no fire of its own to light it with, and every dwarf in this room has walked past the flame-forge's open door for three days.
**Grimm (flat, to Cinder, about the wall, one panel, no ceremony — and it is the first time in this saga anyone has offered a grummpling a *lease*):** "You've put a door in my wall."
**Cinder (silent: one panel, arms folded, entirely unbothered, and the art team is to draw her looking, once, at the cold hearth, and then at the boy, and then back at the wall — and give her no dialogue whatsoever)**

## Page 3 — *"The Anvil's Warm"*

### P3.1 — Alone at the door (half page)
**Art:** Later that morning, and the chapter's held image before the turn: **Grudka Emberhand** at the Khazdûrin forge's door. Alone — no clan, no fire-keepers, no retinue, no writ, no gift in her hands (this is the tallest flame-dwarf in the realm on foot, in working clothes, and the art team must draw the *walk up* in the panel's background: a single set of prints in the grit of the terraces, and no others). She stands in the doorway and looks at the room the way a smith looks at a joint: the swept floor, the scrubbed anvil, the kindling in a cold hearth, the boy at the block, and the clan of grummplings in the wall behind him. **Grimm** stops scraping. Nobody in the room says anything for one panel.
**Grudka (and *this* is the beat the whole volume has been holding: 03a's line, spent, verbatim, unaltered — delivered gruffly, half-turned, as a job of work and not as a blessing):** "*The mountain's a relative. So's a* forge. *So's a* kid who can't hear his own grandfather's face. Come on, dwarf. The anvil's warm.*"

### P3.2 — What the boy hears (half page)
**Art:** Four frames, and the art team's only job here is *face*. **(1)** Grimm: the scraper still in his hand; he has been told something he has never once said out loud — that he cannot hear the mountain — by a woman who has never once said it kindly, and it is *not* an insult (play it as a dwarf being *seen*, and let the reader find the second meaning themselves: the stone-song went quiet for his family, and a forge is the one thing in the realm that has never needed the song). **(2)** Maren, still sweeping, watching Grudka, holding her judgement. **(3)** Tilda, delighted, with no idea. **(4)** Cinder — one wide frame, arms folded, on the block's edge, and *pleased*, and no line. Under the frames, small: the *kindling*, in the cold hearth, and the reader can already do the arithmetic of what the next page is.
**Grimm (quiet, after a beat, and it is the smallest and truest thing he has said in the volume — one sentence, and the art team must not underline it):** "…It's not warm yet."
**Grudka (flat, already turning to go and fetch her people, and this is the *whole* philosophy of the flame-clan in six words):** "It's the *anvil* that's warm, dwarf. Not the fire." *(a beat, from the doorway, without turning round)* "Fire's just how you find out."

## Page 4 — *Fire, Carried Down*

### P4.1 — The pot, one last time (half page)
**Art:** Midday on the terraces, and the volume's pot motif is paid its last turn — **the fire now goes the other way**: two of the flame-clan's fire-keepers walk down the terraces from the First Fire carrying a **clay pot** (an ordinary one; not Bjorn's — the art team: the shelf keeps *that* one, and it must be shown on the forge's shelf in the background of a later panel with its rag folded on top) with a coal in it, and the clan's youngest walking ahead to open doors. The street's reaction is the chapter's *warmth*: dwarves stopping, someone knocking on a window to call another dwarf out, a foreman pulling his shift off the rail to watch, a family standing in their doorway with their hands together — and the art team's key direction: **nobody bows, nobody cheers, and it does not occur to anyone to stand aside.** A clan that went a century with its own hearth cold, carrying fire into the one forge in the realm that went cold with it: it is a *transaction between two proud houses*, and the street's job is only to watch, and the reader's job is only to notice that the pot is going *into* a forge this time.
**Torvik (flat, from the crowd's edge, wrapped hand, to Urka — and the realm's new reader gets the coda's one piece of public business because he is standing there in a reader's coat):** "…That's the Khazdûrin's door."
**Urka (quiet, watching, and the boy's line is the realm's):** "Aye. They're *carrying* it to them, Da."

### P4.2 — The hearth takes (half page)
**Art:** Inside, in six frames, drawn as **work, not miracle** (the art team's absolute rule for this page: *the same as Ch. 20's First Fire — honest hearth-orange, no bloom, no colour from the stone, no rings, no glow on any person*): **(1)** Grudka on her knees at the hearth, doing it herself — no fire-keeper, no ceremony, the Forgemother of the flame-clan laying kindling in a Deep dwarf's hearth; **(2)** the tongs; the coal; **(3)** the breath — a small flame at the centre of an old hearth, exactly as small as Ch. 20's was in the First Fire, the art team keeping the two images identical in *size* so the reader feels the rhyme; **(4)** the kindling taking; **(5)** **the anvil's face**, close: the iron going from grey to warm — draw it the way a smith sees it, a sheen, not a shine; **(6)** the room, wide: the whole family and the clan around a hearth with a fire in it, and **Grimm**, the last panel of the page, with his hand *out* toward the anvil and not on it yet.
**SFX (small, on frame five, and the lettering team should set it as the sound of a room getting quieter):** *(iron, ticking.)*
**Grudka (flat, standing up, dusting her knees off, to nobody, and she never once looks at Grimm while she says it):** "…There. Now it's a *forge*."
**Art note (final frame of the page):** **Cinder**, on the block's edge, and for one frame the clan-mother is standing up — the art team: the grummplings' whole attitude in a single panel, and no line.

## Page 5 — *The Knot and the Braid*

### P5.1 — Norga unties it (half page)
**Art:** The braider at the forge door, in her apron, with her kit, because **a braider in this realm is what a clerk is in another one**, and this is a piece of *business*: **Norga** (who tied Grimm's contract braid in Ch. 15 — *own hand, own hair, mine's knot* — and said, kindly, *it's a tight one, I did it careful*) has been called up the terraces to do a job she has never done in her life. Four frames, all close, all hands: **(1)** the **mine's knot** in Grimm's beard, drawn exactly as it was drawn in Ch. 15, the art team going back to that page's angle (the knot is *still there*, and the reader who has been counting braids all volume should feel it like a stone in a boot); **(2)** Norga's fingers on it, and the *check* she does first — a workman testing a joint before she opens it; **(3)** the knot **coming apart** — and this must be drawn as *untying*, carefully, strand by strand, not as a cut: **nobody cuts a braid in this chapter**, and the realm's horror (Ch. 14–18's whole cost grammar) must be present as a *negative*; **(4)** the loose strand still in his beard, held apart, and Norga's palm under it, and the mine's knot, whole, in her hand — **not thrown away, not burned, not surrendered**: put in her kit, with the tags and the twine, where a braider keeps things that are *finished*.
**Viv (flat, immediately, from the doorway, on the record, because the lawyer is leaving tomorrow and she has one clause left in her):** "— Log it. **The mine's knot comes out on the signer's say-so.** Say the words, or it goes back in."
**Grimm (flat, and this is the *third* time in this volume a dwarf has had to say a thing out loud in front of witnesses, and the art team should let the reader remember the other two):** "…It's not a *word* any more. Take it out."
**Norga (quiet, tucking it away, and the realm's whole legal history is in her hand):** "It was a good knot. …I'll keep it with the others."

### P5.2 — The flame-clan braid (half page)
**Art:** The adoption, and the art team's direction: **it must look like a woman tying a braid, because that is what it is.** Grudka — who has stood in this room for two pages with her arms folded — takes the chair, sits down like somebody about to do a job she has done a thousand times, and ties a **flame-clan braid** into Grimm's beard, in the flame-clan's knot, with the clan's own cord off her own beard (the art team: she takes one of *her* strands and cuts it with her own knife, in one frame, without a word about it, and nobody in the room comments). Five frames: the chair; her hands up; the strand; **the knot, tied high and tight beside the nine family braids**; and then — the volume's title, at last, as a panel: **Grimm's beard, drawn from the same three-quarter angle used on every page since Ch. 14, so the reader can count: nine tight, three loose, and the new one, tied in another house's cord, *tight*, and the mine's knot gone.** Thirteen braids. **Maren** in the doorway, with the broom, watching — one panel, and *no line*, because Maren has spent this volume drawing the dead and does not need to say anything about this.
**Grudka (gruff, standing up, done, dusting her hands — the adoption ends the way the whole volume has ended everything, with a dwarf finishing a job):** "That's four houses in that beard now, boy. …You'll be *feeding* us at the festival."
**Grimm (quiet, one hand up to the new braid, and the two words are the only ones he has):** "…Aye. I will."

## Page 6 — *The First Work*

### P6.1 — A hinge for the kitchen (half page)
**Art:** The forge's first working day, and the chapter's comedy, played absolutely straight: **Grimm's first work at his father's forge in a hundred years is a hinge for his mother's kitchen door** (which has been wedged shut with a folded slate since Ch. 14, and *every* reader of this volume has seen that slate). Four frames: the iron in the fire; the drawing-out; the **eye of the hinge** punched with a drift, clean; and the fitting — the door going on, and being opened and shut, and opened and shut again, three times, by **Maren**, who says nothing at all during any of it and inspects the swing the way she inspects her own drawings. Around it, the room working: Tilda holding the drift, Cinder's clan watching from a shelf like a row of judges, **Askstone** in use for the first time since the mountain answered — the hammer *asking* a piece of iron a question, and getting an answer, which is all it was ever for.
**Maren (flat, after the third swing, with the door standing open, and this is the kindest sentence she has ever said in her life and she has clearly been saving it):** "…I'll not be needing the wedge any more."
**Tilda (delighted, from the shelf, to the clan):** "That's *high* praise."

### P6.2 — The knock (half page)
**Art:** The afternoon, and the world arrives: **a knock at the forge door** — and the art team's key panel of the page: the doorway, from *inside*, with a **stranger** standing in it. Not a customer, and not a client: a neighbour, with a hand out and a **broken thing** in it — and the art team should give the reader a *badly* broken thing, ordinary and unfixable, something with no value: a **pot hook**, snapped at the eye, the sort of thing a dwarf would make himself rather than buy. The dwarf is embarrassed (drawn: a man who has walked past a cold forge for a hundred years and got his courage up); a child behind him; a wife in the street looking away; and behind them the street, where two more dwarves have *stopped walking*, and one has taken his hat off, and one is coming up the steps. **Grimm** at the door, in the doorway of his father's forge, with the door standing open and the fire at his back — and this must be drawn as the panel the volume has been walking toward since Ch. 14: **a Khazdûrin, at work, in daylight, being asked for something small.**
**Neighbour (embarrassed, holding the hook out, and this is one of the great lines of the volume for how little it asks):** "…I know it's not much. I know. I'd not ask a *Khazdûrin*."
**Grimm (flat, taking it out of his hand — and this is the answer, and the art team must letter it exactly as plain as a dwarf says a plain thing):** "**Bring the other one.** …We're open."
**Art note (last frame, small, and it is the volume's own joke finally told: the cart, at the end of the street, with Marginalia's note under it):** *"The cart notes the first customer of a Khazdûrin forge in one hundred years, and that the man brought a *broken pot hook*, and that the dwarf in the doorway said *we're open*, and that the cart has been on nine realms' roads and has never seen a sentence do more work."*

## Page 7 — *The Warm Anvil*

### P7.1 — The hand (half page)
**Art:** Evening, the forge banked, the family gone up to the house, and the chapter's held image — **the one silent beat, and this chapter gets the volume's last one**: **Grimm** alone with the anvil. The panel is one frame, large, and the art team's direction is uncompromising: **no glow, no colour, no stone-song, no spark** — he simply puts his **palm flat on the anvil's face**, the way his grandfather did and the way his father did, and the anvil is **warm** from a day's work, and the boy who cannot hear his own family's gift stands there with his hand on iron like a man checking a heartbeat. **Askstone** on the bench beside him. The fire banked low. And the reader is given the whole volume in one object: the mountain answered a realm, and a forge answered a boy, and nobody in the story will ever say either of those things out loud.
**Art note:** no balloon, no caption, no SFX. If the lettering team is tempted to put anything on this panel, the answer is no.
**Maren (off-panel, from the house, calling down the street, ordinary as weather, and the line must be lettered small and half-heard):** "— *Grimm! Your supper's going cold!*"
**Grimm (up, hand off the anvil, unhurried, and the answer carries up the street in the dark and the reader should get to hear it in a warm room):** "…**Coming.**"

### P7.2 — Roderick's panel (half page)
**Art:** **Roderick — exactly one panel, no dialogue, and this is his sixth threshold** (a river, a mine-rail, a road, a doorway, a stone table, and now *this*): the middle of the fifty-eight, at dusk, and the door with the mark **chiselled off** (Ch. 16's panel, the same angle, the same stone; the erased nine-strand mark's ghost still legible) — and this time the mark is **dry**, and the rain is not falling, and **the light from the relit forge at the end of the street is on it.** The Reforgers' captain stands with his hand flat on somebody else's erased name while the realm's first fire in a century reaches the stones of a street he has orders about. **No explanation of who chiselled the mark off, in this chapter or ever** — the art team carries it in the *light*, and the last frame of the page holds the erased mark with the warm light on it and nothing else in the shot at all.
**Art note (page's last two frames, small, unemphasised, and neither is to be captioned):** **(a)** **Drek** — at the Cut's rail above the Deep Cut with the day's book under his arm, in a mine that is still working and still his, looking down. No line, and no reader is to be told what to do with it. **(b)** **Nereia** — at the cistern, at the table's end of a good day: her hands **empty** (the flask has been gone since Ch. 19), the old depth-marks on the wall above the water, the level **a finger and a half lower** than Ch. 19's, and the diviner doing the only arithmetic in this chapter that nobody has been told about. **She says nothing in this chapter at all** — her clock is Volume 6's — and the art team should give this frame the same size as every other incidental frame on the page.

## Page 8 — *The Geode*

### P8.1 — The crawl (half page)
**Art:** The Khazdûrin crawl-space under the forge floor, lit by one thumbnail lantern, and the art team's key direction: **this is a working site, not a treasure room.** The grummpling clan has been digging since the frost left — a proper little shaft under the forge's floor, shored with boot-heels and a spoon handle, with a windlass made of a spool and thread, and a **ladder** that is clearly a ladder (a respectable one; grummplings are *competent*, and this is the volume's last piece of design comedy: the equipment is tiny and the *engineering* is real). **Nib** at the face with a chisel made from a nail; **Chuckle** on the windlass; **Nibble** at the ladder foot with a bucket; **Boulder-Nib** and **Qrunk** and **Peb** behind, in a queue, because grummplings queue. And the wall they are working: **stone with the frost gone out of it** — and the art team should draw, in the rock's face, the thing the clan has been following for a year: a **seam**, ordinary, mineral, unlit.
**Bramble (from above, lying flat on the floor over the hole, head down it, thoroughly at home, one line and it is the reader's):** "…You're under the *forge*. You know that, right? You've dug a *cellar* under the *Khazdûrin*—"
**Nib (muffled, from the face, entirely unbothered, and this is the volume's last joke before the payoff):** "*MOVE!*"

### P8.2 — Warm (half page)

**END OF VOLUME 4.**

*The Mountain-Ask closes the way it opened: with a cold forge and somebody deciding to do something about it. It opened on twelve braids and a hammer in cloth and a family that had gone quiet a century ago, and it closes on thirteen braids, an open door, and nine hundred anvils that answered. In between, a realm found out that its law had a hole in it the size of one clause, and that the clause had been cutting braids for four hundred years; that its mountain had been waiting to be asked in the right grammar; that a mortal's pencil, a boy's copied stroke, a champion's hands and nine hundred lines of the realm's own bad handwriting could amount to a *record*; and, in the coda, that a realm can lose its fire, keep it in a pot for a hundred years, give it away twice, and end up with a forge, an election, a blank line, and a small dusty person asleep under a coat. The costs stayed costs: the coal that carried a century is on a shelf with nails in it; the cistern is a finger lower and will be lower next year; the mine is still a mine and its machines are still turning and somebody has been logging the depth; a champion's hands are wrapped and will be for a while; and the realm's new law has one line in it that nobody has been able to write. **That is what an answer costs when it is a real one** — and the volume leaves it standing: a fire in the middle of a dark hall, and nine hundred forges still cold, and a queue of dwarves on the terraces with pots, patient, in no hurry, because the mountain is warm now and there is fire at the top of the hill. Volume 4 ends with a boy asleep against an anvil he has been hammering for three weeks, in a room his family could not enter a month ago, and — on a wall at the rim, in a house where a mortal woman has been drawing a dead man's face for thirty years — one empty frame, kept, waiting, and *hung where people can see the space*.*

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 21)

- **This is a coda and it must not feel like an episode** (all pages). There is no antagonist in this chapter, no crisis, no reveal, and no reversal: every event on these twelve pages is either **a consequence** of Volume 4 or **a gift**. The art team's rule of thumb: *draw work.* Almost every panel is somebody doing an ordinary job well — sweeping, tying, digging, sewing, reading out a rule, lighting a fire. The volume's heat belongs to the *ordinary*, and the coda is where the saga says so out loud by having nobody say it.
- **No bloom, no song, no `#e07a3f` on any panel** (all pages). Ch. 19 spent the stone's colour; Ch. 20 established that the mountain is **warm, not lit**; Ch. 21's fire is **hearth-orange, lamp-yellow, and ash-grey**, and the only warmth in the chapter that is not fire must be drawn as *touch* — a hand on iron, a hand opening over a geode, a grummpling asleep under a coat. **If any page of this chapter could be mistaken for the Naming's bloom, redraw it.**
- **Grudka's line is 03a's, verbatim, and it is spent here** (P3.1): *"The mountain's a relative. So's a forge. So's a kid who can't hear his own grandfather's face. Come on, dwarf. The anvil's warm."* Delivered **gruffly, half-turned, as a job of work**; this is the volume's emotional peak and it must look like a woman telling a boy something obvious. Her reply to him (*"It's the anvil that's warm, dwarf. Not the fire… fire's just how you find out."*) is the chapter's thesis and is best lettered small. **She gets four lines in this chapter and no more**, and the **adoption is a *braid*, not a speech** (P5.2): flame-clan knot, clan cord cut from her own beard with her own knife, tied **tight**, beside the nine family braids — and the reader must be able to **count thirteen** in a beard that has been countable since Ch. 14.

Ch. 15's contract braid (the mine's knot, in the signer's own hair) is **untied by Norga, strand by strand — nobody cuts a braid in this chapter, ever**, and the knot is *kept in her kit*, not burned: the realm's whole cost-grammar of the last eight chapters must be present as a *negative*.
