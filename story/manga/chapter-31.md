# AURELION · Volume 6 — Chapter 31: "Nine Posts"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc F: "The First Song" — the report goes home, and the realm discovers it has acquired a colleague.** Nine days back up the road from the sea-gate, the Nine carry three things nobody in the Barrens has ever carried: a *foreign office* (the sea's **keeper-Above**, entered in the reef-roll by a duet), a **year the sea could not bind**, and a **reading that got answered in water**. The chapter is the realm's digestion of all three, told the way this saga tells everything institutional — in **waybills, entries, margins and a deputy's desk** — and its four movements are exactly the four things the realm does with a thing it did not invent: it **enters** it, it **staffs** it, it **sends it to the furthest door**, and it **reads it aloud at every circle until somebody says otherwise.** Concretely: the post at the edge opens its **first door** — the barn lease of the fourth man in the queue, read at last on the tenth day, not refused, and entered with the words *read; not refused* — because a post that may refuse a deed is a post that can also *open* one, which is the half of the Collar nobody in the realm has said out loud yet. The deputy's limit is drawn as a rule in the register: **a deputy may hold a door; a deputy may not be a reader**, so the office stays a collar and not a person. The **reading-road** opens nine posts in one week and the realm's newest doctrine is tested on the realm's own: **Ulrik the Unbroken is refused at his own post** — his supply warrant carries the wrong date, the reader says so, and the war-chief queues, waits, and files a complaint *praising the reader*, which is the single funniest thing this culture has ever done and also the whole point of Volume 6. The **school** sets its second term's assignment (*find a door that says yes, read it anyway, and write down what it costs to be welcomed*) and its first four refusals go up on the wall in a frame. **Kha** is nine days out with a collar in a case and an escort and writes one line home that ends the argument about whether eleven is old enough: *I'm not sitting still. I'm being accurate.* And the realm takes the sea's office into the horn — **the first office it did not invent** — which is where the volume's real subject arrives: the Barrens has spent five volumes making everyone else accept *its* procedure, and this is the first time it has to accept somebody else's, and it does it by the book, and Morgatha's count **stops** when she understands what the reef's missing year means. Then the two coda images: the deep, where a merfolk who has not answered in a year finally answers with one note, and a map, where a 312-year-old cartographer draws nine rings on a road, one wet ring at the bottom of the world, and — at the very edge of the sheet, in a place he has never charted — **a blank with a line round it**, because the sea's reply has to go somewhere.
> **Cast:** **The road party** (nine; **Morgatha** — *the count's first stop of the volume, and the debt she reads aloud*, **Viv** — *the advocate who learns the other half of her own doctrine on an empty beach*, **Kharza** — *a letter home and a reply that ends an argument*, **Kaelen** — *the smith who trues a flag-pole*, **Fenn** — *nine rings, one wet, one blank*, **Sprocket** — *the fifth entry*, **Azrael** — *one line about answering*, **Bramble** — *a door that said yes*, **Nereia** — *the second office, accepted*) + **Marginalia** (the cart, which has an opinion about sand) · **Captain Roderick Vane** (**his one silent panel** — the flag-pole trued with a plumb line, no dialogue, nobody watching) · **the gate-clerk** and the **deputy** (the camp's first post-clerk, drawn, unnamed, with a rule and a register) · **the fourth man in the queue** (**the barn lease**, ten days, read at last; drawn, unnamed, delighted) · **the troll surgeon** (who takes a sample of the well and shrugs) · **the founder** (**out at the mark**: four frames, no face, a coat on a nail) · **the founder's daughter** (one frame, a box, a doorway) · **Ulrik the Unbroken** (**refused at his own post, and glad**) · **the school in the well** (**Taurus's margin**, four framed refusals, the second term's assignment) · **Kha** (**drawn for the first time — nine days out, twelve years old by dawn, collar in a case**) · **Whalesong** (**the notch cut at last, and one note sung back**).
> **Locations / threads:** the four gullies on the coast road → the post at the edge → the stockade and the well → the horn's copy in the west (a reader's desk) → nine crossings in one week → the school in the well → the mark, and the camp's books → the flats → the gate → the Deep-Listening post → Fenn's map.
> **Arc beat:** Ch. 31 must do the thing Volume 6 promised in Ch. 30 and cannot do at sea: **show the realm changing its own law to make room for somebody else's.** Four movements, one stop, one hook, no fight, no villain: **(1) the entry** — the council enters the sea's office (the *keeper-Above*) into the horn, the first foreign office the Barrens has ever carried, and Morgatha's count **stops** when the report's worst line is read (*the reef did not grow a ring for a year; the realm could not bind*) — because her realm spent a year unable to bind a vote, and the realm's answer to a year of unboundness is the same sentence in both languages: **an unbound year is a debt, and debts get read aloud at every circle until they are settled.** **(2) the staff** — the post at the edge opens its first door (the barn lease; *read; not refused*) under a deputy whose office is limited by a line in the register (*a deputy may hold a door; a deputy may not be a reader*), and the realm's newest doctrine is tested from the other side for the first time: refusal is not the whole office, and the realm has never once said so out loud. **(3) the road** — nine posts in a week, nine readers, nine clerks, arms stacked, and the realm's own war-chief refused at his own post for a wrong date, which he takes as a *compliment* and files as praise: the doctrine is real when it bites the hand that wrote it. **(4) the other half** — the chapter's private arc, Viv's: she stands on the flats and reads a law aloud where there is no door, no listener and no clerk, and gets the second half of her own doctrine from the exercise — *a reading is a request to be heard; a door is only the place where somebody is* — and writes it in the margin of the horn's copy in her own hand, which is where Volume 5 began. Then the two closers: **Whalesong answers** (one note, back down the tube, the first thing the post has sung in a year — unexplained, unexplained, unexplained) and **Fenn leaves a blank at the edge of the charted world**, because a reply has to go somewhere and the saga has one way to draw a place it is not allowed to show yet.
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**a foreign office entered; *read; not refused*; four refusals framed; the fifth entry**), the door (**a deputy's limit; Ulrik refused; the other half of the doctrine**), the reading (**out loud, where nobody is**), the count (**stops once, at a year that could not bind**), the carrying (**a debt read aloud until it is settled**).
> **Not in this chapter:** **Titanvein** (unbarred since Volume 6 began, and still deliberately unstaged) · the stone-song (`#e07a3f` zero) · **the sea's reply explained** (no; it gets *louder*, not clearer, and nothing in this chapter may say what it is) · **the Leviathan** (*never drawn, never voiced*) · **Aurelia Vox's duet-verdict**, **Elune**, **Coraline**, **the tide-guard's first bar** (all held) · **the fourth cell** (open, unmentioned) · **Irongate Vane** (unmentioned) · **the twins' letter** (Fenn's column stays blank) · **Wren's 03a line** · the Scar-Reading Chord · the white · the *empty* inside the mountain · **Kaelen's pilot uses** · Orvane's garden · Khazur's seal-or-fight · the **Re-Oath vote** · and **any new named character** (rule 7: the deputy, the queue, the school's students, the two merfolk fishers and the escort are drawn and never named).

## Page 1 — *The Four Gullies*

### P1.1 — What the sea left on the road (half page)
**Art:** The coast road, going back up; four gullies in nine days; and the art team's rule for the whole page: **the sea's reply arrives as arithmetic.** Marra walks the party down to each gully mouth at low water and reads the reef-roll aloud — the sea's copy, a strip of horn notched nine times, and beside it the paper the realm made of it — because the motion (Ch. 30 P8.2) said the sea's reply to any reading so made shall be *sung by the full chorus*, and Pearl entered a **duty** while she was at it: **the roll is read at water on the way home, so the sea hears its own copy inland.** Four gullies, four readings, four frames: **(1)** the first: gulls, mud, nothing — the party standing at a trickle; **(2)** the second: a fisher's boat drawn up, the fisher listening from ten paces and not coming closer; **(3)** the third: nothing again, and Bramble's verdict; **(4)** the fourth — and the art team must draw it *flat and small* and let the reader do the work: the gully's water is nine inches deep and moving, and while the notched roll is read, the level at the sand **rises one finger**, holds, and sits back down. Marra writes it in a tally-book without a word, and the page's last detail is the tally: three gullies, four readings, **one** finger.
**Marra (flat, tally-book open, and the selkie's register is *schedules and notes*, which is exactly why the beat lands):** "\u2014 Third gully: nothing. Fourth gully: the water came up a *finger* while you read the roll and went back down without a wind behind it. — I'm not saying anything clever about it. I've been writing *weather* for eleven years and this goes in the same book, on the same line, and if the *sea* wants to explain it, the sea can *write*." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her looking at the notch-strip)* "…Nine notches and one finger. Fenn. Put it on the map. Put it on the map *small*, or I'll never hear the end of it."

### P1.2 — The count, on the road (half page)
**Art:** And the volume's clock resumes properly for the first time since the Barrens — the art team's rule: **the corner count runs while the party walks**, in the frame's corner, small, from **one** upward, and the reader learns what it is now: not a war-song count (no war), not a rage count (nothing to hold in), but the count a person does on a long road because **her father taught it to her and the song is the only thing she has that means *home*.** Four frames: **(1)** the party in file on the cliff path, the cart behind, the sea still in the frame as a band of light; **(2)** Morgatha at the front, walking, lips moving — **one… two… three** in the corner; **(3)** Kharza beside her, rolling her sleeve down over the clause because the sun is going and the wind is coming up, and the two of them arguing *procedure* while they walk — the art team must draw this as affection, because it is; **(4)** the page's last frame: the camp's watchtower on the horizon, at dusk, with its **bell** drawn tiny — and the reader remembers, without being told, that this camp rings a bell and that the last time they were here an old sage was walking east.
**Kharza (flat, mid-argument, to her captain, and the exchange is the chapter's warm-up and the reader's own question):** "\u2014 So the sea writes *offices* now. We put a collar on a chair at the edge because it was ours to put; they put a **keeper-Above** in a horn-roll in a cave under the world and it says *she may be answered from the surface*, and when we get home and I read that aloud, my realm is going to ask me *whose office is it* — and I don't have an answer, because I'm nineteen years old and I've been to one parliament." *(a beat)* "…You're counting."
**Morgatha (flat, still walking, and the corner count ticks):** "\u2026Four. — I count on roads. My father counted on roads. He taught me the song on a road *exactly* like this one and I've never told anybody that, so you can have it for free." *(a beat)* "…And we're going to have *exactly* the conversation you're dreading, in a room, with the horn, and you're going to be *right* in it, so stop pre-worrying. Pre-worrying's a *slave* habit. That's an order."

## Page 2 — *The Post Opens a Door*

### P2.1 — The tenth day (half page)
**Art:** The post at the edge, morning, day ten — and the art team's staging rule for the page: **this must be drawn as an ordinary working morning happening in a place that is now an institution.** The queue is *shorter* than it was and better organised; the flag-pole is still bare; the **deed-register** on the desk is open and *written in*; the **deputy** — a camp clerk in a plain coat with the realm's plate-type ledger hand, drawn, unnamed, and never once given a name because the office is the point — sits behind the desk with the **collar** hanging on a nail *behind* her, not on her, because **the collar is the reader's and the reader is nine days out.** And the fourth man in the queue — the barn lease, ten days, a neighbour's signature, a story he has told twice — stands in front of the desk while his deed is read *aloud*, all of it, the whole stiff legal sentence of it, by a deputy who may hold a door and may not open one. Four frames: **(1)** the queue, in order, for once — because the post has begun issuing *numbers*, and the numbers are a slab of the same leather as the collar, and the man with the barn lease has number one; **(2)** the deed read aloud, the man's hands, and the art team must draw **the whole camp listening** the way they listened to Kharza in Ch. 28: clerks in the windows, the ward door open, the well in the background; **(3)** the register, written in the realm's plate-type: **ten days · held · read · not refused · opened**; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the fourth man walking away with his lease and his *barn*, and stopping twenty paces off to look back at the post like it is a *person*.
**The deputy (flat, reading aloud, and the art team must draw her mouth moving on every word because the realm's whole reform is *the reading is out loud*):** "\u2014 ***…the said barn, with the lean-to and the water-right, to hold for the term of four harvests, the neighbour's mark witnessed…*** — that's the last of it. It's read. It's not refused." *(a beat, and she turns the register around and makes him look at his own entry)* "…**'Read; not refused.'** That's the whole of it. Ten days ago this post couldn't open a door because a post that can close a door is a door, and I'm not the reader, so I could *hold* your paper and not *open* it. Today we read it out and it stands — which means the realm's got a **new thing** in it as of this morning and I'd like you to walk out of here knowing you're the first." *(a beat, and the art team must draw her stamping nothing at all, because the realm's mark is the horn and the horn is west)* "…Off you go. Number two."

### P2.2 — A deputy's limit, and the rule for the office (half page)
**Art:** And the realm's *first formal rule about a thing nobody expected*, staged as the chapter's paperwork: the horn's copy arrives from the west with a fresh entry — because the post has been reporting, in the realm's own style, and the council has been answering, in the realm's own style — and the entry is this page's centre. Four frames: **(1)** the courier, the case, the plate-type duplicate pinned beside the cut hand; **(2)** the entry, legible on the page: ***the post at the edge: the deputy's office is entered: a deputy may hold a deed, may read it aloud, and may not sit as reader; where the reader's chair is empty the post reads, and does not open, until the chair is filled.***; **(3)** the **collar on its nail** behind the desk, drawn close, with its two stitchings; **(4)** and the page's last frame: the deputy, alone at closing, taking the collar down, looking at the inside of it for a while — the smaller lettering against the leather — and **hanging it back up**, and the art team must give her no balloon at all, because the reader has just watched a person decide, for the second time in the chapter, to be an office and not a person.
**Viv (flat, reading the entry aloud to the post at the day's end, and the lawyer's register is *approval withheld*, which is how the reader knows the rule is good):** "\u2014 …That's the right rule and I hate it. Ask me why: because a *deputy* who can't be a reader is a rule about **not being two people at once**, and there isn't a realm on this road that writes those — we've just written one, at the edge of the world, in a stockade, because a nineteen-year-old's chair is nine days out and a village's barn is ten days in." *(a beat)* "…Write this under it, in the same hand, and I'll sign it: ***no office of the realm is filled by whoever is nearest.***" *(a beat, and the art team must draw her closing the register)* "…That's five clauses now, Kharza. I keep *adding*. Do you know what my profession calls an advocate who keeps adding clauses? — *Drafting.* It's the highest compliment there is and it means you're not finished."

## Page 3 — *The Report*

### P3.1 — Six lines, drafted on the road (half page)
**Art:** The report the realm asked for — and the art team's rule: **it must be short enough to read in a frame and specific enough that the reader can check it against the chapters they just read.** Viv drafts it on the cart's tail across two evenings, and the page shows the draft growing: the sea's law (a song, and a door); the chorus; the judge who votes by duet; the scribe's rubric; the first line; the reef's missing year; the motion; the duet-office; the notches; the sound at the Deep-Listening post and **what the listener said about it** — which is the page's problem, because the listener's sentence is the one sentence in the report nobody can file. Four frames: **(1)** the draft on the cart, ink wet, with Fenn's map pinned beside it and Sprocket's ledger under it, because this party now travels with three records of the same journey and that is the chapter's quietest joke; **(2)** Viv striking a line out (the reader should try to read what she struck: *the sea is a realm where law is music* — struck, with a note in the margin: *too easy; say what it DOES*); **(3)** the final six lines, legible; **(4)** Sprocket copying it flat into his ledger for the fourth column, and the art team must draw his hand steady, because the record-keeper has been waiting since Chapter 26 for a report that is not about a war.
**Viv (flat, drafting, to nobody, and this is the chapter's neatest piece of doctrine):** "\u2014 Six lines. Watch: **one** — the sea's law is sung, and a thing sung true cannot be unsung, and a document is a *song nobody is singing*. **Two** — their parliament is a chorus of three voices, and the session ends when the tide says so, four times a day. **Three** — their Thalarch has no voice; her part is carried; she votes by handing her keeper her hand. **Four** — their archive is a reef, one ring a year, and **they did not grow a ring last year**, because a realm that cannot bind a verdict cannot bank a year. **Five** — their motion: the first line stays in the water, a keeper is entered *above*, the sea answers any reading sung from the surface. **Six** — and this is the one I can't draft: *at the listening post, three hundred and sixty-two days in, the deep answered. The keeper says it was the first line, sung back, in a key we don't have.*" *(a beat)* "…I've written *we don't have* in an official report to a realm that keeps everything in a book, and I want it on the record that I *hate* it."

### P3.2 — Read aloud at the post (half page)
**Art:** And the report is **read**, which is the only way anything enters this realm — at the post's door, at dusk, with the lamp lit, the queue gone home, the camp gathered the way it gathers (Ch. 28's grammar: clerks with ledgers, samplers with boot-caps in their hands, the troll surgeon with his sleeves wet, the gate-clerk with the gate-book, the deputy with the register, and **Roderick** at the back with his coat done up), and the art team must repeat, *exactly*, the composition of Ch. 28's yard reading with one difference: **this time the reading is about the world outside and the camp is listening to a place it will never go.** Four frames: **(1)** Kharza reading the report aloud (her register: flat, clear, no flourish); **(2)** the camp's faces on the *fourth* line — the missing year; **(3)** and then the page's wrong beat, and the art team must stage it as *domestic*: at the well, behind the crowd, the rope goes slack — somebody drawing water finds the bucket **higher than it was**; the troll surgeon goes over, looks down the well, comes back with a sample in a cup, holds it to the lamp, and **shrugs**; **(4)** the gate-clerk, in the same panel, writing in the gate-book, and the art team must make the line legible: *well: risen one finger during reading, no rain, no fault. — noted.*
**The troll surgeon (flat, cup in hand, to the whole yard, and the mender's line is the best joke in the chapter and it must be delivered without any humour at all):** "\u2014 Water's clean. Water's *up*. There's no word in my trade for water going up on its own and I've been a mender thirty-one years, so I'm entering it as **weather** and going back to my patients. …**Next person who says the word 'sign' at me tonight does their own stitches.**"
**Bramble (quiet, at the edge of the crowd, to Kaelen, and the barometer must be drawn *low* for the first time in the volume — no SFX, no joke):** "\u2026It went up while she *read*. — Nobody else is going to say it out loud, so I will, and then I'll shut up: **the sea's copy was in the cart, and the roll's read at every water, and the water's *here*.**" *(a beat)* "…I've been nine faces and I know a *message* when I'm standing in front of one. I just don't know who it's *for*."

## Page 4 — *The Year That Could Not Bind*

### P4.1 — THE STOP (FULL PAGE — one panel, no dialogue)
**Art:** The volume's first stop, and it is **the quietest panel in the saga**, and the art team's instructions are absolute. The setting is the post, at night, after the reading: the lamp low; the report on the desk with the sixth line still showing (*…in a key we don't have*); the deed-register open beside it; and **Morgatha**, sitting on the post's step with her back to the desk, **forearm bare**, the clause and the cut road on it, looking at nothing at all — not at the grey, not at the well, not at anyone. In front of her, out of focus, the stockade goes on: a lamp in the map-room window, the ward's door, a sampler crossing with a box. The band of **grey** crosses the top of the frame and is drawn, as always, as *paper with nothing on it*. **No count in the corner of this panel. No dialogue, no caption, no SFX, no Titanvein, no bloom.** The reader must understand the arithmetic from the chapter alone: the sea spent **a year** in which it could not bind a verdict; the Barrens spent **a year** in which it could not hold a vote; the realm of the horn has just been handed, by a realm it has never met, the exact shape of its own wound — *a year of unboundness* — and the captain of the realm is sitting on the step of an office her own law invented, in the country it did not, holding the arm that carries the statute. The single craft note: **her mouth is not moving, and that is the panel.**

### P4.2 — What the realm does with a year (half page)
**Art:** And the answer is the realm's, and it is the reason this saga keeps returning to this culture: **the year is entered as a debt and read aloud at every circle until it is settled.** But it is not the Barrens' year — it is the sea's — so the realm does something it has never done: it **enters a foreign office and a foreign debt in the same line.** Four frames: **(1)** the horn's copy, laid out at the post's desk, with the entry being made in the realm's cut hand: ***entered: the office of keeper-Above, of the chorus-parliament of Thalassa, filled by our reader's kin-of-road (Nereia Thal, tide-diviner, of the Nine); entered: the sea's year of unboundness, to be read aloud at each circle until the reef is told to have grown its ring.***; **(2)** **Kharza** reading it back aloud, once, at the post's door, to an audience of three (the deputy, the gate-clerk, Viv) because that is how the realm enters things; **(3)** Viv's face, because the *legal* invention here is enormous and the art team must draw her *not saying so*; **(4)** and the page's last frame: **the well**, drawn from above, one finger's rise marked in chalk on the stone by the gate-clerk, with a second chalk-mark being added beside it in a different hand — and the art team must draw the second mark as **the same shape as a rest**.
**Morgatha (flat, dictating, and this is the captain of the Barrens deciding the volume's institutional question in three sentences, with her sleeve still up):** "\u2014 Write it in one entry, both halves, and don't dress it. **It's their office and it's our reader, and the realm's going to have to learn to hold a thing it didn't make** — the way we hold a *reading*: out loud, on the record, with the door kept open so that any fool in nine realms can walk in and check. — Second half. **The sea's year goes in as a debt.** Not a shame, not a poem: a **debt**, read aloud at each circle until the reef's told to have grown its ring, because I spent a year of my realm's life unable to *vote* and I know exactly what a realm looks like on the other side of one, and it looks like **this camp**, and it looks *fine*, and that's the *frightening* part." *(a beat)* "…Also, on the record: nobody is allowed to make a *song* about the sea's year. I've met this realm. Somebody's already got a *verse*."

## Page 5 — *Nine Posts, One Week*

### P5.1 — The waybills, week one (half page)
**Art:** The reading-road's first week, drawn as **nails on a board** — and the art team's rule: **no victory parade, no map-with-flags; waybills, pinned and numbered, like a shop's orders.** The setting is the whole *west* delivered in one panel of paper: a board at the horn's wall in the Barrens with forty-one slips, then the camera closing on nine of them, one per crossing, each legible enough to read for a moment: *crossing one: read, four doors, one refusal, written*; *two: read, no refusals, the reader asked for the deed to be read twice*; *three: the company's post; they have put a price on readings; we read anyway, for free, twenty paces down the road*; *four: bought ford; the keeper read the realm's own receipt back to us, out loud, from the post* — and so on, with the ninth slip marked **pencil** because the ninth post is the one at the edge, and the chair is not filled. Four frames: **(1)** the board, from the wall's other side, of a roomful of clerks doing nothing dramatic; **(2)** the nine slips, close, three legible; **(3)** the **ninth slip**, in pencil, with the note *chair empty; deputy reading; nothing opened*; **(4)** and the page's last frame: a hand inking the ninth slip over in the realm's plate-type — because Ch. 29 said *pencilled, not yet inked* and this is the week it gets inked — with the clerk drawn small and unnamed and *pleased*.
**Viv (flat, reading the board, and the advocate's summary is the chapter's score line):** "\u2014 Nine crossings, one week, and **nobody was refused a reading and eleven people refused a deed** — write that down, Sprocket, that's the number this whole volume turns on: *nine realms of tribunals cost a season; a reading costs a road.*" *(a beat)* "…And look at three. **They've started charging.** Of course they have; it's a *company*. And look at what we did — we moved twenty paces down the road and read for free, which means the realm's answer to a company is not a *fight*, it's a **queue**. …I've never been so happy about anything so boring in my life."

### P5.2 — The war-chief, refused (half page)
**Art:** And the doctrine bites the hand that wrote it, and the art team's staging rule: **play it absolutely straight, as bureaucracy, and let the reader laugh.** A supply wagon, drawn by the realm's own colours, at the head of the line at the second post; the reader (a young orc, drawn, unnamed, with a **collar on** — the realm has already cut two more for the interior posts, canon from here: *Vol. 6 adds collars by need, one per post*) with the warrant in her hand; and behind the wagon, on foot, in a plain coat with his sleeve rolled, **Ulrik the Unbroken**, listening to his own quartermaster's warrant being read aloud and find the fault. Four frames: **(1)** the wagon and the line; **(2)** the reader's finger on the date and her flat, level voice; **(3)** **Ulrik reading the warrant himself**, twice, and then standing *out of the line*, on the verge, and waiting — the art team must draw him **waiting**, because this is the realm's greatest living fighter being made to wait by a girl of twenty with a piece of paper, and he does it *without one sign of displeasure*; **(4)** the page's last frame: the corrected warrant re-read and passed, and the war-chief filing a paper *about* the rule — and the art team must letter the top line of it, because the realm's own war-chief is filing **praise**.
**Ulrik (flat, to the reader, at the verge, and the realm's warmest voice gives the doctrine its best day):** "\u2014 Wrong date on the warrant, and you read it out in front of my own wagons, and I *know* the quartermaster spent the morning deciding whether to ask me to fix the date instead of standing in the line. — Don't fix it. **Never fix it.** If my warrant's wrong, the post says no; if the post doesn't say no to *me*, there's no post — there's just a *morning* where everybody's polite and nothing gets read." *(a beat, and the art team must draw him writing, on the verge, with his coat on the ground beside him)* "…And I'm filing this, and you can read it out at the circle: ***the war-chief's warrant was refused today for a wrong date, by a reader of twenty, and it cost the realm one hour and gained it the whole road.*** — Sign it, girl, and let's move; I've got four hundred arms and nine crossings and I'm *late*."

## Page 6 — *The School's Second Term*

### P6.1 — The assignment (half page)
**Art:** The school in the well, its second term, and the art team's brief: **this page must be the warmest institutional page in the volume, and it must contain no teacher — Taurus's margin does all the talking.** The chamber from Ch. 25/29's memory: the shaft's lamps, forty benches, the hinge on the inner door, the wall with the four framed refusals. **END OF CHAPTER 31.**

*The report went home and the realm did what it does: it entered, it staffed, it sent the newest word to the furthest door, and it read the whole thing aloud where anybody standing in the yard could hear it. The post at the edge opened its first door — a barn lease, ten days in the queue, read out in full and entered with the words* read; not refused *— under a deputy whose authority is bounded by a line in a register, because the realm worked out, one week after inventing the office, that a post which can close a door is a post that can also open one, and that neither is the reader's job. Nine crossings opened in seven days and the doctrine bit its author's hand: a war-chief's own warrant was refused for a wrong date, and Ulrik the Unbroken stood on the verge, waited his hour, and filed praise. A school framed its first yes at the same height as its four noes, and a changeling with a child's map marked a door* YES *before anybody else did. A captain sat on a step in the dark and found out that the sea spent a year unable to bind anything, which is the exact shape of her own realm's wound, and the realm's answer to it was the one thing this culture has ever had: enter it as a debt and read it aloud at every circle until somebody says otherwise. An advocate who has spent five volumes telling nine realms that the answer to a locked door is a reader stood on an empty beach and got the other half of her own law out of the emptiness — a reading is a request to be heard; a door is only the place where somebody is — and wrote it in a margin, in her own hand, where this whole story began. A tide-diviner accepted a foreign office for the length of a road and refused to let it become a life. A captain trued a flag-pole nobody had asked him to touch. And on a shelf of stone above the deep, a merfolk who has answered nothing for three hundred and sixty-two days cut the notch he had refused and sang one note of the first line back down the tube, because a listener who only listens is a bucket, not a partner — and then sat down to wait for the flood, which is the entire job, and the entire religion, and the entire point. The reef has a ring it did not grow. The well is up a finger. The sea's reply went onto the map as a blank with a line round it, because the saga has exactly one way to draw a place it is not allowed to show yet, and Fenn Thistledown has been drawing it that way for three hundred years: **leave it empty, and put a line round it, and wait.***
**The record's tenth card is hide, and the sea's mark on it is a ring with a gap in it and nine notches beside it: a road, a debt, and a circle that has not closed — which is what this realm calls a hope.*

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 31)

- **This is the institution chapter, and its manner is *furniture and paper*.** Nothing in it is won by force: the chapter's events are a deed read aloud, a rule written in a register, waybills nailed to a board, a frame hung on a school wall, a desk installed by a window, a plumb line, a chalk-mark, a notch cut in wood. Draw all of it flatly and let the reader find the drama in the arithmetic.
- **The post's first door (P2).** The queue is *organised* (slab-leather numbers); the **deputy** (a camp clerk, drawn, unnamed, plate-type ledger hand) sits with the **collar on its nail behind her, not on her**; the barn lease of Ch. 29's fourth man is read **in full, aloud**, and the register takes the realm's plate-type entry: **ten days · held · read · not refused · opened**. **The realm's rule about deputies, canon from here:** *a deputy may hold a deed, may read it aloud, and may not sit as reader; where the reader's chair is empty the post reads, and does not open, until the chair is filled* — and Viv's rider, signed: ***no office of the realm is filled by whoever is nearest.***
- **The report (P3) is six lines and must be lettered legibly**, because the reader can check it against the last two chapters: the sea's law and the document problem \u00b7 the chorus and the tide-clock \u00b7 the voiceless Thalarch \u00b7 the reef's **missing year** \u00b7 the motion (line stays in the water; a keeper entered above; the sea answers readings sung from the surface) \u00b7 and the sentence Viv hates: *the deep answered; the keeper says it was the first line sung back, in a key we don't have*. **The reading of the report is staged on Ch. 28's yard-reading composition, deliberately.** The well rising **one finger** during the reading is the chapter's only strangeness: it is *entered as weather* by the gate-clerk, sampled by the troll surgeon, and chalk-marked on the stone — and the second chalk-mark is **shaped like a rest.**
- **The stop (P4.1) is Volume 6's first, and it is the quietest panel in the saga.** One frame, full page: the post's step at night, the lamp low, the report on the desk, Morgatha with her forearm bare and her **mouth not moving**, the stockade going on out of focus behind her, the **grey drawn as paper** across the top of the frame. **No count in the corner, no dialogue, no caption, no SFX, no Titanvein, no bloom.** The reader must do the arithmetic alone: the sea could not bind a year; the Barrens could not hold a vote for a year. **Volume 6's stops are *understanding*, not rage — keep them that way.**
- **The realm's answer (P4.2) is one entry with two halves**, in the realm's cut hand: the **office of keeper-Above** (filled by Nereia Thal, *of the Nine*) **and the sea's year of unboundness, entered as a debt, to be read aloud at each circle until the reef is told to have grown its ring.** This is canon and it matters later: **the Barrens' response to any unbound year is to read it aloud at every circle until it is settled** — same sentence as the fourth cell's open line, different language. Morgatha's rider: *nobody is allowed to make a song about the sea's year.*
- **Nine posts, one week (P5).** Waybills on a **board**, not a parade: nine slips, three legible for the reader (*crossing one: read, four doors, one refusal, written* \u00b7 *crossing three: the company's post; they have put a price on readings; we read anyway, for free, twenty paces down the road* \u00b7 *crossing four: bought ford; the keeper read the realm's own receipt back to us*), and the **ninth slip still in pencil** (*chair empty; deputy reading; nothing opened*) — inked over in this chapter, by an unnamed clerk, and the pencil-to-ink change is the beat. **The doctrine's test: Ulrik the Unbroken refused at his own post** for a wrong date on a supply warrant, and standing **on the verge** to wait; his filed complaint is **praise** (*it cost the realm one hour and gained it the whole road*). **Canon: Vol. 6 cuts more collars by need — one per post** (the reader at the second post wears one).
- **The school (P6).** Second term's assignment, lettered on a slate: ***find a door that says YES; read it; write down what it costs to be welcomed.*** The four refusals stay framed; the **fifth frame arrives as a yes** (a seam-port bakery that asked for a copy) and its cost is entered on its own small slate (*a loaf and a morning, and they will expect the reading again on the same day next year*); and by the page's end **a sixth frame is hung empty beside it**, because the realm files its successes and failures the same way. Kha's line goes on the wall in the same frame as the refusals: ***a door that says no teaches you the law; a door that says yes teaches you what it costs.*** Taurus's margin carries the school's complaint about its own building (stairs before desks).
- **Kha (P7) appears on the page for the first time**: eleven, twelve by dawn, in a coat cut down from a bigger one, her sister's travel-horn on her back, the collar in a case, an escort of **two carriers and a mule** drawn unnamed and *careful with the case*. Her letter home — *I'm not sitting still; I'm being accurate* — arrives at the post and is read there, and Kharza folds it **inside the sleeve with the clause on it**. The child's **margin correction** (*'don't be brave' is a rule for people who are already brave*) is the school's work proving itself.
- **The books, open (P8).** Clause four working: the realm's **clerk-auditor** (drawn, unnamed, a Barrens accountant) at the map-room table with the survey book, sample ledgers, costing sheets and wage rolls; **Oros in the second chair reading his own book**, unimpressed; the audit finds four discrepancies, **all four in the founder's hand and all four against the camp's own interest** (the melancholy note, in his hand: *the grey moved; we buy time*); and the next morning a **second desk by the window with one of the eleven worn scoops hung above it as a wall-piece** — the founder's answer to being audited, drawn as furniture, never explained. **The founder gets no face in this chapter**: a coat, a back at the mark, a doorway. **His daughter appears once**, at the gate with a box, looking out at the mark and not walking to it.
- **Viv's arc (P9) is the chapter's private engine.** Her practice readings on the **flats** — no door, no clerk, no listener — must be drawn *empty*: wide panels, scrub, windbreak, and the **gate-clerk** who arrives in the composition **one panel late, three evenings running, doing the camp's accounts on a rock**, never commented on. The sixth clause she gets out of the emptiness: ***a reading is a request to be heard; a door is only the place where somebody is.*** She enters it **in her own hand** in the margin of the horn's copy (*entered by the advocate, at the edge, on the flats, alone*), and **Kharza adds a line under it in hers** (the realm's version: *read at the door, held at the counter, and asked for at the flats*). This pair of margin lines is canon and Volume 6 will quote it back at her.
- **Nereia's acceptance (P10.1) is a *choice with a term***: she will carry the sea's keeper-Above office **for the length of the road and not one day longer**, and she says the reason plainly (the line stays in the water; she stays where she is useful; the sea may grow a keeper out of people with nowhere else to be). Her flask is **mended, full, filled without a funnel**; the well's two chalk-marks sit in the frame, the second shaped like a rest; and her warning is the volume's hook, stated once: **the water answering is still happening, and next time she wants all of them standing next to her.**
- **Rule 6 (Roderick)**: P10.2, **exactly one panel, no balloon, no caption, no count, nobody watching** — the post's bare **flag-pole trued** with a plumb line: coat off, wedge and mallet, one blow, then away with the mallet over his shoulder. The **deputy watches from the doorway with the register open and writes nothing.** The gate-clerk's supper-table footnote is the only comment: *the flag goes up when there's a reader in the chair.*
- **The answer, again (P11.1)**: the deep may gain **nothing** — one shade, one emptiness, nothing in it. Whalesong **cuts the notch he refused in Ch. 30**, **sings one note of the first line back down the tube** (drawn as a single clean line of the sea's ring-script, recognisably the opening of Ch. 30 P9.1), and the only physical change permitted is **a faint line of haze at the tube's mouth** — small enough that a reader might miss it. **Nothing in this chapter explains the reply**; his last line is *the flood's at dawn; I'll know by the flood.*
- **The coda (P11.2)** uses the chorus composition **with one chair removed** (this is the road party, not the Nine), the cart's **plate of bread** in the frame's corner, the count running to **eleven** and taking a **rest**, twenty identical buckle copies in a cloth (the rim foundry's pattern travelling east), and **Bramble's child's map with one door marked YES** — her assignment's first yes, never given a panel of its own.
- **Fenn's map (P12.1) is the volume in one drawing**: nine rings with clerks' squares, the road's **rest** between the sixth and seventh (with the legend note *rest — see bottom*), the **wet ring** at the sea-gate in a different ink (*heard*), and, in the sea's band, **a blank with a line round it** and the legend entry ***? — heard twice***. The nine figures and the boat-wagon are drawn small, walking south. **The saga's captions live bottom-right in his hand.**
- **The card (P12.2) is the tenth hide card, Volume 6's second.** All accumulated marks continue; the new movement is a **second ring inside the salt-ring's clean middle, unfinished, its lower arc left as a gap**, with **nine short notches** cut beside it (the road's nine posts) — and the rest-bar now carrying the volume's count: **eleven — and a rest.** The cart's line, in tiny italics in the margin: ***“…maps don’t lie. they wait.”*** Caption: ***Here number the named.***
