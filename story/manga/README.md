## What each chapter file contains

1. **Header** — cast (with first-appearance flags), locations, the arc beat, the motifs advanced.
2. **Page-by-page panel script** — `P#.#` panels: the **Art** line (what the artist draws, with the art team's key details flagged) and the **dialogue/caption/SFX** lines (what the letterer types).
3. **Production notes** — art & lettering rules: the Unraveling's visual grammar, motif tracking, re-read details, and *what not to do* (protected beats).

## Pilot canon quick-reference (what is already locked on the page)

- **The Unraveling's visual grammar** (Ch. 2, established; Ch. 6 extends): full color → washed → line-art → white (erasure). A withered thing is not dead — it is a drawing someone stopped working on.

It's hot, and it's yours, and it doesn't cool for anybody.”* Her hands stay **bound** for the rest of the volume; nobody asks her about it.

## Season 2 · Volume 5 — *The One Throat* (the Barrens) — the on-page state

- **The realm's law has no text** (Ch. 22 P3.2, P8.1, P10.2). Viv asks for the code's document and is shown a **forearm**: the Barrens keeps its statute in **oath-script cut into arms** and in **memory, rehearsed in rooms**, and its record is a **horn** (the debt-horn, carved). Amendments are not passed in a chamber — **a clause is not law until the clans that vote for it cut it into themselves**: *in my realm a clause costs a vote; here it costs skin* (Viv, P10.2). The art team's device for the realm's *sound*: **a field of forearms** read from behind and above (invented P9.1).
- **The debt-line** (Ch. 22 P1.1–P2.2) — the border pass, and its black slab cut over with oath-script in hundreds of hands: **every traveller reads their own worst debt aloud before entering** (not a punishment, not a fee: the realm's courtesy), kept by **Drog**, who reads *his* debt back in exchange. **Morgatha's reading pays Ch. 3 P4.2**: *Tarkhan, my brother … his oath went with him. Unkept. Unfinished. It's mine now. I carry it.* The realm's answer is silence and then the **palm-out debt-acknowledgement**, all volume. Bramble watches the palms go up and is drawn looking at her own hands — **unexplained, and never explained**.
- **The menders** (Ch. 22 P3.1). The troll river-caste's gardens, fords and bridges: the realm's hands, all repair and no menace (canon: the subverted monster). **Glintfin's 03a line is spent verbatim** and **Nereia's flask — cracked since Ch. 16 — is mended** (thread, glue, a mender's clamp; the mend is *visible*), the team's first “the troll is on our side” beat, carried by a **fixed object and not a speech**. **Nereia has no dialogue in Ch. 22 at all**; her markings do the scene, and **the sea's first line stays Vol. 6's**.
- **The Ultravote** (Ch. 22 P5.1–P5.2, P9.1–P9.2) — the bowl, the ring of **clan stones** (one per clan, each mended and cut like the people who own it), the **throat-stone** at the centre, and **the speaker's silence**: Durgan the Unbent may only voice the assembled will of *every* clan, so with one stone empty **the realm may read and may not decide** (one line in Ch. 22; **his 03a line is held** for the chapter the clause passes). The realm spent a year holding **readings** because they are all it can do: hundreds of voices, out of time, each clan reading its grievance *to* the absent clan — the page drawn as a field of forearms.
- **The carrying — the volume's crime, and its engine** (Ch. 22 P6.2). A blood-debt that cannot be collected from its debtor **comes home** (the code's *fifth holding*) to the dead man's nearest blood, where it may be held as **surety** until answered. The Reforgers' cell did not break one word of the code: **it read the code and used it** — a lawful ask may not be refused, so the debt was moved onto **the house of Tarkhan**, which now holds the realm's War-Chief. Viv's finding (P6.2): *“He didn't break your law. He read it, and then he used it, and you've no word in your whole grammar for a man who does that.”* **The Barrens has no word for fraud** — the hole the volume's clause exists to close.
- **The surety-hall, and the door with no lock** (Ch. 22 P6.1, P7.1, P11.2). **Ulrik the Unbroken is *housed*, not imprisoned**: a warm room, a fire, a chair made for him, a garden, a mended window, a goat — and **a bolt leaning inside the doorframe, in reach, dusty**, because nobody has needed it in a year. He is huge, kind, unbruised and **mender-mended** (a repaired catalogue of a body). His refusal is the volume's question, and the chapter lets him *win* it: *“I'd rather be held than be the reason the realm's law was a lie.”* **Morgatha does not answer that in Ch. 22** — she answers with procedure.
- **The count is the volume's clock, and it runs forward** (Ch. 22 P1.2, P7.2, P9.2, P12.1–P12.2). The pilot's war-song count returns as the frame-corner device — **one… two… three… four** — and Vol. 5's rule, stated once: **when it stops, that is the panel** (Ch. 4 P8.1). **One stop in Ch. 22 (P7.2, at her father's argument — no dialogue, no Titanvein, full width)**; the count resumes as *sound* on P9.2 and is the **card's own corner** with a new, **empty rest-bar** under it. **Titanvein (`#d1495b`) is reserved and does not appear** — canon bars it before the Ultravote's own chapter, and the Ultravote's own chapter is **Ch. 24, where it is spent once (P4.2)**.
- **Khura, the house of Tarkhan, and the horn** (Ch. 22 P4.2, P6.2, P9.2, P11.2; paid Ch. 24). The oath-keeper is the volume's **antagonist-by-law and its future**: she hides nothing, hands over the debt-horn the moment she is asked, refuses to let anyone *“stand in my yard and work on it”*, brings her house's stone back into the circle in a realm that thought it never would, and will **vote against the captain with her face up**. **She is the one who reads the new clause aloud when it passes.**
- **The clause, drafted and not yet law** (Ch. 22 P10.1–P10.2, P12.1). At the camp, with no pen and no paper, Viv and Morgatha draft it — ***the oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code*** — and Viv's warning is the price of the volume: it will not cost a vote here, **it will cost skin**, and the captain will have to stand in front of nine clans and offer her **arm**. The chapter's last drawn frame is **Morgatha's forearm on the throat-stone, with a clean patch of skin where the clause will go** — Vol. 4 ended with a blank line in a book; Vol. 5 opens with a blank **arm**.

- **The maze: a building that is the realm's document** (Ch. 23 P2–P5). The Barrens' law has no paper, so a disputed *word* is tested in the **maze** — a labyrinth cut into red rock whose walls carry the **entire code in cut script**, clause after clause, in hands that change every few metres, worn smooth at hand height by ten thousand walkers, and lit by an ordinary lamplighter on a shift. The walls **contradict** each other at the turns (third holding against fifth, debt against guest-right), so a walker must *choose* and may walk back — which is why nobody in the queue looks ashamed. **The centre chamber is the one room with bare walls**, because the centre is where somebody has to *say* it. The rule, cut over the gate: **everybody loses; the loss is carved; the maze goes to the loser** — a queue of people who have come to lose, and the realm's whole philosophy in one sentence (**Horn**: *“Everybody loses. That's the record. You want to lose somewhere that counts, you queue here.”*).
- **Viv's three terms, cut into a gatepost in front of the queue** (Ch. 23 P3.2) — the realm's first procedural amendment in a hundred years: **(1)** the walker names what was lost, **in words**, read where the walker says; **(2)** the cut goes in at the **declaration**, not the decision; **(3)** **the record reads both ways** — a wall of losses is a record of walkers, not a wall of shame. **The queue applauds, lightly**, because it means *their* loss will be read out next time.

Morgatha walks the maze with the clause, reading the walls out loud, and **loses**: at the centre, **Tauron** fights her by making her choose between the fight and the words — she takes a winning exchange on purpose, her mouth stops moving for one beat, and **the clause falls out of the room**. He stops, stands up, and **says the clause back to her perfectly** (he spent four corridors *listening*), then declares **his own loss** because the holder of a thing answers for it — and **his 03a line is spent verbatim as the yield**: *“You lost. Which means the maze is yours. Walk it. It'll teach you more than any map.”* The count **stops** once (P5.1, full width, **no Titanvein**) — Ch. 4 P8.1's rule, restated as Volume 5's. **Maze** (the labyrinth-keeper) gives the map; **Horn** (the horn-carver) cuts the record.

The clause goes into the **debt-horn's duel-record** — the realm's first *legal* entry, cut **as the walker says it**, with her **two holes** under it: (1) no word for **who** may invoke it (a feud can dress as a life); (2) no word for a life **freely given away**. Durgan's only movement is **one nod**. And at the gate at dusk, at the bottom of a wall of ten thousand losses, the realm has now **published a legal critique** of the clause its own captain wants passed — *in a realm with no paper*, on a horn and a slab.

The cyclopes' valley: map-stones laid flush in the workshop floors, the face-wall with the **great eye shut** (a forge is built *around* its smith — architecture, not anatomy), and the **map-room** — the only place in the Barrens with *pages*: maps on rails, the copyists' bench (**Crystal · Stone · Fire-Knob**, the kobold cave-carts), and cut over the door: ***A READING MAY NOT BE REFUSED. A COPY IS THE ASKER'S WORK.*** By the door hangs the **request-shelf**: every lawful ask ever made in this room, kept as a horn-sliver with the asker's name and mark — *a room that cannot say no keeps the names of everyone it has said yes to*.

Viv's finding: *“There's no break-in. No bribe. He came in, he asked properly, and this room gave him the realm.”* — **every realm in this fight has one rule it cannot say no to**.

Crystal's ledger adds the second finding: *one copy made … the asker's companions read with him, **twice** — **four** of them.* **The four are not identified, drawn, or speculated about in Volume 5** — the volume has a later, quieter place for that answer. Viv: *“Four people have seen every ford, every debt and every holding in this realm. …Print it, cut it, nail it up.”*

The 312-year-old mapper spends the week **learning to cut a country by hand**, because the realm has no ink for maps and the kobolds' tradition is a chisel: *“Three hundred years, and every map I've drawn's been my hand. Turns out that's not the trick. The trick's the hand after mine.”* And at the same bench, **Sprocket cuts his first record into horn** — the king of the mine's paper, learning the realm's grammar, unexplained and uncaptioned. **Fenn's north-west ledger changes again:** with a *new* copy of the map in his own hand (made here, never said aloud), the **six pins** stay, and the **twins' thread stays open**.

## Season 2 · Volume 6 — *The First Song* (the sea-gate, the road, and the first door) — the on-page state

> **The volume’s contract:** the sea’s law is sung, the sea answers, and the realm of readers is asked — by somebody who *cannot write* — to keep the sentence. Canon advancing through Ch. 30–32: the sea-gate and the tide-clock; the Shell and the chorus-parliament; **the first line given words once** (*nothing that is heard is lost*); the Deep-Listening post and the notch-board; the memory-reef and its un-grown ring; the realm’s collar, deputy rule and nine posts; **the flood session and the sea’s ear**; **the second line** (*then come in*) and **the five articles** of the sea’s instrument; and **the flood reading**: at the top of every water, at nine posts, a school and a camp, the realm reads something aloud.

- **The sea-gate, and a door a lawyer cannot argue with** (Ch. 30 P1–P2). A wet black arch in the tide-flats, clear at low water, drowned at high; a column of chiselled **tide-marks** up the left pier with the lowest nine worn smooth; three tide-guard with their shields **stacked** (because they are not needed); a dry-land bench for people waiting for water; and a **cut bell-rope** (unexplained forever). **The law:** *you sing for the door or the door's shut* — no tolls, no rank, no papers. Viv arrives with the realm's documents and discovers the chapter's whole engine: **a document is a song nobody is singing**, and the sea will not refuse it — it will not *hear* it. The party sings the Barrens' clause at a rock, badly and truly, and the door opens, and Marra states the volume's premise: **the sea does not require beauty, it requires truth — nobody can lie in tune.**
- **The Shell, and a court with an adjournment** (Ch. 30 P3). A chamber under the rock: dry ribbed sand at the floor, two roof-shafts, **shell-lamps** (living, tended by a child lamplighter, drawn and unnamed), **no chairs — a ring**, a reed-and-bone chair with one arm-rest worn by hands, and the **tide-clock**: chiselled marks at the quarters and a **reed float in a cut channel**, with a wet band above the line showing where the water has recently stood. **The session ends when the water says so, and nobody in the realm has ever called it rude** — Volume 6's suspense device (four low waters a day; every scene has a clock). The parliament is **voices, not a crowd**: **Tide, Current, Stream** (three parts, lettered as a stacked chord), **Coral** the scribe (records by **singing the thing back**; rubric, canon: *you may not enter anything you cannot sing back*), **Pearl** the judge (verdicts by **duet**; no gavel, none needed), **Shell** the herald (announces in song, naming visitors by tide and object). **The Thalarch Maelis never speaks and never sings** — a small old scar at the throat; her part is carried by **Song-Part**, who leans toward her like an ear; and **she votes by handing Song-Part her hand**.
- **The first line, spent** (Ch. 30 P9.1). Ch. 3's melody finally gets words, once, in the whole saga: ***nothing that is heard is lost*** — set in the sea's ring-script *and* in plain type. **The realm's crisis stated by procedure failing**: a small kelp-farm verdict cannot close because the first bar is anchored to the first line and the first line has not been heard in a year (“we are not dying; we are **quiet**”). **The ask** (Maelis crosses the ring and hands her hand to Song-Part) is for Nereia to sing it home; **her refusal is arithmetic, not feeling**: sing it in the Shell and the reef takes it and the surface goes deaf forever. **The answer is the Barrens' procedure arriving at the bottom of the sea** — Kharza, writing in wet sand: *don't move the line; post a keeper: two voices, one office, one wet, one dry* — and the sea's motion, entered by duet: the first line stays in the water, a **keeper-Above** is entered in the reef-roll, the first bar may be answered from the surface, and the sea's reply to any reading so made is **sung by the full chorus**.
- **The reef, and the ring that did not grow** (Ch. 30 P6). Acres of coral in concentric rings, **one ring a year**, with a tight dark knot at the centre, tended by krakenkin whose tentacles move over the rings like a reader's fingers; this year's outermost lip is **thin, low and level**, because the realm could not bind. **Mnemothe's line, spent** (*I remember the first song. I remember it stopping … a memory with no reason*), and the reef **grows one ring at the ninth mark** while the chorus holds the note.
- **The Deep-Listening post, and the answer nobody asked for** (Ch. 30 P7). One stone seat, one mast, one long **shell trumpet pointed down**, and a **notch-board** with three hundred and sixty-one notches and no gap. **Whalesong's line, spent verbatim** (*the Leviathan's silent; I'm still listening; that's not hope, that's manners*). Then the hook: **the deep answers — drawn as lettering without a voice, in the page's margin, exactly as Vaelthorn is drawn, and heard by nobody in the party: “That was the first line being *sung back* at us, in a key we don't have.”** The notch he cuts that day is left **uncut**, and the chapter ends on him going back to the shell. **The Leviathan is never drawn, voiced, or named as answering.**
- **Read it; we'll sing it back** (Ch. 30 P10). At the next low water the Barrens' clause is **read aloud** in the Shell and the chorus (and, uncovered for the first time, the **tide-guard**) **sings it back**: *a person may come and ask; the asking may not be refused* → ***a voice comes to the door; the door answers; a true thing is not turned away*** — the sea's roll of notches going from eight to nine, and Pearl's verdict on the two doctrines: *your realm's rule is a reading may not be refused; ours is a thing sung true cannot be unsung … we are going to read at every door we own and sing back everything we hear.* Viv's own summary, which Volume 6 will spend itself arguing with: *the sea's door wanted somebody to be **heard**.*
- **The shore, and the four small things** (Ch. 30 P11). Fenn draws the sea as **one line** with the legend entry *heard* and a deliberate **blank out at sea**; Bramble wrings out her mask's tag and **still cannot read it** (the saga's withheld name, played as a joke on top of a withheld thing); Azrael's line admits he is glad about a door he cannot lie to; Sprocket underlines his **fourth entry**. Marra's plan: the boat stays on the wagon, she stays on the boat (the sea has a *reply* and nobody knows who it is for), and she has a list from the reef-roll to read at four gullies on the road home.
- **The card, and the count's restart** (Ch. 30 P12). The **ninth hide card** (Volume 6's first): the new movement is a **salt-ring** — a drop of seawater let dry into the hide, a faint circle with a clean middle, with **one notch** drawn small at its edge and never explained — and the marks now continue **past the rest**, so the record that stopped on purpose in Volume 5 has started again on the other side of the pause. Corner: **thirty-six… one** (the rest and the restart, Morgatha counting once at the fire with no balloon), and the **flask at half** — Nereia's water-flask level is **Volume 6's corner device**, with the Ch. 22 mend visible on the glass. Caption: **Here hear the named.**
- **The post opens its first door** (Ch. 31 P2). Day ten, and the post at the edge is a working institution: a queue that takes **slab-leather numbers**, a **deputy** (a camp clerk, drawn, unnamed) whose **collar hangs on its nail behind her, not on her**, and the **barn lease** of Ch. 29's fourth man read **in full, aloud**, and entered in the realm's plate-type: **ten days · held · read · not refused · opened**. The rule that follows, canon: *a deputy may hold a deed, may read it aloud, and may not sit as reader; where the reader's chair is empty the post reads, and does not open, until the chair is filled* — and Viv's signed rider: ***no office of the realm is filled by whoever is nearest.*** The chapter's point, made by a clerk: a post that may refuse a deed is a post that can also **open** one, and the realm has never once said so out loud.
- **The report, and the well** (Ch. 31 P3). Viv's **six lines** — the sea's law and the document problem · the chorus and the tide-clock · the voiceless Thalarch · **the reef's missing year** · the motion and the keeper-Above · and the sentence she hates (*the deep answered; the keeper says it was the first line sung back, in a key we don't have*) — read aloud at the post's door on Ch. 28's yard-reading composition. Then the chapter's only strangeness: while the report is read, **the well rises one finger**. The troll surgeon samples it and shrugs, the gate-clerk enters it as **weather** (*well: risen one finger during reading, no rain, no fault*), and the second chalk-mark on the well's stone is drawn **in the shape of a rest.** Bramble is the only person who says the obvious thing aloud: *the roll's read at every water, and the water's here.*
- **The stop — Volume 6's first, and the quietest panel in the saga** (Ch. 31 P4.1). One full-page frame, no dialogue, no count, no SFX, no Titanvein, no bloom: the post's step at night, the lamp low, the report on the desk with the sixth line showing, and **Morgatha with her forearm bare and her mouth not moving**, the stockade going on out of focus behind her, the **grey drawn as paper** across the top. The arithmetic the reader does alone: the sea could not bind a verdict for **a year**; the Barrens could not hold a vote for a year. **Volume 6's stops are understanding, not rage.**
- **The realm's answer, in one entry with two halves** (Ch. 31 P4.2). Canon, and it matters later: ***entered: the office of keeper-Above, of the chorus-parliament of Thalassa, filled by our reader's kin-of-road (Nereia Thal, tide-diviner, of the Nine); entered: the sea's year of unboundness, to be read aloud at each circle until the reef is told to have grown its ring.*** The first **foreign office** the Barrens has ever carried, entered in the same line as a debt — because this realm's answer to any unbound year is the same sentence as the fourth cell's open line, in a different language. Morgatha's rider: *nobody is allowed to make a song about the sea's year.*
- **Nine posts, one week — and the doctrine bites its author** (Ch. 31 P5). Waybills on a **board, not a parade**: nine slips, three legible (*crossing one: read, four doors, one refusal, written* · *crossing three: the company's post; they have put a price on readings; we read anyway, for free, twenty paces down the road* · *crossing four: bought ford; the keeper read the realm's own receipt back to us*), and the **ninth slip still in pencil** — *chair empty; deputy reading; nothing opened* — **inked over in this chapter** by an unnamed clerk. Then the test: **Ulrik the Unbroken is refused at his own post** for a wrong date on a supply warrant; he reads the warrant himself, stands **on the verge**, waits his hour, and files a complaint that is **praise**: *it cost the realm one hour and gained it the whole road.* **Canon: Vol. 6 cuts more collars by need — one per post** (the reader at the second post wears one, at twenty).
- **The school frames its first yes** (Ch. 31 P6). Second term's assignment, on a slate: ***find a door that says YES; read it; write down what it costs to be welcomed.*** Kha's line goes on the wall in the same frame as the four refusals: ***a door that says no teaches you the law; a door that says yes teaches you what it costs.*** The fifth frame arrives as a **yes** — a seam-port bakery that asked for a copy, costed on its own small slate (*a loaf and a morning, and they will expect the reading again on the same day next year*) — and by the page's end **a sixth frame is hung empty beside it.** Taurus's margin asks for **stairs before desks**.
- **Kha, on the page at last** (Ch. 31 P7). Eleven, twelve by dawn: a coat cut down from a bigger one, her sister's travel-horn on her back, the **collar in a case**, and an escort of two carriers and a mule drawn unnamed and *careful with the case* — because the realm does not send a war-party to deliver an office, it sends **a route**. She reads her own office aloud to the mule (*reader at the edge … and she may refuse*) and says she will not write home to ask whether her sister was scared. Her letter, written at the school, arrives at the post and is read there: ***I'm not sitting still. I'm being accurate. — K.*** — with a **margin correction** that is the school proving itself: *'don't be brave' is a rule for people who are already brave.* Kharza folds it **inside the sleeve with the clause on it** and answers the office question for good: *it's hers; the realm's rule is the collar, not the girl.*
- **The books, open — clause four working** (Ch. 31 P8). A **Barrens accountant** (drawn, unnamed) in the camp's map-room with the survey book, sample ledgers, costing sheets and wage rolls; **Oros in the second chair**, reading his own book, unimpressed; four discrepancies found, **all four in the founder's hand and all four against the camp's own interest** (the margin note: *the grey moved; we buy time*); and the next morning a **second desk by the window with one of the eleven worn scoops hung above it** — the founder's answer to being audited, drawn as furniture, never explained by a balloon. **The founder gets no face in this chapter**: a coat on a nail, a back at the mark, a doorway. **His daughter appears once**, at the gate with a box, looking out at the mark and not walking to it.
- **Viv's sixth clause, and the margin** (Ch. 31 P9). Practice readings on the **flats** — no door, no clerk, no listener — drawn empty and wide, with the **gate-clerk arriving in the composition one panel late, three evenings running, doing the camp's accounts on a rock**, never commented on. Out of the emptiness she gets the other half of her own law: ***a reading is a request to be heard; a door is only the place where somebody is*** — entered **in her own hand** in the margin of the horn's copy (*entered by the advocate, at the edge, on the flats, alone*), with **Kharza's line added under it**: *read at the door, held at the counter, and asked for at the flats.* Canon; Volume 6 will quote it back at her.
- **The second office accepted, with a term** (Ch. 31 P10.1). Nereia will carry the sea's **keeper-Above** for **the length of the road and not one day longer** — the line stays in the water, she stays where she is useful, and the sea may grow a keeper out of people with nowhere else to be. Her flask is mended, **full**, filled without a funnel; the well's two chalk-marks sit in the frame; and her warning is the volume's hook, said once: **the water answering is still happening, and next time she wants all of them standing next to her.**
- **The answer, again** (Ch. 31 P11.1). At the Deep-Listening post, Whalesong **cuts the notch he refused in Ch. 30** and does the one thing the post has never done in 362 days: **sings one note of the first line back down the tube** (drawn as a single clean line of the sea's ring-script, the opening of Ch. 30 P9.1), because *a receiving end isn't a partner, it's a bucket*. The deep gains **nothing** — one shade, one emptiness — and the only permitted change is **a faint line of haze at the tube's mouth**, small enough that a reader might miss it. *The flood's at dawn; I'll know by the flood.* **Nothing in this chapter explains the reply.**
- **The card, and Fenn's blank** (Ch. 31 P12). Fenn's map is the volume in one drawing: **nine rings** with clerks' squares, the road's **rest** between the sixth and seventh (*rest — see bottom*), the **wet ring** at the sea-gate in a different ink (*heard*), and — out in the sea's band — **a blank with a line round it** and the legend entry ***? — heard twice***; the nine walk south, the boat-wagon behind them. The coda uses the chorus composition **with one chair removed**, the cart's plate of bread in the frame's corner, **twenty identical buckle copies** in a cloth (the rim foundry's pattern travelling east), **Bramble's child's map with one door marked YES**, and the count running to **eleven** and taking a **rest**. **Card 26** (the tenth hide card): a **second ring inside the salt-ring's clean middle, unfinished, its lower arc left as a gap**, with **nine short notches** beside it; rest-bar: **eleven — and a rest**; the cart's line in tiny italics: ***“maps don't lie. they wait.”*** Caption: **Here number the named.**
- **The flood, and the sea's newest organ** (Ch. 32 P1–P2). The gate drowned, **Marra's boat finally waterborne** (rope still on the cart's tail), and the parliament **walking up the drowned stair** — tide-guard first, tridents covered, then **the Thalarch's reed-and-bone chair carried up and set on the third step**, in the swell. **Pearl convenes the flood session**: not to bind but to listen. Canon: **the Shell is a mouth at low water and an ear at flood**; sessions **bind at low water and listen at high**; and the roll takes its **tenth notch — cut square**, because it is not a note. Coral's tablet held over her head; Marginalia's plate tied to the tide-guard's rope line *out of respect*.
- **The post, at dawn, and a phrase with a bar on it** (Ch. 32 P3). Nine days after the note: a **thread of current going *up* the shell's throat, against the tide** — no sound, no glow, no shape. Four fresh marks cut on the board's **edge**, where Whalesong has never cut anything: a line, a line under it, four marks, and a **rest** (*the phrase expects an answer, and the answer needs a second voice*). Two krakenkin carry the board up nine hundred feet of drowned stair and every merfolk on the steps touches it once. Whalesong's one sentence, said once and **never to be repeated**: *that's not the whale; that's what the whale is standing on.*
- **The second line** (Ch. 32 P4). Coral sings the phrase back; the answer rises through the arch **in a different ring-script weight** (a hand, not a print); the three voices take it and **fail honestly** — *three of us, four hundred thousand years of practice, and we are standing in the water singing a **hole*** — because half of it is in a key the water does not have. Then the four words, given to the page **once**, ring-script over plain type: ***nothing that is heard is lost* · *then come in***. **Canon and permanent: the sea will always sing only half of its second line**; the rest must be *written*.
- **The ask, and the five articles** (Ch. 32 P5). Maelis's hand, Song-Part's voice: **keep the sentence — write it, read it where the water cannot hear, bring it back where the water can.** Viv's instrument, entered in the **horn's copy** below the collar's terms and her own margin clause, in **five articles**: *(one)* the sea may not be bound by anything written; *(two)* the sea may be answered by anything written, read aloud at its water; *(three)* the keeper-Above may not hold an answer — read at the water and released; *(four)* the second line is kept in the record, sung in part by the sea and read in full on land, answerable by any person who can read; *(five)* **Kharza's own**: *nothing that is heard is lost, and nothing that is written is heard until somebody reads it out loud.*
- **The reef's lip grows** (Ch. 32 P5.1). At the flood, the memory-reef's outermost rim carries **a fresh paler line of growth — the first in a year** — tended by Coral-Kin, watched by Mnemothe; and the roll, above the water, gains the **square hole** beside the tenth notch: the sea's first entry that is not a note.
- **The flood reading** (Ch. 32 P6). Nine narrow panels in one row, one per post, the same act at the same hour; a register page headed **READINGS, NOT LAWS**; and the rule stated at the edge post by **the deputy**, standing beside a chair he may not sit in: *it doesn't bind anything — that's the point of it.* At the school the well's water stands on the lowest step, a child reads the four words off a slate, the slate goes on the wall **at the same height as the four refusals**, and Taurus sets the second term's exam: **what does a sentence do to a door that has already said no?**
- **“…Again.”** (Ch. 32 P7). In the camp's map-room at the flood hour, Oros reads the survey's **first year** aloud — the founder's twenty-six-year-old hand, *we are eight… and this is not a tragedy, it is a survey* — to the **second chair, empty**; and the founder stands in the doorway, coat and scoop, the grey drawn as blank paper behind him, **no balloon in his panel and his face not drawn**. His one word, from the doorway: ***…Again.*** Oros's answer is to reach for the next volume. *(Rhyme, never remarked on: Whalesong said the same word into a tube in Ch. 30 P12.1.)*
- **The keeper-Above's first act** (Ch. 32 P8.1). Nereia reads the second line down at the drowned arch and the sea answers upward — the two scripts **side by side in one panel, seam visible**. Her markings take a new pattern forever: **wide, slow rings — the pattern of listening**. And the flask ends its four-volume career: the last measure of grove water goes into the sea at the top of the flood, and it comes back **filled with flood water** — hollow to the office, not to her thirst.
- **Rule 6, at the pier** (Ch. 32 P8.2). **One panel, no balloon, no caption, no count, nobody watching**: Roderick paying out a **lead-line** off the pier's seaward face, reading the gate's depth **by hand** off fathom-marks he tied himself three volumes ago, with the gate's throat drawn as a **dark mouth in the band of light** and nothing else in the frame. The gate-clerk's supper-table footnote is the chapter's only comment on him and stays deadpan.
- **Kha's office, filled** (Ch. 32 P10.2). Nine days out of the Barrens to the furthest door: the collar **lifted off its nail and worn for the first time** (brass, cold, *worn like a shirt and not like a crown*), the chair sat in, the register set down in front of her like a kitchen. Her first act is a **reading**: the deep's four words, plain type, and nothing else — **her own Line stays unspent** (03a) — and the chapter's only word beside the filled chair is the deputy's, the same word he said beside the empty one: ***…Next.***
- **The realm reads itself out loud** (Ch. 32 P11). The horn's five articles entered and read at the post in the rain; **Sprocket's sixth entry** (*the record has been read aloud eight times today by people who are not me — I have decided this is the finest thing that has ever happened to me*); and the tally: **roll's notches ten · posts reading daily nine · schools holding a flood hour one · wells up a finger four · chapters of the sea's second line heard one half · articles five · card twenty-seven.**
- **The card** (Ch. 32 P12). The **eleventh hide card**, Volume 6's third, and its own movement: **a square hole punched clean through the leather** — not cut, not burned: *punched*, matching the roll's square notch — so the reader sees the **white page through it**, the saga's oldest image for a thing that is not there yet and its newest image for a door. Nine notches on the lower edge; count **eleven… seventeen — and a rest**; and on the gate's stone, beside four hundred years of tide-marks, cut by somebody with a knife and a stone for an anvil: ***READ HERE.*** Cart's margin: *“says 'then come in.' rude, honestly. i was already in.”*
- **The problem is stated at a stone, and nobody is clever about it** (Ch. 33 P2). Crossing eight's way-house, six slates at the stone's foot (*a lawful ask may not be refused* · *a clause is not law until the clan cuts it* · *the record reads both ways*), **Durgan the Unbent** reading the four words twice like a man testing a bridge, and the Speaker's finding: *I can voice a will. I cannot voice a welcome — a welcome isn't a thing we've got a standing for.* **Khura** adds the shape of it: she will read the welcome at her own house's stone and then **read her no after it, in that order, out loud, at the same size.** And in the way-house's back room, **Sprocket** finds the realm's answer with a scrap of horn and a palm held up: **the horn has two sides** — *the inside's what we owe, the outside's what we say* — and cuts the first test line on a horn's outside: ***read me and owe me nothing.***
- **The children answer first** (Ch. 33 P3). The school's second term returns **thirty-one essays** on *what does a sentence do to a door that has already said no?* — pinned the width of the wall, mistakes left standing, one nine-year-old's chisel answer legible as the chapter's epigraph: ***the no stays. the sentence moves the door.*** Taurus puts **the four words in a frame of their own, glass and all, at the same height as the four refusals** (*I'll not have the realm's first welcome pinned lower than its refusals*), sets the third term (*read the welcome at a stone that's said no to somebody you know, and write down what it does*), and **leaves Bramble's misspelt WELCOME in the frame uncorrected.**
- **The war-chief reads it, and the no is read after it** (Ch. 33 P4). At **Tarkhan's stone** — the house whose stone came back into the circle in Ch. 24 — with the **filed praise from Ch. 31** pinned on the crossing's board for anyone to read, **Ulrik the Unbroken** asks for the reading at his own clan's marker: *I want to hear a sentence in this realm that doesn't owe me anything and that I don't owe.* The welcome is read (plain type, ring-script above it); **Khura reads her no after it** (oath-script, the same size on the page); and Ulrik's palm goes **out flat on the stone** — the realm's old debt-acknowledgement, given for the first time to a sentence that is not a debt.
- **The door in the horn — the stop** (Ch. 33 P5). The circle's house as a workshop: the six-foot **debt-horn** on its cradle, six hundred entries deep, with **one blank strip** at the mouth end nobody has ever used; **Horn** the minotaur carver turns it and states his terms (*a welcome isn't a line; a welcome's a door — show me the door and I'll cut it*); and then **Volume 6's second stop, a full page, silent**: a **square, hand-width opening worked clean through the horn's wall**, edges smoothed and not polished, horn-dust on the lip, the four words cut around it in plate-type — ***nothing that is heard is lost*** / ***then come in*** — and **the light coming through the door in a small square on the far wall.** No dialogue, no count, nothing lettered anywhere on the page.
- **The company's exploit, and Viv's clause** (Ch. 33 P6). At **crossing three** the company's post has grown a counter and a board (*READINGS: 4 FEATHERS. HEARINGS: RATES BY APPLICATION.* and, newer: ***ASKINGS: THE SEA-GATE. THE DEEP. — WRITTEN OR SUNG.***), and a courteous clerk with clean hands has already read the welcome down the road and filed a writ claiming **passage at clan fords without fee**, a welcome being a permission. The realm's answer is delivered **by the crossing's own reader**, from a slip **Viv drafted on the road and Kharza cut that morning**: ***the welcome is read beside the stone and opens nothing. The stone's law stands. No person may be bound by the welcome and no person may use it. Read daily; it binds nobody; it may not be refused.*** — and the company's clerk copies the ruling into his own book, because a company that loses a case wants the ruling. **The company is not defeated**; its board is still standing at the frame's edge on P12.
- **The count changes its office, and the refusals are filed honestly** (Ch. 33 P7). Canon, stated once in the margin of P7.1 and never explained again: **the count now counts readings, not crossings** — one at crossing nine, two at crossing eight, three at Tarkhan's stone, four/five/seven/eight across the week's montage, **six** overleaf on a verge, and **nine** at the mark. **Two refusals of the reading itself** are filed as refusals, with reasons, and the reader **walks twenty paces and reads to the road**, which is nobody's yard and everybody's: *a refusal of a reading is not a refusal of the realm … the count is readings; refusals are not failures of the reading, they are the reading working.*
- **“An ear, entered.”** (Ch. 33 P8). The founder's letter arrives at the horn — four lines, in his own old hand, and the first thing he has ever asked the realm for as a person: ***I would like a reader at the mark. Mine. On the books. — G.*** Oros reads it twice, and Viv supplies the word the realm's grammar lacks, from an inventory of what its code *does* have terms for (who may speak · who may read · who may cross · who may be held · who may owe): **an ear — a person who is read to.** Entered at the mark with a reader (the sage) and an ear (G.), cut small by **Khura's graver** on the horn's outside.
- **The mark, and two chairs** (Ch. 33 P9). **The chapter's quietest page, and the founder's first personal beat in the saga.** Oros carries **the second chair** from the map-room out to the mark and reads **year one, day one** in the same words used at Ch. 32's empty chair; the founder's **boots** enter the top edge of the panel and then leave it; the book changes hands **silently**; and then the man in the grey coat reads his own twenty-six-year-old sentences aloud at the place that wrote them — **no face drawn, no crowd, the grey in blank paper** — while **one thread of movement** goes through the blank at the top of the frame (unexplained, unremarked, never to be referenced again). The survey's **second volume** is opened to its first blank page: ***year twenty-seven. we are eight. i am being read to.***
- **The second register, read out** (Ch. 33 P10). Three entries, lettered legible and read by **Durgan** with his hands **behind his back** (the second register is not a will and he refuses the gesture): ***one: the welcome — read beside the stone and at the water; opens nothing; may not be refused; may not be used.*** · ***two: the readings — nine crossings, the posts, the school, the camp, the mark; read daily; if it is not read, it is not kept.*** · ***three: an ear, entered — the mark; a reader: the sage; an ear: G.*** Durgan's finding: *this is the first entry I've read out that asks nobody for anything … I don't know what keeps it. The scribe says the reading keeps it.*
- **A letter read at the horn** (Ch. 33 P10.2). Kharza reads her sister's letter (P3.2's text, lettered exactly) aloud at the horn, as the custom is, and the realm **pins it in the register's own column** rather than among the waybills — a twelve-year-old's accuracy filed as a record. **Kha still has no line of her own, by rule** (03a); her letter is a letter.
- **The fire, and Roderick's log** (Ch. 33 P11). The chorus panel with the cart's plate — and the cart's **second plate** on the tail-board (*READ: WELCOME — OPENS NOTHING*); Fenn's map with nine crossings ticked in the realm's cut style and **the twins' column still blank, his hand away from it**; the flask drawn **full** (canon: *never again at a level — it has stopped counting*); **Rule 6**: one panel, no balloon, no caption, no count, nobody watching — **Roderick's log**, a column of his own ruling headed READINGS with nine entries, and the realm's second register copied beneath it by hand. Morgatha's closing line draws the chapter's distinction: *cut a clause once and it's there forever; say a welcome every day or it isn't there at all … that's a lot of mornings. Good. I've got a lot of mornings.*
- **The stone, and the card** (Ch. 33 P12). Crossing nine at the year's turn: Kha reading to eleven people and one dog, the well standing with three chalk-marks and a fourth fresh, the company's counter still twenty paces down the road with its queue of two — and the realm's newest cut on the stone, at a standing reader's hand: ***here is read the welcome. it opens nothing.*** The **twelfth hide card** (Volume 6's fourth) carries its own movement in two parts: the punched hole from Ch. 32's card now has the four words cut **around** it, worked clean through so both sides of the leather agree — and beside it, in ash, small and patient, **an ear.** Corner: **nine — and a rest.** Caption: ***Here read the welcome.***
- **The first suit under the second article** (Ch. 34 P1–P2). A courier brings a **case** to the third morning's camp, and the case holds the chapter's two objects: a stamped writ on Terranian plate-type (the company moves the sea's court; the company appears by counsel) and, under it in oiled cloth, **a shell** — the sea's own token, cut with a tide-mark, which is how the chorus-parliament summons anybody dry. At the shore: the company's **pier office**, four months old, with a counter, a waiting room, a stove, a **rates board** (*ASKINGS: 11 FEATHERS · ASKINGS AT THE SEA-GATE: WRITTEN OR SUNG*), fourteen people in a queue, and **Fenn's own coastline on the wall with a company stamp across the corner.** Canon, new: **the road the realm's readers walk — drain-cut, weighbridges, graded fords, posts and slats — is the company's road, built and kept by charter**, and the free readings run on it. The chapter says so plainly: *the realm read our sentence on our road.*
- **The Shell, one season on** (Ch. 34 P2.1). Same chamber, three new things: a **borrowed bench** on the dry side with the company's clerk and a register-officer standing **behind** it (nobody sits but the Thalarch), the party along the wall on camp stools in **Ch. 3's chorus composition rotated ninety degrees**, and **Marginalia's plate tied to a tide-guard's rope with the cart's cord, dry, out of respect** — canon: the plate appears on every wide panel of the Shell from here. Pearl opens the session with jurisdiction in four sentences; the register-officer reads the company's **charter** into the record; and the sea's chorus takes it up — **and stops at the word *discoveries*, twice, at the same place**, the chord thinning to nothing and Coral writing **a gap at its width** into the roll. Canon: **the sea has refused four words in nineteen years of Coral's scribing, and every one of them was a lie somebody was being paid for.**
- **The company's case, and Viv's answer** (Ch. 34 P3–P4). The clerk's figures, drawn as the ledger's own ruled columns: **one thousand and sixty askings placed; eleven thousand six hundred and sixty feathers taken; askings down to two hundred and nine against six hundred in the same season last year.** Viv's answer is an advocate's inventory, made standing on the dry sand with four objects laid in a line (the horn's copy, the week's slat-slip, the punched card, a blank sheet): **who was refused** (two refusals in a season, both filed with reasons, both given the reading twenty paces on, on the verge), **who was charged** (the company charges four feathers at its posts; the realm charges nothing and answered a price with a *queue*), **who was cheated** (*nobody sold these words* — the deep gave the second line at the flood, in the water, to nine persons, unasked and unpriced, and asked the realm to keep it in **writing**, because the sea cannot write). Kharza reads the **week's slips** into the record and Coral sings each line back; Maelis's hand goes flat once for the entering.
- **The word in the charter** (Ch. 34 P5). **Rule 6**: one silent panel, nobody watching — **Roderick** alone in the antechamber, coat off, finger travelling the charter's lines, **his thumb coming down flat on the single word *carriage***, his face unchanged; then the charter closed on its cord, set **square to the door**, and him gone. Viv finds it open at the clause she came up to look for, reads the room once, and reads the other side's own charter into the record — *carriage, not possession, not ownership, not title* — and the chorus sings *carriage* clean where it could not sing *discoveries*. Viv's concession is the chapter's honesty: **the company's clerks do honest work and the realm would be stupider to lose them.** Canon, Viv's formulation: *a sentence read aloud to nobody who pays is not a loss, it is a **publication*** — and a publication is what the deep asked for in its own grammar.
- **The examination, and the reading at the water** (Ch. 34 P6). Pearl asks four questions and the room answers, the company's clerk **against his own interest** (*did the sea sell? no … was any person refused? the realm says no and my own clerks will say the same … a charter isn't a person; a factor isn't a person, he's an* office*; I have never once been able to make my employers be a person in that article*). Then Kharza reads the four words **at the water** at the shaft's mouth — the saga's third rendering of the sentence, lettered identically to Ch. 32 P4.2 — and the chorus sings it **clean, all three parts, first taking.** Canon: **the sea does not testify; it sings or it doesn't, and this room heard both.**
- **The release: the ledger, read and released** (Ch. 34 P7–P8). Pearl's order, lettered flat: the company's **two hundred and nine banked answers** are to be read aloud at the water once, in order, **by the company's own clerk** (*not a punishment, a courtesy*), and released; **the receipts stay.** The tear is drawn as bookbinding — the clerk's left hand on the receipts column, his right on the answers, **torn along the ruled fold page by page**, the art team's four-stage rhythm, and Kaelen looking at the **spine**. The pages go into the shaft's mouth at low water in a tide-guard's basket and **dissolve like paper**; the chorus sings each answer back as it is read so that it can let it go, the letters rising and **dissolving upward** — the only chord in the volume drawn that way. **Nereia is not at the shaft's mouth** (the keeper-Above may not hold an answer, not even to release one). The two hundred and ninth answer read aloud: *is a thing still mine if I gave it away?* — and the record's answer at the taking: *nothing that is heard is lost.* Kaelen re-sews the ledger's spine with **Marra's boat thread** and a sailmaker's needle, tests it by its boards, and hands it back: *two hundred and nine pages of receipts, and the stitching's better than it was when it was new … because somebody else fixed it.*
- **The four findings** (Ch. 34 P9.1). Canon, cut into the roll and sung to bind, with **Maelis's hand given four times in one composite panel of four presses** (the only composite-vote panel in the saga): ***one — carriage may be priced and the water may not*** (a company may sell a seat, a stamp, a walk, a receipt, or a place out of the rain; **no person may be charged for a reading of the sea's second line or refused one**) · ***two — an answer is not property*** (nothing heard at the water may be held, banked, bought, sold or entered as stock; a **receipt** may be kept, stamped and charged for) · ***three — 'person' means a person*** (a charter is not a person, an office is not a person, and **no company may hold the sea's offices — reader, ear, or keeper-Above — nor may any office be bought, sold or inherited**) · ***four — the ledger is released***, entered as done: **writ to done in eleven days.** Then the clerk asks, for himself and not for his company, **may I be read to?** — and Kharza reads the four words for him at his own pace, lettered small as a person's copy, and his palm goes flat on nothing at all.
- **What a realm pays for** (Ch. 34 P10). Morgatha's decision, on the coast road, cut by Kharza at sunset and nailed beside the free readings: the realm **contracts** the company's clerks for the shoreline's posts at posted rates and will not haggle over a clerk's morning; the realm pays **nothing** for words, at any water, in any weather; and **the realm's readers never sell askings** — *a realm pays its carriers and never pays its witnesses.* The company survives by being redefined: the rates board is relettered by the clerk himself, badly, **with the old letters showing through the new paint** — ***WE CARRY. THE SEA DOES NOT OWE. · ASKINGS: 9 FEATHERS, POSTED. · READINGS AT THE WATER: FREE, AND NOT OURS TO SELL.*** — the re-sewn ledger and its two hundred and nine receipts on the counter, a **third chair** for the queue, a bigger stove, and a second map beside Fenn's stamped coastline: **the shoreline's nine free slats, in the realm's cut style, artist unstated.**
