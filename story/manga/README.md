## What each chapter file contains

1. **Header** — cast (with first-appearance flags), locations, the arc beat, the motifs advanced.
2. **Page-by-page panel script** — `P#.#` panels: the **Art** line (what the artist draws, with the art team's key details flagged) and the **dialogue/caption/SFX** lines (what the letterer types).
3. **Production notes** — art & lettering rules: the Unraveling's visual grammar, motif tracking, re-read details, and *what not to do* (protected beats).

It's hot, and it's yours, and it doesn't cool for anybody.”* Her hands stay **bound** for the rest of the volume; nobody asks her about it.
- **The realm's new form** (Ch. 20 P7.2, P8.1), made in a square and used before any sovereign rules on it: **a word is cut in a **bar** at a hearth and the bar **goes home with the signer**; the word is **read whole — both halves — before the thumb goes down**; **whoever holds the bar while it is signed is marked with it.** The signature is a **thumb-print pressed into hot iron, once in a life** — small, permanent, **unforgeable** — and the **mountain's own stroke** (Echo's mark, the shape off his slate, the mark the raw wall answered with) is **cut into the head of every bar**: the realm's first archive mark. **The mine keeps the presses for *orders*; the realm's *oaths* move to the fire.** Torvik's nine-month burn **goes quiet** under the mark he pressed himself. **Khazur refuses to rule** (*“I'll not write this one. ## Season 2 · Volume 5 — *The One Throat* (the Barrens) — the on-page state

- **The realm's law has no text** (Ch. 22 P3.2, P8.1, P10.2). Viv asks for the code's document and is shown a **forearm**: the Barrens keeps its statute in **oath-script cut into arms** and in **memory, rehearsed in rooms**, and its record is a **horn** (the debt-horn, carved). Amendments are not passed in a chamber — **a clause is not law until the clans that vote for it cut it into themselves**: *in my realm a clause costs a vote; here it costs skin* (Viv, P10.2). The art team's device for the realm's *sound*: **a field of forearms** read from behind and above (invented P9.1).
- **The debt-line** (Ch. 22 P1.1–P2.2) — the border pass, and its black slab cut over with oath-script in hundreds of hands: **every traveller reads their own worst debt aloud before entering** (not a punishment, not a fee: the realm's courtesy), kept by **Drog**, who reads *his* debt back in exchange. **Morgatha's reading pays Ch. 3 P4.2**: *Tarkhan, my brother … his oath went with him. Unkept. Unfinished. It's mine now. I carry it.* The realm's answer is silence and then the **palm-out debt-acknowledgement**, all volume. Bramble watches the palms go up and is drawn looking at her own hands — **unexplained, and never explained**.
- **The menders** (Ch. 22 P3.1). The troll river-caste's gardens, fords and bridges: the realm's hands, all repair and no menace (canon: the subverted monster). **Glintfin's 03a line is spent verbatim** and **Nereia's flask — cracked since Ch. 16 — is mended** (thread, glue, a mender's clamp; the mend is *visible*), the team's first “the troll is on our side” beat, carried by a **fixed object and not a speech**. **Nereia has no dialogue in Ch. 22 at all**; her markings do the scene, and **the sea's first line stays Vol. 6's**.
- **The Ultravote** (Ch. 22 P5.1–P5.2, P9.1–P9.2) — the bowl, the ring of **clan stones** (one per clan, each mended and cut like the people who own it), the **throat-stone** at the centre, and **the speaker's silence**: Durgan the Unbent may only voice the assembled will of *every* clan, so with one stone empty **the realm may read and may not decide** (one line in Ch. 22; **his 03a line is held** for the chapter the clause passes). The realm spent a year holding **readings** because they are all it can do: hundreds of voices, out of time, each clan reading its grievance *to* the absent clan — the page drawn as a field of forearms.
- **The carrying — the volume's crime, and its engine** (Ch. 22 P6.2). A blood-debt that cannot be collected from its debtor **comes home** (the code's *fifth holding*) to the dead man's nearest blood, where it may be held as **surety** until answered. The Reforgers' cell did not break one word of the code: **it read the code and used it** — a lawful ask may not be refused, so the debt was moved onto **the house of Tarkhan**, which now holds the realm's War-Chief. Viv's finding (P6.2): *“He didn't break your law. He read it, and then he used it, and you've no word in your whole grammar for a man who does that.”* **The Barrens has no word for fraud** — the hole the volume's clause exists to close.
- **The surety-hall, and the door with no lock** (Ch. 22 P6.1, P7.1, P11.2). **Ulrik the Unbroken is *housed*, not imprisoned**: a warm room, a fire, a chair made for him, a garden, a mended window, a goat — and **a bolt leaning inside the doorframe, in reach, dusty**, because nobody has needed it in a year. He is huge, kind, unbruised and **mender-mended** (a repaired catalogue of a body). His refusal is the volume's question, and the chapter lets him *win* it: *“I'd rather be held than be the reason the realm's law was a lie.”* **Morgatha does not answer that in Ch. 22** — she answers with procedure.
- **The count is the volume's clock, and it runs forward** (Ch. 22 P1.2, P7.2, P9.2, P12.1–P12.2). The pilot's war-song count returns as the frame-corner device — **one… two… three… four** — and Vol. 5's rule, stated once: **when it stops, that is the panel** (Ch. 4 P8.1). **One stop in Ch. 22 (P7.2, at her father's argument — no dialogue, no Titanvein, full width)**; the count resumes as *sound* on P9.2 and is the **card's own corner** with a new, **empty rest-bar** under it. **Titanvein (`#d1495b`) is reserved and does not appear** — canon bars it before the Ultravote's own chapter, and the Ultravote's own chapter is **Ch. 24, where it is spent once (P4.2)**.
- **Khura, the house of Tarkhan, and the horn** (Ch. 22 P4.2, P6.2, P9.2, P11.2; paid Ch. 24). The oath-keeper is the volume's **antagonist-by-law and its future**: she hides nothing, hands over the debt-horn the moment she is asked, refuses to let anyone *“stand in my yard and work on it”*, brings her house's stone back into the circle in a realm that thought it never would, and will **vote against the captain with her face up**. **She is the one who reads the new clause aloud when it passes.**
- **The clause, drafted and not yet law** (Ch. 22 P10.1–P10.2, P12.1). At the camp, with no pen and no paper, Viv and Morgatha draft it — ***the oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code*** — and Viv's warning is the price of the volume: it will not cost a vote here, **it will cost skin**, and the captain will have to stand in front of nine clans and offer her **arm**. The chapter's last drawn frame is **Morgatha's forearm on the throat-stone, with a clean patch of skin where the clause will go** — Vol. 4 ended with a blank line in a book; Vol. 5 opens with a blank **arm**.
- **The card's register changes** (Ch. 22 P12.2): the record's cards are **no longer stone — they are hide, cut with a knife and rubbed with ash**, pegged flat at the corners; the cut grammar is the realm's oath-script; the accumulated marks continue (Wren's down-stroke, Fenn's circle-with-a-tail, the horizontal, Bark's ring, the six-strand braid, the charcoal laughing man signed *K.*, the pressed thumb-print, the flame-clan's knot) — and Volume 5 adds its own: **one word of oath-script scratched at the card's lower edge, legible as writing and never translated.** Cards: *Here count the named* (Ch. 22) — the seventeenth card, the first of Volume 5.
- **Fenn's ledger changes** (Ch. 22 P4.1): with the goblin courier network in front of him and nothing in its hands, he stops writing *no letters* and starts **logging days** — the first honest movement on the twins' thread since Ch. 16.

- **The maze: a building that is the realm's document** (Ch. 23 P2–P5). The Barrens' law has no paper, so a disputed *word* is tested in the **maze** — a labyrinth cut into red rock whose walls carry the **entire code in cut script**, clause after clause, in hands that change every few metres, worn smooth at hand height by ten thousand walkers, and lit by an ordinary lamplighter on a shift. The walls **contradict** each other at the turns (third holding against fifth, debt against guest-right), so a walker must *choose* and may walk back — which is why nobody in the queue looks ashamed. **The centre chamber is the one room with bare walls**, because the centre is where somebody has to *say* it. The rule, cut over the gate: **everybody loses; the loss is carved; the maze goes to the loser** — a queue of people who have come to lose, and the realm's whole philosophy in one sentence (**Horn**: *“Everybody loses. That's the record. You want to lose somewhere that counts, you queue here.”*).
- **Viv's three terms, cut into a gatepost in front of the queue** (Ch. 23 P3.2) — the realm's first procedural amendment in a hundred years: **(1)** the walker names what was lost, **in words**, read where the walker says; **(2)** the cut goes in at the **declaration**, not the decision; **(3)** **the record reads both ways** — a wall of losses is a record of walkers, not a wall of shame. **The queue applauds, lightly**, because it means *their* loss will be read out next time.
- **The walk, the duel, and the yield** (Ch. 23 P4–P5). Morgatha walks the maze with the clause, reading the walls out loud, and **loses**: at the centre, **Tauron** fights her by making her choose between the fight and the words — she takes a winning exchange on purpose, her mouth stops moving for one beat, and **the clause falls out of the room**. He stops, stands up, and **says the clause back to her perfectly** (he spent four corridors *listening*), then declares **his own loss** because the holder of a thing answers for it — and **his 03a line is spent verbatim as the yield**: *“You lost. Which means the maze is yours. Walk it. It'll teach you more than any map.”* The count **stops** once (P5.1, full width, **no Titanvein**) — Ch. 4 P8.1's rule, restated as Volume 5's. **Maze** (the labyrinth-keeper) gives the map; **Horn** (the horn-carver) cuts the record.
- **The realm's first legal entry is a *critique*** (Ch. 23 P5.2, P9.2). The clause goes into the **debt-horn's duel-record** — the realm's first *legal* entry, cut **as the walker says it**, with her **two holes** under it: (1) no word for **who** may invoke it (a feud can dress as a life); (2) no word for a life **freely given away**. Durgan's only movement is **one nod**. And at the gate at dusk, at the bottom of a wall of ten thousand losses, the realm has now **published a legal critique** of the clause its own captain wants passed — *in a realm with no paper*, on a horn and a slab.
- **The old-forges and the archive** (Ch. 23 P6–P7). The cyclopes' valley: map-stones laid flush in the workshop floors, the face-wall with the **great eye shut** (a forge is built *around* its smith — architecture, not anatomy), and the **map-room** — the only place in the Barrens with *pages*: maps on rails, the copyists' bench (**Crystal · Stone · Fire-Knob**, the kobold cave-carts), and cut over the door: ***A READING MAY NOT BE REFUSED. A COPY IS THE ASKER'S WORK.*** By the door hangs the **request-shelf**: every lawful ask ever made in this room, kept as a horn-sliver with the asker's name and mark — *a room that cannot say no keeps the names of everyone it has said yes to*.
- **The margin — the volume's crime, exactly** (Ch. 23 P7.1–P7.2). **Ash-Eye gives the family map back** to the Barrens, with the Rift's fault written into its margin in his own hand (canon's beat: the blame shared, not exported), in front of One-Eye and Iron-Eye — and **Oros standing beside his own handwriting and saying nothing**. On the request-shelf: a sliver reading *copy of the holdings, all fords, all debts, the Rift-line included*, with the room's keeper-note — *asked lawfully; a lawful ask may not be refused; copy made, one* — **the signature GORAKH**, in a hand this room's sage taught, and beside it **the same family mark as the map's century-old margin** (the cell-leader is the *heir* of the family that drew the map). Viv's finding: *“There's no break-in. No bribe. He came in, he asked properly, and this room gave him the realm.”* — **every realm in this fight has one rule it cannot say no to**.
- **The copy-ledger, and the four** (Ch. 23 P8.2). Crystal's ledger adds the second finding: *one copy made … the asker's companions read with him, **twice** — **four** of them.* **The four are not identified, drawn, or speculated about in Volume 5** — the volume has a later, quieter place for that answer. Viv: *“Four people have seen every ford, every debt and every holding in this realm. …Print it, cut it, nail it up.”*
- **Fenn's week, and Sprocket's first cut** (Ch. 23 P8.1). The 312-year-old mapper spends the week **learning to cut a country by hand**, because the realm has no ink for maps and the kobolds' tradition is a chisel: *“Three hundred years, and every map I've drawn's been my hand. Turns out that's not the trick. The trick's the hand after mine.”* And at the same bench, **Sprocket cuts his first record into horn** — the king of the mine's paper, learning the realm's grammar, unexplained and uncaptioned. **Fenn's north-west ledger changes again:** with a *new* copy of the map in his own hand (made here, never said aloud), the **six pins** stay, and the **twins' thread stays open**.

Morgatha reads her brother's debt out to her brother's household, in full, from the horn, and asks for **corrections, not permission** — and **Khura** takes up the graver and cuts the witness-line the clause is missing (*“Line three's got no witness. A life-clause with no witness is a rumour, captain”*), marking the horn on her own house's behalf: **the family the clause costs becomes its co-author.** At dawn, **Ulrik tests the weight of the bolt and puts it back exactly where it was** — no line, no caption — after being given, for the first time in a year, a reason to walk out.

The debt Gorakh cut onto the War-Chief's family cannot be paid by the family and the clause leaves it **unpaid**; the realm's answer is the rider: ***a debt this holding releases comes home to the realm, and is carried by the one throat until the realm is done with it.*** Nobody is forgiven, nothing is cancelled: the Barrens votes, in writing, with knives, to be **owed less and carry more**. **Khura's terms**: the debt stays on the horn, the horn stays in **her house**, the entry reads *unpaid*, and her reason for voting no is spoken into the record so no great-grandson reads her house as simple. The realm's grammar has nine words for a hand that breaks the code and none for a hand that *keeps* it and takes a realm with it, so Viv enters **“the lawful hand”** in the horn's **margin**, under **GORAKH**'s name — a note, not a vote (a crime needs its own circle). **Roderick's single silent panel** (rule 6) sits in the same page's background: hands at his sides, one not quite closed, watching his institution's crime get a name in a language he will have to read in a horn.

The surety-hall is drawn **unchanged** — door open, **dusty bolt still leaning inside the frame**, washing, goat, bench — because the law has moved and the *house* has not been told what to be instead. **Khura to the child**: *“Not on your arm, love — on the slate. Your arm's got thirty years.”* **Ulrik's arithmetic is about fords**, and his fuse is the volume's hinge: the realm has just been told in writing that a life outranks a word, and a cell is out there with every ford of the realm on paper, **and both of those are true** — he is **not walking out**, because his debt to the realm is the first honest thing anybody has handed him in a year and he means to pay it like a war-chief.
