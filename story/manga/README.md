## Season 2 · Volume 5 — *The One Throat* (the Barrens) — the on-page state

- **The realm's law has no text** (Ch. 22 P3.2, P8.1, P10.2). Viv asks for the code's document and is shown a **forearm**: the Barrens keeps its statute in **oath-script cut into arms** and in **memory, rehearsed in rooms**, and its record is a **horn** (the debt-horn, carved). Amendments are not passed in a chamber — **a clause is not law until the clans that vote for it cut it into themselves**: *in my realm a clause costs a vote; here it costs skin* (Viv, P10.2). The art team's device for the realm's *sound*: **a field of forearms** read from behind and above (invented P9.1).
- **The debt-line** (Ch. 22 P1.1–P2.2) — the border pass, and its black slab cut over with oath-script in hundreds of hands: **every traveller reads their own worst debt aloud before entering** (not a punishment, not a fee: the realm's courtesy), kept by **Drog**, who reads *his* debt back in exchange. **Morgatha's reading pays Ch. 3 P4.2**: *Tarkhan, my brother … his oath went with him. Unkept. Unfinished. It's mine now. I carry it.* The realm's answer is silence and then the **palm-out debt-acknowledgement**, all volume. Bramble watches the palms go up and is drawn looking at her own hands — **unexplained, and never explained**.
- **The menders** (Ch. 22 P3.1). The troll river-caste's gardens, fords and bridges: the realm's hands, all repair and no menace (canon: the subverted monster). **Glintfin's 03a line is spent verbatim** and **Nereia's flask — cracked since Ch. 16 — is mended** (thread, glue, a mender's clamp; the mend is *visible*), the team's first “the troll is on our side” beat, carried by a **fixed object and not a speech**. **Nereia has no dialogue in Ch. 22 at all**; her markings do the scene, and **the sea's first line stays Vol. 6's**.
- **The Ultravote** (Ch. 22 P5.1–P5.2, P9.1–P9.2) — the bowl, the ring of **clan stones** (one per clan, each mended and cut like the people who own it), the **throat-stone** at the centre, and **the speaker's silence**: Durgan the Unbent may only voice the assembled will of *every* clan, so with one stone empty **the realm may read and may not decide** (one line in Ch. 22; **his 03a line is held** for the chapter the clause passes). The realm spent a year holding **readings** because they are all it can do: hundreds of voices, out of time, each clan reading its grievance *to* the absent clan — the page drawn as a field of forearms.
- **The carrying — the volume's crime, and its engine** (Ch. 22 P6.2). A blood-debt that cannot be collected from its debtor **comes home** (the code's *fifth holding*) to the dead man's nearest blood, where it may be held as **surety** until answered. The Reforgers' cell did not break one word of the code: **it read the code and used it** — a lawful ask may not be refused, so the debt was moved onto **the house of Tarkhan**, which now holds the realm's War-Chief. Viv's finding (P6.2): *“He didn't break your law. He read it, and then he used it, and you've no word in your whole grammar for a man who does that.”* **The Barrens has no word for fraud** — the hole the volume's clause exists to close.
- **The surety-hall, and the door with no lock** (Ch. 22 P6.1, P7.1, P11.2). **Ulrik the Unbroken is *housed*, not imprisoned**: a warm room, a fire, a chair made for him, a garden, a mended window, a goat — and **a bolt leaning inside the doorframe, in reach, dusty**, because nobody has needed it in a year. He is huge, kind, unbruised and **mender-mended** (a repaired catalogue of a body). His refusal is the volume's question, and the chapter lets him *win* it: *“I'd rather be held than be the reason the realm's law was a lie.”* **Morgatha does not answer that in Ch. 22** — she answers with procedure.

The pilot's war-song count returns as the frame-corner device — **one… two… three… four** — and Vol. 5's rule, stated once: **when it stops, that is the panel** (Ch. 4 P8.1). **One stop in Ch. 22 (P7.2, at her father's argument — no dialogue, no Titanvein, full width)**; the count resumes as *sound* on P9.2 and is the **card's own corner** with a new, **empty rest-bar** under it. **Titanvein (`#d1495b`) is reserved and does not appear** — canon bars it before the Ultravote's own chapter, and the Ultravote's own chapter is **Ch. 24, where it is spent once (P4.2)**.

The oath-keeper is the volume's **antagonist-by-law and its future**: she hides nothing, hands over the debt-horn the moment she is asked, refuses to let anyone *“stand in my yard and work on it”*, brings her house's stone back into the circle in a realm that thought it never would, and will **vote against the captain with her face up**. **She is the one who reads the new clause aloud when it passes.**

- **The maze: a building that is the realm's document** (Ch. 23 P2–P5). The Barrens' law has no paper, so a disputed *word* is tested in the **maze** — a labyrinth cut into red rock whose walls carry the **entire code in cut script**, clause after clause, in hands that change every few metres, worn smooth at hand height by ten thousand walkers, and lit by an ordinary lamplighter on a shift. The walls **contradict** each other at the turns (third holding against fifth, debt against guest-right), so a walker must *choose* and may walk back — which is why nobody in the queue looks ashamed. **The centre chamber is the one room with bare walls**, because the centre is where somebody has to *say* it. The rule, cut over the gate: **everybody loses; the loss is carved; the maze goes to the loser** — a queue of people who have come to lose, and the realm's whole philosophy in one sentence (**Horn**: *“Everybody loses. That's the record. You want to lose somewhere that counts, you queue here.”*).
- **Viv's three terms, cut into a gatepost in front of the queue** (Ch. 23 P3.2) — the realm's first procedural amendment in a hundred years: **(1)** the walker names what was lost, **in words**, read where the walker says; **(2)** the cut goes in at the **declaration**, not the decision; **(3)** **the record reads both ways** — a wall of losses is a record of walkers, not a wall of shame. **The queue applauds, lightly**, because it means *their* loss will be read out next time.
- **The walk, the duel, and the yield** (Ch. 23 P4–P5). Morgatha walks the maze with the clause, reading the walls out loud, and **loses**: at the centre, **Tauron** fights her by making her choose between the fight and the words — she takes a winning exchange on purpose, her mouth stops moving for one beat, and **the clause falls out of the room**. He stops, stands up, and **says the clause back to her perfectly** (he spent four corridors *listening*), then declares **his own loss** because the holder of a thing answers for it — and **his 03a line is spent verbatim as the yield**: *“You lost. Which means the maze is yours. Walk it. It'll teach you more than any map.”* The count **stops** once (P5.1, full width, **no Titanvein**) — Ch. 4 P8.1's rule, restated as Volume 5's. **Maze** (the labyrinth-keeper) gives the map; **Horn** (the horn-carver) cuts the record.

The clause goes into the **debt-horn's duel-record** — the realm's first *legal* entry, cut **as the walker says it**, with her **two holes** under it: (1) no word for **who** may invoke it (a feud can dress as a life); (2) no word for a life **freely given away**. Durgan's only movement is **one nod**. And at the gate at dusk, at the bottom of a wall of ten thousand losses, the realm has now **published a legal critique** of the clause its own captain wants passed — *in a realm with no paper*, on a horn and a slab.

## Season 2 · Volume 6 — *The First Song* (the sea-gate, the road, and the first door) — **COMPLETE (Ch. 30–37)** — the on-page state

> **The volume’s contract:** the sea’s law is sung, the sea answers, and the realm of readers is asked — by somebody who *cannot write* — to keep the sentence. Canon advancing through Ch. 30–32: the sea-gate and the tide-clock; the Shell and the chorus-parliament; **the first line given words once** (*nothing that is heard is lost*); the Deep-Listening post and the notch-board; the memory-reef and its un-grown ring; the realm’s collar, deputy rule and nine posts; **the flood session and the sea’s ear**; **the second line** (*then come in*) and **the five articles** of the sea’s instrument; and **the flood reading**: at the top of every water, at nine posts, a school and a camp, the realm reads something aloud.

- **The sea-gate, and a door a lawyer cannot argue with** (Ch. 30 P1–P2). A wet black arch in the tide-flats, clear at low water, drowned at high; a column of chiselled **tide-marks** up the left pier with the lowest nine worn smooth; three tide-guard with their shields **stacked** (because they are not needed); a dry-land bench for people waiting for water; and a **cut bell-rope** (unexplained forever). **The law:** *you sing for the door or the door's shut* — no tolls, no rank, no papers. Viv arrives with the realm's documents and discovers the chapter's whole engine: **a document is a song nobody is singing**, and the sea will not refuse it — it will not *hear* it. The party sings the Barrens' clause at a rock, badly and truly, and the door opens, and Marra states the volume's premise: **the sea does not require beauty, it requires truth — nobody can lie in tune.**
- **The Shell, and a court with an adjournment** (Ch. 30 P3). A chamber under the rock: dry ribbed sand at the floor, two roof-shafts, **shell-lamps** (living, tended by a child lamplighter, drawn and unnamed), **no chairs — a ring**, a reed-and-bone chair with one arm-rest worn by hands, and the **tide-clock**: chiselled marks at the quarters and a **reed float in a cut channel**, with a wet band above the line showing where the water has recently stood. **The session ends when the water says so, and nobody in the realm has ever called it rude** — Volume 6's suspense device (four low waters a day; every scene has a clock). The parliament is **voices, not a crowd**: **Tide, Current, Stream** (three parts, lettered as a stacked chord), **Coral** the scribe (records by **singing the thing back**; rubric, canon: *you may not enter anything you cannot sing back*), **Pearl** the judge (verdicts by **duet**; no gavel, none needed), **Shell** the herald (announces in song, naming visitors by tide and object). **The Thalarch Maelis never speaks and never sings** — a small old scar at the throat; her part is carried by **Song-Part**, who leans toward her like an ear; and **she votes by handing Song-Part her hand**.
- **The first line, spent** (Ch. 30 P9.1). Ch. 3's melody finally gets words, once, in the whole saga: ***nothing that is heard is lost*** — set in the sea's ring-script *and* in plain type. **The realm's crisis stated by procedure failing**: a small kelp-farm verdict cannot close because the first bar is anchored to the first line and the first line has not been heard in a year (“we are not dying; we are **quiet**”). **The ask** (Maelis crosses the ring and hands her hand to Song-Part) is for Nereia to sing it home; **her refusal is arithmetic, not feeling**: sing it in the Shell and the reef takes it and the surface goes deaf forever. **The answer is the Barrens' procedure arriving at the bottom of the sea** — Kharza, writing in wet sand: *don't move the line; post a keeper: two voices, one office, one wet, one dry* — and the sea's motion, entered by duet: the first line stays in the water, a **keeper-Above** is entered in the reef-roll, the first bar may be answered from the surface, and the sea's reply to any reading so made is **sung by the full chorus**.
- **The reef, and the ring that did not grow** (Ch. 30 P6). Acres of coral in concentric rings, **one ring a year**, with a tight dark knot at the centre, tended by krakenkin whose tentacles move over the rings like a reader's fingers; this year's outermost lip is **thin, low and level**, because the realm could not bind. **Mnemothe's line, spent** (*I remember the first song. I remember it stopping … a memory with no reason*), and the reef **grows one ring at the ninth mark** while the chorus holds the note.
- **The Deep-Listening post, and the answer nobody asked for** (Ch. 30 P7). One stone seat, one mast, one long **shell trumpet pointed down**, and a **notch-board** with three hundred and sixty-one notches and no gap. **Whalesong's line, spent verbatim** (*the Leviathan's silent; I'm still listening; that's not hope, that's manners*). Then the hook: **the deep answers — drawn as lettering without a voice, in the page's margin, exactly as Vaelthorn is drawn, and heard by nobody in the party: “That was the first line being *sung back* at us, in a key we don't have.”** The notch he cuts that day is left **uncut**, and the chapter ends on him going back to the shell. **The Leviathan is never drawn, voiced, or named as answering.**
- **Read it; we'll sing it back** (Ch. 30 P10). At the next low water the Barrens' clause is **read aloud** in the Shell and the chorus (and, uncovered for the first time, the **tide-guard**) **sings it back**: *a person may come and ask; the asking may not be refused* → ***a voice comes to the door; the door answers; a true thing is not turned away*** — the sea's roll of notches going from eight to nine, and Pearl's verdict on the two doctrines: *your realm's rule is a reading may not be refused; ours is a thing sung true cannot be unsung … we are going to read at every door we own and sing back everything we hear.* Viv's own summary, which Volume 6 will spend itself arguing with: *the sea's door wanted somebody to be **heard**.*
- **The shore, and the four small things** (Ch. 30 P11). Fenn draws the sea as **one line** with the legend entry *heard* and a deliberate **blank out at sea**; Bramble wrings out her mask's tag and **still cannot read it** (the saga's withheld name, played as a joke on top of a withheld thing); Azrael's line admits he is glad about a door he cannot lie to; Sprocket underlines his **fourth entry**. - **The card, and the count's restart** (Ch. 30 P12). Read daily; it binds nobody; it may not be refused.*** — and the company's clerk copies the ruling into his own book, because a company that loses a case wants the ruling. **The company is not defeated**; its board is still standing at the frame's edge on P12.

Canon, stated once in the margin of P7.1 and never explained again: **the count now counts readings, not crossings** — one at crossing nine, two at crossing eight, three at Tarkhan's stone, four/five/seven/eight across the week's montage, **six** overleaf on a verge, and **nine** at the mark. **Two refusals of the reading itself** are filed as refusals, with reasons, and the reader **walks twenty paces and reads to the road**, which is nobody's yard and everybody's: *a refusal of a reading is not a refusal of the realm … the count is readings; refusals are not failures of the reading, they are the reading working.*

Crossing nine at the year's turn: Kha reading to eleven people and one dog, the well standing with three chalk-marks and a fourth fresh, the company's counter still twenty paces down the road with its queue of two — and the realm's newest cut on the stone, at a standing reader's hand: ***here is read the welcome. it opens nothing.*** The **twelfth hide card** (Volume 6's fourth) carries its own movement in two parts: the punched hole from Ch. 32's card now has the four words cut **around** it, worked clean through so both sides of the leather agree — and beside it, in ash, small and patient, **an ear.** Corner: **nine — and a rest.** Caption: ***Here read the welcome.***
- **The first suit under the second article** (Ch. 34 P1–P2). Same chamber, three new things: a **borrowed bench** on the dry side with the company's clerk and a register-officer standing **behind** it (nobody sits but the Thalarch), the party along the wall on camp stools in **Ch. 3's chorus composition rotated ninety degrees**, and **Marginalia's plate tied to a tide-guard's rope with the cart's cord, dry, out of respect** — canon: the plate appears on every wide panel of the Shell from here. - **The company's case, and Viv's answer** (Ch. 34 P3–P4). The clerk's figures, drawn as the ledger's own ruled columns: **one thousand and sixty askings placed; eleven thousand six hundred and sixty feathers taken; askings down to two hundred and nine against six hundred in the same season last year.** Viv's answer is an advocate's inventory, made standing on the dry sand with four objects laid in a line (the horn's copy, the week's slat-slip, the punched card, a blank sheet): **who was refused** (two refusals in a season, both filed with reasons, both given the reading twenty paces on, on the verge), **who was charged** (the company charges four feathers at its posts; the realm charges nothing and answered a price with a *queue*), **who was cheated** (*nobody sold these words* — the deep gave the second line at the flood, in the water, to nine persons, unasked and unpriced, and asked the realm to keep it in **writing**, because the sea cannot write). - **The word in the charter** (Ch. 34 P5). **Rule 6**: one silent panel, nobody watching — **Roderick** alone in the antechamber, coat off, finger travelling the charter's lines, **his thumb coming down flat on the single word *carriage***, his face unchanged; then the charter closed on its cord, set **square to the door**, and him gone. - **The release: the ledger, read and released** (Ch. 34 P7–P8). - **What a realm pays for** (Ch. 34 P10). - **Bramble's feather** (Ch. 34 P11.1). - **The question that has no price** (Ch. 34 P11.2, P12). - **A shift and a voice** (Ch. 35 P3). - **The stand-off, and carriage entered** (Ch. 35 P3.2, P4). - **The refusal, at the roll** (Ch. 35 P7.1). - **The ruling: a voice present by refusal is present** (Ch. 35 P7.2). Pearl gives the answer in one nod and then in plain words, and the scribe cuts it exactly: ***a voice present by refusal is present; a no, entered in order before the oath, stands in the count as a voice; the Chorus-True is a thousand present, and presence includes the ones who are here to say no.*** And beneath it, in the scribe's small citation hand, **the first sea-law ever learned from dry land**: *adopted from: the realm of the Barrens, the amended gatepost of the walker's maze, Ch. 23 — the record reads both ways; and the stone's own words: the no stays.* Pearl's own gloss, which the art team must letter: *I'd have it written down that what it learned was not mercy. **It was arithmetic.*** Nereia then reads the new line **at the water in both scripts, seam showing**, her markings in the wide slow rings of listening.

- **The card** (Ch. 35 P12). The **fourteenth hide card** (Volume 6's sixth), and its movement is the whole chapter: **a tally in columns of fives cut into the hide, ten columns' worth — and the last column carries nine cuts and no tenth**, the thousandth stroke **drawn across the nine in ash**, applied and not carved: a no, entered in order, standing in the count. - **The ask, and the crown that witnesses** (Ch. 36 P1). The Tidesong Crown is drawn at last, and Ch. 36 rules it forever: **a low circlet of four hundred years of coral and drift-bone**, worn at one side, polished by hands, one hairline crack mended with a coral graft, **no gems, no light, no glow** — *a witness's chair, not a throne.* It asks the realm its one sentence, lettered small with no tail on the ellipsis, mirroring the Everbloom's ask in Ch. 12: ***…Give me your part.*** Pearl's statement of the ritual's law: *the crown is not a source … it has sat at the front of this reef for four hundred years and watched, and what it is asking is to be able to say it watched the realm choose.* **The first attempt fails** — four courses true, two out of register, three frays out — and Coral's entry diagnoses it as a **habit**, not a refusal: *every one of us learned our part alone, because singing it where somebody could hear was how you got laughed at … the reef is not disappointed. **The reef is inexperienced.***
- **The one page, and the legal basis of a song** (Ch. 36 P2). Viv and Azrael draft the **six-line contract** on a rock with wet stone and the one sheet of paper in the realm, and Viv's rule is the chapter's legal spine: *the parts are theirs — the realm may sing them and may not keep them, and may not sell them, and may not give them away*, because *a realm that improvises a ritual in public is a realm that is going to invent a **debt** by accident.* And Bramble's question — *if she says no and the oath can't have a no in it, and she's one of the thousand, then what's the count supposed to say?* — is the chapter's hinge, with Azrael's answer: *the no's not a hole in the page. - **The dry side, and the children's relay** (Ch. 36 P3). The company closes its eleven stations for the three days **at full pay**, the stand-off book entered in the clerk's own hand (*all stations closed at the sea's call; fifty-nine singers released to their realm; wages paid for the days as worked; no charge carried to any asker; askings held in order*), and **thirty-six askings wait in a basket, in order** — *somebody's brother is third in that basket.* On the shore road, the **fence rail of Ch. 35 becomes the mechanism of the third low water**: an old merfolk teaches four notes to the rail's children, the children teach other children, and the notes are handed along the rail like a bucket — canon: **the relay is why the oath works at all.** Kharza files the day at the crossings' register and asks the sea, in writing, what the fourth line does to a voice that has already given its part away.
- **The no gets a note** (Ch. 36 P4). The second attempt gets **ten courses true** and dies at the eleventh — the gap drawn as the same *gap-in-a-wave* the reader learned in Ch. 35 — and Pearl puts the question to the thousand: *is an oath for a thousand voices agreeing … or is an oath a thing a realm does with its whole self, including the part of it that is not going to say yes?* Then the second law the deep learns from dry land in two chapters, cut into the roll with the citation beneath it: ***a voice present by refusal is present, and a no is a part: it is sung first, at the water, by the voice that carries it, and every voice that follows sings with it and never over it; an oath that cannot be sung with a no in it is an oath sung over somebody, and this realm does not swear over people*** — cited to the Barrens' gatepost and second register (*the record reads both ways*). Then **the Chorus-True**: not a contract and not paperwork — **consent** — sung by a thousand, with **Nereia as the first voice**, and the page's lettering in three registers: the outer ring ***we give our parts***, the middle rings ***the parts are ours; the singing is the realm's; the crown is a witness; the year was what it was***, and at the centre, small, in the flat held note, one voice: ***…no.*** No balloons, no captions, no SFX, and **the corner deliberately empty** — the count returns overleaf.

The old ones **drop fifty-year solos mid-panel** and open their mouths on the chord instead; the fifty-nine company singers set **thirty-six asker's slips face down on the coral** and sing their own; the children of the shore sing the four notes from the outer rim, their notation **fraying in air** because they are half out of the water; and in the eleventh course a boy **loses his line**, is carried by two unnamed women *without a look*, and comes back **a quarter-tone out and singing anyway** — entered by Coral exactly as written: *one part carried by two voices; one part given a quarter-tone out and entered as given; the count holds.* **And Song-Part sings the Thalarch's part in the open for the first time in the history of the office**, with Maelis's consent given as it has always been given — **her hand, flat, once**, at the same size as Ch. 34's four presses — and canon holds absolutely: **no healing, no restored voice, a voiceless ruler sung *for*.**

- **What binds and what doesn't** (Ch. 36 P10). - **Nereia's arc, paid** (Ch. 37 P3.2, P8.2). The penalties are read one last time as **uncollectable** (*no name to charge; the family is not the man*), and the department proposes **one further reading** and the disposition; on the wall, in the same frame, the Contract, the void-clause, the white chalk-line, the dictionary tablet with the word ***kept*** cut into it, the collar-roll, the bowl, and the **eighth chair with its lamp** being trimmed by a clerk at the same hour it has been trimmed every day since Ch. 38. At noon the calling is read at the gate for the **fifty-second time**, and a party in road clothes is standing on the north-east road in it.
- **The party stands at the rail, and the calling is read to his face** (Ch. 45 P2–P3.2). The realm's oldest political wound and its newest arrival share one room: the party enters **inside the line and outside the table** (*where a party stands while a realm reads him something*), is asked to swear nothing, sign nothing and touch nothing, and hears the calling read whole **twice** — once at the gate-clerk's desk by the desk's own rule (*a desk reads what is handed to it*), once at the chamber's reader's place by **Pyrethra**, with the reader's stool beside her still empty. - **What was kept on his name** (Ch. 45 P4.1–P4.2). The trial's last page is arithmetic, read out: **61,204 voices**; every promise in the realm read into the record; **4,206 promises made on the cut-out name and not one of them called in**; and **406 promises dead with the eleven names burned in two hundred years**, read out with the rest *because this realm reads its whole page*. Viv's finding is two sentences — **a man is not a promise, and a promise is not a man** — and then the volume gives its antagonist his one clean speech, undefended and unrefuted: *a promise is a hand on somebody's shoulder for the rest of their life… they did it because your realm raises people like that. I came to see the thing that raises them.* Morgatha gives the outside eye: *four thousand two hundred and six promises kept by people who were never going to be paid, and a man who thinks he's the one being held — my code would call that pair of facts an execution; his religion calls it an argument.*
- **The two dispositions, and the instrument** (Ch. 45 P5–P6.2). The realm rules in its own genre, a narrowing: **not purged** — *a verdict that takes a party is a hole, and this realm has made one hole in four thousand years and keeps it on the wall for a reason* — **not forgiven** (forgiveness is a judgment and a judgment is a reading, and he has asked for nothing), and **re-contracted**, which is what this realm has always done with a breaker: the party's Oathfire is entered as **an instrument of the realm's law** — *it has always burned the name off a promise that was broken; henceforward it burns on the side it was earned on… the burning shall be his own* — the **chain**, which travels with him, and **the realm takes no promise from him**: no promise asked, none entered, and the line under the disposition **left deliberately empty** on Viv's motion, *because the moment we take a promise off that man, we've made him a party to something, and this realm has never once bought its safety with a signature.* Grimm supplies the instrument's own line (*it doesn't only burn what breaks; it listens to what holds — same fire, other side: turn it*), Lyra makes the ruling say its own name, and the gold ruling **hangs over the table and not over the party** — the gap drawn as the chapter's most important measurement. Azrael asks him the volume's question and gets the volume's answer: *what does the fire burn? — the name. it always burned the name. I never minded the name. I minded the shoulder.*
- **The keep ended: the first door of the charter opened** (Ch. 45 P7–P8.2). At dusk on the gate's own road the keeper's office opens the **charge-charter**'s first lawful door: the call was the calling, the calling was answered at the fifty-second reading, and the roll's one empty column is filled at last — **released on: the answering of the call · the seven are free of the charter · fed by the springs, at dusk, per the practice · charges: closed.** Nobody makes a ceremony; **Emberlyn** counts them once with her thumb and rules that the release changes **nothing** about supper (*the pot's on at dusk, the barrow comes down at the sixth hour, the old one's hip wants the bowl warm*), and when the party comes to the roadside to watch them go, **Bramble** gives the chapter the only sentence anybody says about it: *they're not going to come. — they go where they're read to. — you stopped reading to them eight years ago; the gate never did.* In the archive that night, **Cindral** shelves the file beside the party's Entry and leaves three instructions in the register: the **name-field stays cut**, the **bowl stays on its shelf**, the **burned page stays in its frame** — *a realm that closes a case by tidying its own hole is a realm that is going to need a new frame* — and **Nigravox**, on the reader's stool at the table's end with the day's book propped open, closes the office's arc in one line: *it is to read the day out loud, whatever the day was, to a room, whatever the room is, and to mean it. Today the day was a man answering. It read him in.*
- **The west writes back, and the realm keeps its hours** (Ch. 45 P9–P10.2). - **The day read out, and the volume's close** (Ch. 45 P11–P12.2). At midnight the gate-reader reads the first day of the tenth month into the dark in full — the field read and reserved, the second book's entries standing, the disposition, the chain, the keep ended, the west's letter entered and not filed, the archive's instructions — and closes on ***the second day's business is on the frame. The gate's open at dawn. The desk reads what is handed to it.*** The Nine get the volume's last quiet page over the market, and the volume's last spoken line is Kaelen's, half-asleep on the bench with his boots up on the box: *he can't make a promise he doesn't keep now — so he won't make any. that's the sentence; nobody had to read it to him.* First light on the second day: the queue, the children with slates, the springs' barrow, seven hounds at the roadside with nobody's name on them, and **the party standing in the queue** — no balloon, no caption, nobody's attention — and the desk's rule on the desk's own face (*a desk reads what is handed to it*) and one ordinary word to close the volume: ***Next.*** Card **#40**: ***Here keep it.*** — the volume's eighth and last hide card, the clause bent into a single closed link through Ch. 41's aperture with one gold stroke on the inner rim, the **seventeenth notch left un-rubbed** with the word *answered* under it, Ch. 44's three-ways row intact at the foot, three hands in the margin, and the volume's summing-line: ***the case: closed by the reading. — the pack: free. — the chain: hung. — nothing was tidied.***
