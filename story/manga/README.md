## What each chapter file contains

1. **Header** — cast (with first-appearance flags), locations, the arc beat, the motifs advanced.
2. **Page-by-page panel script** — `P#.#` panels: the **Art** line (what the artist draws, with the art team's key details flagged) and the **dialogue/caption/SFX** lines (what the letterer types).
3. **Production notes** — art & lettering rules: the Unraveling's visual grammar, motif tracking, re-read details, and *what not to do* (protected beats).

## Pilot canon quick-reference (what is already locked on the page)

- **The Unraveling's visual grammar** (Ch. 2, established; Ch. 6 extends): full color → washed → line-art → white (erasure). A withered thing is not dead — it is a drawing someone stopped working on.
- **The scar's cost** (tracked, no exposition): Ch. 1 P2.2 white thread appears → Ch. 2 P7.2–P7.3 threads spread → Ch. 3 P8.3 visible, coat open → Ch. 3 P11.3 longer → Ch. 5 crosses the heart-line → Ch. 6 P10.2 the scar goes *white* once (the only time in the pilot). Rule: *sigils spend*.
- **The Name-Return** (Kaelen's signature Chord, canon 04): say a person's name back, aloud, *with the memory that goes with it*. Names are witnesses, not cures. Die with your name and the gray cannot take you (Thornring, Ch. 5). From Ch. 7: the realm supplies the memory, Kaelen supplies the name — a witness circle, not a solo.
- **The Mosaic Nine** (all named in Vaelis's record, Ch. 6): Kaelen, Lyra, Grimm, Morgatha, Nereia, Azrael, Viv, Bramble, Fenn — plus Roderick (the captain's seat) and Marginalia (the cart's plate). The two empty chairs stay empty until Ch. 6.
- **The First Dawn** (Ch. 6 P5–7): the named Chord of the S1 finale — Everbloom (Lyra) + Hundredfold (Kaelen) — "a grove re-grown, *and* a human's memory of it kept." Its hue is a *new* color (neither Sigil's — "rain on new leaves"; do not reuse it for anything else). The Chord rule (spine #5): two users who have *chosen* each other — Ch. 2 P8.4 (Lyra's new ring, for a stranger) + Ch. 3 P9.3 (the private corner) are the choosing; protect those two panels.
- **Lyra refuses the Crown-Touch** (Ch. 6 P7; canon 03: "refused for most of S2"). The pilot's Re-Oath is carried by the grove's vote + the Chord + the Name-Return, *not* by her crown. The tree will ask again; she keeps not-knowing.
- **Orvane is good** (Ch. 4–5): the reveal is a tragedy, not a twist; the realm defeats him with its *own law* (the grove-council vote, every branch); he tends the garden (the cruel rhyme with the saga's final image, planted). ## Season 2 · Volume 5 — *The One Throat* (the Barrens) — the on-page state

- **The realm's law has no text** (Ch. 22 P3.2, P8.1, P10.2). Viv asks for the code's document and is shown a **forearm**: the Barrens keeps its statute in **oath-script cut into arms** and in **memory, rehearsed in rooms**, and its record is a **horn** (the debt-horn, carved). Amendments are not passed in a chamber — **a clause is not law until the clans that vote for it cut it into themselves**: *in my realm a clause costs a vote; here it costs skin* (Viv, P10.2). The art team's device for the realm's *sound*: **a field of forearms** read from behind and above (invented P9.1).
- **The debt-line** (Ch. 22 P1.1–P2.2) — the border pass, and its black slab cut over with oath-script in hundreds of hands: **every traveller reads their own worst debt aloud before entering** (not a punishment, not a fee: the realm's courtesy), kept by **Drog**, who reads *his* debt back in exchange. **Morgatha's reading pays Ch. 3 P4.2**: *Tarkhan, my brother … his oath went with him. Unkept. Unfinished. It's mine now. I carry it.* The realm's answer is silence and then the **palm-out debt-acknowledgement**, all volume. Bramble watches the palms go up and is drawn looking at her own hands — **unexplained, and never explained**.
- **The menders** (Ch. 22 P3.1). The troll river-caste's gardens, fords and bridges: the realm's hands, all repair and no menace (canon: the subverted monster). **Glintfin's 03a line is spent verbatim** and **Nereia's flask — cracked since Ch. 16 — is mended** (thread, glue, a mender's clamp; the mend is *visible*), the team's first “the troll is on our side” beat, carried by a **fixed object and not a speech**. **Nereia has no dialogue in Ch. 22 at all**; her markings do the scene, and **the sea's first line stays Vol. 6's**.
- **The Ultravote** (Ch. 22 P5.1–P5.2, P9.1–P9.2) — the bowl, the ring of **clan stones** (one per clan, each mended and cut like the people who own it), the **throat-stone** at the centre, and **the speaker's silence**: Durgan the Unbent may only voice the assembled will of *every* clan, so with one stone empty **the realm may read and may not decide** (one line in Ch. 22; **his 03a line is held** for the chapter the clause passes). The realm spent a year holding **readings** because they are all it can do: hundreds of voices, out of time, each clan reading its grievance *to* the absent clan — the page drawn as a field of forearms.
- **The carrying — the volume's crime, and its engine** (Ch. 22 P6.2). A blood-debt that cannot be collected from its debtor **comes home** (the code's *fifth holding*) to the dead man's nearest blood, where it may be held as **surety** until answered. The Reforgers' cell did not break one word of the code: **it read the code and used it** — a lawful ask may not be refused, so the debt was moved onto **the house of Tarkhan**, which now holds the realm's War-Chief. Viv's finding (P6.2): *“He didn't break your law. He read it, and then he used it, and you've no word in your whole grammar for a man who does that.”* **The Barrens has no word for fraud** — the hole the volume's clause exists to close.
- **The surety-hall, and the door with no lock** (Ch. 22 P6.1, P7.1, P11.2). **Ulrik the Unbroken is *housed*, not imprisoned**: a warm room, a fire, a chair made for him, a garden, a mended window, a goat — and **a bolt leaning inside the doorframe, in reach, dusty**, because nobody has needed it in a year. He is huge, kind, unbruised and **mender-mended** (a repaired catalogue of a body). His refusal is the volume's question, and the chapter lets him *win* it: *“I'd rather be held than be the reason the realm's law was a lie.”* **Morgatha does not answer that in Ch. 22** — she answers with procedure.
- **The count is the volume's clock, and it runs forward** (Ch. 22 P1.2, P7.2, P9.2, P12.1–P12.2). The pilot's war-song count returns as the frame-corner device — **one… two… three… four** — and Vol. 5's rule, stated once: **when it stops, that is the panel** (Ch. 4 P8.1). **One stop in Ch. 22 (P7.2, at her father's argument — no dialogue, no Titanvein, full width)**; the count resumes as *sound* on P9.2 and is the **card's own corner** with a new, **empty rest-bar** under it. **Titanvein (`#d1495b`) is reserved and does not appear** — canon bars it before the Ultravote's own chapter, and the Ultravote's own chapter is **Ch. 24, where it is spent once (P4.2)**.
- **Khura, the house of Tarkhan, and the horn** (Ch. 22 P4.2, P6.2, P9.2, P11.2; paid Ch. 24). The oath-keeper is the volume's **antagonist-by-law and its future**: she hides nothing, hands over the debt-horn the moment she is asked, refuses to let anyone *“stand in my yard and work on it”*, brings her house's stone back into the circle in a realm that thought it never would, and will **vote against the captain with her face up**. **She is the one who reads the new clause aloud when it passes.**
- **The clause, drafted and not yet law** (Ch. 22 P10.1–P10.2, P12.1). At the camp, with no pen and no paper, Viv and Morgatha draft it — ***the oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code*** — and Viv's warning is the price of the volume: it will not cost a vote here, **it will cost skin**, and the captain will have to stand in front of nine clans and offer her **arm**. The chapter's last drawn frame is **Morgatha's forearm on the throat-stone, with a clean patch of skin where the clause will go** — Vol. 4 ended with a blank line in a book; Vol. 5 opens with a blank **arm**.
- **The card's register changes** (Ch. 22 P12.2): the record's cards are **no longer stone — they are hide, cut with a knife and rubbed with ash**, pegged flat at the corners; the cut grammar is the realm's oath-script; the accumulated marks continue (Wren's down-stroke, Fenn's circle-with-a-tail, the horizontal, Bark's ring, the six-strand braid, the charcoal laughing man signed *K.*, the pressed thumb-print, the flame-clan's knot) — and Volume 5 adds its own: **one word of oath-script scratched at the card's lower edge, legible as writing and never translated.** Cards: *Here count the named* (Ch. 22) — the seventeenth card, the first of Volume 5.
- **Fenn's ledger changes** (Ch. 22 P4.1): with the goblin courier network in front of him and nothing in its hands, he stops writing *no letters* and starts **logging days** — the first honest movement on the twins' thread since Ch. 16.

- **The maze: a building that is the realm's document** (Ch. 23 P2–P5). The Barrens' law has no paper, so a disputed *word* is tested in the **maze** — a labyrinth cut into red rock whose walls carry the **entire code in cut script**, clause after clause, in hands that change every few metres, worn smooth at hand height by ten thousand walkers, and lit by an ordinary lamplighter on a shift. The walls **contradict** each other at the turns (third holding against fifth, debt against guest-right), so a walker must *choose* and may walk back — which is why nobody in the queue looks ashamed. **The centre chamber is the one room with bare walls**, because the centre is where somebody has to *say* it. The rule, cut over the gate: **everybody loses; the loss is carved; the maze goes to the loser** — a queue of people who have come to lose, and the realm's whole philosophy in one sentence (**Horn**: *“Everybody loses. That's the record. You want to lose somewhere that counts, you queue here.”*).
- **Viv's three terms, cut into a gatepost in front of the queue** (Ch. 23 P3.2) — the realm's first procedural amendment in a hundred years: **(1)** the walker names what was lost, **in words**, read where the walker says; **(2)** the cut goes in at the **declaration**, not the decision; **(3)** **the record reads both ways** — a wall of losses is a record of walkers, not a wall of shame. **The queue applauds, lightly**, because it means *their* loss will be read out next time.

Morgatha walks the maze with the clause, reading the walls out loud, and **loses**: at the centre, **Tauron** fights her by making her choose between the fight and the words — she takes a winning exchange on purpose, her mouth stops moving for one beat, and **the clause falls out of the room**. Not the killing (that was the fourth cell's) but the **use**: *the debt that came home to the Barrens' chair came home because he asked for it — he had no duty to, and he needed a lever, and the realm was the only realm in the world that could make a law in a week.* The registrar's own confession (*he wrote the ask, I typed it, I've kept it nine years*) is the chapter's honesty, and the founder's justification is lettered **as a list**, because that is how he thinks: nine realms · four hundred years of treaties · a wound a mile wide growing a finger a decade · no realm moves while its own house is in order · **the Barrens has the Ultravote, a procedure that can invent law in a week** · the wound is nobody's clan grievance → *the hole had to be opened inside the realm's own honour.* His canon sentence: *“I did not have your brother killed … **I used him.**”* — no apology for the lever, **one apology, seven words, for the chair**, and the man would do it again.
- **The fourth stop, and the volume's fourth and last** (Ch. 28 P4.1). Full page, one panel, no dialogue, no count, no Titanvein, no bloom: the hearing room — a cold kettle, three sheets of paper, four chairs, the gate-clerk forgotten in the doorway — and **a band of grey across the top of the frame**, because *the wound is in the window and not in the room.* Morgatha does not cry and does not reach. Then (P4.2) her terms: **give me the fourth cell**, on paper, every road and every name, nine years of it — *and I'll take your reading … there's no forgiveness in this room, and I've not come for any.* The founder's answer is the volume's method stated by its antagonist: **they read** — they use the same rules the realm does, and they've been nine years ahead of him because *he* keeps trying to stop them with rules and *they* keep arriving with them.
- **Oros's line, spent at last — and the teacher stays** (Ch. 28 P5). The old sage arrives at the gate on foot with a stick, a lamp and no appointment, and **the whole camp stops working**, because everyone knows the date, nine years ago, when he got to the founder's chair and turned round and went home without a word. 03a's line is delivered verbatim in a camp yard, with no music in the framing and no reaction shot for four panels: ***“I taught you to*** see *the wound. I never taught you to call the* patient *the* doctor.”* — plus eight words of his own, and then the map-room, where he reads **the survey book** and finds the entry the volume turns on: **year ninety-seven, day two hundred and ten: *it moved four fingers overnight. The maps are all wrong. I have not told the camp. — G.*** **Oros stays**: a chair, a lamp, the book, and eleven years of a student to argue with. His verdict on the newest law in the world: *“do not put a reading post on that shore. Put a* rule *on it: a reader who may say no.”*
- **The party's answer — *smaller*** (Ch. 28 P7–P8). Viv's dislike of a clean offer (*we'd be his witnesses in nine years*), and the five-clause document signed at the table with four chairs: one reader and one clerk · **the clerk's office is the deed-register, first: no deed, no door** · ***the reader may refuse — a reading that cannot be refused is a door; a reader who may refuse is a wall*** · the post is paid by the realm, not the camp, and the survey's books are open for as long as it stands · not an alliance, and the realm's answer to the first cell's crimes stands unanswered until a circle hears it. **Kharza's objection** (*that's backwards from the gatepost*) and **Viv's correction** — *a reading may not be refused at a **door**; a clerk is not a door, a counter can be bought; the realm will never refuse a reading, it will refuse a **deed*** — is the volume's last piece of law. **Canon, from here: the Barrens has no seal — Morgatha presses the debt-horn's worn mouth into the wax**, so the realm's mark on its first treaty with anybody is **the impression of a horn.**
- **The last reading is in a yard, and it is not applauded** (Ch. 28 P9). There is no hall in a stockade at the edge of the world, so the reading happens between the gate and the ward: clerks with ledgers, samplers in grey-booted coats, the troll surgeon with his hands still in the wash-water, and four ward-patients **carried out in chairs because the old cyclops with the wrapped hands asked to hear it** — and at the end, the samplers pick up their boot-caps and stand, and **the clerks close their ledgers**, which nobody in that camp has ever done during a reading. Kharza's own addition, lettered as separate from the clause: *“you've all been standing on the edge of my realm's worst debt for a hundred years, and not one of you ever told us. …Now you've got a reader. Start with the book.”* Then the aftermath, off the page and by the realm's own procedure: the old cyclops has the clause **chiselled into his chart-boards** by the mender; two camp clerks ask Kharza whether the realm will read the survey's *older* ledgers too, and she **doesn't know and says so**, and they say *then we'll ask your clerk*; and the samplers, at the north corner at dusk, read **their own sample-log entries aloud** — awkward, private, for the first time in a hundred years. **Azrael's mirror line:** *“I broke one word, on a page, for a town … he's what I'd have been if I'd had the paperwork. It's not a comfort. It's a warning, and I don't know who it's for.”*
- **The fourth cell, on paper — and the captain's answer to it is drawn, not spoken** (Ch. 28 P10). Nine years, eleven dated sightings, a strip-map of the mine-road east of the pass in the layered-hide style, the founder's pencil margins (*no sample, no chain, no chart*), four confirmed members, two captured and lost to a Silk court — and **one recent report, three weeks old, and therefore *after* Ch. 23: four of them signed into the old-forges map-room as *pilgrims from the Rift*, asked for the holdings of the Barrens and the seam, and were given a reading and left.** Their pattern, canon from here: ***they take nothing anybody didn't hand them.*** Morgatha reads all nine years in one night, then **cuts the fourth cell's road into her own forearm**, below the clause, small and plain — a *note*, not a law — and leaves **one horn-sliver** on the table. No dialogue on the page; the only lettered text is the file tab: **FOURTH CELL — OPEN — ELEVEN SIGHTINGS — CUT OVER?**
- **What the party carries out, and the count** (Ch. 28 P11–P12). At the gate: the founder hands over **the whole survey** — one hundred years of soundings, every chart, every failure, and **the names of the four in the front**, because nobody in nine realms had ever asked who they were — and Viv gives him the realm's *sliver* of the horn's margin, the note and not the law. Morgatha's last judgement, with her back to him: *“You'll be read at, Founder. … it says* the lawful hand*, and it does not say* forgiven*, and that's the difference between my realm and yours: **mine keeps them both on the page.**” On the road up: **Seam-Market at the clerk's table by the first wayshrine with a queue**; Fenn drawing the camp and the survey line with the grey left as **a blank with a line round it**; Sprocket's column getting **its second entry** (*they are not sorry, and they will still be right about the wound — carry both*); Oros at the gate with a lamp behind them. The closing frame is the volume's first view of the Rift from the same angle four chapters on, with three things added: the camp, the rope-and-peg survey line stopping at the **second mark**, and **a new small building just outside the gate — one room, a lamp, a chair, a desk, a flag-pole with no flag**, and on its door, cut in the realm's register: ***THE READING-POST AT THE EDGE. CLERK FIRST: DEEDS BEFORE DOORS.*** The **chair is empty**, and who sits in it is Ch. 29's business. Count: **twenty-five … twenty-seven … twenty-eight … twenty-nine … thirty**, one stop. **Card 23** (the seventh hide card) cuts **a fourth short line below the law-lines on the card's left edge, unconnected, with nothing under it** — the exact shape of the road on the captain's forearm — and **no document anywhere ever connects them.** Caption: **Here meet the named.**
- **Rule 6, carried into the camp** (Ch. 28 P3.1): **Roderick's one silent panel** is at the hearing-room door, not entering and not speaking, holding one sheet out at arm's length — the fourth cell's last sighting report, whose witness-line carries a clerk's signature block reading **I. VANE** — and nobody in the frame looks at it and nobody in the chapter mentions it. (Ch. 27's founder-manuscript beat: a blank signature line on the camp's charter of incorporation, filled here by the founder's own hand.)
- **The realm's answer is a collar** (Ch. 29 P1–P3). The post's furniture arrives before its reader does; a **queue** forms outside a door with no one behind it; and the realm sends the office east with **Ripple** (03b, river-caste mender) carrying the horn's *copy* on his back — cut hide sewn in the realm's style, the newest entries in the record's hand, and a plate-type duplicate pinned beside it because nobody in the camp reads the cut hand. The entries (read aloud at the post's door): the post granted · **the reader is Kha, of the Ulrik clan, student of the school in the well, youngest of the code, eleven** · the office and its inward limit · the circuit of the west · the school. **Kharza's beat** is the coda's emotional centre: a reader who has read everybody else's law aloud having her own family read aloud by her own realm — *the newest word goes to the furthest door* (the realm's oldest rule about new law, so an eleven-year-old gets the edge and the test). **Canon, the collar:** outward (in the realm's register) **READER AT THE EDGE · OF THE FIRST CELL · THE BARRIER OF THE NINE**; inward, against the skin, smallest lettering in the chapter: **and she may refuse**; brass **buckle** on an outward hinge, cut to a child's neck by a mender the way a mender does anything — *nothing in this trade is meant to be permanent; a knot's a thing you can let out*. Nine of them, one per post, **a mender's thumb on every strap**. The realm's sentence: *an entry is a debt; an office is a coat.*
- **The clasp, and the smith** (Ch. 29 P3.1). The buckle is from Khazadüm's rim foundry, **plate nine hundred and eleven** — a class demonstration piece for nine thousand dwarves learning that a hinge has a direction — and it has been in the pocket of **Kaelen Voss's** too-big coat (his father's) since Ch. 21. He fits it himself (Fenn holding the lamp, Bramble holding the strap) and says the plainest line he has said in twenty chapters: *it's a **buckle** … buckles are meant to come off; that's the entire design.* **His one piece of work in Volume 5**, and the first thing he has ever said is his. **And the post's first act is a refusal** (P3.2): the barn lease, the neighbour's signature, and four people in a stockade independently agreeing that *the office is not installed and the office may refuse* — the post's first entry, in plate-type: *no reader present; deed held; the assistant's office may not open a door it cannot close.*
- **The rest — the volume's fourth and last stop** (Ch. 29 P4.1). Full page, one panel, no dialogue, no count, no Titanvein, no bloom: the stockade's north corner, a plank bench, a lamp on a hook, a nineteen-year-old with the collar in her hands, the camp going on behind her, the **grey** drawn as paper across the top — and in the corner where the count lives, **a single bar-rest and no number**. No reaction shot follows it on the page; the consequence is nine hundred miles away, in a dispatch written in a child's hand with a teacher's corrections in the margin.
- **The west, in waybills, and the war that gets renamed** (Ch. 29 P5–P6). **Quick** (03b) brings forty-one dated sheets: the crossings read, one refusal written, and — the news that matters — the company that bought a ford at Ch. 27 has begun **offering readings and charging for them** (and lost one argument to a herd of cattle). **Ulrik's dispatch** is read aloud in the war-chief's plain hand: *there was a war and I was good at it and the war's gone … a war-chief without a war is just a man with a coat and I've got four hundred of the other thing now.* The council's answer travelled east in the same bundle: ***the coat is entered as a* circuit** — nine crossings, one reader and one clerk each, deeds before doors, **arms stacked and counted at the first post and kept in the realm's own care** — with the realm's sentence under it: ***a road can't be held; only kept.*** **Morgatha's amendment is one word in the margin: *collar*.** The coda's price-law, entered: **a reading is not a product · a copy is the asker's work · a clerk's wage is paid by the asker's realm, not by the realm that taught them to read.** Fenn draws the road as **nine rings** — and leaves a deliberate **gap** between the sixth and seventh crossing that he cannot justify and does not erase.
- **The school's first term** (Ch. 29 P7). Forty benches in the well; four realms on them (the Barrens 19 · the Seams 11 · one Nocturne clerk sent by a regent's office · one Terranian stone-hauler's daughter who walked in off the road); Grimm's **hinge** from the rim foundry on the school's inner door (and **Grimm's letter** — three pages, one of them a drawing — the reader's first sight of him in eight chapters); the exam rule in the school's own frame: ***the exam is a door: find one that has never heard the clause, read it, and write down the refusal***; and the term's first filed results: **four refusals, on a wall, not in a drawer** (Taurus's correction in a schoolmaster's hand: *the realm's first honest list of doors that said no*). **Bramble's application** goes in on a sliver, in a changeling's hand, with no realm on the line — and comes back fastened to the school's door: ***enrolled: term three, when the road comes home.***
- **Fenn's rests, and the record-keeper's column** (Ch. 29 P8). Four deliberate blanks on a drawn map, each **bounded and empty** (paper), with the legend rewritten in Fenn's own hand — *surveyed · holding · disputed · **rest*** — because a road with a rest in it draws like a song. Sprocket's third entry: *Day eighty-one. The cartographer has invented a mark for the world being unfinished on purpose. …it works.* And his decision, said to Fenn's face and never revised: **the blank column is not a lapse, it is a rest, and he is not closing it** (eighty-one days, still no letters out of the Seams). **Azrael's coda line:** at the post's lamp, hands not touching the collar — *the whole trick of a broken oath is that it doesn't end; it stays open; you keep picking it up … that thing's got a buckle. Whoever thought of it was the first person in this story to make an oath you can put down on purpose.*
- **The horn's entries, and the open line** (Ch. 29 P9). Read aloud at the post's door with the whole camp listening and **Oros in a chair at the back** (one frame, no line): the post · the office and its inward limit · the circuit and the stacked arms · the school · and then the volume's last legal act: ***the fourth cell: entered* open. *Not a treaty, not a hunt, not a pardon: an open line, to be read aloud at each circle until it is read closed*** — and the margin-word ***the lawful hand*** read back and **left a note** (*a crime needs its own circle; the first circle of the Nine is held for it when the road comes home*). Morgatha's dictated two lines are the volume's closing law: the fourth cell will not be closed by her alone, and *nobody in this story gets to be finished by a courtesy.*
- **The rest, paid** (Ch. 29 P10–P11). The founder is **not in this chapter**: a coat on a nail, a scoop on a rack with eleven worn ones, a back at the far edge of four working frames, and **three words at the second mark** (*…Log it. Four fingers.*); his daughter walks past an open door with a box and does not go in, and nothing explains it. **Oros's page**: two chairs, a second lamp, and a margin in the survey book — *set this against the entry for year eleven, where a copy of this book was filed under* disputes *— O., of the old-forges*; his one line is a question the room does not answer (*where's the boy?*). Then the fire: the count ticks to **thirty-six**, and takes the volume's first **rest** — *a rest is not an ending; it's a place in the line where you stop on purpose so the next part's got somewhere to start.* The **chorus panel returns** (used only for the Nine) around a fire, one line each, with **Marginalia's plate of bread in the frame's corner** and the cart's line in the margin in tiny italics.

## Writing rules (for future chapters)

1. **Canon lock wins.** If a panel conflicts with the cast book (03/03a/03b/03c), the power system (04), or the canon spine (05 §I), the panel changes — not the bible. The quick-reference above is the on-page contract; the bibles are the source.
2. **Pilot lines are verbatim.** Every Tier-1 "pilot line" in 03-characters.md must land in the pilot, word for word (they're the franchise's advertising copy; the TCG and the anime reuse them).
3. **Costs are on the page, not in the dialogue.** The scar's white threads, Lyra's rings, Morgatha's count, Nereia's flask, the cart's plate — the team's resources are *drawn*, so the reader learns the rules by watching (re-reads pay).
4. **The chorus panel** (a ring of one-frame-per-voice, table-centered) is used *only* for the Nine. It is the team's visual identity.
5. **Vaelthorn is negative space.** The art draws *around* him. His eyes are the only ink. His first line is written on the page but *heard by no one*.
6. **Roderick gets exactly one silent panel per chapter** while he keeps the post (Ch. 8 onward). No dialogue; the captain's stillness is the line.
7. **New places named in a chapter get a line in the location docs** ([../../location/](../../location/)); new named people get a line in [../03b-ensemble.md](../03b-ensemble.md) or [../03c-roster.md](../03c-roster.md) before the chapter is called done.
