# AURELION · Volume 4 — Chapter 15: "The Contract Braid"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc D: "The Mountain-Ask" (the count of the twelve days — a realm that keeps its accounts in hair; a mine that has been swearing *by* the mountain for a century; a lost school of listeners with one apprentice and one slate; and a dwarf who signs, in front of his mother, to buy his realm twelve days he cannot promise to use)**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Grimm (**the signer — the count changes**), Kaelen, Lyra, Morgatha (**the count turns into *time***), Nereia (the flask — **four days of water**), Azrael (the wall), Viv (**the case — nine thousand counts**), Bramble, Fenn (the map, the days cut into the frame's edge), Marginalia, Sprocket. **The support** — **Roderick** (his one quiet panel: the edge of the anvil haul). **The stone realm** — **Maren Ashwood** (the ink wall, the sketchbook, the last blank frame), **Tilda Ashwood**, **Norga** (**the mine's braider — the knot**), **Pick** (the miner-young; what the crack *sounded* like), **Urka** (Torvik's son), **Skaal** (tags, stamps, and a tune), **Echo** (Khazri's echo-young — the slate with the same line on it), **Mountain-Speaker Khazri** (**silent; one frame, and he must not speak**), **Duelist Molka** (**the festival's book — the law is read *from* it, never *by* her in this chapter**), **Grand-Anvil Khazur** (the audit's own calendar), **Orik Khazur** (his house's anvil goes down the hill with the rest), **Warden Bastion** (at the pass, silent), **Stonehand Drek** (**the deal — honest, itemized, and not what it looks like**), **Torvik** (**silent — a man watching his own hand**), **Pebble** (**buttoned**), the **Haul** (nine hundred anvils, carried downhill at night, by their owners).
> **Locations / threads:** the forge terraces (the frost on the family door, and the street being *kind* about it) → the **Hall of Speakers** (the lost school of listeners) → the **Signing Hall** at the Cut's mouth (contract braids, the ledger crate, the braider's knot) → the anvil haul on the cart road → the Cut's galleries (the second hairline; the bore-heads) → the house at the rim (Maren's sketchbook) → the mine's water-lamps (Nereia's four days).
> **Arc beat:** The count of the twelve days begins, and the chapter keeps it the way the realm keeps everything — cut into the frame's edge, one tally a page. The realm now knows a Khazdûrin's hand takes heat out of the gate-stone, and it is *kind* about it in the way a place is kind about a funeral: the house that hasn't answered the street in a hundred years is being called unlucky, politely, in front of its son. The team goes looking for a dwarf who can hear and finds the **Hall of Speakers**: a room of chairs with nobody in them, a practice that has been silent since the mining began, and one apprentice — **Echo** — copying the same line onto a slate he cannot read, because it is the last thing his teacher ever said and nobody in the realm knows what the mountain had said that made him say it. The mine, meanwhile, is *moving*: **nine hundred anvils** come down the cart road at night, because the realm's law says the Anvil-Naming is held where the most anvils stand — and the anvils go where the coal is, and the coal is Drek's. The realm's court of account, the one place where every braid in Khazadûm is read out loud once a year, is being quietly relocated into a mine. And Drek offers the last Khazdûrin a deal that is itemized, honest, and worse than a lie: **twelve days of no cutting, in exchange for Grimm standing in the gallery** — because the hairline the mountain draws comes *around* him, and a man who cannot hear the mountain can still be used to *find* it. Grimm signs. Norga ties the contract braid in front of the realm and the mine's mother, and the count the whole chapter has been teaching the reader becomes **thirteen**. Viv finds the crime (nine thousand oaths sworn *by* something that never gave permission — the theft of a witness, triable only at the Naming) and the trap (the clause that keeps nine thousand dwarves from saying anything the mountain says, outside the Cut). Nothing is answered. A braid is owed. The court opens in nine days.
> **Motifs advanced:** the count (**days**, cut into the frame's edge; **braids** — twelve become thirteen, and the reader's clock breaks), the account (the realm's whole law as an *audit* — the Anvil-Naming is a court, not a festival), the empty (**a school of listeners with no listeners; a slate with one line on it; a mine that answers the mountain with machinery**), the theft of a witness (**the mine's oath — sworn *by* the mountain; the saga's first villainy that is entirely legal**), the door (a family's own door gone cold from the outside), the ink (a mortal's sketchbook — the archive, and the last blank frame), the flask (four days; the mountain drinks), the scar (steady; the lean, unremarked), the record (**the card: *Here owe the named*** — the count in the corner goes on going up), the road (the cart road, and a realm's hearts going down it). **Not in this chapter:** the stone-song; the Mountain-Ask; Bastion's sentence; Khazri's voice; Molka's voice; Grudka's voice; Torvik's voice; the geode; the golems; the *empty* under the Cut. **The mine is not defeated here. The ask is not made here.**

## Page 1 — *Days Eleven, Ten, Nine (the count, cut into the frame)*

### P1.1 — The street that is being kind (2/3 page)
**Art:** The forge terraces, morning. The art team's device for all of Volume 4, introduced here and to be used on every page of it: **the day count is cut into the frame's edge** — a dwarf's tally, chisel-struck into the panel's own border, one mark per day since the Nine came through the gate (this page: the marks read *eleven* in the first frame and *nine* in the last; the reader is given the count without a single character ever mentioning it). **The Khazdûrin forge's street door** now wears the frost the gate-stone showed: a **palm-print of white** on the iron, and — the detail that makes the page cruel — **candles**: somebody has left three, and a scrap of cloth, and a child's chalk drawing of a mountain with a face. Not vandalism. *Respect.* The street is working around it: two neighbors lowering their voices as they pass; a vendor's boy putting a coin down carefully so his hand does not touch the door.
**Market dwarf (to another, politely, quietly, and this is the whole realm's position on the Khazdûrins in one sentence):** "It's not the boy. It's the *house*. That house hasn't answered this street in a hundred years, and now the stone's *stopped answering it*. You tell me which way the arrow points."
**Maren (from the doorway, calm, with a wooden spoon — the mortal who married into the loudest family alive and has never once been impressed by it):** "Good morning, Orla. The arrow points *downhill*, like everything else in this town."
**Grimm (behind her, flat, watching the candles on his own door — and the reader should notice his hand go up and *start* at the braids and come down again without counting):** "…They brought the *good* candles."

### P1.2 — Three frames (bottom strip)
**Art:** Three small frames. **(1)** The frame-edge tally: **nine** — and it is cut into the *trough* of a mine-cart's rail in the foreground, which is the first sight of the thing the page has been hiding. **(2)** **Nereia**, at the shaft-head vent with the flask in both hands, markings at low tide (`#7fd1e0`), the steam off the neck visible in three thin lines; she is doing the arithmetic and not saying it. **(3)** **Pebble**, mid-climb on a stair cut for someone four times his height, **coat buttoned to the throat**, his clan at the bottom of the stairs waving goodbye as if he goes up this mountain every day — *and nobody in his clan has ever thought this worth mentioning* (the clan's mood: totally unbothered; one of them hands him a *sandwich* for the trip; the art team: this is the funniest panel in Volume 4 so far and it must be played completely straight).
**Marginalia (tiny italics):** *"The cart notes a grummpling ascending the mountain with his coat buttoned, and that his family has packed him lunch, and that the cart has decided, on the evidence, that this is *normal*."*

## Page 2 — *The Hall of Speakers (the chairs with nobody in them)*

### P2.1 — A school with one student (half page)
**Art:** The **Hall of Speakers** — a long, low room at the mouth of one of the old listening-galleries (the art team: the room is *acoustic architecture*; the ceiling is a shaped vault, the walls are pierced with tuned slots, and the whole room is built like the inside of an ear). One stone chair per speaker, carved with its holder's name, arranged in a shallow arc facing **a wall of raw bedrock** — not a dais, not a throne: a *wall*, the thing they listened to. Eight chairs. Seven empty. **Khazri** stands at the side (canon 03a: he went silent when the mining began) — a slate on his knee, chalk in hand, **nothing written on it**; he may be drawn and he must not speak and he must not be made pitiable. **Echo** (03b: Khazri's echo-young) sits on the floor at the bedrock wall with his ear flat against it, entirely unselfconscious, like a boy at a radio.
**Echo (flat, matter-of-fact, getting up — this is the exposition and it must sound like a boy describing the weather):** "Chairs are the speakers. When a chair's holder dies, the chair keeps the name and the hall keeps the *seat*. There's eight of us ever. Right now there's one, and it's him, and he *stands*." *(a beat; a jerk of the thumb at Khazri)* "The last thing he said in here was: *say that back*. I was six. Nobody knows what *that* was. He's not going to tell you — that's not stubborn, that's the *job*."
**Kaelen (quiet):** "…The job's to not say it."
**Echo (and the line that lands, said without any weight at all, because he has said it a thousand times):** "The job's to *not be wrong about it*. …You say it wrong once in this room and the realm builds a mine on the answer." *(he turns; the slate in his hand has one line of chalk on it, drawn over and over — a single unbroken stroke that could be a word in no alphabet)* "I keep the shape of it. That's what an apprentice is for, up here. Keeping the shape of a thing we can't read."

### P2.2 — The realm's law, and the thing that makes Viv's hands shake (half page)
**Art:** The Hall's outer bench, the team around Viv's folder — **Viv, standing, hatless, reading the festival's law out of Molka's book** (the *Anvil-Naming's book of account*: the art team: it is huge, iron-bound, and it *has* been kept perfectly — it is the one ledger in this realm, and it is the realm's court-record; **Molka's hand is on the cover and she does not speak a word in this chapter** — the rules are read *from* her book, by a lawyer, in front of her, and she listens). Viv's face does two things on this page: it *settles* (a lawyer finding the case) and then it goes *pale* (a lawyer finding the case).
**Viv (the em-dash, reading, flat, professional — the law, stated once):** "— *The Anvil-Naming is held where the most anvils stand.* *At it, every braid in the realm is read out.* *Debts are settled, cuts are witnessed, names are given, and the year's word is judged.* …It's not a festival, it's a **court**. It's their audit. Everything these people are is *read out loud* once a year in front of everybody, and the venue is decided by where the anvils are."
**Viv (next, turning a page, and her voice drops — this is the chapter's first horror):** "…Second thing. Old law, older than the mine. *No one may swear **by** the mountain.* Swear *to* it, swear *in front of* it, swear *on* your own braid — fine. Swear *by* it and you have taken a witness that never gave testimony. They have a name for the crime. It's four words long and it's the ugliest phrase in this realm's language."
**Viv (quiet, closing the book with two hands, and this is the sentence that arms the rest of Volume 4):** "**The theft of a witness.** …They call it *by the mountain*, nine thousand times a year, in a hole, and the realm has never once prosecuted it."
**Lyra (quiet, from the doorway, looking at the seven-empty chairs):** "Because they'd have to admit in court that they can't hear it *answer*."
**Viv (flat, the em-dash, not smiling — and it is much worse than smiling):** "— Oh, we're going to have such a *good* nine days."

## Page 3 — *The Signing Hall (the price of coal)*

### P3.1 — The bench at the shaft-mouth (2/3 page)
**Art:** The **Signing Hall** — not a hall; a bench, a crate, and a queue, set up at the Cut's mouth where the daylight stops (the art team: it is *deliberately* pleasant; a brazier, a kettle, a ladle of soup for signers; the mine has the best manners in the realm). The **ledger crate**: a heavy iron box of contract-slips — but the realm's contracts are *braids*, so the crate holds **tags**, one per dwarf, hung on rails like a loom's warp (a thousand tags deep, going back into the dark). Men, women, grey-braided and young; a queue that is *not* desperate — that is the point; they are workers choosing work, and the work is wrong, and the soup is good. **Skaal** stamps tags at the crate and *hums* again (the Ch. 14 P11.2 horror, continued and never remarked on).
**Skaal (cheerful, the salesman's honest economics):** "Coin's triple the great-forge, coal's free, and the widow-stay's paid if you go *under* and don't come up. My brother called it blood-money. My brother's forge has been cold since the seam tax and he's got two daughters, so it's *money*, and I don't argue with him about it because I'd *win*."
**Viv (to the queue, dry, holding the festival's book against her chest — the lawyer, no appeal in her voice, just facts):** "Ask him what the third column says."
**Skaal (not a flicker — he has answered this a hundred times and it is all written down):** "Terms are on the *plate*, lady. You swear *by the mountain*, the mine keeps your braid and cuts it if you break, you don't talk the mountain's business outside the Cut, and the mine feeds your house either way. It's on the plate. Nobody hides it."
**Kaelen (quiet, looking at the plate — the boy who can't read a contract and knows exactly what one costs):** "…What does the mountain get?"

### P3.2 — The braider (bottom third)
**Art:** Three frames. **(1)** **Norga**, the mine's braider, at a stool with his tools: the knots are *his expertise* — he rebraids the miners' family braids when they fray, which is why everybody trusts him (the art team: his hands are kind and quick and he is *good at this*; the awfulness is entirely in the arrangement). **(2)** A **father and a son** signing together at the bench: the father grey, the son about Kaelen's age; the father's knot, then the son's; the father's palm flat on the plate while the boy watches, and the father does not look at his son — he looks at the *queue*, the way a man looks at weather. **(3)** **Torvik**, the miner-captain (03a), standing at the crate with a slip in his hand, watching Norga's fingers, **saying nothing** — and the reader, who knows from the pilot's thicket of canon that this man will break exactly here, should watch his hand close on the slip.
**Father (flat, ordinary, no villainy in him anywhere — the economics of a realm that has been eaten slowly):** "You want me to be sorry? The forge is cold, the coin's real, the boy eats. …You want me to sign in *ink* like a Seams clerk?"
**Norga (to the son, tying the knot, gentle, professional — the line that makes the reader's skin crawl because it is *true*):** "Braid's tight. You'll feel it pull when you lie — that's the knot, not the law. Now go on. Everybody's got one."
**Pick (in the queue behind them, and he is *proud*, and this must be played with total sincerity — 03b: he signed, and he heard the first crack, and both are true at once):** "Third year. I hear more'n the speakers do, and I'm *not even* a speaker. …It likes me down there."

## Page 4 — *Nine Hundred Anvils (the court is moved)*

### P4.1 — The haul (2/3 page)
**Art:** The cart road at night, from above, in one long panel (the art team's key image of the chapter): **a column of dwarves carrying anvils downhill**, and each anvil is a *family's own* — faces carved with house-marks, some older than the street they came from, some with children riding on them, some with the whole family walking alongside and **not one of them singing**. It is a *funeral procession that isn't a funeral*, happening on a Tuesday, and the mine's lamps light the road because the mine *paid for the lamps*. At the head of the column, the mine's horn sounds (not a bell — the Voss bell's grammar belongs to another realm; a horn is the mine's sound). **On the roadside: Orik Khazur**, the Grand-Anvil's son, in work clothes, holding the *family's own anvil* on a shoulder-cart with two cousins, and his father standing behind the hedge watching his house's anvil go by without being able to say a word against it.
**Orik (to nobody, walking, cheerful and bleak):** "It's the *law*, Da. Most anvils, most anvils' house. …Every forge on the street owes the Cut for coal, so every forge's anvil's *down there*, so the Naming's down there, so the *year's read out* down there." *(a beat)* "He's not taking the festival. He's taking the **court**."
**Khazur (quiet, from the hedge, the mountain's lawyer, and this is the line that shows how deep the trap was built):** "Aye. And if I stop it, I stop the Naming — and a year without an audit means nine thousand dwarves who owe an oath they cannot settle. He's not fighting the law, boy. He's *using* it, and the law's *mine*, and I'd have to break my own to stop him."

### P4.2 — The captain and the lamps (bottom strip)
**Art:** Three frames. **(1) Roderick's one quiet panel** — the edge of the haul, at the roadside wall: the captain with his hand flat on a cart's edge, watching a realm carry its hearts downhill, no dialogue, exactly one panel (the art team: his *third* rail in three chapters — a river, a mine, and now a road; the man who can't open a door is standing at every threshold in the world). **(2)** **Molka**, at the head of the road with the festival's book under her arm, **silent**, watching the anvils pass — and the reader is allowed to notice that she has stopped walking, which for Molka (canon 03a: the duelist) is the same as shouting. **(3)** The mine's lamps being *handed out* to the column by mine hands; and a dwarf's small line, said without irony, which is what makes it land.
**Dwarf (plain, to a neighbor, carrying):** "He lit the road for us. …That's *decent*."
**Neighbour (plain):** "He's paying for the funeral, Bran."
**Dwarf (after a beat, and still walking):** "Aye. It's *his* funeral."

## Page 5 — *The Deal (itemized, honest, worse than a lie)*

### P5.1 — Two dwarves at the edge of the light (half page)
**Art:** The Cut's upper gallery mouth, night, the mine's lamplight behind and the road's column below (the art team: the composition is Ch. 13's stair reversed — *Drek stands in the light, Grimm in the dark*, and neither is where the reader wants them). **Drek** comes to the meeting without guards, without a lamp of his own, and — the art team's key direction — **he brings the itemized plate**, a real contract-plate with the terms struck into it, and puts it on the ground between them like a man offering a map. Around them: the machines, **idle**. The cutting has been stopped *already*.
**Drek (flat, warm, west-country, no theatre — the deal, and he does not pretend otherwise):** "Cut's stopped. Two days now. I did that before I asked you, because I'm not going to buy a thing with a thing I haven't done yet." *(a beat)* "Twelve days on the plate. Deepest gallery, every shift, your hand on the wall, and I want you *standing in it* while the realm gets ready to ask."
**Grimm (quiet, and this is him being *good* at this — Grimm is not naive, which is why the arc hurts):** "The line comes when I'm in the room. You want the line."
**Drek (no hesitation, and this is the villain's honesty, and it must be *refreshing*):** "I want the *line*. Of course I do — you've been standing in my mountain eleven days and it's drawn twice for you and once in a hundred years for anybody else. I've got a vein to find and a realm to feed and a boy with a dead ear who can still walk the thing *closer to me than my own men*." *(a beat; and then, quieter, and this is the man under the theology)* "And I want to hear it. That's the honest part and I'll say it in front of you. When that line goes across the wall, I'm the first man in the gallery, every time. I've never once heard it. Not once. A hundred years of my family's hands in this rock and I've got *nothing* in my ear but the machines."
**Drek (last — the line the chapter is built on, and it is the truest thing he says all volume):** "You think I cut my braids because I stopped *believing*? A braid's a promise to keep **listening**. …I couldn't stand wearing a promise I'd stopped keeping. So I cut them off and I stopped lying to every dwarf who looked at my face."
**Grimm (a long beat; and then, flat, to the plate on the ground):** "Twelve days. No cut. And I'm in the room when the realm asks — not you."
**Drek (picking up the plate and holding it out — and this is a *deal*, and he *will* keep it):** "Then sign it, Khazdûrin. Nothing's free in a mine."

### P5.2 — The argument (half page)
**Art:** The camp, the same night — the art team: the ring of faces around the fire, one frame per voice, no chorus panel (rule 4: the ring is the Nine's *at a table*; this is an argument, and it is shot close). **Viv** on her feet. **Kaelen** standing with his back to the fire. **Morgatha** sitting, unbudged. **Lyra** watching Grimm, not the argument. **Bramble** flat on a rock. **Nereia** with the flask. **Azrael** with his wings tight. **Fenn** with the map folded. **Pebble** at Grimm's boot with his coat buttoned *and one hand flat on the pocket*.
**Viv (the em-dash, furious, precise — a lawyer's fury, which is the worst kind):** "— The third clause *silences you*. Say one word of what that mountain says, outside that hole, and you've broken the contract and they cut your braid — and a dwarf whose braid is cut *is a dwarf whose word exists nowhere*. You are signing away the only voice in this realm that might actually be worth hearing."
**Morgatha (flat, the hunter, her corner of the frame — and the count in the frame's edge is cut into *her* side of the panel, which is the page's quiet joke and should not be explained):** "Twelve days. I'll count them. I've counted worse things and I've counted them *worse*."
**Kaelen (quiet, not arguing — and this is the pilot's rule: the team's line, not a speech):** "You do what you have to. …Then I'm standing in that gallery too, and we'll find out together what we can't hear."
**Grimm (flat, standing up, taking his hat — and the last line belongs to him because he has earned the whole arc in one sentence):** "It's not twelve days I'm buying. It's nine, and the *court*. …If I stand in that hole and can't hear it, then when the realm asks, at least there'll be a Khazdûrin in the room where the asking happens. That's the whole of what I've got."
**Pebble (tiny, immediate, arms out — and it is the funniest and most painful line of the chapter):** "…I go too."
**Grimm (looking down at the buttoned coat, one beat, and then — a blacksmith's assessment):** "*Absolutely* not."

## Page 6 — *The Braid (the count becomes thirteen)*

### P6.1 — The bench, in daylight, in front of everybody (2/3 page)
**Art:** The Signing Hall, morning, and it is **crowded** — because the mine did not have to advertise it; a Khazdûrin signing is a thing dwarves come to *watch* (the art team: the crowd's mood is not hostile; it is *hungry*; half of them came to see the frost's boy take the mine's knot, and half came to see a Khazdûrin spit on the plate, and the mine has arranged for either ending to be a good day). The **plate** is on the bench. The **contract** is read aloud by a mine clerk — *all three columns* — and Viv stands at the crowd's edge holding Molka's festival book **open** to the law, making a record, which nobody stops because it is legal and it is also, unmistakably, a warning. **Norga** with his tools. **Maren**: in the crowd, at the front, with a **pot of soup for the queue** (she came to feed the miners — that is not a metaphor, she is *actually* carrying food, and it breaks the mine's staging completely, and Drek notices and hates it and says nothing). **Grimm** kneels at the bench. Norga's hands. The knot.
**Clerk (flat, the plate read out, and the reader hears the third column *in a legal voice* — it is much worse that way):** "…*Sworn by the mountain. The braid is the mine's. The Cut's business stays in the Cut. The mine feeds the house.* …Sign by hand or knot, signer's choice."
**Norga (tying it — the chapter's title, on the page, in a workman's mouth):** "**Contract braid.** Own hand, own hair, mine's knot. …There. You're on the books, Khazdûrin." *(a beat, and then, kindly, because Norga is kind)* "It's a *tight* one. I did it careful."
**Maren (and she does not shout, does not weep, does not stop it — she looks at her son's beard with one *more* braid in it than it had this morning, and says the sentence that will be the arc's knife):** "…Wash your hands before you eat. You've been in that man's ink."
**Grimm (quiet, to his mother, and he *is* nineteen here, in one line, for the only time in the volume):** "Ma."
**Maren (already ladling soup, not looking at him, and this is love with nowhere to put itself):** "I heard you the first time, boy. I've been hearing you since you were *small*."

### P6.2 — The pocket (bottom third)
**Art:** Three frames, comic relief that is not relief at all. **(1)** **Pebble** at Norga's boot, small, entirely serious, *biting the braider's trouser-leg* — the crowd laughs; Norga laughs; and the panel must also contain, at its edge, **Drek not laughing**. **(2)** Grimm hauling Pebble up by the collar; the coat still buttoned; the pocket *bulging* — and Grimm's face doing the thing he does with a stone that won't sit flat: the reader is *screaming* at the panel. **(3)** Grimm, one eyebrow, flat, and the chapter's last joke before the horror.
**Grimm:** "What've you got in there."
**Pebble (with the dignity of a grummpling who has been *planning* something for eleven chapters):** "…*Nothing*."
**Bramble (aside, flat, to Viv, and the reader should hear the affection):** "That's the third lie he's told this volume and it's the only one he's *proud* of."

## Page 7 — *The Theft of a Witness (Viv's page)*

### P7.1 — Nine thousand counts (half page)
**Art:** The camp at night, Viv's page — the art team: the folder, the festival's book, Sprocket's chalkboard with **three columns** (he has found a way to be useful and is *radiant* about it), and Viv standing between them like a general at a map table. Her numbers, on the board, in chalk: **9,411** (signatures in the crate's tags), **1** (the crime's name, written in the dwarf tongue — the art team should have Anvil-Old or a dwarf's hand write it on the board, four ugly letters), **12** (days), **0** (pens).
**Viv (flat, fast, the case, laid out — this is the chapter's engine room and it must *sing*):** "— Here's the case, and it's clean. **One:** every signature in that crate swears *by* the mountain. That is the theft of a witness; it is void; it is triable. **Two:** the trial's venue isn't a court, it's the **Naming** — every braid, read out, in front of the realm, once a year." *(she taps the board)* "**Three:** he stopped the cut when he made the deal. Nine days of no cutting is nine days we can *use*. **Four:** the mine *paid for the road lamps*, which means the mine's hands are on record carrying the anvils down, which means the venue move is *documented*, which means when the realm's court sits, it sits in a *mine* with the mine's fingerprints on every chair."
**Viv (last — the em-dash, quiet, and this is where the reader learns that Viv's real power is not law but *staging*):** "— So we don't fight the venue. We *use* it. He built a courtroom for us and put his own name on it. All we need is a dwarf the mountain answers, one old anvil, one keeper of the book, and the realm's whole attention — and I have *four of those five already*."

### P7.2 — Seven empty chairs and one shape (half page)
**Art:** The Hall of Speakers again, at dusk, the whole team there this time. **Echo** copying his line; **Khazri** standing (the art team: this is the chapter's second chance to make the reader *hurt* and it must not take it — Khazri is calm, not tragic; he is a man on duty). And the team asks the *real* question — the one the series has to ask in this realm: **what would it even sound like?** And **Pick** is there, and answers, because he is the only dwarf alive who heard the crack, and the answer must be *felt*, not explained.
**Pick (quiet, bare, and every word of this is a clue for Volume 4's payoff):** "It wasn't a *sound*. That's what nobody gets. In the gallery, when it went, it wasn't *noise* — it was like standing next to somebody who was about to *say your name*, and then the room *changed its mind*. My dad says that's just the rock settling. …He's never been down there when it happens."

**Marginalia (tiny italics):** *"The cart notes that seven chairs with nobody in them is the quietest thing it has ever drawn, including the Hollow."*

## Page 8 — *The Gallery (what standing in it buys, and what it costs)*

### P8.1 — The line comes (2/3 page)
**Art:** The deepest gallery, at shift, **idle machines** (the cut is stopped — the deal, *kept*) — and the mine's whole workforce watching from the galleries above, standing along the rails like a theatre (the art team's key image: nine thousand dwarves' worth of *quiet*, and a boy with a beard of thirteen braids standing alone at the wall with his palm flat on the rock). **Kaelen (quiet, the smith's son, and he does not console him — the saga never consoles):** "Then we'll come back tomorrow. …And every day. You don't get to *solo* this one, dwarf."

### P8.2 — Torvik's hand (bottom strip)

**SFX:** *(none. The gallery holds his voice the way it holds everything — as an echo, and not as an answer.)*

## Page 9 — *Two Brothers and a Captain (the cost center)*

### P9.1 — Norga and Pick (half page)
**Art:** The miners' washroom — a real room, steam, towels, a hundred dwarves scrubbing off a shift (the art team: after three pages of cathedral-scale mine, one *human-scale* room; the saga's rhythm). **Norga** rebraiding his brother's family braids because Pick frayed one on the winch; **Pick** with his hands in the basin, talking to **Grimm** through the steam, and the scene is *warm* — the reader must like these two.
**Pick (flat, through the steam — the chapter's real question, asked by the least important dwarf in the realm):** "Nobody in my family ever heard the song. Grandmother says our line's been *tone-deaf* for four generations. You lot heard it for *nine*." *(a beat)* "That's not a thing that just *stops*. My dad says the mine's got to be *feeding* on it. Your dad — grandfather, sorry — the war one. He'd know, wouldn't he."
**Grimm (quiet, and he *flinches* — because the boy has said the thing he has been avoiding: that the family's silence is not his ear, but the *room's*):** "…He died before I was born. I don't even know his face."
**Pick (immediate, absolutely certain, a young dwarf's total confidence — and this is the seed of the arc's turn):** "Then somebody in this mountain knows it. You're a *Khazdûrin*. Half the forges up here were in your family's hands two generations back. Go and *ask them what he looked like*."
**Grimm (one beat; and then, quietly — a dwarf discovering something he can actually do):** "…That's not a mountain question. That's a *street* question."

### P9.2 — The wall above the Cut (half page)
**Art:** The Cut's outer wall, dusk, **Azrael** on the parapet with his wings folded and **Kaelen** below him; below them, the whole worked valley, the lamps going on. And the two S2 threads in two frames: **(1)** Kaelen's scar — the *lean*, drawn again, one degree, unremarked, and this time the art team adds the smallest possible tell: the **coat's edge**, drawn as if the *fabric* were pulled, once, toward the deep gallery — no color, no glow, no thread. **(2)** **Nereia's flask**, hung on a nail in the wall's shadow, and the art team's key direction: **the flask has a crack in the glass** — a hairline, from the heat; the flask is *the clock and the clock is breaking*, and she has said nothing to anybody.
**Azrael (dry, watching the valley, and it is a line about himself that the reader may keep):** "You know what I did in the sky-court for three years? *Standing on walls.* Watching a thing I wasn't allowed to touch. …There's a trick to it. You have to decide, every morning, that you're watching *instead* of leaving, and not instead of *arriving*."
**Kaelen (quiet, eyes on the scar's pull):** "And if you can't decide?"
**Azrael (flat, honest, wings tight — the fallen angel's whole theology in one sentence):** "Then you're just a man on a wall, and the wall's the only thing that's got anywhere to be."

## Page 10 — *Water and Stone (the two clocks)*

### P10.1 — A song that doesn't work (half page)
**Art:** The mine's water-lamps — the Cut's cistern level, drawn with a **staff and a mark** (dwarf bookkeeping: the water's depth cut into the stone in old hands' marks, and *the marks are all above the current level* — the mountain is going dry, quietly, and the art team must let the reader do that arithmetic). **Nereia** kneels at the cistern's lip, both hands in the water, marks low-tide (`#7fd1e0`), and sings — **one frame, mouth open, ripple-lines in the water, no lettering at all** (her drawn voice, canon: faint ripple-lines, no words). The water does not rise. The **steam** comes up off it, thin, and keeps coming.
**Nereia (after; flat, the diviner, capping her flask — and this is a Vol. 6 door left firmly shut):** "Water answers a song. That's the only law my realm ever agreed on, and I've been singing at this puddle for ten minutes like a woman at a *well* in a story." *(a beat)* "Stone doesn't answer anything. …Four days of water, three days of body, and the flask's *cracked*."
**Viv (the em-dash, without looking up from the board):** "— Noted. Item: *our hydromancer is a consumable.*"

### P10.2 — The Grand-Anvil's calendar (half page)
**Art:** The mountain-court, the Grand-Anvil alone at his stump with the embarrassing cushion, the court empty, the *unstruck cliff* behind him wet in the lamplight. **Kaelen** and **Lyra** admitted, no ceremony. **Khazur** with Molka's festival book *open on his knee* — the same book Viv read, in the sovereign's own hands, which tells the reader everything about who actually runs this realm. A small beat first: he moves the cushion before they can see it, badly, and Lyra — the elf who notices everything — does not smile, which is the kindness of the page.
**Khazur (flat, the mountain's lawyer, the sovereign, and the oldest-man-in-the-room voice — the chapter's political spine in one speech):** "Nine days. I can't void those contracts by writ — there's no writ, that's not how we keep faith up here. The only court we've got is the Naming, and it's *nine days* away, and it's going to be held in *his* hole with *my* house's anvil in the front row." *(a beat)* "So here's what I'm going to do, and you'll not like it. Nothing. I'm the Grand-Anvil and my job in nine days is to sit down, open this book, and *read out the year* — every braid, every cut, every oath — in front of the realm, in a mine, with a shorn man holding the lamps." *(he looks up)* "The only thing I can do legally in nine days is *read very carefully*."
**Lyra (quiet, the elf, one line — and it is the *yes* that turns the volume):** "Then we'll spend nine days making sure the realm's *listening* when you do."
**Khazur (a beat; and then — the sovereign's whole character in four words, spat like a man clearing a forge):** "…*Huh*."

## Page 11 — *The Wall of Faces (the archive)*

### P11.1 — The sketchbook (half page)
**Art:** Maren's front room, night, the ink wall — and now the reader sees **the source**: a sketchbook, on the table, open, the paper foxed, full of *drawings of faces* — some done from life, most done from *descriptions* (the art team: marginalia in Maren's hand on her own drawings — "Brother-in-law says the jaw was wider than I've got it", "His sister says the laugh went sideways"). **Grimm** sitting for her, in a chair, in the pose of a man being painted: chin up, deeply uncomfortable, *allowed to be off duty*. Through the door, the sound of **Tilda** and the neighbor's kids washing up.
**Maren (drawing, flat, not looking up — the chapter's emotional core, delivered as craft):** "I can't hear the song. I'm not a dwarf, I'm a Seam girl who married the loudest family on this mountain because I liked the *noise*. Nine generations of this family's faces live in that song, and when the song went quiet, the faces went with it — so I got paper." *(a beat; the pencil keeps moving)* "Your grandfather's page is drawn off a brother-in-law. He says the jaw was wider. That's the *whole archive*, love. A brother-in-law's jaw."
**Grimm (quiet, chin up, being drawn):** "…So I'm the only one who remembers *nothing*."
**Maren (and here she *does* look up, and this is the sentence the arc's ending will have to answer):** "You're the only one who's *owed* something. That's different, and it's worse, and I've watched you carry it since you were nine." *(back to the pencil)* "Sit still. You've got your grandfather's *scowl*, and I want it on paper before you go down that hole again."

### P11.2 — The frame, turned (half page)
**Art:** The wall, after. The art team's key image of the page: Maren takes **Durin Khazdûrin's frame off the wall** — the newest iron, the oldest paper — and turns it **face-down** on the bench beside the sketchbook, and leaves it there. Not into a drawer. Not hidden. *Waiting.* And above, the **empty frame** at the wall's edge — the blank one that has been visible in Ch. 14's establishing panel and never explained (the reader has *seen* it; the art team must not have put a nail through it). **Grimm** watching. Nothing is said for two panels. Then:
**Maren (plain, practical, the mortal's verdict):** "When you've looked at his face — I mean *looked*, not flinched — turn it back. Not before. I'm not having him up there next to you while you're carrying him like a *bill*."
**Grimm (quiet):** "…And the empty one?"
**Maren (and this is the page's last line, and it is delivered with total maternal practicality, which is what makes it enormous):** "That's the *tenth*." *(she stands, wipes her hands, goes to see to the washing-up — the conversation is over; the reader is left holding her meaning alone in the room with a dwarf and a backwards frame.)*
**Marginalia (tiny italics, from the windowsill, at the smallest possible type — the cart's rule is that it does not editorialise, and this is the closest it has ever come):** *"The cart notes a woman with paper, a boy with a beard, and one frame turned to the wall. The cart has drawn *nine generations* of this family and will not draw the tenth. That one's not the cart's."*

**END OF CHAPTER 15.**

*The count of the twelve days began with a tally cut into a panel's edge and ended with twenty-five thousand marks of a realm's whole life walking downhill at night with the mine paying for the lamps. In between: a school of listeners with seven empty chairs and one apprentice keeping the shape of a line he cannot read; a bench where a realm sells its word for three times a forge's coin and a good soup; a shorn man who told the truth and kept his promise, both; nine thousand oaths sworn by something that never gave testimony; and a dwarf who did the arithmetic — twelve days, nine of them the realm's, one braid of his own — and paid it in front of his mother, and got told to wash his hands. Nothing answered. Nothing was supposed to. The court opens in nine days, in the mine's own hole, on the mine's own floor, with an old anvil in the front row and a realm's ears wide open — and the lawyer has the case, and the sovereign has the book, and the one thing the whole mountain is waiting on is a nineteen-year-old with a dead ear and six old women's memories of his grandfather's jaw.*
**The record's cards are being cut now, and a braid mark grew a strand. Volume 4 counts.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 15)

- **The tally is a device, not decoration** (all pages): the day count is **chiselled into the frame's edge** — one mark per day, starting at *eleven* on P1.1 and reaching *nine* on P12.1 (Page 1 carries both ends of that because the chapter is *about* the sliding of days). Cut it in stone; on P12.1 only, cut it into the cart's tailgate — the one tally in the chapter that isn't the realm's. **No character ever mentions the tally.** It is the reader's clock and it is the chapter's title.
- **The realm is *kind* about the frost** (P1.1): candles, a cloth, a child's drawing, lowered voices. This must be drawn with real tenderness and read as unbearable — the prejudice in Khazadûm is not cruelty, it is *sympathy with an arrow in it*. Maren's counter-line ("The arrow points downhill, like everything else in this town") is the mortal's whole position: she lives at the rim, she feeds everybody, and she has no patience for a street that treats her son as weather.
- **The Hall of Speakers** (P2.1, P7.2): the room is built like the inside of an ear — shaped vault, tuned slots, and a raw bedrock wall where a dais would be. Eight chairs, named. Seven empty. **Khazri stands, is drawn, never speaks** (his canon 03a line is the arc's turning point; if it is spent here the volume's second half has no floor). **Echo** carries the exposition and must be *cheerful*, not tragic; his slate's single repeated stroke is the chapter's quietest horror and must never be explained or "read".
- **The Signing Hall is pleasant** (P3.1, P6.1): brazier, kettle, soup, good manners, the terms **on a plate** in the open. **Nobody in this realm is hiding anything** — that is what makes the mine work, and the page must not use a single villain's-light. **Skaal** hums; **Norga** is *good at his trade*; the father-and-son sign for the plainest reason on earth; and **Pick is proud**, sincerely, of a braid that will be used against him. **Torvik has no dialogue in this chapter** — his 03a line ("I signed the oath because it was *law*…") belongs to his defection; here he gets one silent frame of a man watching his own hand close on a slip (P3.2).
- **The haul is the chapter's key image** (P4.1): nine hundred anvils, family marks, children riding, **no singing**, and the mine's lamps because the mine paid for them. A funeral procession that isn't one, on a Tuesday. **Orik's line and Khazur's line together state Vol. 4's political problem and must not be paraphrased elsewhere**: *the court goes where the anvils go, and the anvils go where the coal is.* Molka (P4.2) is drawn **stopping** — she is the keeper of the book and she has been outflanked by her own law, and she may not speak until her forge-duel scene.
- **The deal must be honest** (P5.1): Drek brings the plate, stops the cut *before* he asks, and tells the truth about wanting the line *and* wanting to hear. His braid-line speech ("A braid's a promise to keep **listening**") is the chapter's best line and the volume's mirror: **it is the same grief Grimm has, arrived at the opposite conclusion.** Drek keeps every promise he makes in Volume 4. That is the rule. He is dangerous *because* he is a man of his word.
- **The braid is thirteen** (P6.1): Norga ties it *kindly*; the plate is read *legally*; the crowd came to watch either a Khazdûrin take the knot or a Khazdûrin spit on it, and Drek has arranged both endings as good days. **Maren brings soup for the queue** — the mine's staging collapses and nobody can say why. Her verdict is three words about hands and ink, and it must be the only verdict she gives; no weeping, no plea, no forgiveness beat. **Grimm gets exactly one line of being nineteen ("Ma.")** — one, in the whole volume so far.
- **Viv's page is the engine room** (P7.1): the case is **four facts and one staging insight**, delivered fast, on a chalkboard, with Sprocket radiant. The crime is *the theft of a witness* — swearing *by* the mountain; the venue is the Anvil-Naming (a court, not a festival); the nine days are usable; the venue move is documented because the mine paid for the lamps. **Viv does not fight the venue: she *uses* it.** (This is the saga's formula — the realm's own law defeating the villain's cell — and Volume 4's arc must resolve through it, not through force.)
- **Grimm's deafness has a visual grammar and it is now fixed** (P8.1): a panel containing Grimm at the wall has **no SFX and no balloons** — a silent window inside a noisy room. The hairline is **bigger** than Ch. 14's and runs *past the frame*; the **bore-heads swing without hands**; and the *tell* (Drek watching the machines, not the line) must be drawn once and never pointed at. **Torvik's silent frames (P8.2) are the detonator** — the reader who knows canon 03a should be able to see the defection being loaded.
- **Two clocks** (P1.2, P9.2, P10.1): the **day tally** (public) and **Nereia's flask** (private) — and this chapter the flask gets its **crack**, which nobody has been told about. The cistern's old depth-marks must all sit *above* the current waterline: the mountain is quietly going dry and no line of dialogue may say so.
- **Maren's wall** (P11.1–P11.2): the sketchbook is the archive's *actual source* — faces drawn from descriptions, marginalia in a mortal's hand. The **frame turned face-down on the bench** (not hidden, not put away: *waiting*) is the arc's emotional hook, and **the empty frame** — visible since Ch. 14's establishing shot — is finally answered with one word: *"That's the tenth."* Do not have any character explain her. **Marginalia refuses to draw the tenth frame** — one italics line, the cart's only editorial in fifteen chapters, and it must be tiny enough that the reader finds it themselves.
- **Rule 6 (Roderick)** — P4.2, frame one: the edge of the haul, hand flat on a cart's edge, no dialogue, exactly one panel (his third threshold — a river, a mine, a road). **Rule 4 (chorus panel)** is not used: the ring belongs to a table and the Nine are not at one. **Rule 3 (costs drawn)**: the tally, the flask and its crack, the cistern's marks, the thirteen braids, the turned frame, the buttoned coat.
- **What Ch. 15 must not do** (reservation list, checked): no stone-song; no vein-light (`#e07a3f` does not appear); no Mountain-Ask; no Chord; **Khazri does not speak; Molka does not speak; Torvik does not speak; Grudka does not speak; Bastion does not speak**; no golems (the First-Waking rite stays offstage — one lamp-lit window in an establishing frame is the limit); the **geode is not opened** (Pebble's pocket stays closed, but the coat is now *buttoned*, and one strand of warmth is all the panel may show); the **sea's first line** is not sung (Nereia sings *at water*, and the water does not answer — that is the point, and the door to Vol. 6 stays shut); the **empty** under the Cut is not described, only *pointed at* by machinery; the **scar's lean** is drawn, never discussed; **no new named characters** (every speaker in this file already has a line in 03a/03b/03c — if a name is needed, promote an existing one); no second silent page (that device is one per volume and Ch. 14 spent it); and **the mine is not defeated, the contracts are not voided, and the realm does not yet know what the question is.**
