# AURELION · Volume 4 — Chapter 20: "The Price"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc D: "The Mountain-Ask" (day thirteen — the first morning of a realm whose whole law was paper and whose paper is void; a miner-captain's hand on fire for an oath nobody can file; the realm's only review is a duel, the duel needs a fire, and the last fire in Khazadûm is in a clay pot on a shelf in the fifty-eight)**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Viv (**the form**), Kaelen (**the man who has to say no**), Lyra, Morgatha (**the count — thirteen, fourteen**), Nereia (**the cistern; the diviner at a fire**), Azrael, Bramble (**uncopyable**), Grimm (**carrying the last coal**), Fenn (the map — a lit mark), Marginalia, Sprocket (**the board — his first total**). **The support** — **Roderick** (his one quiet panel: the cold hearth). **The stone realm** — **Miner-Captain Torvik** (**the hand; 03a's line, spent**), **Duelist Molka** (**03a's line, spent; her palms**), **Forgemother Grudka Emberhand** (**opens her own hearth — silent**), **Bjorn** (the pot; three words), **Bjornka**, **Urka** (reading for his father), **Norga** (the braider), **Tova · Pick · Skaal** (the ranks), **Echo** (the witness-mark, cut), **Grand-Anvil Khazur** (**refuses to rule**), **Orik Khazur**, **Mountain-Speaker Khazri** (one dry line — he is allowed to talk now), **Stonehand Drek** (**the last move he has**), **Maren Ashwood** (the verdict of a maker), **Tilda Ashwood**, **the anonymous leech** ("I can dress a hand. I can't dress a *law*."), the **flame-clan** (the mourning cord), nine thousand dwarves (**queuing**).
> **Locations / threads:** the Cut above ground (the chit-desk's chalk) → the leech-room → the flame-forge on the terraces (**the First Fire**) → the fifty-eight (**the pot**) → the signing at the forge → the mine's books → the house at the rim → the terraces at dawn (**fire carried out**).
> **Arc beat:** Day thirteen — the day after the mountain answered — and the realm wakes up with no paper. Nine thousand plates have named the signers and turned on the clause, so every plate in the mine is void, and nine thousand dwarves go down the Cut on a *handshake*, because a realm whose whole ledger is in its hair has never once needed the ink to work. It holds for a day, and Viv tells them exactly what that means: it is the strongest thing she has seen in nine realms, and they will all be dead inside a year if they cannot put a word in a thing they own. Then Torvik — who signed the mine's oath because it was law — shows up with his own right hand burning, because the law he signed has no conscience and the plate that carried it cannot be filed any more, and the leech who dresses his wound can't dress his law. The realm's only review of an oath is a forge-duel, and a forge-duel is held in the First Fire, which has not been lit in Khazadûm since the Cut opened — because the flame-clan's hearth is dead, and the last ember in the mountain is the one Bjorn has kept alive in a clay pot for the Khazdûrin forge. So the chapter's engine is simple: a man wants his oath judged, the champion takes the challenge, and to judge anything at all the realm has to accept a *neighbour's* coal into its own first hearth. The duel is fought the way this realm fights — bars in one fire, iron struck and never the dwarf — and Torvik does the one thing no one expects: he stands in the fire and will not raise his hammer, because he does not want *out* of his oath, he wants the law that made it to have a conscience. The champion ends it by taking his finished bar into her bare hands, and the realm watches a verdict become a *form*: the word goes into a small bar cut at the hearth, the signer presses his own thumb into it while it is hot, the bar goes home in his own pocket, whoever holds it for him is marked too, and the mountain's own stroke is cut into the head of every one of them. Nine thousand dwarves sign themselves at Grudka's hearth for a night and a day, and by dawn the realm is carrying fire up the fifty-eight in pots, one door at a time. The price is real and it is paid in skin: a hand that can no longer work a shaft, a champion's palms, a clan's hundred years of pride, a century of coal in one pot, and the mine's monopoly on certainty broken by the fact that nobody, anywhere, can counterfeit a burn.
> **Motifs advanced:** the record (**a realm's first law that costs a hand to sign**), the witness (**the champion holds your word and is marked with it**), the kept thing (**the last coal; the pot on the forge's shelf; the bar on a nail**), the stone grammar (**the mountain's stroke, cut into the head of every bar — the realm's first archive mark**), the count (**Morgatha thirteen → fourteen; the queue: 1 → 4,006 and rising**), the door (**every doorway in the fifty-eight takes fire, including the one with the mark chiselled off — never remarked**), the lamp (the cistern, a finger lower; the diviner at a war-hearth), the money (**Drek keeps everything that holds a room together and loses the one thing he was selling**). **Not in this chapter:** the stone-song's bloom (the mountain is *warm* now, not lit — do not restage the colour); Bastion's sentence (spent, Ch. 19); the geode; the braid-adoption; the Khazdûrin forge's relight (the volume's coda); the re-cutting of the year's nine hundred names; the sea's first line; Kaelen's scar-lean (Vol. 5); and what the Count's realm is going to ask her — that is Volume 5's first page.

## Page 1 — *Day Thirteen (a realm with no paper)*

### P1.1 — The chalk (2/3 page)
**Art:** The Cut's above-ground yard at first light, and the chapter opens on an *absence*: the **chit-desk's plate-window is shuttered**, with a hand-lettered notice on it in chalk that reads like a notice a man wrote at midnight (*NO PLATES. THE MINE'S SORRY. WE'RE WORKING ON IT.* — the art team: the apology must be sincere and it must be the funniest thing in the chapter, and nobody in the frame may laugh). And around it, the mine's morning happening *anyway*: a clerk with a **chalk** marking names on the shaft-wall next to the window (his handwriting getting worse as the board gets fuller — he has not done this since he was an apprentice); a foreman at the shaft-head taking a shift **out loud** (*"Collar? …Colley, aye, you're on nine"*); a row of miners holding their own **tags** in their fists, because there is nothing left to hand them to; and, at the gates, a gang arriving **any-way** — hats in hands, waiting to be told to go home, and not being told. In the frame's corner, ordinary and unremarked: the first **children** of the day, sent down with the second shift's bread.
**Viv (flat, standing at the window with her arms folded, to Khazur, and this is the page's politics):** "— Nine thousand plates, void. Nine thousand dwarves, down the hole anyway. Nobody's asked me for anything and nobody's *stolen* anything." *(a beat)* "Your realm ran a mine for a day on a **handshake**. That's the strongest thing I've seen in nine realms and you'll all be dead inside a year if you can't put a word in a thing you *own*."
**Khazur (hoarse, dry, watching his own yard):** "We'll not be dead in a year. We'll be *poor* in a year, which up here is the same thing with more time in it."
**Marginalia (tiny italics, and the cart is parked precisely where the carts are not allowed, and nobody has the paper to fine it):** *"The cart notes a mine working off chalk, memory and manners, and that the only dwarf who looks frightened is the clerk with the chalk."*

### P1.2 — Three frames, three prices (bottom third)
**Art:** **(1)** **Sprocket's board** against the yard wall: the column **VOID: 9,000** with a heavy line under it, and a **second column crossed out** — the first column he has ever crossed out in his life (the art team: the strike-through is neat; he did it with a straight-edge). **(2)** The **forge terraces**, mid-morning: rows of forges, **no fire in any of them** — dwarves filing, fitting, turning cold iron by hand-lamp, a foreman reading a dimension off a slate, a whole industry running on *lamplight* — and the art team's key direction: **nobody in the frame finds this strange.** Draw it as work, not as mourning: this realm has been working without its own fire for so long that the horror is a *habit*. **(3)** **Nereia** at the cistern, filling the mine's tin cup from the tap, and the level on the wall: the depth-marks all sit above the water, and the lowest one sits above it by a **hand**. There's no paper to write it in, so I've started *telling* them." *(a beat)* "You should hear how they take it. Nine days of law in the book and one *diviner in the dark* is what gets them to move."
**Fenn (flat, in the margin, map open on a barrel, and the log is one line):** *"Day thirteen. The realm went to work with no paper. Nobody stole anything and the lamps are all lit. I've drawn nine realms, and this is the first morning I'd have bet the other way."*

## Page 2 — *A Hand on Fire (Torvik)*

### P2.1 — "I can't dress a law" (half page)
**Art:** The Cut's **leech-room**: a clean, ugly, well-run room (the mine does its medicine the way it does its accounts), a stone bench, a brazier that is *not* lit, a rack of tools. **Torvik** sits on the bench with his right forearm on a folded cloth, and the art team's key direction for the wound: **no glow, no colour, no supernatural sheen** — it is a **burn**, ordinary and real, hot-red and blistering, and it has spread **past the wrist** since Ch. 19 P10.2 (the reader saw the first of it there; it has had a night). It does not smoke. It simply does not cool. And the **mine's leech** — a middle-aged dwarf with the realm's flattest face, no name, no dialogue tags of his own, and the best-written line on the page — dresses it, knowing it will not take.
**The leech (flat, wrapping, without looking up — a professional being honest and hating it):** "It's a burn with no *source*. There's no plate, so there's no tag, so it goes in no ledger, so the mine can't file it, treat it, or pay for it." *(a beat)* "I can dress a hand. I can't dress a **law**."
**Torvik (flat, watching his own arm):** "It came the night the mountain said the year was answered." *(a beat)* "It was there before. Nine months now, since I signed for the deep galleries. It's just *faster*."
**The leech (the question he is not supposed to ask, and he asks it anyway):** "…Why now?"
**Torvik (a beat; and this is the whole man, in four sentences):** "Because a plate's *void* and my hand's still burning. Paper can be voided. The hand's still mine." *(he stands)* "…I want it **judged**."

### P2.2 — Up the cart road (half page)
**Art:** Four frames walking uphill, and the art team's direction: **the realm's archive is a mouth**, so the news travels *without a single line about it being spread* — **(1)** the leech at the clerks' window (in the background), **(2)** a man at the shaft-head telling two men, **(3)** those two at a soup stall telling four, **(4)** the cart road to the terraces, and the whole street already turning to look. **Torvik** walks it with his arm wrapped; **Urka** — his son — is following at thirty metres and has been following since the yard, and stops when his father reaches the forge doors. In the frame's edge: **Drek's** clerk, writing the *hour* — because the mine files everything, including a thing it can no longer file.
**Torvik (quiet, at the forge doors, to nobody — three frames' worth of a man making his mind up):** "The Naming's got a page for an anvil and a name. It's got a page for a *year*. …There's no page for a hand."
**Norga (flat, from the forge step, hair-tie between her teeth, and she is the braider and she says the practical thing):** "You'll be wanting the **champion**, then. There's no other review in the realm."
**Marginalia (tiny italics, in the margin, small):** *"The cart notes that a realm with no archive told nine thousand people in an hour, and that the cart would like the record to notice it did not need to tell one of them twice."*

## Page 3 — *The Review (the flame-forge)*

### P3.1 — The champion and the rules (2/3 page)
**Art:** The **flame-forge** on the terraces — the clan-house of the flame-dwarves, and the realm's oldest building that is not a mountain: a long hall with **one great hearth** at its end, hooded, ash-cold, the hearth-front worn into a shallow bowl by centuries of feet. And the art team's key direction for the chapter's central object: **the mourning cord** — a braided length of clan cord **across the hearth's damper**, tied in the flame-clan's knot, with the two ends sealed in wax and cut short: it has been there since the day the mountain went quiet, and it is *not decorative*, it is a **rule**: no fire in this hall until the mountain answers. Around her hall: the realm, arriving — not a mob: a crowd that knows where to stand in this building, families at the edges, children put on shoulders, the six old women of the fifty-eight with their sketchbooks, the mine's men in their shift coats, and **Drek** at the back in his plain dark coat with two clerks and no chair. **Molka** comes through them with the festival's book under one arm and **two hammers** in her other hand — hers (worn, banded, a champion's) and one **plain**, unmarked, off the wall. She stops at the hearth, reads the *challenge* out of the book out loud in a flat voice, and sets the plain hammer on the hearthstone where Torvik can take it.
**Molka (flat, official, and this is 03a's line, spent verbatim — delivered the way an artisan reads a clause, and only the last four words have any weight in them):** "You want to break an oath? Then **stand in the fire with me. The forge doesn't do appeals.**"
**Torvik (flat, immediate):** "I've no want to break it. I want it *judged*."
**Molka (a beat; and then the practical sentence that turns the page, said without any drama at all):** "…Then we've a problem, captain. The review's held at the **First Fire**." *(she looks at the cold hearth, and at the cord, and at the clan standing in their own hall)* "And there's no fire in Khazadûm."

### P3.2 — Three rules (bottom third)
**Art:** Three frames: **(1)** Viv, on the record, reading the duel's clauses out of the book, because a duel in this realm *is* a court and she is not going to let one happen without a transcript; **(2)** the realm's faces as the rules land — this is a *public*, a small town's discipline, no cheering, no betting, a few miners doing the arithmetic with their eyes; **(3)** the hearth, the cord, the clan — **and the flame-clan silent in their own house**, arms folded, watching the realm ask them for fire. And in the frame's corner: **Grudka Emberhand**, the Forgemother, standing at the hearth's end with her back to the ash, wearing her working clothes, saying **nothing at all**.
**Viv (flat, fast, reading — three clauses and no commentary until the last line):** "— **One**: two bars in one fire; the bars are the words. **Two**: you may strike the iron and you may not strike the dwarf. **Three**: the duel ends when a bar is *worked true* or *let go cold*." *(a beat; she looks up)* "That's the realm's whole review. Four hundred years of law, and the only thing it knows how to do with a broken word is put it in a fire and see what a dwarf is made of." *(a beat)* "…There's nothing in it about a man who has *nothing left to break*, and I want that on the record before anybody burns."
**Marginalia (tiny italics):** *"The cart notes that a forge-duel has three rules and none of them is about winning, and that a realm that keeps its law in a book about lamp-oil has been settling its honour at an anvil for four hundred years without ever once writing down what happens when somebody simply *stands*."*

## Page 4 — *The Last Coal (fire, at last)*

### P4.1 — Bjorn's pot (half page)
**Art:** The **fifty-eight**, midday, and the whole street out on its doorsteps: **Bjorn** the anvil-old coming down it with the **clay pot** in both hands, in his work coat, with the pot's rim covered by the same folded rag it has had on it for a century; a knot of neighbours walking alongside him and nobody offering to carry it; and behind him **Bjornka**, fifteen, keeping the old man's pace like a young dog on a rope. **Roderick's one quiet panel, placed anywhere on this page with no emphasis and looked at by nobody:** the **cold hearth-stone of the flame-forge**, up the hill, and the captain's bare hand flat on it, and no dialogue. Then: at the forge doors the crowd opens; Bjorn stops in front of **Grimm** (who has come up the hill with the pot — the art team: Grimm stands with **Askstone** in his belt, unwrapped, because the hammer is a hammer now) — and the old man holds the pot out to him. The family's fire in a neighbour's pot, handed back on a doorstep with the realm watching.
**Bjorn (flat, holding it out, and this is three words and a century):** "It's yours, lad. It always was."
**Grimm (quiet, taking it — and the art team must draw him **not** standing straighter; he should look about nineteen years old):** "…Aye."
**Grimm (next, to the hall, loud enough for the front of the crowd, and it is not a speech — a workman telling a room what he is about to do with his hands):** "It's one coal. It's my grandfather's, and it's the last fire in this mountain, and it's no good to my family cold." *(a beat)* "**Where do you want it?**"

### P4.2 — The cord is cut (half page)
**Art:** The lighting, in six frames, and the art team's key direction: **this must be drawn as a *rite* that nobody in the room has ever attended.** **(1)** The clan's knot, and **Grudka's** hands on it. **(2)** Grudka cutting the cord with her own knife — the clan's rule is that only the Forgemother cuts it, and *she does not explain*, and nobody in the hall breathes. **(3)** The damper open: the hearth's inside, and the art team should draw what a hundred years of cold looks like — grey ash, no ember, the grate rusted into the shape of what it used to hold. **(4)** **Grimm setting the coal in** — with the tongs Bjorn hands him, and his own hands steady, because he is his grandfather's hands. **(5)** Grudka's breath: **the coal catching**, small, at the centre of a hearth built for nine hundred. **(6)** **The room**, wide: nine hundred dwarves in a hall, and every single one of them has taken one step **back** — draw it as a ripple, and let two children be held tighter, and one old woman with her hand over her mouth, and no cheering at all. The light is **honest warm orange from the hearth** (the art team: **no `#e07a3f`, no bloom, no rings — that colour belongs to the stone, and this is fire, and the realm can make this one itself**).
**SFX (the sequence's only line, at frame five, small):** *(a breath. Then the sound a fire makes when it decides.)*
**Bjorn (quiet, to himself, watching a hundred years of his own shifts go up in somebody else's hearth — and this closes the sentence from Ch. 19):** "…It'll do."
**Art note (the page's last frame):** the **pot**, empty, set down on the forge's own shelf beside the clan's oldest tools — *not* thrown away, *not* returned — and the rag folded on top of it. Nobody in the chapter ever remarks on the pot again.
**Khazri (dry, from the edge of the crowd, hands in his sleeves, his first line since Ch. 19 and it is allowed to be a small one — the Mountain-Speaker is a person now):** "…Forty years I stood at a wall. This is *warmer*."

## Page 5 — *The Duel (bars in one fire)*

### P5.1 — Two bars, one hearth (half page)
**Art:** The duel set up in five frames, in the realm's own working grammar: **(1)** the **bars** — two lengths of mine-iron, cut from the Cut's own stock, and the art team's key note: they are *not* ceremonial; they are the same stock the shaft-heads are made of, and **Drek** gave them, freely, on the record, because Viv made his clerk write **GIFT** on the chit (a detail the reader will want later). **(2)** The **two anvils**, brought in and set facing across the hearth, and the realm's hands doing that: **Orik Khazur** and two cousins, and — one frame, no comment — **Orik** taking his own father's anvil's place in the carry, because the mountain-court's anvil is one of the nine hundred and everyone knows which one. **(3)** The bars going into the fire, in the tongs. **(4)** The realm's ring around the hearth: no chorus panel (the ring belongs to the Nine at a table), but **each of the Nine in the crowd, one frame each, one line total between them** — Morgatha's, and it is her number, **thirteen**, and the art team must put it in a corner where the reader who counts will find it. **(5)** Molka handing Torvik the **plain hammer**, hilt-first, with the champion's own hammer on her shoulder.
**Molka (flat, workmanlike, the last thing said before the fire):** "Pick it up when you're ready. There's no clock in this. There's only the *iron*."
**Torvik (taking the hammer, and he weighs it the way a man weighs a tool he has held ten thousand times):** "Aye."

### P5.2 — The man who will not lift it (half page)
**Art:** Three frames, and the chapter's first real turn. **(1)** Torvik puts the hammer down on the anvil — *sets* it down, deliberately, squares — and stands with his wrapped arm at his side, watching **his bar in the fire**. **(2)** The bar, in close, going from black to dull red to bright — the art team should draw the *scale* flaking, the iron softening, the shape sagging the way iron does when nobody is answering it; and a smith in the crowd (**Tova**, flame-forge, Ash's sister, in her own hall) instinctively starting *"— turn it, you'll lose the—"* and **choking it off** with her hand over her own mouth, appalled at herself. **(3)** The realm's confusion, in one wide frame: nine hundred faces reading the same book — *he's yielding; no, he's not; nothing's finished; is it over; it can't be over, nothing's been judged*. And in three small corners: **Urka**, white-faced, in the front rank; **Drek**, at the back, hands in pockets, watching the *iron*; **Grudka**, at the hearth's end, watching Torvik's hand.
**Viv (quiet, to Sprocket, on the record, and she is the only person there who has seen a legal proceeding fail before):** "…Write it down. 'The fighter declines to strike.'" *(a beat)* "He's not surrendering. He's *asking*. Somebody in this room had better work out what for."

## Page 6 — *Two Hammers on One Bar*

### P6.1 — The champion works (half page)
**Art:** Four frames, and the art team's direction: **for three of them this must look like nothing more than two smiths at a job.** Molka takes Torvik's bar out of the fire **with the tongs** (her right, under the rules — she may strike his iron) and lays it on **his own anvil**, and strikes it: once, twice, three times, working a word that is not hers, in front of everybody, unhurried, and *she says nothing at all*. And the man who has slung his hammer down is a **miner-captain with twenty years on the deep galleries**, and the fourth blow is his, because a dwarf cannot stand at his own anvil and watch somebody else true up his iron: he takes the hammer up and *works*, and for two panels the page is exactly what this realm looks like when it is happy — **two dwarves, one bar, two hammers, alternating, no words, the iron coming true.** Around them: the crowd leaning in; Tova with her hands over her mouth; Orik Khazur with his arms folded; the old women's pencils down.
**SFX:** *(iron, struck and turned and struck. In time, then out of time, then in.)*
**Molka (the only thing she says in the whole sequence, flat, mid-blow, a professional to a professional):** "Turn it. …*Again.*"

### P6.2 — "I just wish it had a conscience" (half page)
**Art:** The bar comes off the anvil **true** — square, clean, sound, a good piece of work done by two sets of hands (the art team: draw it *beautiful*, and draw the crowd's involuntary smith's *approval* before they remember what it is for). And then Torvik stops: hammer down, hands on the anvil, and he says 03a's line, flat, without any appeal in it — a statement of fact about himself and his realm, made in front of nine hundred people, with his burn wrapped in a cloth. The art team's direction for the frame: **do not make it a plea.** He is not asking. He is *reporting* — the way he has reported a gallery's condition every morning for twenty years. And Molka, across the anvil from him, does not answer him. She looks at his bar for a long beat — one full panel, silence, her hand flat on the iron — and then she says the verdict in two words.
**Torvik (flat, standing over his own finished word, and this is 03a's line, spent, unaltered):** "**I signed the oath because it was law. The law's still on my hand. I just wish it had a conscience.**"
**Molka (a long beat; then, flat, the champion's finding):** "…The bar's *sound*." *(a beat)* "There's nothing here for the fire to judge, captain. You've made your word true, in front of the realm, with the champion's hands on it. The book's got no page for a *good* oath that nobody's broken."
**Art note (the page's final frame, held):** **Drek**, at the back, in the same frame, watching the *iron* and not the men — one panel, no line, and the reader is not to be told what to make of it.

## Page 7 — *The First Burn (the word goes in the hand)*

### P7.1 — Her palms (2/3 page)
**Art:** The chapter's money page and the volume's thesis, drawn as **one physical act**: Molka reaches out and takes Torvik's bar **off the anvil in her bare hands** — both palms, no tongs, no leather — and holds it out across the anvil to the man it belongs to. The art team's direction, in order: **(1)** the hiss and the smell must be drawn, not lettered (a thin line of steam off her palms; the crowd's flinch drawn as a *recoil in the ranks*, not as noise); **(2)** **her face does not change** — the champion of the flame-clan, who has been in more fires than anyone alive, and whose hands are the most valuable object in this hall, doing the arithmetic out loud in her head and paying it; **(3)** the bar *in her hands*, with **the mountain's stroke cut into its head** — because **Echo**, who has been in the front rank the whole time with a chisel and a hammer, has already cut the witness-mark in (the art team: the mark is the single stroke, the one on his slate, the one from the raw wall at the Naming — cut small, cut clean, cut *by a nineteen-year-old*); **(4)** Torvik taking it, from her hands, into his wrapped one. Six frames. No dialogue until the last one.
**Molka (quiet, through her teeth, flat as the supply list — and this is the sentence that becomes the law of Khazadûm):** "**Hold your own word, then. It's hot, and it's yours, and it doesn't cool for anybody.**"
**Viv (flat, instantly, on the record — the lawyer catching a rule the moment it is made and nailing it before anyone can turn it into poetry):** "— Note that. *The signer holds it himself.* Write the hands." *(a beat)* "Sprocket. **That's the clause.** Don't let anybody call it a custom by tomorrow."

### P7.2 — The mark (half page)
**Art:** Torvik presses **his own thumb** into the bar's hot head — one push, one beat, and the art team must draw the moment exactly: a working man's grin going *off* his face as it takes, his jaw, his eyes not leaving the iron, and **nobody holding him** (Ch. 15's bench, inverted: the mine used to tie the braid; the realm now burns the mark, and the difference is who puts the hand down). And then the page's quiet payoff: his **right forearm**, under the cloth — the plate-burn that has been on him for nine months, the one with no source and no file — **going quiet**, drawn small, in the same frame: the red receding, the heat going out of it, the wound finally cooling under a mark he made himself. The mine's **leeches** are in the crowd; the anonymous one from P2.1 is looking at it; and he says nothing, because there is nothing in his trade for it. Around them: **Khazur** with his arms folded and his jaw set; **Khazri** (near, watching the hands); **Grudka** (at the hearth); the six old women with their pencils out again and drawing for the first time in the chapter.
**Viv (reading out, for the record, the form as it is being made — three lines, flat, and the sovereign is standing next to her hearing his realm's law change without one word from him):** "— **One**: a word is cut in a bar at a hearth and the bar goes home with the signer. **Two**: the word is read *whole* — both halves — before his thumb goes down. **Three**: whoever holds the bar while it is signed is marked with it."
**Khazur (flat, dry, and this is the sovereign's whole reign in two sentences):** "I've read out a year for four hundred years' worth of other men, and I'll not *write* this one. …It's your hands. You'll write it."
**Khazri (quiet, from beside him, and the Speaker is allowed one dry line per chapter now):** "Aye. That's how the good ones get written."

## Page 8 — *The Queue (nine thousand, one at a time)*

*The mountain answered and the paper died, and the first thing the realm did without it was go to work anyway, on chalk and memory and manners, which is what a realm does when its whole archive has always been in its hair and its neighbours' mouths. ## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 20)

- **No bloom, no song, no colour from the stone** (all pages). Ch. 19 spent `#e07a3f` and the volume is done with it: **from this chapter the mountain is *warm*, not lit.** The only light in Ch. 20 is **fire** — honest hearth-orange, lamp-yellow, and lamplight — plus the grey air of a district with nine hundred cold forges. If a panel in this chapter looks like the Naming's bloom, redraw it. The stone's warmth may be *drawn* only the way a smith draws it: iron that works well, a hearth that takes quickly, ash that is warm the next morning.
- **The chapter's object is the hearth and its cord** (P3.1, P4.2). The flame-clan's **First Fire** is the realm's oldest hearth, dead since the day the mountain went quiet, **closed by a braided mourning cord sealed in wax** — and the realm's reviews have all been held at working forges since, which is *why* the realm's law has been running on substitutes for a century (this is one line of Fenn or Viv's if it is said at all; the art team carries it in the empty hall). Only the **Forgemother may cut the cord**, and Grudka does it **without one word** — she has no dialogue in this chapter; her line is Chapter 21's (canon 03a: the braid-adoption). Her silence here is the point: the proudest clan in the realm accepting a **Deep dwarf's** coal into its own hearth is the act, not a speech about it.
- **The last coal pays off** (P4.1–P4.2). The pot Bjorn kept alive for a hundred years (Ch. 14, 17, 19) comes down the fifty-eight in the old man's hands, is **handed to Grimm on a doorstep** ("It's yours, lad. It always was."), and is **set into the First Fire**. It burns out, and the **pot goes on the forge's own shelf**, with its rag folded on top, and **nobody mentions it again in the chapter**. This is the volume's clearest cost-grammar: a century of coal spent in one night to light the realm. Do not add a magic ember; do not make the fire burn blue; **the fire is fire.**
- **The forge-duel's rules are canon now** (P3.2, P5.1–P5.2) and must be lettered once, flat, out of the book, in Viv's clause-voice: **(1) two bars in one fire; the bars are the words. (2) you may strike the iron, never the dwarf. (3) the duel ends when a bar is worked true or let go cold.** The duel is the realm's **only review of an oath** (canon: 03a, Ch. 14 P2.1) — a court that happens at an anvil, held by the realm's champion (**Molka**, flame-dwarf). **Molka's 03a line is spent here, verbatim, at the challenge**, and it must be read like a clause and not like a threat. **Torvik's 03a line is spent here, verbatim**, at his own anvil, standing over a bar he has just made true — **played flat, as a report, never as a plea** (he has reported a gallery's condition every morning for twenty years).
- **The duel must not look like a fight** (P5–P6). There is no striking of bodies, no choreography, no impact frames. The tension is entirely in **the iron, the fire, and the realm's inability to read what is happening**: a man who will not raise his hammer, a champion who works his iron *for* him (allowed by rule one), and a courtyard of nine hundred dwarves watching a word get made and no crime get judged. The turn is **Torvik taking the hammer up** — because a dwarf cannot stand at his own anvil and watch somebody else true up his iron. For two panels this page is simply what this realm looks like when it is happy: two smiths, one bar, alternating, no words.
- **The verdict, the palms, and the clause** (P6.2–P7.1). Molka's finding is two sentences and no rhetoric (*the bar's sound; there's nothing here for the fire to judge*), and then she does the one physical thing the chapter exists for: **she picks the finished bar up in her bare hands and holds it out to him.** Draw the **steam, the smell, the recoil in the ranks, and her face unchanged**; she is the champion of the flame-clan and her hands are the most valuable object in the hall, and she has done the arithmetic. **Her palms stay bound for the rest of the volume**; the art team must draw them in every frame she appears in from here, **and no character may ask her about them in this chapter.** The clause the realm takes from that act is Molka's own sentence: **hold your own word — it's hot, and it's yours, and it doesn't cool for anybody.**
- **The new form, exactly** (P7.2, P8.1): **(1)** a word is **cut in a bar** at a hearth, and the bar **goes home with the signer**; **(2)** the word is **read whole — both halves — before the thumb goes down**; **(3)** whoever **holds the bar** while it is signed is **marked with it**. The consequence must be drawn, never explained: **the mine keeps the presses and the paper for *orders*, and the realm's *oaths* move to the fire.** The **mountain's stroke** (Echo's mark from Ch. 19 — the single stroke off the boy's slate, the one the raw wall answered with) is **cut into the head of every bar** by Echo and then by the readers: **the realm's first archive mark.** And the burn is a **thumb-print pressed into hot iron, once in a life** — small, permanent, unforgeable, and the reason Drek cannot sell it. **Maren's line names it** ("you've all just made yourselves *uncopyable*") and **Bramble's closing frame takes it personally** — she is a changeling who has worn nine faces and could not name her own; the realm has just written its own in skin. That juxtaposition is the page's whole job: do not add anything to it.
- **Khazur refuses to rule** (P7.2). The form is *made* by the realm in a square and *used* before the sovereign ever rules on it — and the sovereign's line is the theme in his mouth: *"I'll not write this one. It's your hands."* **Khazri gets exactly one dry line** (he is allowed to talk, sparingly, from Ch. 19; he is not a speech-giver). **The leech is the chapter's anonymous conscience** ("I can dress a hand. I can't dress a **law**") — he stays unnamed, per rule 7's reverse: **do not promote him, do not name him, do not give him a second chapter.**
- **Drek's move is a *business* move and must be played without menace** (P9.1–P9.2, P11.2). He loses the word and keeps **everything that holds a room together** — oil, rail, cord, coal, iron, roads, ferry-writs — priced in chalk where anybody can read it, no clause. His two human beats: the **rack** (he will not tip nine hundred families' hair into a furnace, even to tidy a ledger: *"even I've got a floor"*), and the **tag** — Torvik's mine-tag in the clerk's tray by the next frame, filed and moved on, **without one word of ceremony.** The **depth-log** (Ch. 19's seed) sits on his desk in exactly one panel; the **bore-heads** are re-set in exactly one panel; **do not make either ominous.**
- **The queue is the chapter's heart** (P8.1): nine hundred dwarves signing their own words at a hearth, drawn as a full working smithy with soup and sleeping children. **The largest frame belongs to Urka reading his father's bar aloud** — the son reading for the father, whole, both halves (the realm's first reading under the new form). **Tova** (03c, flame-forge) signs first, at her own clan's hearth, sleeves rolled. **Norga** ties each bar's cord in the mine's old knot **and a second knot of her own** — unexplained, hers. **Molka stands at the hall's end holding other people's bars**, because the clause she made says the holder gets marked and hers are marked already; that is the champion's whole character change and **nobody comments on it.**
- **Bastion is not in this chapter** (his sentence was spent in Ch. 19; if he is drawn at all it is one distant frame on the pass, going back up, and no line). **The chorus panel is not used** (rule 4: the ring belongs to the Nine at a table) — the Nine appear *inside the crowd*, one frame each at most, and **Morgatha's number (thirteen → fourteen) is placed in a corner, unexplained**, as always.
- **The pot, the fire and the chimneys are one motif** (P4, P10.1): a neighbour's pot holding a family's last coal for a century, **inverted** into a street queuing up a hill with pots to carry the fire *out* — the funeral that isn't a funeral, this time in reverse. **The doorway with the mark chiselled off opens like every other door, takes fire like every other door, and is never remarked on by anybody in the frame, ever** (rule 7's oldest thread in this volume).
- **The clock changes again, for the third time in the volume** (all pages). Ch. 14–17's tally was the twelve days; Ch. 18–19's was the **reading-line** and then the **naming-line**. Ch. 20's is **the queue** — dwarves signed at the flame-forge — and it is the first clock in the volume that is *happy*: it rises **1 → 409** (Sprocket's first total, P8.2) → **2,000** (P10.1) → **4,006 and rising** at the close (P12.1), and it is *never given a total at the end*, because the realm is going to be signing for years. **The count is spoken flat and out loud by whoever is nearest the fire** — and the book's own honest note is that this one will take longer than a night. **Morgatha's count is separate and unexplained (thirteen → fourteen).**
