# AURELION · Volume 4 — Chapter 14: "Twelve Braids"

> **Season 2 · Pages 1–12 · Arc D: "The Mountain-Ask" (the stone realm — a realm that counts what a person owes in their own hair; a mountain that has stopped answering; and the heir of a cold forge, who has to find out whether he's the only one who can't hear it or the only one it's talking to)**
> **Cast:** **The team** — Kaelen (the coat, the scar, the heartbeat — first Vol. 4 beat), Grimm (**the arc's heart — counted, not spoken**), Pebble (**homecoming; the geode, one frame, unopened**), Lyra, Morgatha (the count, *upward*), Nereia (the flask — refilled at the grove; the mountain drinks), Azrael, Viv (the file on a realm with *no paper*), Bramble (the mask in the beard-capital of the world), Fenn (the map — a road drawn *solid* at last), Marginalia, Sprocket. **The support** — **Roderick** (his one quiet panel: the rail above the Deep Cut — a wall with no door). **The stone realm** — **Maren Ashwood** (Grimm's mother; the mortal Seam-woman; the wall of faces in ink — the ark of a family the song stopped keeping), **Tilda Ashwood** (his aunt), **Bjorn** (the anvil-old who has kept the Khazdûrin forge's coal alive for a century — in a clay pot, on a shelf, like a candle in a sickroom), **Dorin Khazdûrin** (the cousin who works the great-forge instead), **Anvil-Old** (keeper of the gate's name-stone), **Pick** (the miner-young who heard the first crack — and signed), **Norga** (Pick's brother), **Urka** (Torvik's son), **Skaal** (a great-forge smith who took the mine's coin), **Echo** (Khazri's echo-young — hears what his elder stopped saying), **Cinder** (grummpling mother), **Nib · Chuckle · Nibble · Boulder-Nib · Peb** (the tunnels, in a mob), **Warden Bastion** (stone giant — **silent: he speaks once a year, and this is not the year**), **Fern · Sprout** (the moss-line), **Grand-Anvil Khazur** (the mountain's lawyer), **Orik Khazur** (his son), **Mountain-Speaker Khazri** (**silent since the mining began — a slate, and nothing on it**), **Forgemother Grudka Emberhand** (the flame-forge, watching), **Duelist Molka** (the Anvil-Naming's keeper — and the realm's clock), **Stonehand Drek** (**FIRST APPEARANCE — the miner; a flame dwarf with a shorn beard and a theology of the silence**). **Back West (one frame each)** — Faelis, Thale, Grain, Bark, Wren, Kit, the Moot's door.
> **Locations / threads:** the FOR road east (the named road, eleven days on) → the braid-line (the realm's border) → the gate of doors and the **name-stone** → the Khazdûrin forge district (the cold room) → **the Deep Cut** (the mine, and its ledger of cut braids) → the mountain-court → the house at the rim (Maren's ink wall) → the deep gallery (where the hairline is) → **Fenn's second section** (the mountain, cut open).
> **Arc beat:** Season 2 opens on the road the first season named. Eleven days east, the gray has a *border* — the grove's root-line — and past it the world is still falling. Khazadûm counts a person's word in their own hair: every dwarf's braids are an account, and the mine has been paying its workers in *contract braids* that no appeal can reach (Viv: *"There's no paper here. That's the problem."*). The mountain has been silent since the mining began — Khazri the Mountain-Speaker has stopped speaking, the grummplings' luck has gone strange, and the stone-song that Grimm's family carried for nine generations is *not there*. The gate's name-stone warms under every dwarf's hand but Grimm's: it does not refuse him, it *takes* — hoarfrost where his palm was — and the reader learns what the arc must answer before Grimm does: the mountain is not angry and it is not dead. It is **empty**, and empty pulls. The Deep Cut answers something no mine should answer (the hairline that runs when a Khazdûrin is standing in the gallery, Ch. 7's *cough*, paid), and Drek — a flame dwarf who shaved his braids and built a religion out of not hearing — says the thing Grimm has never let himself say out loud. At the mountain-court the Grand-Anvil lays out the realm's whole choice in two hands (seal the mountain and starve the Seams; fight open and lose the mountain's children), and the Nine offer the third answer that is the entire saga in one word: **ask**. In the cold room, Grimm puts his hand on his own family's wall and says the family's name — and the mountain says nothing, and one hairline of hoarfrost on the anvil goes to water, and nothing else happens, *because it must not yet*. The Anvil-Naming is in twelve days. The anvil has waited a century for a name. An anvil is named for the hands that wore it — and there is one hand left in the family, and it has never once, in its whole life, heard the mountain answer.
> **Motifs advanced:** the count (**braids** — the realm's own count, and Grimm's; the reader is given a number to keep, and the chapter ends with him unable to finish it), the name (spoken at a gate, cooling; the family's name unsaid in the room it belongs to), the door (**a door is a question** — carried from a boy's rule into a realm of doors inside doors), the kept thing (a century of coal, kept in a pot by a neighbor; the geode, warm, unopened), the clock (**twelve days** — the Anvil-Naming; and Nereia's flask, drawn every page it can be), the empty (the mountain is not a monster and not a corpse — it is *empty*, and the Void's grammar shows up in stone for the first time), the mirror (**Drek** — what Grimm becomes if the grief wins), the ink (a mortal's wall of faces — the only archive left when a realm's memory goes quiet), the road (drawn **solid** behind the cart — the named road becomes a line on the map), the record (**the cards change hands and change material: Vol. 4's card is carved**), the scar (steady; and it *leans* — first Vol. 4 note of the Hollow). **The stone-song is NOT paid. The Mountain-Ask is NOT struck. Bastion does NOT speak. Khazri does NOT speak. The answer does not come in this chapter.**

## Page 1 — *Eleven Days East (the road, drawn solid)*

### P1.1 — The FOR road, in color that has an edge (2/3 page)
**Art:** The FOR road, eleven days east of the great tree (the Ch. 7 P5.2 gate-stone long behind; the name carries — the art team: on the stone-roads the name is cut *deep*, dwarf-fashion, a foot wide, and the road is still the road — the first *named* thing to cross a realm line in the saga). The Nine + the cart, walking; the country has changed from grove to scrub to the long gray shoulder that precedes a mountain range — and, along the frame's bottom edge, the **gray**, still going, and a **hard line where it stops** (the art team's key direction of the page: the Unraveling has a *border* now, and the border is the grove's root-line — everything past it is still being taken, at the same pace as ever; the reader is not allowed to think the world is winning, only that *one realm* is). Behind the cart, faintly, the road they walked, and above it, on **Fenn's map**, that same road drawn **solid** for the first time (the dotted line of Ch. 12 P12.1, inked in — the map maker's small miracle; the art team: one frame, the nib still on the paper). **Marginalia** on the cart's step, writing; **Sprocket** at the tailgate; **Pebble** on Grimm's shoulder, *facing forward*, which the reader has never seen him do.
**Marginalia (tiny italics):** *"Day eleven. The cart notes that the road is on the map now. The cart also notes that the cart is on the road, which the cart finds suspiciously tidy."*
**Fenn (flat, not looking up from the map):** "Eleven days, and the line's solid. …I've been drawing that gap for sixty years. It took a boy on a step to close it."

### P1.2 — Four frames, going west (bottom strip)
**Art:** Four small frames, left to right — the leave-taking the reader was owed after Ch. 13's close, each one *quiet*, none of them a goodbye speech. **(1)** The grove gate: **Faelis** and **Thale** (the gray hand — still gray; the rule holds) on the court side; Faelis does not bless them or raise a hand; she says the realm's answer and lets the road be the road. **(2)** The undercroft door at the tree's root — **Grain**, at work on a door that has a name in its lintel now, not looking up; the chisel moving; the ring that Bark cut catching the first light. **(3)** The Moot's door at the tree, upright, the **Voss bell** on its peg; **Wren** rolling the section (it has a crown on it now), **Kit** holding the roll's far end, standing on her tenth chair to reach. **(4)** Tiny, at the page's far edge, three hundred miles of green away: Ashenmere's roofs, and one plate shining at the border grove's ring — Marta's, which stays.
**Faelis (the matriarch — the realm's word, shortened to what a road needs):** "We'll know when it answers. Groves hear stone by root. …Go and come back with something *said*."
**Grain (from the door, flat, not turning — the first line he has ever said while working):** "Come back with a straight beard, dwarf. That's the whole wish."
**Kit (calling after them, from the chair):** "Bring the mountain a *name*! They like names!"

## Page 2 — *What the Cart Carries*

### P2.1 — The flask, the sky, and the file (half page)
**Art:** Four beats across one panel, the cart's own inventory of the road. **The flask:** **Nereia** kneeling at the grove spring on the morning of the first day — *the last fill before stone* (the art team: draw the spring, the flask, and the *level*, full to the neck; below the flask, small, the water-ring she came out of in Ch. 13, still at its level; she does not say goodbye to it; she caps the flask). **The sky:** **Azrael** on the cart's roof, wings folded by choice, *back* the way they came — not watching the sky (the reader's reward for Ch. 13). **The file:** **Viv**, umbrella up against *dust* now, a folder she has clearly made herself, reading aloud from notes *about* the realm they're entering — the pitch of her voice flat, the content appalling. **The board:** **Sprocket** chalking a new column (he has added nine rows and left them blank).
**Viv (the em-dash, the cold — the realm's law, delivered as evidence):** "— Khazadûm, file one. They don't *paper*. Their law is *braids*: a dwarf's beard is a ledger and every braid is an entry — so many for the family, so many for what you *owe*. You break your word? They cut the braid. It doesn't grow back *into* the account. There is **no court, no appeal, and no pen.** The forge-duel is the only review."
**Viv (next, quieter — the actual horror, added without a beat of irony):** "…And they've been hiring here. Thousands of miners wearing a *contract* in their hair, signed at a shaft mouth by a man who isn't in their ledger."
**Nereia (flat, the tide-diviner, one hand flat on the flask's cap — the markings *low tide*, `#7fd1e0`):** "I filled it at the grove. First fill in a year that wasn't my own sea. …Somebody tell me how long water lasts on a mountain." *(Nobody tells her. The art team: nobody tells her.)*
**Sprocket (flat):** "Nine blank rows. I'm told it's worse than the count."

### P2.2 — Twelve braids (half page)
**Art:** The cart's tailgate at dusk, the fire small, the others asleep or walking the wheels. **Grimm**, sitting with **Askstone** across his knees — and the hammer **wrapped in cloth**, the cloth *not* opened (the art team's key direction of the page: he has carried it eleven days and not once looked at it; do not draw the head; the reader has seen this hammer for eleven chapters and this chapter will not show it to them). **Pebble** asleep in his beard. **Kaelen** on the tailgate's other side; **Lyra** standing, and the frame holds the two of them looking at the dwarf without looking at him — the saga's whole method. Grimm's beard is drawn **plain and countable**: nine tight braids (family) and three loose, *unfinished*, faded (debt) — twelve, and the art team will draw them the same way **every page of Volume 4** so the reader can *read* him.
**Kaelen (the smith's son, no pity in it — just the trade):** "Nine and three."
**Grimm (flat, a workman's accounting — the only way he can say it):** "Nine for the family. Three for what the family owes. My grandfather's account. My father's account. Mine — that one's still *open*." *(a beat)* "You don't braid a debt that's paid. You cut it. Cutting's the payment."
**Kaelen:** "…And they're loose."
**Grimm (and this is the chapter's real first line, said to a fire):** "Loose means *open*. A dwarf can walk a mountain with three loose braids and everybody knows exactly what he's carrying. …It's the tidiest honest thing in the world." *(a beat — quieter)* "I like it up here. Nobody's ever going to look at my beard and ask me how my *handwriting's* doing."
**Lyra (quiet — the mirror, five words, and she is looking at his beard, not at him):** "…You count yours."
**Grimm (not looking up):** "Yeah."
**Lyra (last, and she does not explain — the reader remembers her rings):** "So do I."

## Page 3 — *The Braid-Line (the realm's edge)*

### P3.1 — The border, made of hands (half page)
**Art:** The braid-line: the realm's edge is **not a wall** — it is the old stone pillars of the seam-road, and every one of them has been **braided** by a century of dwarf-hands (the art team: the pillars are carved in interlace, nine strands, from the base to the crown, by *different hands over different decades* — you can see where the style changed; the border is a family album you walk through). Beyond it, the ground goes up. **Warden Bastion** — a twenty-metre stone giant, moss-veined, standing at the pass's shoulder (the art team's key direction: **he does not speak**. Canon 03a: he speaks once a year. *This is not the year.* Draw his silence — his head is turned, watching the road, and one moss-line on his arm has gone *gray at the tips*, which the reader will file and be right to file). **Fern** and **Sprout** (the moss-line giants, small as hills beside him) are *tending* Bastion's arm — two giants working a third like gardeners, and this is the realm's whole character in one image.
**Sprout (moss-young, to the Nine, cheerful — a giant's voice is a landslip at low volume):** "He'll not talk. He talks *once a year*, at the Anvil-Naming, and the whole realm comes to hear him say the same four words. …Twelve days."
**Fenn (flat, the map maker, making a note — the reader's arithmetic):** "Twelve days to the festival. …The realm's got a *clock*, and it's the nicest one anybody's ever pointed at us."

### P3.2 — The welcome, from below (half page)
**Art:** The scree at the pass's foot **erupts**: the grummplings come up out of the ground like rain falling upward (the art team's comedy panel of the chapter — forty centimetres of rock-kin, boulder-bellies, each carrying something *wrong* for the situation: a spoon, a boot, a lump of quartz, a smaller grummpling). **Cinder**, the clan-mother, arrives last, unhurried, riding the crowd's noise like a chair. **Pebble** is *gone* from Grimm's shoulder and reappears at the front of the mob like a returning general. The grummplings' one idea per grummpling is on full display: **Nib** has found "the geode" (it is a potato), **Chuckle** has found "the geode" (it is a boot-heel), **Nibble** is *holding* "the geode" (it is a smaller grummpling named Peb), **Boulder-Nib** has found the *tunnel* the geode is definitely in and will not be discussing anything else. Pebble's own pocket stays *closed* (the art team: one frame of Pebble with a hand pressed to his own pocket, *not showing anybody* — nothing else; the reader does not get to see it and neither does the mountain).
**Cinder (the clan-mother — her canon 03a line, spent where it belongs, at the door of the mountain, about her own):** "Pebble's got one idea. It's the *best* idea in the mountain. I've seen *kings* with worse."
**Pebble (tiny, arms out, the whole clan behind him, and this is a grummpling's version of a speech):** "…*Home.*"
**Bramble (aside, to Viv, flat, and it is the funniest and saddest line on the page):** "…They've all got a theory. Every single one of them's wrong. They're the *happiest* people I've ever met."

## Page 4 — *The Gate of Doors (the name-stone)*

### P4.1 — The stone that knows hands (2/3 page)
**Art:** The gate: **doors inside doors inside doors** — a dwarf gate is not one door but a *sequence* of them (the art team's key architecture of Volume 4: the realm's whole law is that a door is a question, and they have built it in series; Kaelen's father's rule, cut in granite, whether or not the masons ever heard it). The outermost is a hundred feet of carved stone standing *open*, and it does not close: dwarves don't shut doors at the border, they *name* at it. In the gate's jaw, the **name-stone**: a block of unpolished bedrock, waist-high, worn smooth in the middle by a century of palms. **Anvil-Old** sits beside it on a stool that is older than he is. The Nine come through in a strip of small frames, one per hand: **Kaelen** (the stone warms — and warms *more* than for the others, one extra frame, unexplained in dialogue and explained by everything the reader knows), **Lyra**, **Morgatha** (her hand flat; her mouth moving on a *number*, not a name — the count, upward, unremarked), **Nereia** (marks the stone wet), **Azrael**, **Viv** (the stone warm; she says "Vivienne Nox" like filing), **Bramble** (the stone warms and her tag is *not* shown — the rule), **Fenn**, **Marginalia** (the cart's plate; **Anvil-Old** lets the cart through on the plate, because the cart is on the record, and this is the funniest small legal moment in the volume), **Roderick** (in the frame's background, hand on the stone, passing — *not* his panel).
**Anvil-Old (the gate's law, said as a thing said ten thousand times):** "Name on the stone, hand on the stone. It doesn't keep the name. It keeps the *hand*. …The mountain knows hands. That's what it's *for*."
**Pick (in the crowd with a scored contract braid in his beard, flat, the young miner who walked to Sylvaris in Ch. 7 — the reader knows him):** "…Khazdûrin. You came."
**Kaelen (quiet, to the stone, half to himself — the reader's line):** "…It's warm."

### P4.2 — The cold hand (bottom third, three frames)
**Art:** Three frames, and the second one is the page's key image. **(1)** **Grimm** steps up: the crowd of dwarves, the returning son, the braids countable; Anvil-Old's face going pleased before it goes wrong. **(2)** **Grimm's hand, flat on the name-stone — and where his palm sits, the stone goes *cold*** (the art team's key direction of Volume 4: not a rejection, not a crack, not a glow — **hoarfrost**, spreading out from under his fingers in a shape that is nearly a *print*, and the wet marks of every other hand on the stone *drying* around it; draw the frost in line-art, white, no color, and let the stone itself stay exactly the stone it was). **(3)** The same frame, one beat later: the crowd's faces; Pick's mouth open; and **Grimm's own face doing the thing he has been doing since Ch. 3 — the small, competent *nothing* of a man who has practiced not having a face about it.**
**Grimm (steady, loud enough for the gate, which is a lie he tells well):** "**Grimm Khazdûrin.** Come home."
**Anvil-Old (quiet, and he is not accusing anybody of anything — he is *keeping the record*, which is worse):** "…Say it again, lad."
**Grimm (quieter; and the frost *spreads* — one hairline further, drawn in exactly one frame, no SFX):** "…Grimm. Khazdûrin."
**SFX:** *(none. The art team: this panel has no sound in it. The gate is loud with dwarves and the panel is silent and the reader will feel it.)*
**Bramble (from the crowd, to a small dwarf who has been staring at her mask since the border — light, warm, the chapter's only mercy):** "It's work clothes."
**Small dwarf (gravely, after a long look):** "…Good beard."
**Bramble (a beat; then, sincerely):** "Thanks. I grew it *myself*."

## Page 5 — *The Forge That Fed (an ember in a pot)*

### P5.1 — A century on a shelf (half page)
**Art:** The Khazdûrin forge district, the upper terraces — a street of forges, and **one cold one** in the middle of them, shuttered, with the family's mark over the door (nine strands, braided, in iron). The neighbors' forges are *working* (the art team: real heat on both sides, orange on the snow — the cold room is cold *because it is a room in a warm street*, and that is the chapter's cruelty). In front of it, **Bjorn** — the anvil-old (03b canon: *keeps Askstone warm; holds the family forge's coal alive for a century*) — coming out with a **clay pot** in both hands: the family forge's last coal, kept burning a hundred years in a neighbor's cupboard, fed in shifts, *never allowed to die*. Beside him, **Dorin Khazdûrin**, the cousin who works the great-forge instead (03c) — holding the door for the old man, not looking at the shuttered forge. Bjorn offers the pot with both hands the way you hand somebody a baby.
**Bjorn (flat, old, and this is the whole realm's love in one line):** "A hundred years I've fed it. Told the wife it was a *lamp*. …It's not a lamp, lad. It's your house's *breath*, and I'm not sorry I kept it."
**Dorin (the cousin — defensive, no villainy in him at all, which is the point):** "…I work the great-forge, Grimm. Pay's real, coal's free, and *nobody's dead in it*. You want to be angry at somebody, be angry at the room."
**Grimm (taking the pot, both hands, and the reader should notice he does not thank him — a dwarf's thanks would be a *braid*, and he has nothing to braid with yet):** "…It's warm."
**Bjorn:** "It's *alive*. Warm's what it does after."

### P5.2 — The door with the rule on it (half page)
**Art:** The Khazdûrin forge's door, alone. **Grimm** in front of it; at the frame's edges (not in the panel's center, ever) **Kaelen** and **Pebble**; the wrapped **Askstone** unhooked from his back and set down against the wall, **still wrapped** (the art team: he sets it down like a man taking off a hat in a house where he isn't sure he's welcome, and he does *not* unwrap it — the whole arc's tool stays in its cloth until the arc earns it). **Grimm (to the door; and this is the second half of his pilot line — canon 03/mosaic-nine — spent at last, eleven chapters after the first half, said to a door, in a smith's voice, with nobody to hear it but the two people in the frame):** "My dad's forge's been cold a hundred years. …**I'm not leaving until it's warm again.**"
**Art note (the last frame of the page):** he puts his palm on the *door*, not on the wall; and the door is **cold**; and above his hand, cut in the lintel, the family's nine-strand mark — and the reader who counted at the gate knows that the frost is on his hand as much as the door.
**SFX:** *(wind off the terraces, thin. Nothing answers.)*

## Page 6 — *The Deep Cut (the mine, and its ledger)*

### P6.1 — The realm's craft turned inward (2/3 page)
**Art:** **The Deep Cut** — the mine as an *inverted nave* (the art team's key image: dwarf-work is *architecture*, so the Reforgers' mine is not a hole, it is a *cathedral built downward*: galleries with load-bearing beauty, stair-vaults, lamps in clean rows — and every bit of it the realm's own craft, hired, paid for, and pointed at the mountain's heart). The **raw Sigil-stone hoard** gleams in the far gallery (quartz-shot bedrock; the art team: do *not* make it pretty — make it *thirsty*). Working faces: **Norga** (Pick's brother, hauling), **Urka** (Torvik's son, at the winch), **Skaal** (03c, a great-forge smith who took the mine's coin — his hands are a smith's; that is the horror, he is *skilled*), and along every working dwarf's beard, the **contract braid** — one per dwarf, *scored* with the mine's mark, tight and new, sitting in among family braids like a debt among children. On the wall above the shaft-mouth: **the ledger** — not paper; a rack of **cut braids**, hundreds of them, in bundles, each tagged with a shaft number (the art team's key direction: the tags are dwarves' work and beautifully made; the content is somebody's word, severed and filed; **do not linger, do not glory, one frame and through**).
**Skaal (cheerful, to Grimm, genuinely proud — the ordinary face of the horror):** "Khazdûrin! Come to sign? Big hands like that, you'd make captain in a month. Coal's free, coin's good, and the mountain don't *ask* nothing."
**Norga (flat, not stopping):** "…He means it don't *answer* nothing. There's a difference. Ask my brother."
**Art note:** the lamps are clean, the work is good, the dwarves are *fine* — and the mine is quieter than a forge should be, on purpose: dwarves sing at work, and this chapter must show the reader a room of dwarves **not singing** (the art team: not one open mouth in the whole gallery; no rule is ever stated about it; nobody ever mentions it; the reader will).

### P6.2 — The shorn man (bottom third)
**Art:** **Stonehand Drek**, introduced the way the saga introduces villains: from below, at the end of the gallery, small in the frame, walking up through his own mine with two hundred dwarves' worth of silence around him (the art team's key image of the chapter's back half: **a flame dwarf with no braids at all** — the beard *shorn* to the jaw, the cut clean and old; his hands are the hands of a mason; he carries nothing; he is *comfortable* here). He stops in front of Grimm. He looks at the twelve braids with what the reader will spend the chapter identifying as *affection*.
**Drek (flat, warm, west-country, a workman — the mirror, and every line of this must be a thing Grimm has thought):** "You're the Khazdûrin boy. My grandfather's hands are in your family's forge. …You want to know why I cut mine off? Everybody asks like it's a *punishment*." *(a beat; he taps his jaw, easy)* "It's *mourning dress*. The mountain's a relative. It *died*. Somebody up here had to sit with the body instead of *singing at it*."
**Drek (next — the line the whole arc is standing on):** "You can hear it, can't you. That's the thing. All you singing families *can't*." *(looking at Grimm's face; and the reader sees Grimm decide something)* "…We'll talk. When the boy's had a night in his own house."
**Grimm (and it is the shortest line he will speak this volume — the whole trap, sprung in one word):** "…Yeah."

## Page 7 — *The Hairline (what the mountain answers)*

### P7.1 — One line across the gallery (2/3 page)
**Art:** The lowest gallery, at shift change, crowded — and then the page **cracks** (the art team's money panel of the chapter: from the ceiling to the floor, one **hairline**, drawn the full height of the panel, thin, in the stone's own color and one degree brighter, running *through* the load-bearing frame of the gallery like a ruler laid on it — and it is **not damage**; the stone around it is fine; the line is the mountain *saying something*, in the only grammar it has left). Every machine in the gallery **turns toward it** (the art team: the bore-heads on their gantries, all together, like heads on a neck — nobody has told them to; the operators' hands are not on the levers; **this must look like machinery doing what machinery does, and read as machinery doing what nothing is allowed to do**). **Pick** gets his ear to the wall in one frame (03b canon: he hears the stone-song's first crack) — and Pick's face is *delighted*, and Pick is a miner with a scored contract braid, and the reader has to hold all of that at once. **Echo** (Khazri's echo-young, in the gallery's mouth) *stops walking mid-step* — one frame, held.
**Drek (quiet, to the room, and then to the boy — the arc's engine, lit on this page):** "There. That. Nine days I've had that, and it never once did it when I was alone in here."
**Drek (next — turning, and this is the line the volume's title is standing behind):** "It's not *coughing*, boy. It's *talking*. …And the only hand in this mountain it talks *around* is a **Khazdûrin's.**"
**Grimm (silence — the art team: he gets one panel of pure face, no dialogue, no balloon, nothing to say, and his hand is on the wall, flat, where Pick's ear was.)**
**SFX:** *(the gallery's lamps; a gantry settling; nothing else. No voice. No song.)*

### P7.2 — Three frames at the rail (bottom strip)

**SEASON 2 · VOLUME 4 — "THE MOUNTAIN-ASK" · CHAPTER ONE.**

*Season 2 opened on the road Season 1 named — and eleven days east, the world stopped being the story's only country. In the stone realm, a person's word is a braid, the mine sells the binding and files the breaking, and the mountain that has been a *relative* to nine generations of smiths has stopped answering any of them. The gate's stone took the heat out of one palm and left frost where a hand had been; the forge that fed a family stayed cold for a hundred years while a neighbor kept its last coal alive in a clay pot like a candle in a sickroom; a man with no braids and a theology of the quiet said out loud the thing the dwarf has never let himself think in words. The realm was offered a seal and a war and chose neither, because a boy asked whether they could simply **ask** — and the ask was made, in the coldest room in the mountain, by a hand on an anvil that has waited a hundred years to be named. Nothing answered. One drop of frost went to water, and nobody in the room saw it, and the reader did. Twelve days, and the horn of an anvil, and a name.*
**The record's cards changed material at the braid-line. Season 2 begins by counting.**

## Production notes (for art & lettering — Ch. 14)

- **This chapter does not pay the arc.** The single hardest direction on this file: **the Mountain-Ask is not struck here, the stone-song is not heard, Bastion does not speak, Khazri does not speak, Drek does not lose anything, and the forge does not light.** The chapter delivers exactly two impossible things — the **cold hand** at the name-stone (P4.2) and **one drop of frost** (P10.2) — and both must be drawn *small*. If either panel is treated as a miracle, Volume 4 has no arc left. Everything else in the chapter is *law, labor, and grief*, drawn plainly.
- **The braids are a clock and must be countable** (P2.2 onward): nine tight, three loose — drawn identically every page of Volume 4, from the same three-quarter angle, so the reader can *read* him the way they read Lyra's rings and Nereia's flask. The chapter's title is a promise to the reader; the last frame of P8.2 is the chapter's real ending and must be one silent frame: **his hand comes up to the braids, counts, and does not finish.** No balloon. No reaction shot. No explanation. The reader has been counting since Page 2 and will feel the stop.
- **The gate's law is *hands*, not names** (P4.1–P4.2): the name-stone is warmed by a century of palms and it "keeps the hand." Every dwarf in the realm passes it and it is *pleasant* for them. Grimm's makes it cold. It is **not** a rejection and must not be drawn like one: no crack, no flare, no sound, no color — **hoarfrost**, in line-art, white, spreading in the shape of a palm-print, and every other wet handprint on the stone drying around it. Anvil-Old's "Say it again, lad" is a record-keeper's sentence, not an accusation; the realm's warmth toward Grimm must stay intact through the whole page or the arc's politics stop working.
- **The wrapped hammer** (P2.2, P5.2, P10.1): **Askstone never comes out of its cloth in this chapter**, and the reason is character, not secrecy — mosaic-nine says it was "made to ask the mountain things," and the dwarf who owns it is not ready to be *seen* asking. Draw the wrap plainly (old cloth, worn rope) from Ch. 14 on; it opens when the arc is earned, and the art team should feel the reader *wanting* it to open on every page it appears.
- **Drek is the mirror, and the mirror must be kind** (P6.2, P7.1, P11.2): he is warm, competent, and *right about everything except the conclusion*. All of his theology must be said in a workman's voice — "mourning dress," a man sitting with a body — and at least one of his lines (P6.2's "You can hear it, can't you. That's the thing. All you singing families *can't*") has to read to the reader, for one beat, as *relief* — someone finally saying what Grimm has feared for a hundred chapters. He is never sneering, never tempted, never demonic; the shorn beard is *grief*, and the chapter that explains so much must leave one thing unexplained: **why he isn't in the ledger of the braids he sells.**
- **The mine is the realm's own craft** (P6.1): dwarf architecture pointed inward — load-bearing beauty, clean lamps, skilled hands, *and no singing*. Nobody may comment on the silence. The braid-rack is one frame and the tags on it are beautiful; the cut braids in the iron basket (P9.1) are ninety-one, taggable, and *not* to be lingered over. Horror in this realm is *well-made*.
- **Maren Ashwood is a mortal running an archive** (P8.1–P8.2): the ink wall is the chapter's emotional engine — twenty-one portraits, three identical jaws, and the grandfather's drawn off somebody's memory because the realm's own archive (the song) has been dark for a century. She never weeps, never lectures, and *feeds everyone in the frame*. The mother and son never face each other in conversation (draw them shoulder to shoulder, facing the anvil-room door). She is the only character in Volume 4 who has *chosen* to live at the mountain's rim rather than inside it, and Ch. 14 should make that look like wisdom and like a wound at the same time.
- **The cold room** (P10.1–P10.2): the silent page has **no lettering at all** — no balloons, no captions, no SFX, no marginalia; the saga's Ch. 3 P3.2 beat, escalated, one per volume. The anvil under its cloth is the volume's relic: draw it beautiful and draw it *cold*. The coal in the clay pot glows **two rooms away, through a door, in the wrong room** — that is the page's one warm thing and it must be at the *edge* of the frame. Grimm says his own name (no hammer), the mountain does nothing, and then he says **Durin Khazdûrin** — and the mountain still does nothing.
