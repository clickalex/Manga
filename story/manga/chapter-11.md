> **Arc beat:** Night. The Nine reach the tree. The spring is empty — the water went where the white went — and the basin's floor is *open*: the root that moved (Ch. 10 P11.3) moved to make a way. The way is the undercroft stair, which the reader has walked once (Ch. 4 — the cell, the faceless three), and which goes further than anyone looked: below the cell's room, into the roots. The under-grove keeps the road down (they never named it — naming is the court's job; keeping is theirs), and Grief-Old leads. The Third follows. In Ashenmere, sealed for weeks behind a white road, Old Tom Rook arrives with Kaelen's letter, having walked it *door to door* (every plate is named ground; the white holds at each doorstep — the mail is late because it is walking the doors). At the bottom of the stair: the Keeping — shelves of kept things with no names on them — and below that, the root-cavern: the great tree's **heart-root**, a tower of wood going down into the dark, and in its grain, the crown-shape — *under roots is the crown* (Ch. 10 P6.1). The spring's water has pooled in a **ring** around the heart-root. Beyond the water: the white — a ring outside the ring, under, faceless, still. It is not coming. It is waiting for a door. Grimm's pilot line comes back around — *a shadow's a door with no handle; I can fix that* (Ch. 3 P9.2) — and he can't fix this one; Bramble names what the door opens onto: the white will come up through the one blank inside the ring — through *him* — unless he answers to a name no one alive remembers. The tree asks Lyra a second time — the same four words — and her answer is the chapter's turn: *"You asked one voice once. He forgot he wasn't the whole realm. Ask the realm."* She spends — a new ring appears at her temple (Ch. 2 P8.4 — the cost, drawn) — and the heart-root grows a new ring in the same moment; the tree's newest ring is a question turned *upward*. Kaelen's scar pulses (the Ch. 7 P11.2 device) — and across the map, in one page: the Voss bell rings on its peg at the Moot (no hand; the Moot writes down the hour; Wren draws the stroke); Pippin's open-mark pulses in Ashenmere; Roderick, under the bridge, has his hand in the Veil-water and feels it shiver. The Third turns from the water toward Kaelen — and Morgatha's count stops for one panel: Tarkhan turned like that, at the end; it meant he still knew which way she was. Senna answers the letter the only way the town has: she carries a plate and the five-year-old sign past the cord onto the white road — the town's road, begun from the town's end. The Nine split: Lyra goes up to ask the realm; Kaelen stays down with the Third at the water's edge — the door, held from the *inside* (Ch. 1 P3.2, paid). The water breathes once. The card: **Here hold the named.**
> **Motifs advanced:** the door (the held door — Aldric's, from the inside; Roderick's, from outside; Kaelen's, from inside; the Third's face — a door with no handle; Senna's sign — OPEN DOORS on the white road), the count (stops one panel — Tarkhan's turn; resumes), the map (Fenn marks the way down — signatures, not names: *a mark's a promise to come back and name it*), the ledger (nothing taken — the bell rang for something *kept*), the bell (the second self-ring; the peg; the hour, written), the water (the ring; thinner; Nereia *in* it; the breath — waking escalation #6), the scar (pooled to the bottom edge; the pulse), the ring (a new ring at Lyra's temple — the cost, drawn — and the heart-root's newest ring, in the same moment), the plate (the town's first plate *out*), the margin (Senna's — in the letter, then on the road), the record (the hexad: "Here hold the named."), the white (the ring beyond the water — under; waits for a door; still no sound, no face, no word)
