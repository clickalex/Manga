# Ashenmere — The Town of the Pilot

> **What it is:** A Seam town on the Terrania–Sylvaris border — "a town built on the line between two worlds." Human timber on one side, elven stone-and-leaf on the other, and the **Veil-water** running under the main road like a pale river. Every page of the pilot's Volume 1 happens here (Ch. 1) or in its border grove (Ch. 2).
> **Canon sources:** [../story/manga/chapter-01.md](../story/manga/chapter-01.md), [../story/manga/chapter-02.md](../story/manga/chapter-02.md), [../story/03b-ensemble.md](../story/03b-ensemble.md) (the Terrania ensemble), [../story/02-races-and-realms.md](../story/02-races-and-realms.md) (the Seams / Terrania).

## Design guide (for the art team)

- **Two rooflines, one town.** Human timber (warm browns, `#a58a6f` / `#8a6f52`) on the Terrania side; elven stone-and-leaf (cool greens, `#7fb069` over `#8d99ae` stone) on the Sylvaris side. The two meet **mid-town** — the seam is visible from the temple hill (Ch. 1 P1.1, the establishing shot; the town's whole story in one wide).
- **The Veil-water** under the road: a pale silver shimmer (`#c9d4d8`) where the Veil is close to the surface. It ripples with no source (Ch. 1 P6.3 — the first tremor). It answers the Voss bell (Ch. 2 P5.3, Ch. 3 P6.2 — the sea's first line, remembered).
- **The laundry lines** are strung with thread in **nine colors** (the Nine-Color Key, see [../character/mosaic-nine.md](../character/mosaic-nine.md)) — the town's whole cosmology, hung out to dry.
- **Weather is a character.** The pilot's weather script: Ch. 1 = morning rain → the rain *stops falling on Kaelen* during the bloom (P8.3, the halo) → rain resumes (P9.3, the world resumed) → dusk, first stars (P6.1). Ch. 2 = overcast dawn → clear by departure. (Canon: Roderick "knows every Seam in the world by its *weather*" — the town's moods are weather, and the weather reports what the Veil is doing.)

## The Festival of Open Doors (the town's law, the Seams' Re-Oath in miniature)

Once a year, **every house feeds a stranger**: a plate set out, bread on it, the door open. The Covenant's three rules, in order: **Open Doors. Feed a stranger. Remember a name.**

- It is the **Seams Re-Oath at town scale** (canon spine #7: *the Seams Re-Oath first* — the town's is first, and it's the one the reader meets: the light enters where the doors are open).
- **Spent on the page:** the plates at every doorstep (Ch. 1 P4.1) → the town follows the Nine at the departure (Ch. 2 P12.1 — "No one cheers (it's not a victory; it's a *sending*)") → Marta's road-bread (Ch. 2 P11.2, Ch. 3 P7.3) → the scarves (one per traveler, the hearth carried) → the court table's nine-colored string (Ch. 6 — the hearth arrives before the people).
- **The running gag:** Hilda's Re-Oath petition is *four hundred pages* (Ch. 1 P4.3 — "I'm on forty") — the town's law is also the town's paperwork; Viv will find it funny, and then *own* it (Ch. 4).

## What the town is *for* (franchise notes)

- **Ashenmere is the franchise's front door.** Every medium starts here (manga Ch. 1, anime S1 ep. 1, the TCG's Set 1 "Crown of Ashenmere," the PC game's opening). The town must be *re-learnable* in one wide (P1.1): two rooflines, one shimmering road, nine-colored laundry, one hill, one bell.
- **The prequel** (*Ashenmere Blacksmith*, Aldric Voss's story) is told in this town — the forge, the bell, the door, five years earlier. The pilot withholds the father's face (he is *ink and bell* only, Ch. 1 P3.2, P10.2); the prequel is his.
- **The town stays.** After the Nine leave (Ch. 2 P12), Ashenmere holds its shape: Senna at the margin, Pippin's open-mark pulsing *once*, Marta's plates still out. The town's part in the pilot is to *hold the door open* — that's the whole job, and it's done.
