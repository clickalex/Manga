## Realm notes (the location side)

### 1 · Terrania — the mortal lands
A thousand nations, one argument. Republics, empires, merchant cities; the "Hundred Courts" of debate. **Humans** (vast sub-races; fast lives), **halflings** (the Seam-market backbone; brew-laws — "a promise is a brew"), **gnomes** (tinker-guilds; a gnome's name is a serial number they *earn* — Fenn's people), **beastkin** (clan by beast-line; the Fence-Laws conflict, Act 2), and the **Openbloods** (humans with visible borrowed Sigils — half-accepted, half-feared; the moral canary of the realm — Pippin, the pilot's canary).
**On the page:** warm copper and timber; the argument is *visible* (markets, petitions, four hundred pages of paperwork — Hilda's). Ashenmere is its border-town face.
**Pilot:** Ch. 1 (all of it) + Ch. 2 (the town sends the Nine).

### 2 · Sylvaris — the verdant realm
Time-cities; "the Centuries" as calendar; the pride of memory. **High Elves** (silver-green hair, long-veined skin, the rings as age), **Wood Elves** (forest clans; **grove-council law — every tree a vote**), **Shadow Elves** (exiles who understand death; keepers of the Under-Grove; the realm's secret sages — they name the Unraveling *first*), **Fauns** (herd-musicians; instinct-law), **Sprites** (30 cm lights; the forest's immune system), **Treants** (40 m, speak in seasons — Rootdeep, the last guard, speaks *once*), **Mothkin** (giant gentle moths; the realm's historians — they write history *in silk*; the silk is the record the team carries).
**On the page:** the Unraveling's grammar is *born* here (full color → washed → line-art → white); the grove is the realm's face (the great tree's rings are names — "a ring is a name the tree gives itself"); the withered court is the realm's *wound*, and the Re-Oath (Ch. 6) is the realm's *answer* — the first realm to re-oath (the grove-council vote, Faelis's line, The First Dawn).
**Pilot:** Ch. 2 (the border grove) → Ch. 4–6 (the court, the King, the Re-Oath). Its color: silver-green `#7fb069` — Kaelen's scar blooms it near the grove (Ch. 1 P8.3).
**Season 1, under the court (Ch. 4, Ch. 7, Ch. 10–11):** the **undercroft** — a stone stair below the inner gate, the one place in the court the green seam never reached (Ch. 4 P8.1: the cell of three, faceless; from Ch. 7 the un-named Third stands at its top). Below the cell's first landing the stair becomes *root* and goes on. Below it, **the Keeping** — the Under-Grove's lowest room (Ch. 11 P5.1): a round room in the root-mass, shelved in living wood with the *kept things* of the un-named (a shoe, a bow, a bell-rope) and **no labels** — the names are what was taken; the things are what the names come home to ("what we cannot name, we keep"). Below the Keeping, the **root-cavern**: the great tree's **heart-root** (a tower of wood going down into the dark; the Everbloom Crown is not an object — it is the tree's *first ring*, a crown-shape in the grain), ringed by the spring's descended water (the song, holding), ringed beyond that by the white. The road down was never named: a tree names its rings, not its roots.

### 3 · Khazadûm — the stone realm
The Mountain-Family law: the mountain is kin, not property. **Deep Dwarves** (multi-braided beards; stone-scarred; the stone-song — Grimm's family gift, gone quiet the day the Reforgers started mining), **Flame Dwarves** (volcano-smiths; pride as a forgeable metal), **Golems** (servants gaining souls; the "First Waking" rite), **Stone Giants** (20 m, moss-veined — the living walls; they *are* the mountain's borders), **Grummplings** (40 cm rock-kin, boulder-bellies, one idea each — Pebble's people; their luck is *statistically real*).
**On the page:** forge-ember `#e07a3f` over stone `#8d99ae`; the mountain is drawn *alive* (it leans, it listens — and, in Vol. 4, it *consents*). Grimm's hammer Askstone is "made to ask the mountain things" — the realm's law, made prop.
**Pilot:** Ch. 3 (Grimm, Pebble, the bridge). Its own arc: Vol. 4 (*The Mountain-Ask* — Grimm's Chord with Kaelen: the stone-song read by a mortal).

**On the page — Vol. 4 (Ch. 14, *Twelve Braids*).** The realm's law is *braids*: a beard is an account (family braids; debt braids, kept *loose* — cutting one is the payment); there is no pen, no court, no appeal; the forge-duel is the only review (canon 03a, Molka). Named places, in the order the reader meets them:
- **The braid-line** (the border) — the old seam-road pillars, every one carved in nine-strand interlace by different hands across a century; the realm's edge is a family album you walk through. **Warden Bastion** stands at the pass, and the **moss-line** giants (Fern, Sprout) tend him like gardeners. *He speaks once a year, at the Anvil-Naming* (canon 03a) — the border's silence is a rule, not a mood.
- **The gate of doors** — the realm's gates are dwarven doors **in series** (a door is a question, cut in granite, whether or not the masons ever heard a boy say it). At the gate's jaw, the **name-stone**: unpolished bedrock worn smooth by a century of palms — *it keeps hands, not names*. A hand rests; the stone warms. Past it, the stone-roads carry the **FOR** name cut a foot deep.
- **The Khazdûrin forge, relit** (Ch. 21, the coda) — the family's own forge, cold a century, swept and warmed by its own line's hands: the anvil's face scrubbed bare and oiled, a new wick, the **hearth lit from the First Fire carried down the terraces in a pot** by the flame-clan (Grudka lays the kindling herself, silently), and the **crawl-space** under the floor, which the grummplings moved into the day the frost left and dug out into a proper little shaft. The volume's last page: Askstone on its stand, the century's pot on the shelf with **nails in it**, **nine hundred other forges still cold**, a queue of dwarves on the terraces with pots — and the dusty child asleep in the ash-sweep under Pebble's coat.
- **The flame-forge (the First Fire)** (Ch. 20) — the flame-clan's hall on the terraces: a long house with **one great hearth** at its end, worn into a shallow bowl by centuries of feet, and **dead since the mountain stopped answering** — closed by a **braided mourning cord** sealed in wax, cuttable only by the Forgemother. Because a **forge-duel is held at the First Fire**, the realm's reviews have been held at *working* forges ever since; Ch. 20 re-lights it from the Khazdûrin pot (**Grudka cuts the cord without one word**) and it is the realm's first fire in a century. The empty clay pot goes on the forge's own shelf with its rag folded on top.
- **The forge-duel (the realm's only review of an oath)** (Ch. 20) — canon 03a, on the page: held at the First Fire, kept by the **champion** (Molka). Three clauses, read out of the book on the record: **(1)** two bars in one fire — the bars are the words; **(2)** strike the iron, never the dwarf; **(3)** the duel ends when a bar is **worked true or let go cold**. It is a *court* and not a fight; the tragedy it cannot handle is a man with nothing left to break.
- **The keeper's election & the blank line** (Ch. 21) — the realm's first election, held on the Court Floor and decided by **knuckles on iron** (Molka, flame-dwarf, one year); the keeper's stool pays Ch. 17's cushion (Khazur carries it himself); and the **objections page** gains its **fourth line** (*Ragna: “Whose is the mine's?”*), ruled on in the keeper's own chalk beside the mine's anvil line: ***BLANK. BY LAW. FILLED THE DAY THE CUT IS THE REALM'S.*** — the realm's one deliberate blank, kept with a year on the waiting (the *other* deliberate blank is Maren Ashwood's **tenth frame**, still empty at the rim house).
- **The new oath form (the burn-signature)** (Ch. 20, made in the flame-forge square and used before any sovereign ruled on it) — the realm's answer to nine thousand void plates: **a word is cut in a small bar at a hearth and the bar goes home with the signer**; the word is **read whole — both halves — before the thumb goes down**; **whoever holds the bar while it is signed is marked with it**; and the signature is a **thumb-print pressed into hot iron, once in a life** — small, permanent, and **unforgeable**, which is why the mine cannot sell it. The **mountain's own stroke** (Echo's mark) is cut into the head of every bar, and the realm's **readers** — elected by the rank, not hired (Viv's clause) — read each bar aloud at the shaft-heads before it is signed. **Torvik** is the first reader; **Molka** (palms bound) holds bars for signers; **Norga** ties each bar's cord in the old mine knot and a second knot of her own.
- **The great-forge & the forge terraces** — the working heart of the realm: rows of forges with the cold one in the middle (the Khazdûrin forge, marked in iron with nine strands). Ch. 20: the terraces work **cold** (filing, fitting, hand-lamps) and the fire travels up the **fifty-eight** door by door in pots and crucibles — nine hundred forges still cold at the chapter's end, drawn as a patient queue. **The cold room** is the Khazdûrin family forge itself: shuttered a century, hoarfrost on the iron, the anvil under a cloth without a name, and the family's last coal alive *two rooms away* — in a neighbor's clay pot, fed in shifts for a hundred years by **Bjorn** the anvil-old.
- **The Deep Cut** — the mine: dwarf architecture pointed inward (galleries like a nave, clean lamps, skilled hands, **no singing**). At the shaft-mouth, the **braid-rack** (cut braids, bundled, tagged by shaft number) and the box of new **contract braids** waiting for tomorrow's signers; in the lower galleries, the vein of **raw Sigil-stone** the Reforgers are cutting, and the hairline the mountain draws when a Khazdûrin is standing in the room.
- **The house at the rim** — where the mountain meets daylight: half dwarf-masonry, half Seam timber and glass, garden boxes on the shelf, and **Maren Ashwood's ink wall** (twenty-one mortal portraits, framed in forge-iron — the family archive a realm keeps in *ink* because the song went dark a century ago).
- **The mountain-court** — a hall cut *around* bedrock rather than out of it: the sovereign sits before a wet, unstruck cliff, on a stump with one new cushion.
- **The shipping dock** (Ch. 17) — the Cut's above-ground loading bench, where forfeited braids leave in twine for the west (a Seam carter's road; destination Terrania, *The Desk*). Ordinary, professional, and the quietest horror in the volume: nobody here knows what is in the bales and nobody here is a villain.
- **The Court Floor** (Ch. 17) — the deepest gallery of the Cut, converted by law and by hand into the realm's court: **nine hundred anvils** in graded ranks facing the hall's raw wall (families' marks forward, aisles between), the **eight Speaker chairs** in an arc at the head, a low stone table with the **iron basket of cut braids**, the **braid-racks dark** at the far end, and the record's exhibit hung at the mouth in a frame **the mine built and bolted on its own rock**. The realm *moves in* for the reading: bedrolls, kettles, washing lines, children asleep across anvils. Nine thousand dwarves sit on their own families' anvils; that is the jury. At the head of the middle aisle stands the **low stone table** — the record's table, where the festival's book lies open, where the **court lamp** burns, and where the realm's witnesses stand when they are called (Ch. 18). Beside it: the **reader's seat**, a stump with a cushion somebody gave the Grand-Anvil for his back. The book itself carries the realm's whole law in its **first rank, above the counting**, and at its back an **objections page** — ruled and blank for four hundred years, cut by a dwarf who expected the realm to argue (Ch. 18: three lines entered, in the keeper's hand); and at its very end **the year's last leaf: the naming of the anvils**, nine hundred ruled lines, a shaft and a door and a family on each, and a blank at the end of every line where the mountain writes the name (Ch. 19: nine hundred of them filled, in the realm's own bad handwriting). The mine's own **working anvil** is one of the nine hundred, and its line is the one that stays blank.
- **The year-closing (the rack-walk)** (Ch. 17) — the keeper of the book walks the braid-racks and **shuts the rack-lamps one at a time**; while the year is unread, no business may be conducted in the Cut. Business resumes when the year's word is judged.
- **The Asking** (Ch. 17) — the Naming's eve rite: the keeper opens the book and asks the year's question, the only question the book permits her — *“Who speaks for the mountain?”* — and the realm answers with the four words it has used since the Cut opened: *“Stone waits.”*
- **The old forge district — “the fifty-eight”** (Ch. 16) — one long working street of forges whose **lintels all carry the nine-strand Khazdûrin mark**, cut two and three generations back by the family that no longer owns a single one of them. The realm's memory is *oral*, so this street is its archive: six old women, six faces, no two alike. One doorway in the middle of the row has had the mark **chiselled off** by somebody, on purpose; the question of who is never answered. The one *drawn* Durin in the mountain lives here — on the back of a slate in **Varda**'s keadsake box, signed **K.**
- **The mine's shipping office** (Ch. 16) — the Cut's above-ground books: a weighing-scale, a stamp, a route map with one brass pin west (**Terrania**), and **bales of forfeited contract braids** in packing twine, tagged by shaft and by year. Standing order, three years: *The Desk* — signed *I. Vane*.
- **The Hall of Speakers** (Ch. 15) — the listeners' school at the mouth of the old listening-galleries: acoustic architecture (a shaped vault, tuned slots) built like the inside of an ear; eight named stone chairs in a shallow arc facing **a wall of raw bedrock**, not a throne. Seven chairs empty; **Khazri stands** and does not speak (canon 03a). One apprentice, **Echo**, keeps a slate with a single repeated stroke on it — the shape of the last thing his teacher said, which nobody in the realm can read.
- **The Signing Hall** (Ch. 15) — where the mine sells itself: not a hall but a bench, a brazier, a kettle of soup, and the **ledger crate** (an iron box of tags on rails, a thousand deep, one per miner). Deliberately *pleasant*; the terms are struck on a plate in the open (*sworn by the mountain; the braid is the mine's; the Cut's business stays in the Cut; the mine feeds the house*). **Norga** ties the contract braids — the mine's knot in the signer's own hair.
- **The anvil haul / the cart road down** (Ch. 15) — the road from the terraces to the Cut, walked at night by families carrying their own anvils, lit by **the mine's lamps because the mine paid for them**: the move that relocates the Naming (and with it the realm's yearly court of account) into the mine's own hole. Law, not force — *the festival is held where the most anvils stand, and the anvils go where the coal is.*
- **The cistern & water-lamps** (Ch. 15) — the Cut's water, measured by **old depth-marks cut into the stone, every one of them above the current level**: the mountain is quietly going dry, and nobody says so.
- **The Anvil-Naming** (the realm's festival, twelve days out at Ch. 14) — every forge carries its anvil down to the great-forge floor and the keeper **names it for the hands that wore it**; the whole realm attends, and the stone giants say their one sentence a year. Kept by **Duelist Molka** (canon 03a). The Khazdûrin anvil is the oldest un-named anvil in the realm — and a name needs an answer.

### 4 · Ulgar — the Barrens
Blood-clan honor-law; the **Ultravote** (all clans, one throat — the Barrens' "king" is a *procedure*, not a person). **Orcs** (massive, scar-ornamented, bone-and-iron regalia — Morgatha's people), **Trolls** (regenerating, river-gardeners — they fix *everything*; subverted monster, canon), **Cyclopes** (9 m, one great eye, forge-sages; keepers of the oldest maps — Gorakh's people), **Minotaurs** (labyrinth-warriors; every dispute a duel of the maze), **Goblins** (1 m, many-armed, marketplace-clever — the logistics heart of the world; the pilot's couriers are their cousins), **Kobolds** (small, gem-eyed, tidy — cave-cartographers; Fenn's project).
**On the page:** bone-iron `#9aa5a6`; the oath-script (legible scars — Morgatha's forearms, the body as book); the war-song count (the pilot's hidden clock, the Barrens' law, made lettering).
**Pilot:** Ch. 3 (Morgatha). Its own arc: Vol. 5 (*The One Throat* — Morgatha's choice; the code rewrites itself: *the oath to keep a life outranks the oath to keep the code*).

**On the page — Vol. 5 (Ch. 22, *War-Song*).** The Barrens' law is **carried, not written**: the code lives in **oath-script cut into forearms** (legible as writing; the oldest clauses faded silver, the newest pink, amendments struck by a second cut *through* the first) and in **memory rehearsed in rooms** — there is no text, no clerk, no paper, and **the record is a horn**. An amendment is not law until the clans that vote for it **cut it into themselves**. Named places and procedures, in the order the reader meets them:
- **The debt-line** (Ch. 22) — the border pass, and its **black slab** cut over with oath-script in hundreds of hands, in hundreds of languages, across centuries. Every traveller **reads their own worst debt aloud** before entering (kept by **Drog**, an elder, who reads *his* debt back in exchange); nobody is detained, nobody pays, and the realm calls it manners. The queue is a court, a market and a liturgy at once, and it never stops.
- **The river-caste's gardens** (Ch. 22) — the trolls' worked terraces along the ford: irrigation ditches, fish-traps, a bridge with **nine labelled repairs**, a mill-wheel with mismatched paddles. The realm's menders fix everything, and **a mender's cut mark** on a wall, a glass or a flask means *this was broken and is not any more* — the volume's visual grammar of kindness. **Glintfin** mends Nereia's flask here.
- **The Ultravote ground** (Ch. 22–Ch. 24) — a shallow, hand-cut bowl in the plain: a ring of **clan stones** (one per clan, each cut with that clan's oath-script and mended like the family that owns it — and one **newest stone**, the house of Tarkhan's, cut for the first time in Ch. 23) around the low, waist-high **throat-stone** at the floor's centre. **The speaker** (**Durgan the Unbent**) may voice only the assembled will of *every* clan — with one stone empty the realm **may read and may not decide**, and the realm spent a year holding *readings* it could not vote on. The clan stones' shadows run long at first light; the bowl is also the realm's gathering place.
- **The vote, and the day the throat changed** (Ch. 24) — the realm's rite, drawn once: a chief stands at their stone, hand flat, **face up**, and gives the word *into the bowl*; the Speaker marks it; **Ulgan's drum** gives four beats and a rest; and the stones speak **in the order they were cut** — oldest first, newest last. Kettles are **put away** on a vote day (readings keep kettles). The realm's first decision in a year: **seven yes, two no, no stone silent**, with the two noes entered **in words, with reasons** (Viv's third term, cut into the maze's gatepost in Ch. 23). Then the **throat changed**: the new clause carried a witness-line, *the code does not read its own witness*, so **Durgan took his hands off the throat-stone for the first time in forty years** and the woman the clause cost — **Khura** — read the new law aloud. The bowl's answer to a reading is **standing, not cheering.**
- **The cutting-tables, and the law in arms** (Ch. 24) — the bowl's floor during the cutting week: planks, lamps, three knives at once, boiled cloth, and the menders' benches at the row's end, because in the Barrens **skin is infrastructure**. *A clause is not law until the clan that voted for it cuts it into itself*: **Targa** of the Barrens-forge forges the first mark (into the captain's forearm, on the throat-stone), and the realm queues; the eleven arms the knife cannot take receive the **first word, small**, palm-side of the wrist, and no child carries a clause. **Sprocket's ledger** keeps the count in the realm's cut grammar (forty-one the first night; ninety-six by the second).
- **The carrying, and the realm's ledger** (Ch. 24) — the rider that names the volume: **a debt this holding releases comes home to the realm, and is carried by the one throat until the realm is done with it.** Nobody forgiven; nothing cancelled. The **debt-horn** — which is **Khura's**, kept in the house of Tarkhan — now carries the clause, the witness-line, the limit, the carrying, the tally and **both noes with their reasons**, the margin-word *the lawful hand* under **GORAKH**'s name, and Tarkhan's own page: **unpaid · held by the realm**. The Speaker's office has two jobs now: voice the will, and **watch the books**.
- **The surety-hall (the house of Tarkhan)** (Ch. 22) — where the realm's War-Chief is **housed**: a two-storey red-stone house with a garden, a goat, washing on the line, a child asleep in the corner, a chair made for a large old man's back, a **mended window**, and **a bolt leaning inside the doorframe** — in reach, dusty, untouched for a year. Not a cell; not a jailer: the code's *fifth holding*, drawn as a life. **Khura**, the house's oath-keeper, keeps the **debt-horn**.
- **The debt-horn** (Ch. 22) — the realm's archival object: a black war-horn carved with cut script, holding the record of a blood-debt — **Tarkhan's name**, the cell, the date, and (in a newer, foreign cut) **the carrying**: a clause moving the debt onto the dead man's nearest blood, where it may be held as *surety* until answered, signed with one foreign name (**Gorakh**). The realm's laws, deaths and debts all live in horns, stones and arms; nothing in the Barrens is written on paper.
- **The maze** (Ch. 22–Ch. 23; the duel culture) — a red-rock labyrinth cut down into the plain, entered by a narrow slot under a **horn-slab** on which every loss ever taken in it is cut (ten thousand strokes; the newest still pale). **Tauron** keeps the centre; **Horn** carves the record; **Maze** keeps the rules and gives the map. **The maze is a building that *is* a document:** every wall is cut floor-to-shoulder with the realm's **entire code** in order, in hands that change every few metres, worn smooth at hand height by ten thousand walkers and lit by an ordinary lamplighter on a shift. The walls **contradict** each other at the turns (third holding against fifth; debt against guest-right; blood-price against ford), so a walker must *choose*, may walk back, and often does — which is why the queue of people who have come to lose is cheerful. **The centre chamber is the only room with bare walls**, because the centre is where somebody has to *say* it. The rules: one walker; the walls are read, not fought; the champion never leaves the centre; **everybody loses, the loss is carved, and the maze goes to the loser** — the realm's gift-logic, in architecture. **Viv's three terms** (Ch. 23 P3.2: the walker names what was lost in words; the cut goes in at the *declaration*; the record reads both ways) are cut into the gatepost in front of the queue — the realm's first procedural amendment in a hundred years.
- **The old-forges & the map-room** (Ch. 23) — the cyclopes' valley east of the maze: stone workshops terraced into red rock, **map-stones laid flush in the floors**, and forge-halls built *around* their smiths (the wall of a face at one end, and in it **one great eye, lid shut**, the lid a worked and mended stone — architecture, not anatomy). The **map-room** is the only place in the Barrens with *pages*: maps on rails (the **family map** — four generations of hide sewn into one sheet, the Rift drawn as a fault at the bottom right); the **copyists' bench** (the kobold cave-carts: Crystal, Stone, Fire-Knob — the realm's tradition is that a map is **cut** by hand, not drawn, and that *a copy is the asker's work*); and, cut over the door: ***A READING MAY NOT BE REFUSED. A COPY IS THE ASKER'S WORK.*** By the door hangs the **request-shelf**: every lawful ask ever made in this room, kept as a horn-sliver carrying the ask, the date, the asker's name and mark — a room that cannot say no keeps the names of everyone it has said yes to. **Keepers:** **Oros the Old Eye** (the sage), **One-Eye**, **Iron-Eye**, **Ash-Eye** (map-keeper).
- **The request-shelf — the volume's evidence** (Ch. 23 P7.2) — among hundreds of slivers, one reading *copy of the holdings, all fords, all debts, the Rift-line included*, with the room's own keeper-note (*asked lawfully; a lawful ask may not be refused; copy made, one*), the signature **GORAKH** in a hand this room's sage taught, and the *same family mark as the map's century-old margin* — and, in the copy-ledger, the second line: *the asker's companions read with him, twice — four of them.* The realm's virtue, documented as the attack surface it is.
- **The courier-nest & the swarm** (Ch. 22) — the goblins' network (canon 03b): satchels, relays, weather-knowledge, no sentiment, and the fastest news in nine realms — and in Ch. 22, **no letters from the south**, which is the season's cold water.

### 5 · Thalassa — the deep sea
Tide-cities; **chorus-parliament; law must be sung true to bind**. **Merfolk** (scale-armor, coral-crown regalia — Nereia's people), **Sirens** (court-vocalists; a siren's verdict is a duet), **Undines** (water-spirits with no fixed body — the diplomats of the deep; they deliver the "first song" fragment), **Krakenkin** (30 m, ancient memory in every tentacle — they *remember* the Leviathan's first dream), **Selkies** (tide-wanderers; the ferry-folk between realms — the deep's only travelers), and **the Leviathan** (not a race: a sleeping whale-god, 20 km, a *load-bearing dream* the realm rests on — the Thalassa arc's MacGuffin: the Reforgers want it as Vaelthorn's first meal).
**On the page:** deep teal `#3e8e9e`; the law is *music* (the first song — the sea's first law — stopped reaching the surface a year ago; Nereia was the voice; the pilot's rhyme: the Voss bell's mortal note *is* the first line, and the sea remembers it, Ch. 3 P6.2); Nereia's mood-markings (the reader reads her *before* her dialogue); the water-flask (the ticking clock, drawn).
**Pilot:** Ch. 3 (Nereia; the Veil-water as Thalassa's shore). Its own arc: Vol. 6 (*The First Song* — the chorus Re-Oath; The Chorus-True, a thousand voices).

### 6 · Emberfall — the infernal realm
**Demons** (Archduke castes; horned, fire-veined, robes of contract-gold — Malachar's people; Oathfire law: contracts are sacred, breaking one burns you), **Djinn** (smoke-and-flame; wish-engineers — a wish is a contract with *clauses*; the Reforgers' "Scribe" is a djinn — comedy + horror), **Imps** (1 m tricksters, many pockets — the chaos-market dealers; the team's information brokers, unreliable, invaluable), **Salamanders** (flame-folk of hot-springs — the fire-realm's gentle counterweight), **Hellhounds** (pack-mind war-beasts, oath-branded collars — Malachar's broken pack = his tragic tell).
**On the page:** oath-red `#d1495b` over contract-gold; hell drawn as a *courtroom* (the villain is a *lawyer*, the fire is the *enforcement*); the Seven Archdukes are a *committee* of oaths (the most fun villains-to-allies conversion in the book, canon 03).
**Pilot:** — (its arc is Vol. 7, *The Oath-Eater*).

### 7 · Seraphel — the sky realm
**Angels/Seraphim** (winged orders, 2/4/6 wings; law-etched halos; the Light-Orders), **Starborn** (sun-children, constellation-scarred; the fate-readers — "the stars suggest, they don't *order*"; they first *see* the gray spreading beyond Vael — the sequel seed), **Wind Sylphs** (storm-sailors; the high-cloud fleets — the fastest combat in the book), **Fallen Angels** (a *faction*, not a race — gray-winged exiles, dimmed halos; the **Wandering Order**: oaths broken to protect — Azrael's people; the moral heart of Seraphel), **Gargoyles** (the Carved Order — angels in stone by night; they *choose* stone to listen better; the secret police who become the team's allies).
**On the page:** dawn-gold `#e8b04b`; the sky is *law* (the halos are etched; the cracks are legible — Azrael's halo crack runs *like a river*); the fallen's gray wings are the color of the Unraveling and the most human color on the page (the pilot's subtlest visual argument, Ch. 6).
**Pilot:** Ch. 6 (Azrael — the pilot's first sky; the Wing-Broken *seeded*). Its own arc: Vol. 8 (*The Gray Wings* — Azrael's trial).

### 8 · Nocturne — the shadow realm
Death is a second life with a second set of manners. **Vampires** (death-nobility; the Regency Council; "the Second Life" is a society, not a curse — Viv's people; the "dead can love the sun" arc), **Ghouls** (humble workers, soil-stained — the realm's working class; the moral plain-speaking voice), **Wraiths** (memory-echoes, candle-lit — the restless dead; the realm's living archive; they hold the stolen memories Vexmourne took — the vault), plus the dead's own politics (the Prime Regent's deal, the name-funerals, the Regency's treason-pin).
**On the page:** candle-violet `#7a6a9c`; the dead drawn with *manners* (the afterlife's etiquette is visible — cards, queues, second-life social climbing); the first lie, made prop (the sunscreen — "the Council says the sun burns. It doesn't burn."); Viv's Veilward double (one frame *late*, never ahead — memory-shadow, the dead's manners made visual).
**Pilot:** Ch. 3 (Viv — the first sun-panel of a dead woman; the Regency pin). Its own arc: Vol. 9 (*The Name-Funeral* — Viv's choice, at her mother's funeral, in the sun, together, saying nothing).

### 9 · The Seams — the in-between lands
The outcasts' patchwork homeland — where the light enters. No crown, no throne: the **Seam Moot** is *a room* (and the room is the law; the Moot's one rule: **a name is a home**). The Seams are made of everyone the realms didn't want: **tengu** (the news-service — Kessa's people; "truth by the blade's length"), **oni** (the mask-culture — a face is a door; the mask is the door *labeled* — Bramble's people; Tessen-Old, the mask-keeper), the **changelings** (never the same face twice — the Seams' children, the Wandering Order's hidden ones), the **in-between** (every mixed, every borrowed, every *almost*), and the **Mosaics** (the rarest: a piece of the First Being — Kaelen, and Null, and the blank edge of the map).
**On the page:** **all nine colors at once** (`#f4f4f0` white light — the only "color" that is *every* color); patchwork architecture (two rooflines, one town — Ashenmere is the template); the Moot's *room* (the realm's sovereign is furniture — a table, nine chairs, the law is the seating plan); the news by paper (Kessa's headlines are the Seams' parliament).
**Pilot:** Ch. 1 (Ashenmere — the town's three rules *are* the Seams' Re-Oath in miniature: *Open Doors. Feed a stranger. Remember a name.*) → Ch. 6 (Bramble; the Seams' children, home at the table). Its arc runs the whole saga (Vol. 10, *The Seams First* — Null, the dark twin).

## The geography rule (for all media)

1. **The Veil-water connects everything.** Every realm borders water, and the water is the Veil's (the shimmer, the ripple, the first song). The ferry (Osric) is the realm-crossing's *face* — the team travels by water, road, and bridge, never by sky (until Ch. 6 — the pilot's first sky is the sky *arriving*, not a route).
2. **The Seams are the center, not the middle.** On Fenn's map, the Seams are the *center* of the nine-pointed crown (the patchwork colors, "the only part of the map with *more* colors than the rest," Ch. 1 P12.1) — the light enters from the middle, and the gray eats from the edges (the Unraveling's geometry: *the difference is sipped from the rim first*).
3. **Every realm has a door and a law.** The door is physical (the grove-gate, the bridge, the Moot's room); the law is procedural (the grove-council vote, the Ultravote, the chorus, the Fire-Court, the purity-council, the name-funeral, the popular oath, the seam-moot). The Reforgers lose at every door *by the door's own law* (canon design rule, 03) — the realm defeats its Usurper with its own mechanism. The team wins; **the realm does the healing.**
