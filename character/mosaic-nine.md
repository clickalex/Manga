# The Mosaic Nine — Lead Character Design Sheets

> The nine leads, one per realm's *face*, designed to be dropped into any medium (manga, anime, movie, TCG, PC game) as a standalone face of the IP. Each sheet: design, on-page palette (hex, for the art team and the card/game UI), motifs & props, pilot anchors, and adaptation notes.
> **Canon:** profiles and pilot lines from [../story/03-characters.md](../story/03-characters.md); realm & sigil system from [../story/02-races-and-realms.md](../story/02-races-and-realms.md) and [../story/04-power-system.md](../story/04-power-system.md); pilot appearances from [../story/manga/](../story/manga/).
> **The Nine-Color Key** (used by the scarves, the record page, and every "nine" graphic in the franchise):

| Realm | Color | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Terrania (Mortal Lands) | copper | `#b87333` |
| Sylvaris (Verdant) | silver-green | `#7fb069` |
| Khazadûm (Stone) | forge-ember | `#e07a3f` |
| Ulgar (The Barrens) | bone-iron | `#9aa5a6` |
| Thalassa (Deep Sea) | deep teal | `#3e8e9e` |
| Emberfall (Infernal) | oath-red | `#d1495b` |
| Seraphel (Sky) | dawn-gold | `#e8b04b` |
| Nocturne (Shadow) | candle-violet | `#7a6a9c` |
| The Seams (In-Between) | white light (all colors at once) | `#f4f4f0` |

> A Seams-colored thing shows *all nine* — that is the whole point (the Seams is where the light enters). The nine-colored scarf, the record page's borders, the Re-Oath hall's banners, the TCG's "Seams" card frame: all nine, in this order.

## 1 · KAELEN VOSS — Human · Mosaic · 16 · Terrania

**The face of the crack.** Protagonist. The only Mosaic of his generation — a piece of the First Being's soul, the world's blank canvas and its load-bearing choice.

**Design.** Lean, blacksmith's hands (callused, one burn on the left thumb he never heals on purpose), messy dark hair, workman's coat too big for him (his father's). The **shifting scar** on his chest — a thin line from collarbone to sternum — is the on-page indicator of which realm he is touching: base **gray-green**, blooming **silver-green** near Sylvaris, **ember** near Khazadûm/Emberfall, **teal** near Thalassa, **gold** near Seraphel (canon: the scar's color is the indicator; Aldric's journal sketch, Ch. 1 P10.4, shows it in five colors — gray, green, ember, teal, gold). The **white threads** at its edge are the *cost* — they lengthen as he spends (tracked Ch. 1 P2.2 → Ch. 5; see the pilot canon list in the manga README).

**Palette.**
- Hair `#2b2b31` · Skin `#e8b88f` · Work coat `#6b5d4f` (soot-brown) · Undershirt `#c9c2b4`
- Scar base `#9aa5a1` · Scar bloom (Sylvaris) `#7fb069` · Scar bloom (Khazadûm) `#e07a3f` · Scar bloom (Thalassa) `#3e8e9e` · Scar bloom (Seraphel) `#e8b04b`
- White threads (the cost) `#f4f4f0`
- **Scar of absence** (Vaelthorn's presence, Ch. 6 only): the scar goes **white** — `#f4f4f0` over the base, the color of *absence*, the one color it has never been.

**Motifs & props.** The hammer (his, not Askstone's — a common smith's hammer, he fixes things with it before he argues) · the **Voss bell** (his father's last work, fist-sized, no ornament — the *mortal note*; it rings when names are returned, and once on its own — the pilot allows it once, Ch. 3 P10.3) · the journal (Aldric Voss's, given by Roderick, Ch. 2 P11.1) · the white threads.

**Pilot anchors.** Ch. 1 P2.2 (first bloom) · Ch. 1 P8.4 (first Name-Return, Raelin) · Ch. 2 P7.2–P8.1 (the grove's vote, the green ring) · Ch. 3 P8.3 ("I'm going to Sylvaris") · Ch. 5 P4 (the great Name-Return, "Arvel") · Ch. 6 P10 (the white scar, the Hollow).

**Pilot line (canon, verbatim — spoken to himself, to a new Mosaic child, and to the audience; three times in the story):** *"You're not a prophecy, kid. You're a person."*

**Adaptation notes.** His art *must* stay ordinary (no eye-glow, no power aura) — the power is the scar and the white threads, both small, both on the chest. TCG: the "Mosaic" frame (white light, all nine). PC game: the cost meter is the white threads; the player *sees* Kaelen fraying. **Vol. 4 (Ch. 17–20):** the volume keeps him *second* on purpose — he carries **Askstone in its cloth** for four chapters and only sets it down when the man it belongs to asks (Ch. 19); he **stands where Grimm can see him** (Ch. 17 P9.1 promise, kept at Ch. 19 P2.1); he **says the name** the mortal half of the Mountain-Ask requires — *“Durin Khazdûrin”* — with the realm's six memories standing behind him (Ch. 19 P6.1, **the Name-Return's rules respected: a name is a witness, not a cure**); and he **declines** to read a realm's dead out loud at a coal-face, handing the words back to the man who owned them (*“You've got the words. I've got the shapes, and the shapes are read.”*, Ch. 20 P10.2). **The scar's lean is Vol. 5's door and stays shut all volume** — the heartbeat stays steady, one degree of tilt, drawn and never discussed. **No pilot-line use in Volume 4** (two remain: a new Mosaic child; the saga's last page).

**Volume 5 (Ch. 29): the one thing he makes.** At the coda he fits the **rim foundry's clasp** — plate nine hundred and eleven, a class demonstration piece for nine thousand dwarves learning that a hinge has a direction, carried in his father's too-big coat since Ch. 21 — to the reader's collar (Fenn holds the lamp, Bramble holds the strap). His line is the plainest he says all volume: *it's a **buckle** … buckles are meant to come off; that's the entire design.* It is the first thing in the saga that is entirely his, and it is the realm's newest institution's only moving part. **Volume 6 (Ch. 31): buckles by the dozen.** He copies the rim foundry's clasp pattern — **twenty identical buckles in a cloth**, sent east with the realm's clerk so every post's collar closes the same way — and his work in the chapter is deliberately *boring*: the realm builds offices by the dozen, and the smith who made the first one makes the rest. **Cover-art rule (audit, Ch. 1–15 pass).** This note is *load-bearing* and applies to every painted cover, poster and key visual: **the nine colors never bloom on his skin.** Vol. 1’s first cover master drew the chest as a nine-color radial mandala with an aura; it was re-rendered and the old file kept beside the new one as *cover-vol1-v1-glow.png* ([../other/assets/](../other/assets/cover-vol1.png)). The approved v2 shows what the saga actually opens on (Ch. 1 P2.2): a **small, faint gray-green mark** — “a smudge of un-ink” — with its **first white thread** at the edge, easy to miss, on purpose. The Nine-Color Key belongs to the scarf, the record’s lights, the crown-citadel’s nine lanterns. On the boy, it is *one mark and the cost.*

**Ch. 40 — the sentence that lands later.** At the gate, watching the collar climb a word an evening, he says the chapter's most dangerous line as a throwaway to nobody — *it'll come up when there's somebody to say it to* — and is asleep again before any panel has understood him. Canon: nobody in the party connects the collar's unfinished sentence to the calling, and the reader is left holding both.

**Ch. 41 — asleep through the largest sentence in the realm's history.** The collar finishes its sentence at the gate at noon, the realm signs itself to a Contract and reads a door out loud for the first time in four thousand years — and Kaelen is drawn once, asleep against the gate-post with his boots up on a bale, with **no line at all**, because the chapter that pays his Ch. 40 throwaway is the chapter that finds out there was somebody to say it to after all: a queue, a crier, and seven hounds. Canon: nobody wakes him, and nobody needs to.

**Ch. 42 — awake for one frame, and asleep by the last page.** The realm's first ordinary morning gives him nothing and he takes nothing: drawn once on the bench outside the gate with his boots up, **awake for exactly one frame** — long enough to look at the board the realm has put up (the timetable, the hours, the lesson) — and asleep again before the page turns, and the chapter must not use him. His volume keeps its one rule: he is never the man who explains the realm to itself. Save him.

**Ch. 43 — one dangerous sentence, from a bench, with his boots up.** He says the chapter's most dangerous correct line about the man who sold a stranger two chips' worth of admission — *he'll be the first honest clerk the west ever had* — from the bench outside the gate with his eyes shut, and he is **asleep before anybody answers him**, because the answer is standing on the crates at the gate going west on the tide. He is drawn twice in the chapter and awake for exactly one of them, and nobody wakes him for either.

**Ch. 44 — the knife, called before it is drawn.** Half-asleep on the gate's bench with his boots up on a bale, watching the crates go west, he calls the west's answer before anybody in the realm has seen a rates board: *the west'll charge two chips for it and then take a knife to the price* — and is out before the last line of it lands. He is drawn three times in the chapter and awake for none of it (the bench in the morning, the bench at noon, the party's table-leg at the ninth hour), and the art team must draw him at the table-leg as **the only figure in the room who has not read the west's letter.** No figure may quote, answer or mention his sentence.

**Ch. 45 — the volume's last line, spoken from the bench with his boots up on the box.** Half-asleep through the disposition, the archive and the letter, he opens his mouth once and lands the whole instrument in nine words — *he can't make a promise he doesn't keep now. so he won't make any.* — and then, before the room can answer him: *that's the sentence; nobody had to read it to him.* The art team is instructed to draw the panel as the boy asleep on the bench with his mouth open and nobody waking him, because the volume's most accurate reading of its own ending is delivered by the one member of the Nine who is not listening. Canon: he is asleep again by the card, and the box is under his boots in both panels.

**Ch. 46 — the sentence from the billet, with his boots on the box.** Seraphel gives him nothing to fix and he takes nothing: drawn on the cart's step through the counting, on the floorboards of the rented shelf with his boots up on the box, and once from the yard's edge at the market watching halos get read like weather. His beat is the volume's cipher, delivered with his eyes shut from the billet on the second evening: *that's how it starts, every time. not with a purge. with a schedule with a blank in it. somebody's going to write a number in that blank, and it's going to be a small number, and it's going to be one — because they always start by counting one.* Canon: he reads Schedule IV correctly and says so to nobody in particular, and the chapter does not let anybody pick it up.

## 2 · LYRA SYLVARI — High Elf · 17 (elven: ~170 rings) · Sylvaris

**The edge and the conscience.** Last true Everbloom bearer. The elf who can't afford to lose anything, learning to choose the loss.

**Design.** Tall, **silver-green hair worn loose** against court custom (court elves bind theirs — hers flies; an exile's tell, and later, the mark of the woman who chose the grove over the crown), **growth-rings visible at her temples** (her age, drawn — a new ring appears when she spends or chooses: Ch. 2 P8.4, Ch. 5; the reader learns to count them). Everbloom's soft green glow under her cloak (the only *bright* thing on a page when she arrives, Ch. 2 P2.1). From Ch. 2 P9.2 on she wears the **nine-colored scarf** (Odette's knitting) — she hates it, never takes it off (the tsundere, in a scarf).

**Palette.**
- Hair `#b8c4b0` (silver-green) · Skin `#e9d5c3` · Temple rings `#9fb8a8`
- Court cloak `#3f4a44` · Everbloom glow `#a3c98a`
- The scarf (the Nine-Color Key, in realm order): `#b87333` `#7fb069` `#e07a3f` `#9aa5a6` `#3e8e9e` `#d1495b` `#e8b04b` `#7a6a9c` `#f4f4f0`
- White threads (her cost, shown when she says Arvel's name, Ch. 3 P12.1) `#f4f4f0`

**Motifs & props.** The rings (her age, her cost, her currency) · the scarf · the great tree (her cathedral) · the Crown-Touch she *refuses* in the pilot (Ch. 6: "I won't give you my self. I'll give you my *rings*.")

**Pilot anchors.** Ch. 2 P2.1 (arrival) · P2.3 (the pilot line, delivered) · P8.4 (the new ring) · Ch. 3 P9.3 (the private corner — the choosing) · Ch. 5 P1–P4 (Arvel) · Ch. 6 P5 (The First Dawn; the refusal).

**Pilot line (canon, verbatim):** *"I've waited three hundred years for something worth this. Don't waste it being brave."*

**Adaptation notes.** Her wall is a *courtesy* — the panels show the wall going up (composed) and down (one crack) as separate visual states; the lettering never explains them. **Vol. 4:** the noticer measures a foreign law against her own — *“The elves keep their law in a tree and the tree talks, and we spent eleven generations learning to answer it properly. These people kept their law in a book about lamp-oil…”* (Ch. 18 P5.1, Ch. 20 P10.2), and she gets **one almost-smile** in the volume (Ch. 17 P9.2), which is her only crack and must not be repeated casually. Her rings are her *health bar*: drawn, countable, spent.

**Ch. 44 — the question that produces the answer.** With the west's letter open in her hands she asks the only question the room has not asked — *did anybody in the west ask us for anything?* — and answers it herself off line seven: **send me the rules.** Not the readings, not a licence, not a stamp: the *shape* of the thing the realm did with them, because a form is the one thing in this world that travels for nothing and belongs to whoever is holding it. Her refusal to vote on the ruling is the chapter's pattern for her: *I don't want to vote on the ruling — I'd like the form to be the answer*, sent the day it is cut, because a clerk nine hundred miles away already has three sentences and nowhere to put them, *and I know that feeling exactly.*

**Ch. 45 — the question that makes the ruling say its own name.** When the chair brings the disposition to the slate she asks the one thing the room has not answered — *so what is the realm contracting him to? because if the answer is 'himself', say it in those words* — and it is her question, not the Court's, that forces the instrument to be written as an instrument: not a chair, not an office, not a punishment, but a fire entered on the side it was earned on. She is drawn asking it at the party's table with her eyes on the slate and not on the man, because the question is for the realm; and when the gold goes up over the table and not over the party, she is the one who does not look at it again.

**Ch. 46 — a grove's eye on a realm that counts instead of voting.** She is drawn at the balustrade with her scarf over her mouth at the top of the realm, at the counting-stand as a witness, and once at the window of the shelf with her temple-rings catching dawn-gold and **counted by the art team on the page** — because her cost is a number and this is the first volume where the realm's whole law is one. Her one line's worth of position is the careful one: an elf of a grove that has never needed a census finds Seraphel's arithmetic *honest* and stays to see what it cannot count, and the chapter gives her no verdict on the schedule — she is holding it, and the volume's treasury of grudges is nobody's yet. Canon: no ring is added, and no bloom, in this realm of light.

## 3 · GRIMM KHAZDÛRIN — Deep Dwarf · 19 · Khazadûm

**The hammer and the heart.** Forge-knight, heir to a family forge that's been cold a century. Loud, loyal, feeds everyone he meets; grieving a *relative* (the mountain) he can no longer hear.

**Design.** Stocky, **beard in twelve braids at debut** (nine tight — family; three loose, unfinished — debt; **the count goes to thirteen in Ch. 15** when the mine's contract braid is tied in, and **stays thirteen through Vol. 4** — contract braid out, Grudka's flame-clan braid in, Ch. 21; the reader is meant to count, and the standard three-quarter angle always shows it), stone-scarred hands, the hammer **Askstone** at his hip (his grandfather's, made "to ask the mountain things" — *old* and *polished* the way a family knife is). The silent beat: his hand *pauses* on stone, listening (the stone-song went quiet the day the Reforgers started mining — the art team gives it one silent beat, Ch. 3 P3.2, no dialogue).

**Palette.**
- Beard — family braids `#7a4a2b` · debt braids `#4a3527` (loose, faded)
- Skin `#c98d68` · Forge-knight armor `#5b5f66` · Harness `#6b5d4f`
- Askstone — haft `#5a4632` (polished old wood) · head `#8a8f98` (steel, worn bright)
- Stone-song (**SPENT — manga Ch. 19 P4.1**): a faint `#e07a3f` vein-light in the rock — the **mountain's** money color, held for Vol. 4 and spent in *The Naming*: **at scale, on the surface, as a bloom**, arriving as **rings travelling the stone's own grain** up the wet hairline of the raw wall (never a beam, never a halo, never on a person, never on the book). Ch. 19 is its one appearance at scale; from Ch. 20 the mountain is *warm*, not lit, and the bloom is not restaged. *(The bloom is seen from outside once — P9.1, Bastion's sentence.)* *(Audit note, Ch. 13: S1's use of the hue is the under-roads' answering ember-line — first cracked open Ch. 10 P9.2, carried as scene-light through Ch. 11–13: under the floor, one line, never a bloom.)*

**Motifs & props.** The braids — **twelve at debut, thirteen from Ch. 15** (count them) · Askstone · the geode (Pebble's one idea: *somewhere's a geode, and it's his*) · the bridge (he rebuilds what falls — "a door that doesn't hold is just a hole with opinions").

**Vol. 4 anchors (from Ch. 14).** The braids become the reader's clock: **nine tight, three loose, drawn identically and countably every page of Volume 4** (Ch. 14 P2.2 states the account; **P8.2 is one silent frame of him counting and not finishing** — no balloon, no reaction shot). The **name-stone** takes heat out of his palm (hoarfrost, no rejection — P4.2). **Askstone stays wrapped** until the arc earns it (P2.2, P5.2, P10.1) — the hammer is *made to ask the mountain things* and its owner is not ready to be seen asking. **Ch. 17: the anvil's walk** — he carries his grandfather's anvil down the fifty-eight with the century's coal lashed into its base-hollow, and the street lays hands on the iron as it passes; **Kaelen carries Askstone** (still wrapped — it opens in the arc's finale, not before); and his ask to Kaelen (P9.1) is the volume's heart: *if the mountain stays silent, say Durin Khazdûrin aloud, with the realm's own memory.* His pilot line's second half is spent at his own forge's door (P5.2). The arc's private mercy so far: **one drop** off the anvil (P10.2). **Ch. 18: his own entry is read out.** Plate nine hundred and six reads **Khazdûrin, Grimm — one word, held, thirteen braids, forfeit to the mine**, in front of nine thousand people, and the art team must draw **his hands empty and nowhere near his braids** (P7.1). Two lines lower on the same plate, in identical type, is a miner's entry — which is what finally puts a man on his feet in the ninth rank. His ask from Ch. 17 P9.1 hangs **undischarged** over Chapter 19 — and **Ch. 19 pays it**: he unwraps **Askstone** at the head of the aisle, lays it on the anvil that has waited a hundred and eleven years, and **asks** (“Are you in there?”, P2.1–P2.2). It is a workman's blow, not a hero's. Then the volume's frost-device turns: the **hoarfrost palm-print melts**, the iron goes *work-warm*, and **he is the first person in the mine to know something is happening, with a deaf ear and his palms** (P3.2). **Ch. 19 P6.2:** the anvil is warm, and he says two words — *“…It's warm.”* — which is the pilot line's rhyme and may never be said verbatim again. The hammer ends the chapter **lying open on its own family's anvil**, unwrapped, unguarded, carried by nobody: the volume's withheld object, finished (P11.2). **Ch. 20:** he carries **the pot** — the family's last coal, kept by Bjorn for a century — down the fifty-eight and gives it up to the flame-clan's hearth (*“It's one coal… it's no good to my family cold. Where do you want it?”*), and the braid-count's last movement is his: the **thirteenth** stays (the void forfeit is still his record), and the new form's bar hangs **on a nail beside the grandfather's frame** at the rim house. **Still held for the coda:** the Khazdûrin forge's relight (his *“Tomorrow, Ma”* is the last beat of Ch. 20) and Grudka's braid-adoption. **Ch. 15: the count becomes thirteen** — the mine's **contract braid**, tied by Norga at the signing bench in front of Maren (P6.1); and his deafness now has a fixed panel grammar (a Grimm-at-the-wall panel carries **no SFX and no balloons** — a silent window inside a noisy room, Ch. 15 P8.1).

**Ch. 21 (the coda).** The count is paid: **the mine's contract braid comes out** (Norga, strand by strand, on his own say-so — nobody cuts a braid and the knot is *kept*) and **Grudka's flame-clan braid goes in** — nine tight, three loose, one new, **thirteen either way, and the reader can count it in the standard three-quarter angle**. He relights his father's forge and its first work is **a hinge for his mother's kitchen door**, its second is a neighbour's pot hook (*“Bring the other one. …We're open.”*); the chapter's one silent beat is **his palm flat on the warm anvil face** (no glow, no song, no caption) and the chapter's last frame is him **asleep against the anvil-block at nineteen**, hammer in his lap. **His pilot line is NOT delivered here** — it was spent in Ch. 3 P3.2, and the volume's closing prose carries the echo instead. **Pilot anchors.** Ch. 3 P3 (debut, the bridge) · P11.1 (the bridge plan) · Ch. 4 P1 (the bridge *holds*) · Ch. 5 (the first death, beside Kaelen).

**Pilot line (canon, verbatim):** *"My dad's forge's been cold a hundred years. I'm not leaving until it's warm again."*

**Adaptation notes.** His comedy is *feeding people* (he carries extra bread, extra stone, extra everything). His tragedy is one silent beat per chapter — do not over-draw it.

**Ch. 40 — the chisel he does not take.** Offered the chisel for Emberfall's first new form, he puts his own away: in Khazadûm a form is a **stake** and a cut is a **debt**, and you never let a neighbour hold the chisel, because then the debt's in his hand and not in yours — *cut your own law; I'll stand here and be jealous.* Canon: the mountain's rule is why the realm's first stone law is cut by the realm's own hand.

**Ch. 41 — the four words the realm asks him for.** The realm finds its oldest sentence cut clean through a leaf and asks the only smith in the room the only question that matters — can a cut through a plate happen by accident — and Grimm answers in his own trade: *no; accidents chip, accidents crack, accidents go wide and they go out to an edge; what you have there is even, narrow, and deliberate, cut from both faces and dressed where it met.* Then the plain version, which is the chapter's title in a smith's mouth: ***somebody wanted a door.*** He puts his thumb on the cut and takes it off; he does not touch the chisel, the leaf, or the finding; and he is asleep sitting up in the afternoon.

**Ch. 42 — a fist, not a hand.** The realm's first roadside forgery is put in his hand at the gate desk under the lamp, and the smith's verdict is craft and nothing else: *round edge, a burr standing, torn one way — that's a drill and a man in a hurry; a cut takes the material out and comes back and dresses where it met.* He touches the **rim** and not the bore. And the chapter gives him the line the realm should be afraid of, delivered as a tradesman's arithmetic: *you don't cut a tray of them unless you've got somebody buying.* He re-hardens nothing and sells nothing; his own shop's new loss (the realm teaching the world to look at a cut, free) is settled by the chair, not by him, and he does not ask for it once.

**Ch. 43 — the cloth, and a thing that is supposed to wear out.** He checks the realm's first publication between thumb and finger at the forge bench and gives it the only number his trade has for paper — **four winters in a pocket** — and says out loud that in forty years of pins, keystones and buckles he has never once held a thing that was *supposed* to fail. He lays the sheet down flat afterwards, which is the only way he knows how to be respectful to a document.

**Ch. 44 — how to cut a thing that will be copied badly on purpose.** He takes the form's proof at the bench's end and gives the forge the rule that makes the whole chapter work — *no ornament a bad hand can improve* — then lays the proof down flat, which is the only way he knows how to be respectful to a document. His verdict on the realm's first form is also its design brief: a stranger must be able to copy it with a nail, or get nothing that looks like it.

**Ch. 45 — turn it.** The realm turns its own fire around in front of the saga's smith, and he gives the instrument its working sentence with a rule laid flat on the blank plate: *it doesn't only burn what breaks; it listens to what holds. same fire, other side. — turn it.* It is his trade's whole grammar — instrument before hand, tool before fear — and it is the reason the realm hands its oldest enforcement mechanism to a man it spent eight chapters refusing to charge. Canon: he is the only person in the chapter who talks about the Oathfire as a *tool* rather than a sentence, and the plate he lays his rule on is the plate the ruling is cut into.

**Ch. 46 — no anvil, no chisel, nothing to weld.** He gets the axle and the last of his own tallow in the courtyard and does the party's maintenance in a realm whose law is shorter than his beard, and the chapter's whole portrait of him is a smith with no work: boots flat on the flagstones, listening to a realm where nobody enforces anything and finding it *worse* than the mine. He is drawn in the ring's audience with his sleeves squared and his hands crossed on his belt, once, and given no line — the volume's shortest paragraph is his, and the art team must draw him *reading the halos*, because that is the only trade skill he has in Seraphel.

## 4 · MORGATHA ULRIK — Orc · 20 · Ulgar

**The blade and the moral engine.** Exiled blood-clan captain. Honorable, furious, afraid of her own rage — Titanvein means her anger is a fuel line, and she has seen what a full tank does. **The count is the tank gauge**: she counts her breaths in the war-song **Ulgan** sings, and Ch. 24 (P4.2) at last puts the two facts in one frame — the vein lit, the count kept, the realm watching and *approving*, because in a realm whose power is endurance, a chief seen holding her own rage in public is the **credential** for carrying a clause about lives.

**Design.** Massive, **bone-and-iron regalia**, scars written in **oath-script across both forearms** — *legible* (the art team's key detail: a reader who's paid attention can see they're *words*; her body is a library, and every page's a promise). One braid over her shoulder that ends **cut** — the oath she's still keeping (Tarkhan's, her brother's, taken by a Reforgers' cell). The **war-song count**: she counts her breaths in a war-song; the art team prints the count in the corner of her frames (**four… three… two… one…**) — the pilot's hidden clock. *When the count stops, that's the panel.* (Ch. 4 P8: one panel, the cell's method. Ch. 6: the rest — she counts to *zero*, on purpose.)

**Palette.**
- Skin (deep orc green) `#5c6e58` · Oath-script scars `#d8c3a5` (scar-white, *words*)
- Bone regalia `#e8e0cf` · Iron `#5b5f66` · Braid `#3a3f3a`
- The cut end (the unfinished oath): the thread shown *loose*, `#d8c3a5` — never re-knotted in the pilot
- Titanvein (rage, Vol. 5): a faint vein-light `#d1495b` under the skin when she's close — **SPENT, manga Ch. 24 P4.2**, at the Ultravote itself: when the house of Tarkhan votes **no** on the clause that would free her father, the vein shows **once**, thin, under the skin, inside the right forearm, **never a glow and never an aura**, and she **holds it** — the war-song count the reader has watched for twenty-three chapters is her Titanvein discipline, and **Ulgan names it out loud as the credential**: *the realm's own power, under a lid, on the day the realm votes on lives.* **It must not appear at a stop** (Ch. 22's rule) and **is never restaged in Volume 5.**

**Ch. 42 — the lesson, taken down with everybody else.** He sits in the second row at the gate in the rain with a slate like the rest of the queue and gets the chapter's driest line, delivered while he is writing: *nothing in it is mine — I'd like it read anyway.* Canon note for the art team: he writes with the same hand everybody else is using, because the page's whole argument is that the realm's new skill is **learnable** and not revelation. **Ch. 46 — read by every halo in the street, before anybody says a word to him.** The chapter is his and it is built to be almost wordless: the market where five separate angels read his statute and look away (and one old woman hands the changeling an extra ring of bread without a word), the bread-seller's kindness that is *not* tolerance (*in this realm we don't judge a book by its crack; we read it*), the walk to the stand, the refusal of the courtesy, both hands out of his sleeves, and **count me** — with the line the whole volume hangs on: *I'd take it as a kindness if you would all stop calling it a fall. it was a walk, and it took three years, and the door was never locked.* Then the hearing, and the shoulders coming down half an inch, because what he wanted was a **date**. ## 7 · VIVIENNE "VIV" NOX — Vampire · 15 (looks) / unknown (actually) · Nocturne

**Ch. 31 — the sixth clause, and the margin.** On the flats below the camp, alone, with no door, no clerk and no listener, she reads the realm's law aloud as practice and gets the other half of her own doctrine out of the emptiness: ***a reading is a request to be heard; a door is only the place where somebody is*** — entered **in her own hand** in the margin of the horn's copy (*entered by the advocate, at the edge, on the flats, alone*), with Kharza's line added under it (*read at the door, held at the counter, and asked for at the flats*). The gate-clerk sits on a rock behind her for three evenings doing the camp's accounts and nobody mentions it. **The scholar and the spark.** Vampire prodigy who carries an umbrella and sunscreen like armor. Sharp, curious, *happier than she's allowed to be* — the dead's society runs on restraint; Viv runs on questions.

**Ch. 32 — the first client who asked her to write without a clause about signing.** Asked by the sea itself to **keep the sentence** (*write it, read it where the water cannot hear, bring it back where the water can*), she does the one thing no tribunal in five volumes ever made her do: she writes **without arguing first** — the sea's instrument, in **five articles**, entered in the horn's copy below the collar's terms and her own margin clause. Articles **one** and **two** are hers (*the sea may not be bound by anything written; the sea may be answered by anything written, read aloud at its water*); **five** is Kharza's; and the chapter's thesis lands in her mouth as a *notice*, not a speech: *four words, because a welcome only ever needs four words — and a law can be refused, and **then come in** cannot.* Her line at the fire: *some doors open because somebody is waiting behind them.*

**Ch. 33 — the inventor of *an ear*.** Faced with the founder's four-line letter (*a reader at the mark. Mine. On the books.*), she does what she has done since Ch. 22 — takes the realm's own inventory apart: who may speak (the throat), who may read (the collar), who may cross (the stone), who may be held (a surety), who may owe (everybody) — and finds the term the code does not have: **a person who is read to.** She coins it (**an ear**), insists the founder be entered as *that* and not as the founder, and gets it cut into the horn's outside by Khura's graver. Her clause for the crossings — *the welcome is read beside the stone and opens nothing; the stone's law stands; no person may be bound by it and no person may use it* — is drafted on the road and cut by Kharza the same morning, which is the chapter's quiet joke at her expense: five volumes of tribunals, and the first argument she ever wins is won in a back room before lunch.

**Ch. 35 — a shift and a voice.** Her definition settles the sea's oldest law against the world's newest employer, worked out at a table borrowed from the company's own waiting room with the contract laid beside the roll's call slip and a finger on each: *whose call is the Re-Oath? not the company's · the call names the reef, and the reef is not on your roster · clause four says* **a week** *— a week of singings, not a voice, not a person, not an office.* The finding she asks to be written, and gets: **a company bought shifts and never bought a chorus place, because a chorus place was never the company's to be offered.** She thanks nobody and shakes nobody's hand; she *stands up with the clerk at the same time*, and the art team must draw two professionals, a table, a paper, and no anger in the panel anywhere.

**Ch. 34 — the publication argument, and a client who is an ocean.** Her case for the realm is the one she has been building since Ch. 32: nobody sold these words; the deep gave the second line at the flood, in the water, to nine persons, unasked; and *a sentence read aloud to nobody who pays is not a loss, it is a **publication*** — which is what the sea asked for in the only grammar it has. She concedes the company's honest work out loud (eleven clerks, weighbridges, the road the realm's readers walk on) and she wins by **definition**, not by penalty: the four findings turn on the sea's own words. The chapter also pays her oldest wound plainly: the instrument licenses **persons**, and a charter is not a person — *five volumes of courts, and the first bench she stands in front of asks the right question.*

**Ch. 39 — the number, and the daily reading.** She does two things no outsider has done in this realm: she reads four days of a realm's testimony **for its grammar instead of its content** and puts ***4,206*** on a slate in front of a table of archdukes — *your realm has spent eight years keeping promises that were made on a name that no longer exists* — and then she asks the chamber for a *habit* instead of a right: **the reserved ninth field read aloud at the opening of every session, in full, in the Court's ordinary voice, until it is signed or void.** Canon, and the volume's sharpest legal fact: the realm could not give her a statute for it, so **the archduke of the small oaths moved it as a practice, kept by whoever holds the stylus.** Her chapter's last image is a fifth line on her slate, unfiled, *till I've read it twice in daylight.*

**Ch. 38 — the reservation.** The volume's first chapter belongs to her in the largest way: she **reads the Court's own form aloud in front of a packed gallery** (a thing nobody in Emberfall has done in four thousand years), asks the question the whole volume then answers (*what does the burning burn?*), works out at a rented table that **six of her colleagues cannot put their own names on a line**, and then does the only thing left to a person whose name is held: she **puts the stylus down** and has the Court enter a **reservation** in its own hand — *not a refusal and not a void; a line a careful party leaves when they intend to come back* — with the sentence the volume stands on: **until it is mine again.** Canon: she does not sign anything twice, and the reserved field is hers to come back to.

**Ch. 37 — the contract's term, and the word the code lacked.** *The document has reached its term.* She files the last piece of Volume 6's law as a formality — *a contract that has been performed is not a contract any more, it is a **receipt**, and this realm keeps receipts in a book and does not keep paper in a pocket* — and then gives the horn of the Barrens the one entry it has never had room for: **a story** (a thing that only lives while it is being told, and dies the moment somebody keeps it), cut in the same stroke as the ring. Canon: from Ch. 37 the realm's books have a place for a thing that was *told*.

**Ch. 36 — six lines on a rock, and one of them about the crown.** She drafts the contract with Azrael in wet stone and one sheet of paper, and the chapter's whole legal spine is hers: *the parts are theirs — the realm may sing them and may not keep them, and may not sell them, and may not give them away*, and *a realm that improvises a ritual in public is a realm that is going to invent a **debt** by accident.* Her line about the crown is the one the art team must letter above the stand: **nothing here is owed to the crown and nothing here is owed for it** — which is the same woman who has spent five volumes refusing to let a realm be bound by anything it did not say out loud.

**Design.** Pale, **candle-gold eyes**, a **Regency pin** in her hair — worn *on purpose*, the find-me pin (if her Council wants to find her, they can; the treason pin, the art team's tell). A tailored coat, a **folded umbrella** under one arm (it is *sunny* — a vampire in the sun, and she is *out* in it; the pilot's first visual joke of the Nocturne realm), a **sunscreen vial** in the other hand (the first lie, named: *the Council says the sun burns. **Adaptation notes.** She is *always* mid-sentence (the reader joins her argument in progress — the lettering starts every frame with an em-dash). Her double is one frame *late*, never ahead. Write the hands.”*) — then tells the realm to **elect its keeper yearly** and put the election in the book. **Her one defeat is drawn, not spoken:** Ch. 18 P9.2, she has no counter to Drek's agreement, and the art team must simply show her *not speaking*.

**Ch. 40 — the document that isn't there, and the field she keeps.** She asks the Court for the **form** that fills an office and gets the realm's oldest disability instead — there is no such form and has not been one since the First Contract — and then watches Emberfall cut its first new form in four thousand years with **no candidate field in it**, which is the realm's own proof that a standing reservation can be held in place: *I am not signing anything, and when I hand the field over it will be mine to hand.* Canon: she signs nothing, the ninth field ends the chapter still ruled and empty — a woman holding an empty field on purpose — and the realm's newest law is not allowed to solve it.

**Ch. 41 — the field read first, and the pen refused twice.** At the realm's largest promise her reserved line is the **first thing read aloud** in the chamber — before the form, before the agenda, on the chair's own order — and when the realm offers her the stylus in public she declines it in public, twice, with her hand flat on her own field, because the realm's new form is exactly the proof that she could have signed anything she wanted. Her closing entry is the whole of the parties' position: ***the realm kept its word to us today and we kept ours; the field stands; nobody signed it for her.***

**Ch. 42 — the guarantee, written before anybody may speak.** The second book is Viv's chapter as much as the reader's: before a leaf is cut she makes the realm put its honesty in writing — *no entry may be collected, cited, or charged; a reading is not a ruling; the guarantee gets read aloud at the top of every reading* — because she has spent a volume watching forms become traps and she will not let the realm build one with a soft floor. Later she supplies the only legal ground the realm has for the roadside chip, out of a law *she* had sealed in the Barrens (***a copy that declares itself a copy of a reading, and not a law***), and her diagnosis of the sale is the chapter's: *the chip doesn't lie; the mouth lies, and the mouth is out of the realm.* Her own field is read first, as always, and the second book's first page does not touch it.

**Ch. 43 — the four lines, and the first statement that promises nothing.** The realm's first publication is drafted at her table out of the only law in nine realms about copies: *this is a copy · it may be copied again, by anybody, for nothing · none of this is sold; nobody's permission is required; bring nothing.* She reads all four aloud before anybody may cut anything, and asks the record to note that this is the first thing this realm has ever said out loud that **promises nothing at all** — which, in a realm where promises hang in the air in gold, is a legal fact and not a joke.

**Ch. 44 — witnesses don't get expenses, and the realm owns nothing but its promise.** She refuses the realm's refund of the two chips of carriage out of the parties' own ledger's wording (*not lent; not advanced; paid*), gives the chapter its first flat truth — *we're witnesses — witnesses don't get expenses* — and then delivers the ownership finding at the Court's table: the realm owns nothing a stranger can take away, because the only property it has ever had that *grows* when somebody else reads it is **its promise**; *no loss has occurred.*

**Ch. 45 — the numbers, and the line left empty.** She reads the trial's last page as a ledger and not as a speech — 61,204 voices; 4,206 promises made on the cut-out name and **not one called in**; 406 dead with the eleven burned names and read out with the rest — and then makes the chapter's legal turn on a blank: the line under the disposition is entered **deliberately empty** on her motion, *because the moment we take a promise off that man, we've made him a party to something, and this realm has never once bought its safety with a signature.* It is the volume's largest refusal and it is a lawyer's refusal, which is what her Ch. 38 reservation has been walking toward for eight chapters.

**Ch. 46 — the fine print, read in a realm that puts its law on paper.** She reads the absence of a gate (*a door nobody keeps is not a welcome; it's an assumption*), finds the field the form does not have (*in this realm, a person with no wings isn't refused — a person with no wings is unrepresentable*), reads the courtesy of the count down to its lining (*something that is not counted here is not protected here; it is not seen here; it is not anywhere here*), and reads Schedule I and IV on the Orders' table with her own pen in her hand and never once on their paper — *that's not a schedule yet; that's a room, and you're leaving it empty until somebody signs a number into it.* Then the practical brief at the ring, which is her only tenderness in public: *nine days. right. then here's what I want before the sun's off that post* — one, two, three — *and I'm asking whether you want us in the room, because the ones where somebody's name is on the table have been the ones where the nine of us stand up.* Canon: her thirty-eight pages of notes are not drawn in this chapter; her ledger is shut twice, and both times the art team must show it.

**Pilot line (canon, verbatim — delivered Ch. 1 P12.2, the first time he says it out loud):** *"I've mapped nine realms for three hundred years. The world's got a* tenth *and it's been* quiet. *That's never a good sign, kid. That's a* hungry *sign."*

**Adaptation notes.** He is the reader's *hand* — his captions are the book's spine (always bottom right, always his hand, always the last word on the page). **Vol. 4 (Ch. 15–20):** he *times the volume* — the twelve days cut into the frame edge (Ch. 15–17), the **cascade delivered early and broken on purpose** in Ch. 17 P11.1 (*“the room holds at the realm”* is the last line, because the chapter's real last word is a silence), the reading-line and the naming-line counted flat through Ch. 18–19, and Ch. 20 P10.1's **first warm mark in three hundred years** — fire added to the map. **His open ledger:** *six pins left, still no letters from the twins* (Ch. 17, 18, 19, 20 — do not close it in Volume 4). Marginalia is a *character* (the lettering team: tiny italics, in the margin, always); its three-hundred-year argument with Pebble and Viv is the pilot's running gag (the gag is *continuity*: the same three lines, in different orders, each time).

**Ch. 40 — the mark that has to be made again tomorrow.** His map gains its first **daily** mark: under the eighth mark of the signature-line, a stroke with a tick and two words in his own small hand — ***re-read*** — because an office with no name in it cannot be *drawn*; it can only be kept, and keeping shows up on a map as a mark that has to be made again every day. Ch. 39's *promises get drawn* gains its practice.

**Ch. 41 — the first mark in nine realms for a door.** The mapper closes the volume's largest day with the one thing his craft has never had to draw: *a road's a line, a river's a line, a law's a line with a wall round it — a door's the only thing I've ever been asked to draw that isn't a thing at all.* His map gains **two strokes and a gap**, lettered in his own small hand: *gate, ash, twelfth day, open both ways.*

**Ch. 42 — the mark he does not draw yet.** He is drawn once at the edge of a Court page with the map unrolled and his hand already on the chalk, and the chapter stops him there on purpose: the realm's first thing-that-is-not-anywhere has happened (a copy made up the road, a lesson taught at a gate, a fee that arrived from nobody) and Fenn's craft has no mark for it. **He draws nothing in this chapter.** The map's next layer — and the mark for a thing with no place on it — is the next chapter's business, and the art team must draw the *unmarked* map pulled out and put away twice.

**Ch. 43 — the first mark in nine realms for a thing that is not anywhere.** Asked to mark a document designed to exist in nine hundred pockets at once, he cuts **two strokes, side by side, equal, with a clear gap** — *nothing else on the page has two of anything* — and explains it as a surveyor explains a boundary: check the words, then check the marks; if the marks are missing, single or lopsided, somebody has changed a word and any reader who has seen a true copy knows it at a glance. Drawn at the bench with the chalk in his hand and the map **rolled and untouched**, because this mark is not for a place.

**Ch. 44 — the map's first line that is not a road.** He draws the water-road dotted to a small circle at a second landing, rules it in two passes the same as every road on the sheet, letters the note at the circle in his own hand, and tells Sprocket why the line stays dotted: *the map's got a line on it now that's not about ground — it's about a habit*, and *a solid line would be a lie by spring.*

**Ch. 45 — the map rolled, not admired.** The volume's last geography page is his, and it is built out of the things that are not roads: a gap, a dotted line to a desk nine hundred miles west, a set of posted hours, and an instrument hanging over one man. He gives the map's key the sentence that closes the volume's whole argument — *readings: the hours, as posted. these are not roads. they are what the roads are for* — and rules the map's last handling with a mapper's bluntness: *we'll need it again, and i'd rather it was rolled than admired.* The west's letter goes inside the roll; the tally slate goes face-down under it; and the volume's handoff to Seraphel is a case, not a monument.

**Ch. 46 — the map gets a layer with no roads on it.** He is drawn at the water-butt with the map unrolled over it, at the post sketching the knife-scars, and at the rail on the climb; his chapter's work is the new layer — **terraces, the stand, the spire, the quarter's rim; and not one road** — because the sky realm's geography is *habit* and the map has no mark for a habit yet, which is his real problem and the volume's. And he gets no line at the star-stand — the one delivered there is Sprocket's, as work and not as wonder (two hundred years of charts, and the count has never come up short) — because the mapper's whole beat in Seraphel is that he is one layer behind a realm whose geography is habit. Canon: the smudge at the chart's corner is not drawn into his map.
